## Assessment

The historical argument now carries. The book establishes more than the general proposition that authority and implementation matter: Chinese membership and acquired legal capacity made an external challenge available against powerful trading partners; subsequent agreements specified consequential administrative and scrutiny duties; industrial dependence gave Chinese export permissions a concrete place in reciprocal bargaining. The current WTO update usefully prevents the first development from becoming a story of uninterrupted institutional progress. These claims are intelligible without pretending to have measured a national change in patience, deception or trustworthiness.

Several episodes now sustain an actual narrative: Woodcock carrying revised instructions to Deng; Wu Yi and Barshefsky disagreeing about whose support the package needed; Prueher obtaining permission to narrow his objective; Xie explaining competing estimates and reciprocal acceptability; the two agricultural witnesses disagreeing about enforcement; Russian residents exchanging established use for a permit; CNPC needing domestic prices that could support imports. These are the book's assets. They should carry more of the explanation than repeated paragraphs announcing that a distinction is practical, consequential or important.

The remaining draft is still too often a lucid commentator standing between the reader and an interesting decision. Some passages deliver the case, its interpretation, a qualification of that interpretation and a practical restatement in rapid succession. The restraint is intellectually valuable; the repeated explanatory sequence weakens pace and authorial confidence. A press-quality revision should chiefly change the distribution of explanation and strengthen synthesis. It should not respond by adding another inventory of sources or by removing every uncertainty.

## 1. Make the synthesis distinguish bargains a reader should value differently

**Priority:** highest substantive return. **Anchors:** conclusion, “The price of a limited bargain,” especially lines 13–19; Chapter 6's permissions section; Chapter 7's final comparison.

The text correctly says that partial agreements can be useful, that limits can change and that price matters. It now needs one sharper comparison that the reader could not get from a generic negotiation manual. The book supplies it: some unresolved issues lie outside an act that has already been delivered; others govern the next occasion on which the benefit must be obtained. The EP-3 crew, a shipment under a retained control system, renewable access to an island and a promise about a review process do not leave the beneficiary with the same exposure.

Replace the conclusion's short paragraph beginning “Limited agreements can also have benefits…” with a comparison along these lines, retaining its existing crew and channel references and adding the already used Chapters 6–7 references:

> A partial agreement should be judged by what the unfinished business can still prevent. The EP-3 crew could leave while responsibility for the collision remained disputed. That argument did not have to be won to accomplish the immediate task. Export permissions posed a different problem. A shipment could reach a factory while the next shipment, customer or use still required approval. The Russian border residents likewise received continuing access on terms that exposed them to expiry and individual refusal. In those bargains, the unresolved future remained inside the benefit being purchased.
>
> Before accepting a narrower agreement, ask which remaining decision could stop the intended benefit, whether the benefit requires repeated permission, and what one's own government must give before it knows that permission will continue. Immediate relief may be worth that exposure. Its price should reflect the exposure, particularly if the reciprocal concession is harder to reverse. The number of subjects left unsettled is a poor measure of how much work the agreement has actually finished.

This is an authorial comparison of established facts, not a new empirical result or a verdict on the undisclosed price of London. It makes the conclusion do something more useful than recall another successful limited bargain. Do not present any one category as automatically acceptable or unacceptable; the distinction changes the question to be priced.

## 3. Give the 2023 sequence the explanatory task its evidence can perform

**Priority:** the largest remaining mismatch between narrative question and recovered answer. **Anchors:** introduction line 47; Chapter 4, “Reopening one channel while another remained closed” through the November summit.

The introduction asks what brought the limited military-channel agreement within reach. The chapter recovers the American preparation of the Sullivan–Wang channel, distinguishes unsuccessful military requests from continued diplomacy, and documents the November commitment. It does not recover the Chinese decision that changed the answer on military mechanisms between June and November. The documented counternarcotics exchange cannot supply that missing explanation. The chapter correctly says so, but its accumulation of intervening encounters encourages the expectation of a causal payoff it cannot deliver.

The available evidence can sustain a strong, narrower question: **what did gaining access accomplish, and what remained unavailable despite it?** Make that the announced question, retain the June refusal after Blinken's access to Xi, and then show what the November texts newly committed the governments to do. Treat the deliberate preparation of Sullivan's channel as a recovered negotiation over the terms of communication, attributed to the American accounts. Do not make it the proven cause of military restoration.

Two immediate edits require no new evidence:

- Move the entire paragraph beginning “The leadership's knowledge of the balloon mission…” (Chapter 4, line 75) to note 17. No later decision in the narrative turns on an established answer. In the body it opens an investigation the chapter does not resolve.
- Compress the delisting qualification at line 109. Retain explicitly that the established exchange concerned counternarcotics, not military channels, and that delisting did not settle the human-rights concerns. The rest of the paragraph's evidentiary explanation belongs in note 28. The source-supported policy price is more interesting than an extended warning against correlating every summit deliverable.

The Kissinger section earns a place because the original book made cultivation important. Its access, Chinese requests and American reception are positive findings. Avoid presenting the July visit as another implied step toward November merely because it sits between June and November. A short transition can say that it answers a different question: whether personal access still offered something the formal defense channel did not. Its unestablished effect should be stated once, not repeatedly.

If the author retains the stronger question of why military restoration became acceptable, the next useful research target is evidence connecting a particular proposal, instruction or exchange to that decision. More summit readouts would not answer it. Failure to recover such material need not block finishing the chapter; narrowing its explanatory task is a legitimate editorial choice.

## 4. Stop reopening Solomon's validation hearing inside every later case

**Priority:** necessary to give the book a forward-moving argument. **Anchors:** Chapter 1's senior-intervention discussion; Chapter 3 lines 125–129; Chapter 4 line 87; Chapter 5 lines 33 and 93; Chapter 7 line 149; conclusion lines 29 and 63.

The manuscript is now faithful to the strength and qualifications of the originals. But it repeatedly tells the reader that a later example does not prove or refute a general proposition, does not establish a comparative ranking, and does not discover something Freeman already knew. That is appropriate for an evidence appendix. Repeated in the narrative, it can make the update sound as though its central achievement is avoiding an invalid claim about another book.

Give the exact strong prediction and its problem their main treatment in Chapter 1. Preserve one clear return in the conclusion. Elsewhere, state the concrete consequence of the episode and put the relation to the formal proposition assessment in the existing note.

In particular:

- Chapter 4's June paragraph should end after Blinken's inability to secure the requested military outcome. Delete the final sentence beginning “The observation belongs in a test…”. The counterexample remains fully visible.
- Chapter 7's final paragraph can keep the differences among an authorization, endorsement and unfinished commercial declaration. Move its sentence about comparative ranking and validation to note 45.
- Chapter 3's final section need not begin with another reminder that Solomon had technical detail and Freeman had recognized learning. That reminder is now in Chapters 1 and 8. Lead with the decision an expert could alter.

This is not permission to soften S-P24 or recategorize unresolved claims as false. It is a division of literary labor: establish the inherited claim accurately, let the cases produce knowledge, and assess the inheritance once the reader has that knowledge.

## 5. Let Chapter 2 own the resolution of the introductory deadline

**Priority:** useful after the more substantive changes above. **Anchors:** introduction lines 15–23; Chapter 2, “The price of another day.”

The documented opening is good. It supplies people, a time limit, competing permissions and a package. But the introduction then follows the deadline through Zhu's meeting, the second letter, signature and the limits of that signature. Chapter 2 later narrates the same chain with fuller evidence. The duplication spends the reversal before the chapter reaches it.

Keep the first letter, stakes, ownership/control example and Deng's account of sector clearance. Consider removing the four paragraphs from “The Americans' stated deadline passed” through “What had the weekend accomplished?” Let Chapter 2 supply that resolution. The bridge into the thesis could be:

> Barshefsky was asking for more than another meeting. She needed a decision about which limits China would defend, which terms could change and what Washington would have to accept in return. The apparent deadline concealed several different decisions, in both governments.

Then integrate the useful choice in the following paragraph—compensation, challenge or rejection—without repeating “The weekend illustrates…”. This is an optional structural compression, not a factual correction. If the author prefers a resolved introductory episode, preserve it and shorten the Chapter 2 replay of the letters instead. The book needs the detailed story once.

## What should survive the cuts

The strongest contrary evidence is already inside the narrative and should remain there: American authorization failures in accession and the 2018 pause; Lamy's own mandate; competing Chinese climate estimates; the European delay on the same canola product; the financial opening that became an operating business; India calibrating cooperation rather than adopting either total separation or total normalization; an operating Russian pipeline beside an unfinished one. These observations prevent both a fixed-national-style account and a blanket account of Chinese nonperformance. They do explanatory work rather than merely supply balance.

The varied endpoints are also legitimate if recommendations stay scoped. The Paris narrative explains a design adopted in 2015; the 2023 crisis narrative establishes commitments and follow-up at its endpoint; the 2025 licensing narrative does not establish every condition in force in September 2026. The notes acknowledge these distinctions. Do not quietly turn them into claims of uninterrupted availability when revising the practical prose. The current WTO check is an example of an update needed because availability directly affects the proposed option—not a reason to append a new status report to every episode.

The next editing pass should therefore be selective: sharpen the cross-case decision, remove redundant interpretation between actions, and align the 2023 question with its evidence. These are enough to improve the reading experience materially. They do not establish that the book has reached a top press's editorial standard, and neither a longer bibliography nor another complete export would establish that.
