# Does the manuscript answer what survives Solomon?

Independent argument audit, 17 September 2026. Reviewed version: `e7a8fe1303e12048eda3432c51f6897688be0f64`. No chapters, proposition records or indexes were edited.

## Finding

The introduction and conclusion already answer the question substantively. They identify continuing practices, new capacities and institutions, and limits on the use of Solomon's stronger diagnostic claims. The principal remaining weakness is the connection between that answer and the assessment apparatus. The fifty-nine entries are a selected inventory of behavioral propositions, not an exhaustive inventory of the book's dated empirical premises. Zero entries classified as revisions consequently does **not** mean that nothing in Solomon's account needs updating. The manuscript should say what its strongest update replaces, rather than leave the reader to infer this from an account of subsequent developments.

The new legal capacity and industrial bargaining material supplies a stronger answer than an unsupported claim that Chinese negotiators became more rigid, deceptive, sophisticated or successful. It need not be converted into a falsification of a behavioral proposition. Nor should the assessment be made to produce a desired number of revisions: the qualifications in the original claims, especially Freeman's, prevent that maneuver.

## 1. Make the strongest historical update an explicit old-to-new comparison

**Priority: high. Placement: the introduction's account of what changed, with a shorter return in the conclusion.**

Solomon's printed p. 164 describes China's international aspirations as exceeding its material capacity to influence events. He treats this disparity as relevant to the negotiating encounter: Chinese leaders can offer expansive judgments without bearing the responsibilities associated with practical influence. Pages 160–161 place the strategic choice between interdependence and autonomy alongside a historical distribution of resources in which the United States occupies the predominant position. These are substantive premises of the account, even though they are not among the fifty-nine extracted behavioral propositions. S-C20 cites pp. 162–170 but extracts American turnover, divided authority and information flows; it does not capture the limited-reach premise.

The 2025 case gives the manuscript a direct, consequential contrast. China controlled an industrial stage on which foreign manufacturers depended; its export administration could grant eligible transactions while preserving the control itself. The June 27 announcement described reciprocal administrative action, and the July software-company statements documented specific American restrictions being lifted. Chapter 6 also distinguishes restored shipments from universal access. This is evidence of practical bargaining capacity in the examined production networks, not merely international aspiration or a new rhetorical style.

Chapter 3 supplies a complementary change: WTO membership and acquired legal capacity allowed Chinese objections to invoke an accepted adjudicative procedure. DS379 changed what the United States had to investigate and explain without automatically delivering the commercial result Beijing sought. This makes the update more precise than saying that China is now powerful. It identifies an instrument, a counterpart obligation and the limitation that survived its use.

**Suggested passage, adaptable to the introduction:**

> One premise of Solomon's account requires a direct update. He described a China whose international ambitions exceeded its capacity to influence much of what its leaders discussed. That is no longer a useful starting assumption in the economic negotiations examined here. In 2025, American manufacturers needed permissions that Chinese officials could grant, and Beijing negotiated those permissions against American restrictions. In WTO disputes, Chinese officials and lawyers could require an answer through rules the United States had accepted. The change was in what China could make consequential across the table. It did not establish a new national temperament, or make every Chinese demand effective.

The supporting references are Solomon pp. 160–164; Chapter 6 notes 36–45, especially EC25-01/06/08/14/18; and Chapter 3 notes 1–3, 5–8, 11–12 and capacity1–3. Existing source registers control their acquisition and reading scopes.

**Necessary bounds:** The cases do not provide a count of all international issues, so do not claim a quantified refutation of Solomon's word *most*. The 91 percent estimate covers separation and refining of four magnet rare earths in 2024, not all minerals, manufacturing or global power. WTO learning also develops a possibility Freeman already recognized in 1999, rather than refuting him. The later breakdown of appellate review and the difference between American and European procedural commitments belong to the update too. Finally, none of this establishes that industrial controls were newly invented in 2025; Chapter 6 already records the earlier rare-earth dispute.

## 2. Explain why the behavioral inventory cannot count the book's updates

**Priority: high. Placement: Appendix 1 opening and, at most, one concise pointer in the narrative apparatus.**

The current status definitions are careful, but their title and zero-revision total can invite the wrong question: if thirty-eight judgments remain unresolved and none is revised, has the book merely accumulated cases? The answer is no. The classification was designed to test particular behavioral claims and their mechanisms. It does not encompass all historically bounded descriptions of China's capabilities or all institutional changes since the original study.

Add a short scope bridge rather than change the preserved objects or force a verdict. Suggested wording:

> These fifty-nine entries are the research inventory's selected behavioral propositions. They do not exhaust the original book's historical premises, or count every change documented in this update. Changed industrial dependencies, legal capacities and institutional responsibilities are assessed in the chapters. A zero in the revision category here does not mean those conditions remained unchanged; it means that this corpus has not justified classifying any of these selected entries as revised under the stated rules.

The distinction is between the classification's unit and the book's larger argument. Do not imply that sixteen entire theories have been validated either. Several retained records support an observable component while expressly withholding the proposed cause or effect: S-C10 supports the importance of authorization but not a general frequency or sequence; S-C16 documents use of deadlines but not their independent effect; S-C17 supports audience management without estimating the effect of confidentiality. Other propositions, including S-P25, retain important observations within an unresolved overall judgment. The totals cannot rank those observations' substantive importance.

The following distinctions should control the bridge and any summary, without becoming another checklist in the main prose:

| Kind of update | Strongest existing example | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Changed historical condition | Industrial dependence and reciprocal permissions in 2025; membership and legal capacity after accession | A changed national temperament or success rate |
| Demonstrated institutional change | The 2018 foreign-affairs reform and later statements of differentiated responsibilities | Uniformly tighter mandates, less concession flexibility, or a restoration of MFA control over every portfolio |
| Documentary correction or reinterpretation | The separate verified factual corrections; fuller claims/assets and EP-3 records | A national behavioral change, or proof of every actor's private motive |
| Updated practical application | Check the function and content of a senior encounter; identify the evidence needed to turn legal success into relief | Refutation of S-P24's comparative prediction or proof of S-P25's asymmetry |
| Unresolved original prediction | Hard-line behavior as a usual sign of leadership conflict; the superior reliability of senior intervention as an endgame signal | Evidence that the mechanism is absent, or was disproved by an unmatched later case |

This clarification would let the manuscript answer the user's original question without misrepresenting what the proposition exercise achieved.

## 3. Keep the positive continuity verdict distinct from validation of its stronger original form

**Priority: medium. Placement: one-for-one refinement of the introduction's verdict paragraph, not a new caveat section.**

The introduction says that the later cases sustain Solomon's central insight that political purposes organize bargaining. This is a defensible interpretive starting point, and its footnote already distinguishes it from a frequency claim. But S-C01 asks a stronger question: whether changes in conduct follow the political purpose of the relationship rather than issue, channel, personnel or institution. That record correctly remains unresolved. Likewise, the continuation of bargaining after signature is strongly documented while S-P25's claim of asymmetric adherence remains undecided.

The reader should therefore receive the useful positive conclusion in the form actually supported. A practical inheritance can survive even when its more ambitious explanatory formulation has not been established. The best continuity is the observed possibility of retaining a political position while negotiating a particular accommodation, the importance of obtaining an authorized commitment, and the work that can remain after agreement. The cases do not establish that these practices are uniquely Chinese or unusually frequent.

**Suggested substitute for the opening and forecast sentences of the introduction's verdict paragraph:**

> Solomon remains most useful when he directs attention to what Beijing wants from the relationship and who can commit it. The later cases repeatedly show defended principles coexisting with particular accommodations, and agreements followed by further bargaining over performance. His more ambitious diagnoses require a different kind of evidence. These records do not establish that a hard line usually signals leadership conflict, or that senior intervention is the most reliable forecast of agreement.

Retain the paragraph's concrete examples and responsibility for choosing risk. This substitution does not withdraw a positive judgment; it identifies what the reader can carry forward and what should remain a hypothesis. It also avoids presenting cautions Solomon or Freeman had already made as discoveries of the update. In particular, Freeman had already described technical specialization, learning and more active multilateral participation. The later record supplies mechanisms and consequences within those developments; it is not evidence that his qualification was mistaken.
