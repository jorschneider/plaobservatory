## Substantive finding and correction

The initial opening inferred too much from Prueher's account of changing his approach. His testimony establishes that Powell permitted him to stop arguing fault; it does not establish that Washington had formally required Chinese acceptance of American attribution before releasing the crew. The sentence about ceasing to make “acceptance a condition of the next step” was therefore flagged. The parent changed it to obtaining permission to “put that argument aside while seeking a narrower result.” That correction is present in the hash above and resolves the issue.

## What the new evidence supports

Zhou's recollection reports a leadership instruction summarized as apology in exchange for release, preference for *apologize*, resistance from the Americans and eventual acceptance of *very sorry*. The chapter attributes these to the participant, preserves the absent original instruction and does not date a particular change of mandate. The interviewed official continues to defend China's collision account; the text does not turn compromise in wording into agreement on responsibility.

The aircraft passage preserves a material disagreement: Prueher recalls permission for a repaired flyout, whereas Zhou recalls refusing it and insisting on disassembly. Neither recollection gives the complete dated series needed to reconcile them. Calling these conflicting accounts is warranted; choosing one as a recovered authorization would not be. The chapter also correctly resists importing Zhou's recollection of additional apology recipients into the actual released letter.

The source credit identifies Shanghai American Studies' interview project and a changed republication title. The reported interview date and publication date remain distinct. The three preserved Zhou files' byte counts and hashes match the development register. This checks preservation, not accuracy of the recollection.

The Carnegie account supports months of preparation over agenda, confidentiality and public statements; Sullivan's ability to travel discreetly; the Vienna dinner; and allocation of messages among American channels. The source chain includes the authors' interviews, a named participant's public interview and earlier reporting. The chapter's framing as an American account is appropriate and does not supply Chinese internal consent or knowledge. It retains actual diplomatic contact in February rather than following the report's overbroad communications-cutoff wording.

No further consequential factual or causal overreach was identified in the new passages. Earlier distinctions—limited letter, continued aircraft negotiation, conditional military access, June contact without the requested military outcome, consultation versus demonstrated Kissinger effect, and November commitments versus completed resumption—remain intact.

## Literary judgment and optional small cuts

The EP-3 opening now gives the reader a negotiator with a problem, advice that changes his approach, and an authorization needed to act. Pairing Zhou with Prueher restores a Chinese participant's task without pretending their memories agree. The public apology challenge makes the costs of the accepted formulation visible. The aircraft counterpoint explains why release did not settle every issue.

Two optional cuts were sent to the parent. Move the specific April 17 intelligence/April 11 decision chronology warning into note 8, leaving the institutional question and uncertainty in the body. Delete the following paragraph beginning “The American packet allows one piece of coordination…”: the chapter no longer asserts general American coherence or Chinese dysfunction, so that defensive rebuttal interrupts the narrative. The parent applied both changes. I reread the revised paragraph and note 8, confirmed removal of the redundant paragraph, and verified that the SHA256 recorded above identifies this revised version. Both changes preserve the source limits where they matter.

The 2023 account remains more chronological than the EP-3 section, but the concrete decisions about creating a channel give it substantially more explanatory value. The intermediary section is more proportionate after condensation. Further continuous manuscript reading should judge the combined chapter's rhythm; this review is neither approval of the whole book nor a recommendation to postpone useful drafting for another general research audit.
