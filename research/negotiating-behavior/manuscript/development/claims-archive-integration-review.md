## Findings

**No necessary factual or interpretive correction identified in the integrated passages.** The additions materially improve the argument: the reader can now see the disagreement over whether a concession remained available, instead of encountering a withdrawal explained only retrospectively.

1. **The amount and its function are right.** CLA-F01 printed 7 and 9 both give $17 million. It was the proposed additional contribution associated with money already drawn from third-country banks, not the total value of all American claims or the entire proposed settlement. The earlier chapter paragraphs preserve that distinction and attribute the forty-percent compensation calculation to Kissinger. No conversion or implied present-day valuation is introduced.

2. **The disputed November assurance is not silently promoted into an established American promise.** In CLA-F01 printed 9, Lin says the December reply failed to conform to the November commitment; Habib disputes a change and offers to reexamine the matter. Chapter 1’s “In Lin’s account” governs the assertion, and the ensuing Habib response preserves the disagreement. The earlier account of November 1973 also correctly distinguishes the American government’s undertaking not to support bond claims from its inability to bar private court approaches. Neither that record nor the newly acquired discussion supplies the missing December memorandum or proves a breached guarantee of immunity.

3. **October 1975 remains a proposal and refusal.** Printed 4 offers withdrawal of the March 21 statement and October 27 aide-mémoire. Printed 5 describes alternative placements of new phrases; the underlying handed-over wording is not reproduced there. “He offered to withdraw” and “what he called symmetrical” are therefore appropriately precise. The source’s nodding does not warrant declaring the documents rescinded or the package accepted. Lin’s printed 11 refusal and continued withdrawal of the money are explicit.

4. **The end of that day’s discussion is not mistaken for a permanently closed channel.** Printed 16 supports Habib’s decision to stop discussing claims in that meeting; printed 17 supports the chapter’s subsequent invitation to study the ideas. The juxtaposition is useful and accurate.

5. **The missing June 14 text remains missing.** The new American records establish that Lin invoked the reply and explained its meaning in these meetings. They do not recover its complete original wording or the internal instruction behind it. Both archival notes and note 18 preserve this limit. The narrative makes no new determination about Chinese leadership conflict or the comparative reliability of senior intervention.

## Narrative continuity

The transitions work. The American channel difficulty of early 1974 leads into a named negotiator’s later attempt to revive the offer; the 1974 disagreement then explains why Habib brought a new package in 1975. Deng’s same-visit objection leads naturally to the 1977 decision to defer the issue. The closing distinction between an offer withdrawn and a completed agreement breached remains necessary, because the new evidence makes the offer’s disputed conditions more concrete. No additional caveat paragraph or repeated methodological warning is needed here.
