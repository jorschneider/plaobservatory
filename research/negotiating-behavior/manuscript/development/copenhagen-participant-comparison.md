# Copenhagen: what the participants could see

15 September 2026. A bounded return to participant evidence for Chapter 3, read alongside the earlier Chinese account, Conrad study and final UNFCCC texts. The [register](copenhagen-participant-register.json) records the three new sources, exact reading boundaries and preserved copies. This adds a scene and an explanation of the compromise's domestic defense; it does not add a recovered Chinese mandate.

## Why these sources change the chapter

The chapter previously explained the institutional problem principally through a subsequent scholar's reconstruction. The new passage lets participants describe a decision, then asks how far their observations support an explanation. The [revised chapter](../chapters/03-usable-expertise.md), “What Copenhagen left unfinished,” carries the substantive account; this memo records how it was constructed rather than repeating that account.

Froman and Saran are witnesses from different delegations, but their recollections are retrospective. The existence of two accounts does not establish that their memories developed independently, nor make every detail common to both. In particular, their accounts of Obama's entry into the meeting should not be fused into a colorful scene: Froman describes an intrusion; Saran describes a question at the door and an invitation. The chapter does not need to settle that disagreement to explain the bargaining.

Their convergence on visible disagreement is more useful than either writer's generalization about how unprecedented it was. Nor should Froman's inference about a mandate be upgraded into a fact because Saran reports understanding the objection. Understanding speech and possessing an instruction record are different forms of evidence. The final text establishes the agreement's content; recollections help explain how people experienced its acceptance.

Ramesh's parliamentary interventions supply a contemporary public defense, with a different audience and evidentiary purpose. His acknowledgement of movement is valuable precisely because it occurs while he is defending the bargain. It does not prove that his account of another government's intentions was correct. The disagreement about what had been conceded also helps prevent a national-style explanation from swallowing the pressures facing other delegations. Saran’s retrospective verdict on the treaty system differs from the minister’s contemporary defense; agreement on wording did not imply agreement about its consequences.

Conrad remains relevant to the organization and substance of the negotiation. The visible encounter cannot confirm every preceding displacement of advisers or isolate its effect. The earlier Chinese public account likewise remains a government defense rather than a secret directive. These materials answer different questions; the new witnesses do not render the existing sources redundant.

## Reading boundaries and version problems

- **CP01, Froman:** complete selected 4,826-word Copenhagen discussion in the Miller Center's HTML transcript, including the lead-up, final negotiations, aftermath and immediate domestic-politics discussion. It was read in three contiguous word ranges: 0–1,649; 1,650–3,299; 3,300–end. This is not a complete reading of the much longer interview. The website labels the interview September 13, 2009, inconsistent with its retrospective content. Its linked PDF filename is `BHO-Froman1-20190913-rx.interview.pdf`; that suggests 2019 but is not treated as verified dating. The PDF returned 403 and was not acquired. The HTML includes redactions.
- **CP02, Saran:** complete publicly rendered chapter, including its visible ellipses, references and reprint/license notice. The body and remainder through the final notes were read; the preserved web capture contains the complete rendering. No paginated PDF was acquired. The 2019 chapter identifies itself as a reprint from the author's 2017 book. Those are one participant chain, not separate witnesses. The source's present-tense statements must be understood at its original writing date. The 2017 book was not acquired or read.
- **CP03, Ramesh:** complete ministerial statement, printed pp. 218–223 (PDF pp. 221–226), and complete reply, printed pp. 242–248 (PDF pp. 245–251). Jaitley's opening challenge on printed p. 224 was read; the remainder of the intervening debate was not systematically read. The full 251-page PDF was acquired, not read in full. Printed pp. 243 and 245 were also rendered and visually inspected, confirming the wording central to the chapter. The reply begins partway down p. 242 and ends early on p. 248.

No scholarly full-reading count changes. There is one selected oral-history reading, one complete public participant chapter rendering and two complete interventions within a much larger parliamentary record. Raw source captures, extracted text and page images remain outside Git.

## What remains unresolved

The internal Chinese instructions, a contemporary complete recording of the final meeting and a common chronology of the precise formulations remain unavailable. Further private records could distinguish substantive objection, a question of authorization and concern about later interpretation. The chapter therefore uses the incident to examine the relation between advice and an accepted decision, not to establish a general Chinese hierarchy rule or a change under Xi.

The separate [2009–2013 machinery comparison](climate-2009-2013-machinery.md) addresses the subsequent institutional history. Its correction is independent of whether one accepts either retrospective account of Copenhagen: the earlier memorandum and reported dialogue establish that recurring cooperation preceded the later named working group.
