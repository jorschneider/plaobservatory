# What later relief left to decide

Research and integration note, 17 September 2026. [Six-document register](trade-2026-permissions-register.json). This is a bounded sequel to the [April–July 2025 reconstruction](trade-2025-export-controls-evidence.md), ending at September 4, 2026 reporting; it is not an exhaustive history of subsequent controls or current legal advice.

## Why the sequel changes the chapter

The original section established that administrative relief could preserve the authority under which it was granted. The later record makes the practical difficulty more specific. A negotiator must identify which obstacle prevents the intended transaction: the instrument imposing a control, the handling of an application, the permission's scope, or a firm's readiness to use it. These call for different requests and cannot be measured by a single aggregate shipment total.

The chapter now carries that inquiry past the July recovery through selected presidential announcements, the operative suspension instrument and a later report about supplier decisions. It keeps the distinction between public claims, operative provisions and reported conduct. No private mandate, completed list of licenses, application dossier or supply contract has been obtained.

## Exclusions and unfinished work

The contemplated September 24 presidential visit had not occurred at this research cutoff and supplies no result. The sequel does not treat a May intention to seek an extension as a completed extension. It does not compare magnet tonnage with different baskets of controlled metals and compounds. Nor does it use every difference between American and Chinese public formulations as proof that one side misrepresented the private bargain.

The strongest next evidence would be paired application and shipment histories, actual general-license terms, and a company's explanation of a refused transaction tied to the instrument it considered controlling. The new section identifies where such evidence would change the practical recommendation. It does not fill those gaps with a national-character explanation.
