## One small correction

**Chapter 2, line 31: retain Barshefsky's ownership of the inference.** The new sentence “His usefulness came from his part in the decision the Chinese needed” makes the benefit sound established. The preceding recollection supplies Barshefsky's reasons for asking Podesta or Sperling to travel. Note 8 correctly says that it does not independently establish what Chinese officials inferred from Sperling's participation. The first sentence's “could” preserves that boundary; the second sentence loses it.

Suggested replacement for the paragraph: “Barshefsky wanted the same travelling companion to reassure Beijing and protect her position in Washington. In her calculation, his part in the decision mattered as much as his title.” This retains the substantive point while avoiding an unsupported finding about the Chinese reception or actual effect. Low priority; not a reason to restore the longer deleted paragraph.
