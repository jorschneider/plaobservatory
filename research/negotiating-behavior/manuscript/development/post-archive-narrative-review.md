## Repairs identified and addressed during the pass

1. **Applied by the parent; revised paragraph checked. Chapter 5, paragraph beginning “Both settlements formalized” (line 99): name the agreements and Solomon.** The preceding paragraphs now discuss the 1995 IP arrangement, Phase One and the WTO procedure. “Both settlements” consequently has an uncertain antecedent. “His stronger claim” no longer has a clear personal antecedent; Lighthizer is more recently prominent than Solomon. This is the clearest repair left by the cuts. Suggested opening: “The 1995 agreement and Phase One both formalized continued negotiation after signature. Phase One’s procedure was bilateral, and the United States had publicly pressed for it. Its existence does not establish Solomon’s stronger claim…” The remaining distinction between procedural continuation and asymmetric demands should stay.

2. **Applied by the parent; revised sentence checked. Chapter 2, paragraph beginning “The American response preserved” (line 41): introduce Shi.** “In his November 12 letter to Shi” is the first appearance of Shi in the narrative. Use “to trade minister Shi Guangsheng.” Note 11 already supplies the full name. The reader can then follow the later summons, authority and compensation exchanges without inferring who this central participant is.

3. **Substantive correction applied by the parent; revised paragraph checked. Chapter 3, paragraph beginning “Stern and Xie had begun” (line 79): remove the categorical claim that exploration would be wasted.** “Time spent exploring an option with a well-informed official would be wasted if someone else could simply disown it” is stronger than the evidence and awkward beside the chapter’s later account of useful expert preparation awaiting leadership decisions. Political superiors’ capacity to reject an option does not make exploring it useless. Delete that sentence and the filler “That was a practical matter.” Retain the narrower, useful next sentence: “The question for each envoy was whether the other could carry a proposal into the decisions of his government.” This preserves the authority problem without requiring envoys to possess final approval themselves.

## Lower-priority tightening

4. **Chapter 2, final narrative paragraph (line 101): mark actual accession before passing to the use of membership.** The chapter ends its concrete chronology in 2000 and then says membership “would give” Chinese specialists another setting; the next chapter begins in 2012 before returning to a February 2002 meeting of leaders of a country that had joined. One short dated clause would close that gap: “China’s accession in December 2001 gave them another setting in which to use those skills.” This is orientation, not a request for another account of the multilateral closing negotiations. Tie it to the existing accession apparatus rather than adding a new digression.

5. **Chapter 6, opening paragraph of “Buying a number” (line 69): compress the recap.** Chapter 5’s “What the numbers promised” has just explained the two-year increment, commercial considerations, prices and supply, and why those qualifications did not erase the obligation. Chapter 6 repeats all these points before reaching its new evidence. Keep a short bridge—“The purchase commitments posed a different test: China had undertaken to buy specified increments of American goods and services over two years”—and proceed to the measured shortfall. The later explanation of causes and Article 7.6.2 preserves the qualifications where they become consequential. The December witnesses’ return later in Chapter 6 should remain; it supplies the human decision to which this evidence contributes.
