# Which WTO appeal route is available now?

15 September 2026. Bounded primary-source update for Chapter 3 and the conclusion, following the continuous-reading memorandum. This is a check of institutional availability and documented implementation stages, not a new study of the substantive patent dispute or a certification of Chinese compliance. No chapter text was edited.

## Finding and proposed use

The historical DS379 lesson needs a counterpart-specific qualification in the body. Ordinary appellate review is unavailable in the current WTO record; appeal arbitration depends on consent. China and the European Union supplied that consent in DS611 and completed an appeal in 2025. China's participation in the interim arrangement cannot by itself secure the United States' consent. Neither the existence of an award nor a party's implementation announcement establishes complete domestic performance.

Place the short paragraph below after the historical DS379 implementation sequence, before the climate transition. The conclusion can then refer back to it rather than repeating the legal machinery. Do not replace the historical finding with a claim that the WTO no longer offers any useful procedure.

## What the originals establish

**Vacancy and adoption.** The WTO's current membership page, captured on 15 September 2026, states that the Appellate Body has no members and cannot review appeals. The last member's term expired on 30 November 2020; that is not the date on which a three-member division first became unavailable. Article 16.4 of the Dispute Settlement Understanding (DSU) withholds consideration of a panel report for adoption until an appeal is completed. Article 25.2 makes arbitration conditional on agreement between the parties. Articles 25.3–25.4 require the parties to abide by the award and apply the implementation and suspension-of-concessions provisions of Articles 21 and 22 with the necessary adaptations. These are different stages, not an automatic domestic remedy. [APP-01; APP-02]

**China–US.** The WTO's current list of Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement (MPIA) participants includes China and the EU and does not include the United States. Nonparticipation is not a legal prohibition on a separate Article 25 agreement; it means that Chinese membership alone supplies no such agreement. The DS543 chronology records a concrete American use of the ordinary appeal route on 26 October 2020 in China's challenge to additional US tariffs. Use that dated action as an example, not a prediction of every future American response. Consultations, panels and negotiated settlements are not abolished by the appellate vacancy. [APP-02; APP-03; APP-06]

**China–EU.** The parties notified agreed DS611 appeal procedures on 4 July 2023. The original agreement, reproduced as Annex A-1 to the award addendum, provides for either party to initiate arbitration if the ordinary Appellate Body cannot hear the case (§§1–2). If arbitration is not initiated in that circumstance, §6 provides a no-ordinary-appeal fallback allowing panel adoption. Section 15 makes the award final and requires the parties to abide by it; the award is notified to, not adopted by, the Dispute Settlement Body. Section 17 applies DSU Articles 21 and 22. The EU appealed in April 2025, China filed an other appeal, and the award was issued on 21 July 2025. This is evidence of an agreed alternative working in this dispute, not proof of universal access or of acceptance of the arbitrators' reasoning. [APP-04; APP-05, printed/PDF pp. 4–6]

**Implementation remained a separate question.** At the DSB's 26 September 2025 meeting, China disputed the award's reasoning while asserting full compliance. It cited a Supreme People's Court notice of 23 September withdrawing the disputed policy and publication of a judicial decision. The EU did not accept full implementation on the information then available. By 1 April 2026 the Commission reported that the notice was accessible from outside China and said that it would continue monitoring compliance. These statements establish positions and reported steps; this pass did not inspect the notice itself or subsequent court practice. The WTO case-summary page stops at China's August 2025 intention to implement and should not be treated as an exhaustive account of later implementation. [APP-08, §§2.2–2.5, pp. 6–9; APP-07]

**Unconfirmed candidate excluded.** Bounded searches did not verify a general Chinese undertaking in 2025 never to appeal into the void. It is not used. That search result does not establish that no such statement exists. The verified no-ordinary-appeal provision here is the bilateral, case-specific 2023 clause, subject to its stated conditions.

## Proposed manuscript paragraph

The route through the WTO has itself become a bargaining question. As of September 2026, its Appellate Body had no members, and an appeal could prevent a panel report from being adopted. China and the European Union participate in an interim appeal-arbitration arrangement; the United States does not. Arbitration under Article 25 requires agreement between the parties. Chinese membership alone therefore cannot give it that route against Washington. The American appeal in the tariff dispute DS543, filed in October 2020, illustrates the obstacle.[^1] With Europe, the alternative has been used. China and the EU agreed procedures for their intellectual-property dispute in 2023; a final award followed in July 2025. Their agreement required them to abide by it.[^2] Even then, legal finality did not settle implementation. China announced domestic steps while criticizing the award; the EU continued to assess compliance.[^3] A negotiator considering litigation must therefore ask two separate questions: what procedure has the other government accepted for reaching a final ruling, and what will still be needed to turn that ruling into changed conduct?

[^1]: APP-01, WTO, “Appellate Body Members”; APP-02, DSU Articles 16.4 and 25.2; APP-03, WTO, “Alternative Dispute Resolution Procedures,” current MPIA participant list; APP-06, DS543 case chronology, appeal notification of 26 October 2020. Current pages captured 15 September 2026. Official links and complete preservation records are in the companion register.
[^2]: APP-05, WT/DS611/ARB25/Add.1, Annex A-1, §§1–2, 6 and 15–17, pp. 4–6, reproducing WT/DS611/7 notified 4 July 2023; APP-04, DS611 chronology, award of 21 July 2025.
[^3]: APP-08, WT/DSB/M/505, §§2.2–2.5, pp. 6–9, meeting 26 September 2025, circulated 14 October 2025; APP-07, European Commission, 1 April 2026. These sources do not independently verify the full effect of the reported domestic changes.
