# Chapter 4 development: obtaining the next action

15 September 2026. Substantive rewrite of the EP-3 half, narrower changes to the 2023 strategic-channel and intermediary sections. [Reading register](crisis-development-register.json). This is a development record, not a publication-readiness judgment.

The former opening announced the solution and then sorted it into categories. The replacement begins with Prueher unable to settle fault, Lieberthal proposing a change of objective and Powell authorizing a different approach. Recollection is identified immediately. The actual April 11 letter, the contemporary legal packet and the public approval statement then establish what Washington maintained and accepted. No private dialogue or setting has been invented.

## New Chinese participant evidence

A complete public Wenhui interview, published December 12, 2018 and dated July 5 that year, was obtained and read. It credits the Shanghai American Studies *40 Years, 40 People* project and identifies a changed title. This is one participant account, not new access to the original mandate or a second independent record of that mandate. The three preserved files are the downloaded gzip payload, decoded HTML and text extraction, with hashes in the register.

Zhou Wenzhong describes a conditional release instruction and compromise over the English apology term. That lets the chapter place a Chinese negotiating assignment alongside Prueher's, with the original approval record still missing. His recollection of the letter's recipients also illustrates the need to use the contemporary instrument for precise terms: the actual release names the Chinese people and Wang Wei's family, not every addition recalled later.

On aircraft return, the two participants' accounts conflict. Prueher recalls a flyout opportunity; Zhou describes refusal. The chapter leaves that disagreement unresolved and follows the contemporary inspection, transport-method and engineering record. Neither memory is substituted for the dated permission that remains unavailable.

The distinction changes the analysis: the evidence now includes a Chinese participant's reported instruction, but still does not reconstruct the private sequence by which wording, wider costs and approval combined. The chapter no longer treats the Chinese side as wholly silent about its assignment.

## Changes to the 2023 account

The strategic-channel passage now describes preparatory decisions about agenda, confidentiality, public statements and allocation of American messages. These details come from the already acquired Carnegie study, especially printed 102–104. Its notes were reread to preserve anonymous interview provenance and distinguish the FT and other recycled public sources. It is an American process account, not Chinese internal confirmation. The report's overly broad suggestion that all official communications had ceased is not adopted; the chapter retains the February Munich encounter.

The Kissinger section now spends less space repeating an absent causal chain. It retains exceptional defense-minister access, previously active formal diplomacy, private rather than envoy status, positive evidence of Blinken consultation and the distinction between a channel's possible functions. No July-to-November causal effect is claimed.

The independent reviewer checked the new participant passages against the Zhou and Prueher texts and the 2023 preparation against Carnegie pages 102–104. That sentence was corrected. The April 17 intelligence chronology warning moved to note 8, and a redundant paragraph defending against national institutional generalizations was removed.
