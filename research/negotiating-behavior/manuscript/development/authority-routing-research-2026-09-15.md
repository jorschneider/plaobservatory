# Political authority and the route to implementation under Xi

Research memorandum, 15 September 2026. This is a bounded primary-source supplement, not a reconstruction of undisclosed negotiating mandates. Companion register: `authority-routing-register.json` (AR-01–09). No chapter or existing source register changed.

The clearest institutional change is a reassignment of coordinating responsibilities, followed by legislation that states the roles of several different authorities. In 2018, the foreign-affairs leading group became a commission; a separate maritime-rights leading group disappeared, and its functions moved into the commission and its office. The 2023 Foreign Relations Law identifies central direction, military exchanges, ministerial coordination, overseas representation and provincial activity as distinct responsibilities. These texts give a negotiator a better starting map of the Chinese system. They do not reveal which official can change a particular price, ownership ceiling, export restriction or operational demand.

## What changed in 2018

Item IV of the March 2018 reform plan converts four central leading groups, including the foreign-affairs group, into commissions and assigns them strategic design, overall coordination, implementation and supervision. Each has an office. The specific transfer in item XVII matters more than the title alone: the Central Leading Group for Maritime Rights Protection is abolished; its responsibilities go to the Central Foreign Affairs Commission and its office, which houses a maritime-rights office. The transferred remit includes directing and inspecting policy implementation, aggregating and assessing intelligence, coordinating emergencies and researching policy responses. This is documentary evidence of consolidation of assigned work. It supplies neither an operational command chain for every maritime encounter nor evidence that information invariably reaches decision-makers in time. [AR-01, items IV and XVII.]

At the commission's first meeting on 15 May 2018, the published report identifies Xi as director and Li Keqiang as deputy director. It directs the commission to coordinate policy and supervise implementation, including the allocation of local resources for external work. The meeting adopted working rules, but the report does not publish them. We therefore have a named apex, a defined coordinating function and an announced rules instrument; we do not have a complete membership list, quorum, escalation thresholds or delegated concession limits. [AR-02, opening two body paragraphs; paragraphs beginning “习近平指出，地方外事工作” and “习近平强调，做好新形势下”; penultimate substantive paragraph.]

The same reform plan also provides a useful counterweight to the idea that central coordination eliminates departmental execution. Item XXXVIII creates the China International Development Cooperation Agency as an agency directly under the State Council. It takes over aid functions from the Ministry of Commerce and aid-coordination functions from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, with responsibilities for strategy, policy, plans, project selection and supervision. Specific execution remains with the relevant departments according to their division of responsibilities. A foreign counterpart seeking a funded project would therefore need to distinguish an aid-policy or project decision from the acts necessary to deliver it. This is an inference for negotiation design from an explicit institutional distinction, not an investigation of any particular aid project. [AR-01, item XXXVIII.]

## What the 2023 law assigns

Adopted on 28 June and effective on 1 July 2023, the Foreign Relations Law expresses Party leadership in article 5 and distributes responsibilities in articles 9–16. Article 9 uses the institutional description “central leading body for foreign affairs”; it does not spell out the commission's proper name. Read alongside the 2018 documents, it describes the central policy and coordination role without providing the body's internal operating rules. [AR-03.]

| Actor | Published responsibility | What the text does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Central leading body for foreign affairs | Strategy and major policy decisions; coordination, guidance and supervision of implementation (article 9) | The instructions for an individual negotiation or a rule that every concession requires Xi's personal decision |
| State Council; NPC and its Standing Committee; president | Respectively manage foreign affairs and conclude treaties/agreements; approve or abrogate treaties and important agreements; undertake state activities and exercise constitutional/legal powers (articles 10–12) | That every political readout has treaty status, or that a summit statement itself completes domestic implementation |
| Central Military Commission | Organize international military exchanges and cooperation (article 13) | That the MFA commands military contacts or operational forces |
| Ministry of Foreign Affairs and other central/state organs | MFA conducts diplomatic affairs, handles leader contacts, and guides, coordinates, manages and serves departmental/local external exchanges; other organs act within their assigned responsibilities (article 14) | A general transfer of other ministries' substantive regulatory powers to the MFA |
| Overseas missions; provincial authorities | Missions represent China under unified MFA leadership; provinces, autonomous regions and centrally administered municipalities act within specific centrally authorized scopes (articles 15–16) | Unlimited negotiating powers for an ambassador or a provincial counterpart |

This is a statutory allocation, not proof that every listed function originated in 2023. Nor should a researcher substitute the law for sector-specific rules governing a permit, acquisition or license. The relevant practical distinction is between securing political support and obtaining the act that changes the counterparty's position. The law makes it possible to ask which organ is expected to perform that act without pretending to know the private instructions behind it.

The law also preserves different routes for performing international obligations and imposing countermeasures. Articles 30–31 address treaty-making and implementation, including good-faith performance and stated constitutional, sovereignty, security and public-interest constraints. Article 33 assigns countermeasure rules and coordinating mechanisms to the State Council and its departments; article 35 assigns the MFA public notification of binding UN Security Council Chapter VII sanctions, with implementation by relevant departments and provincial governments. These are specified responsibilities. Article 33's statement that relevant decisions are final should not be expanded here into a general claim about judicial review, or treated as the legal basis of every later commercial restriction. [AR-03, articles 30–35.]

## Coordination orders after the law

The published account of the 27–28 December 2023 Central Conference on Work Relating to Foreign Affairs again locates ultimate diplomatic authority in the Party center and instructs regions and departments to coordinate and implement its decisions. The participating organizations show the breadth of the undertaking: presentations came from the Central Propaganda Department, International Department, Ministry of Commerce, CMC Joint Staff Department, Yunnan and China's UN mission. Attendance also included local governments and some centrally managed financial institutions. The conference thus addressed officials whose work reaches beyond formal diplomacy. Attendance does not make every participant a commission member or give each a veto over a bargaining proposal. [AR-04, final four body paragraphs.]

The complete published report of the 10–11 April 2024 national conference on local foreign-affairs work is just one paragraph. It identifies Wang Yi as director of the commission's office and combines central leadership and discipline with local advantages and active initiative. That pairing matters: the instruction is not simply to await instructions on every detail. It is to act within a politically directed and coordinated system. The report supplies no measurement of local discretion and no example of an authorized local concession. Its displayed publication date is 11 April; the URL includes 12 April. The register keeps the event date and displayed date separately. [AR-05, sole body paragraph.]

The two conference accounts document instructions and the audiences receiving them. Their praise for the system's achievements remains the issuers' assessment. Neither is the full text of Xi's or Wang's speech, and neither demonstrates that departments agreed in advance on a particular negotiation.

## A public example: one channel opens while another remains unsettled

In May 2023, Wang Yi met Jake Sullivan in Vienna as a Politburo member and director of the commission's office. The Chinese readout reported substantive discussion and agreement to continue the strategic channel. On 31 May, the Defense Ministry's answer about a proposed defense-minister meeting nevertheless said that dialogue required principles, respect and appropriate conditions. It also disputed the suggestion that military contact had entirely ceased. These are different forms of access: an active strategic channel did not establish an agreement to hold the proposed defense meeting. [AR-06–07.]

The contrast does not reveal an interagency struggle. It could reflect coordinated policy, distinct bargaining decisions or other undisclosed instructions. The May exchanges also predate the Foreign Relations Law's entry into force; they cannot demonstrate an effect of that law. Their narrower value is operational: a negotiator can verify one channel's availability without assuming that its availability settles every other channel.

At the 15 November summit in California, the Chinese readout named the military contacts the leaders agreed to restore or conduct, on a stated basis of equality and respect. On 30 November, the Defense Ministry said the two defense establishments were communicating and coordinating implementation. The documented sequence is leader-level agreement followed by departmental arrangement. It does not show that every mechanism was already functioning, or promise that a call would be answered during the next crisis. [AR-08, paragraph beginning “两国元首同意推动”; AR-09, complete answer.]

## Consequences for the manuscript and a negotiator

The earned revision is to the institutional description. Today's counterpart operates within a commission-and-office structure with stated coordination duties, a statutory division of foreign-affairs responsibilities and repeated instructions linking local and departmental work to central objectives. This is more specific than saying that a powerful leader dominates the system. It is also more useful than assuming that political concentration makes every person below the apex powerless.

A negotiator can work with incomplete information by specifying the next observable act: which authority must issue the notice, arrange the call, approve the project or change the regulation; who will transmit the agreed task; and what evidence will show that it has occurred. These are questions about completing a proposed transaction, not demands to expose confidential instructions. Where the counterpart cannot supply the answer, the uncertainty belongs in the arrangement's sequence and timing. A favorable senior meeting can justify proceeding to technical preparation; it should not require the other side to incur all implementation costs before the responsible bodies act.

Escalation has a tradeoff. It may obtain priority or settle a conflict that a technical counterpart cannot resolve. It can also consume scarce political attention and turn an adjustable administrative question into an explicit political position. The present sources do not estimate either effect. They support matching the requested act to a responsible institution and retaining an implementation route after senior agreement. They do not support a universal rule to bypass ministries, insist on personal access to Xi, or treat requests for referral as a tactic rather than a possible description of authority.

Cabestan's previously completed article reading remains relevant because it treats concentration and continuing organizational differentiation together. This pass inspected that memo and its evidence register, then reread the relevant original documents; it did not reread the whole article. The parent researcher's new Taylor–Garlick reading is outside this memorandum's source claims. Neither institutional study should turn the formal map presented here into a measured finding about concession flexibility. Establishing such an effect would require the instructions and choices behind specific offers, with comparisons capable of separating institutions from the issue, counterpart, stakes and available alternatives.
