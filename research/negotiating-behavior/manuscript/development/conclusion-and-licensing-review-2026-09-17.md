## Findings

The conclusion now gives a historical answer before practical advice. It identifies expanded legal capacity, industrial dependence and administrative instruments without claiming that conditional bargaining began recently. Its departure from Solomon's weak-power premise is restricted to the supply relationships demonstrated in these cases. The note expressly avoids purporting to refute his statement about **most** international issues by counting a few selected examples. The juxtaposition with the 1970s and pre-Xi intellectual-property enforcement prevents a false claim of novelty.

The licensing inference is proportionate. JL Mag confirms receipt of general licenses; the regulation establishes what that category can permit. Neither the chapter nor conclusion supplies an invented term, customer annex, quantity or measured reduction in processing time. The final Chapter 6 sentence about sparing repeated applications is expressly confined to approved scope. Management's expected commercial advantage remains its assessment, not a demonstrated government motive or measured profit. The 2025 opening of the conclusion is a dated episode, with the later sequel expressly located in Chapter 6.

The practical distinction between a completed benefit and continuing permission is useful without becoming a universal prescription. The enforcement tail distinguishes constituency interests from proven policy causation. The authority section shows negotiators making counteroffers and accepting bounded uncertainty; it does not advise them to acquire unknowable private instructions before acting. Public domestic-purpose statements remain attributed explanations. S-P24's stronger comparative prediction remains unvalidated, while continued bargaining remains insufficient by itself to establish S-P25's asymmetric demands.

No further cut is necessary for this bounded pass. The closing return to Prueher resolves the question of action under uncertainty; it is not merely a repeated method disclaimer. The reordered Chapter 6 explicitly signals its return to spring–summer 2025, so its placement after the 2026 mechanisms does not silently collapse chronology.
