# EP-3 cable integration: independent review

16 September 2026. Review of Chapter 4's revised opening through the aircraft-return section, with the associated new notes. This is an independent source check of the new bargaining narrative, not a whole-chapter or whole-book certification. No chapter edited by this reviewer.

## Actionable corrections

1. **Opening third paragraph, “Neither man wanted the crisis to continue.”** The cables establish stated preferences, not transparent motives. EPD03 paras 6, 23, 29–34 record Tang repeatedly calling for prompt settlement; EPD05 para 1 and EPD06 para 1 expressly present the embassy's suspicions about deliberate internal delay as impressions. Use **“Both men said they wanted a prompt resolution.”** This preserves the dramatic impasse without granting privileged access to either man's motives. The following “Their preference” can become “That shared position.”

2. **Delivery paragraph, “before either government explained the outcome to its public.”** EPD08 paras 1 and 8 establish the Chinese/English difference within the meeting. They do not by themselves establish the time of every Chinese and American public statement that day. Remove the added chronology; **“The two expressions' different force was present in the room”** carries the important finding. This is a support correction, not a finding that a public explanation definitely preceded delivery.

3. **Jost paragraph, “as he authorized departure.”** EPD08 para 8 records Tang announcing that the Chinese government had decided to allow departure. It does not identify Tang personally as the decision-maker. Use **“as he announced the decision to permit departure.”** The distinction is particularly important in a passage about authority and information.

4. **Note `draft-impasse` omits two paragraphs supporting its uses.** Add **para 8** for Prueher's stated thirty-five years of flying experience and **para 19** for Tang's qualified treatment of maritime consultations. The latter is used later in the chapter as pre-Xi continuity evidence. The new paragraph is accurate; the locator should include its actual basis.

## The new narrative's substantive claims that survived checking

The April 7 high-level-clearance assertion, less definite release/aircraft timing and noon delivery all appear in EPD04. The chapter appropriately attributes clearance to Zhou; no complete intermediate draft is pretended. The April 8 rejection of *apologize* followed consultation with Powell, while other Chinese amendments were incorporated. The authority-letter arrangement changed its delivery timing without requiring a new special envoy. On April 9, Prueher accepted one unidentified minor change; the chapter correctly declines to supply its contents. [EPD04 paras 1–7; EPD05 paras 2–4, 8–9; EPD06 paras 2–5.]

The April 11 exchange is particularly well grounded. Advance signed copies were requested and refused; the account preserves both the Chinese explanation and American concern without inventing a plan to misuse a copy. The transfer memorandum was revised after Lu's claim that no time remained to change it. The 4:50 meeting and projected arrival intervals are documented, as are the subsequent 5:30 delivery and permission for the charter's 6 a.m. arrival the next day. The narration distinguishes these prospective assurances from an already completed journey. [EPD09 paras 3, 6–7, 10–14; EPD08 paras 2–4, 13.]

The two-letter distinction is now independently verified: EPD08 para 3 records the Powell–Qian authority letter; para 4 records delivery of the Prueher–Tang settlement letter; para 14 reproduces the latter. The full Powell–Qian authority letter remains missing. The separate correspondence cannot be merged simply because both were presented to Tang. EPD07 para 5 also supports the narrower claim about Keith declining further substantive argument to preserve Prueher's channel.

Tang's insistence on facts, evidence and legal claims remains visible. The narrative does not adopt the cable's tendentious headings as neutral judgments. It also preserves the continued disagreement over responsibility at the moment the release decision was announced. The reported wording difference is not turned into a finding that the two governments agreed on fault or that a mistranslation accidentally created the settlement.

## Incorporate the completed Jost/CIA reading in note 8

The current body already keeps the institutional account separate from proof of the cause of release. It does not need another theory paragraph. Update the stale reading and access claims in note 8, and preserve the qualifier on the CIA record. A workable replacement is:

> Tyler Jost, “The Institutional Origins of Miscalculation in China's International Crises,” *International Security* 48(1), 2023, 47–90, especially pp. 80–90 and notes 142–191; article read in full, with the appendix's complete methods, EP-3 coding and personalism sections read as specified in JCI-01–02. His institutional account does not identify a demonstrated information correction that caused release. CIA information report C00785245, distributed April 17, 2001, JCI-04, pp. 1–2, is explicitly unevaluated; its source and date of information are redacted. It describes likely senior Chinese recognition of pilot responsibility after crew release and forecasts continued demands in the April 18 talks. It cannot date a pre-release change of belief. EPD05 and EPD08 now document the separate authority and settlement correspondence; the full authority letter and intermediate complete settlement drafts remain unacquired.

Link the [full-reading memo](../../literature/readings/jost-crisis-institutions.md) and [register](../../sources/jost-crisis-institutions-register.json); use the register's archived valid CIA PDF if providing a direct scan link. The live official attachment currently redirects. The CIA report's para 3 also says Beijing expected Washington to resist ending reconnaissance but would continue pressing the demand. That is useful evidence about the difference between demands and expectations, but it remains the report's assessment. It is unnecessary to interrupt the now stronger cable narrative to insert it in the body.

The newly acquired cables replace the old blanket claim that the underlying diplomatic record was unavailable. They do not supply Chinese internal approval records or establish that the reform-era institutional mechanism has continued unchanged under Xi. The source improvement should strengthen the concrete narrative without inflating either causal claim.
