## Assessment and one requested correction

The replacement now explains decisions. The reader can follow an administrative choice opposed by American producers, a further adjustment China sought but did not obtain, and a later domestic reversal. That sequence supplies an answer to the opening question about what a legal victory might make commercially usable. It also makes the procedural agreement more than a specimen of detailed drafting: the governments preserved a route for disagreement after implementation.

The climate sequence is stronger for a different reason. Xie's account provides a Chinese description of the relationship between national decision and reciprocal acceptance, rather than leaving Stern to speak for both delegations. The modeling disagreement also gives technical expertise an intelligible role. It informed a political choice among alternatives; it did not mechanically produce the national target. Lima and Paris then establish why bilateral endorsement was useful without being sufficient for multilateral assent.

**Change the heading “An opening shaped by an earlier failure” at line 63.** “What Copenhagen left unfinished” would fit the evidence better. Alternatively, identify the failure as obtaining the assent of the whole conference. The surrounding paragraphs establish a substantive consultation-and-analysis bargain alongside failure to adopt the accord by consensus. An unqualified failure heading obscures the distinction the text successfully explains. This is an interpretive framing correction, not a new claim that Copenhagen achieved its participants' full purposes.

No other consequential factual correction is requested on the reading performed here.

## Bounds that the revision preserves

- **The Zibo puzzle is limited honestly.** Lines 21–23 use the land inquiry to explain how a finding could be made again, then explicitly separate it from the unexplained magnitude of the petrochemical-related CVD increase. Neither the land example nor the common arithmetic increment is treated as a reconstruction of the missing calculation. Preserve that separation even if the source discussion is shortened later.
- **The apparent relief is not a fabricated before-and-after duty bill.** Line 33 names the original dumping margin and revised deposit rate separately, retains prospective application and intervening-review limits, and identifies the favorable administrative decision rather than estimating taxes actually saved. The 2016 coda does not become a WTO compliance ruling or a claim about every entry.
- **The May agreement is procedural bargaining.** The chapter does not convert the agreed sequence into an American admission of noncompliance, immediate retaliation or proof of undisclosed bargaining intensity. September positions remain opposed. The absence of an examined later compliance judgment does not become an explanation of China's private strategy.
- **The Chinese climate account is not a numerical concession ledger.** National choice, discussion and mutual acceptability are all present in Xie's wording. The prose does not invent an exchange in which a particular American increment bought a particular Chinese increment. The difference between the two participants' starting points remains in the note rather than being resolved through an invented common chronology.
- **Institutional changes have concrete scope.** The trade half concerns China's use of membership after entry. The climate half concerns specified procedures, target forms and multilateral assent. Neither claims that Chinese technical competence, differentiation, bureaucratic variety or selective cooperation began after Solomon. The close is consistent with the corrected proposition assessment rather than covertly reintroducing “revised” classifications.
- **Paris acceptance remains narrower than guaranteed performance.** The target forms are distinct; the text separates obligations to prepare, communicate, maintain and pursue action from compulsory achievement of the stated result. Adoption, consent and delivery are not collapsed. Finance announcements are not presented as disbursements.

## Optional small edits for continuity

These are choices for the author, not conditions for accepting the substantive argument.

1. **Orient the reader at the return to May 2012 (line 41).** The previous paragraph ends in 2016. “Four years earlier, China had prepared for continuing disagreement” would make the backward turn immediately legible. The date already prevents a factual ambiguity; this would improve the movement of the narrative.
2. **Cut the separate work-plan qualification at line 75.** The preceding paragraph already says what the plan assigned and called for, and note 19 explicitly limits plans versus completed results. The standalone paragraph returns briefly to the voice of an evidence audit just as the chapter is ready to resume the two envoys' work. Removing it would preserve the material limit.
3. **Tighten the methodological lead-in at line 91.** “The record does not show a numerical trade…” is a valuable restriction. The next sentence, “That narrower finding is substantial,” tells the reader how to value it when the following practical implication already demonstrates its importance. Cutting that sentence would give the implication more force.

The chapter should retain its current balance of trade and climate. More compressed legal terminology alone would not improve it: the actual contested calculation and two different uses of procedural agreement are what make the legal half useful. Likewise, the chapter need not force a cultural explanation onto either case. Its contribution is to show which consequential decisions Chinese specialists could now contest or help make, and where authorization by their own government or assent from other governments remained necessary.

## Outstanding evidence, without postponing the draft

The most valuable missing trade material is still specific: the May 18, 2012 calculation and Attachment 3, plus the reasoning behind the later remand. Their recovery could explain the magnitude of the CVD rise or the withdrawal of the AD adjustment. Until then, the chapter should answer what decision the legal victory changed, as it presently does, rather than promise a causal decomposition of the 53.80-point increase.

For the climate account, a better private chronology could establish how the participants moved from national estimates to acceptable proposals and whether particular choices changed during those exchanges. The current public participant accounts support a narrower but useful conclusion already: joint endorsement could be negotiated while each government retained the authority to choose its target. No further source acquisition is required to state that conclusion with the present attribution.
