# Trade-process research and Chapter 5 redevelopment

15 September 2026. This pass revises the chapter’s explanation, not just its sentences. The earlier version repeatedly described what the record could establish while leaving its protagonists’ problems abstract. The new chapter follows a concrete question: what would make another promise worth accepting when neither government trusted performance to follow from signature?

## New primary reading

[DEV-TR01](trade-process-register.json), the US government's February 22, 2019 public meeting transcript, was read on complete pages 1–6, 8 and 14–16. Page 15 was visually inspected. This is selected reading of an eighteen-page document, not inspection of the private meeting or video. It shows Trump objecting to the proposed document’s title while Lighthizer treats the MOU as a contract; Liu agrees with Lighthizer and then accepts the change of name. Lighthizer expressly retains the same document. The chapter does not attribute the printed laughter cue to Liu or invent his reaction. The exchange establishes public disagreement within the US side, not a legal rule about every MOU or evidence that the disagreement caused the May breakdown.

[DEV-TR02](trade-process-register.json), the published February 27 House hearing, was read at printed pp. 13–16 and 55–58; p. 57 was visually inspected. This eight-page selection includes the relevant complete exchanges with Neal, Brady, Schweikert and Sánchez, with adjacent discussion. It adds the complaint mechanism’s stated purpose, which the opening statement alone could not supply. Lighthizer anticipated anonymous company complaints and unilateral proportionate action after escalation. His invocation of Chinese reformers is an American negotiating premise, not independent proof of a particular Chinese coalition. His general accusations and members’ company-specific allegations are not adopted as verified findings.

Both government PDFs were retrieved directly from GovInfo, preserved outside Git, and hashed in the accompanying register. These public transcripts do not replace the missing spring 2019 drafts or internal Chinese instructions.

## Rechecked existing evidence

The complete substantive Xinhua May 2019 account of Liu’s three concerns and the January 2020 post-signature briefing were reread. The latter explicitly combines reform and opening with continued Party leadership and the socialist system. The chapter attributes that explanation to Liu; it does not claim to establish the internal motive for every concession. Existing source ID: CN-TRADE-LIU20200115. The October 11 public transcript was reread through the trade, tariff, enforcement and phased-agreement exchanges; earlier complete reading remains separately recorded in TUS12.

The English agreement’s Chapter 7, through the first page of Annex 7-A, was reread directly. Article 7.4.1 permits an appeal without company-identifying information and imposes confidentiality. This connects an observed American concern to an accepted provision, without proving which delegation drafted it or how it operated. The original bilingual review continues to control the response, urgent-meeting and termination distinctions. The twenty-one- and forty-five-day periods share the date of receipt; they are not summed.

## What the rewrite changes

The chapter begins with an encounter, gives the participants different objectives, and develops the bargain through the 2018 pause, spring 2019 failure and smaller autumn settlement. It places domestic procedural discretion, confidentiality and remedies inside that conflict. The purchase numbers remain precise but no longer interrupt the opening explanation. Critical scholarship is used to distinguish explanations near the end, rather than supplying an abstract list of mechanisms before the reader has seen the choices.

Practical judgments now arise from those choices: record the status of approval as well as wording; test a smaller package without demanding prior renunciation of principle; identify the implementing act; and establish how a dependent firm can report failure. Each is advice derived from the cases, not a newly measured causal effect or a promise of success. The text retains the costs of the recommended choices: documentation cannot prevent reversal, discretion can complicate verification, confidentiality cannot guarantee anonymity, and a unilateral remedy can threaten the agreement itself.

## Work still needed

The rewrite needs a separate literary and inference review, and the manuscript as a whole needs comparable redevelopment. It still lacks a Chinese internal account of the 2019 approval sequence. Contemporary supply-chain bargaining may materially change the later chapters’ balance of power; that research is being pursued separately. This memo records progress toward the publication objective, not its completion.
