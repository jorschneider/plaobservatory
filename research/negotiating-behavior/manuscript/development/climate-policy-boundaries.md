# Climate bargaining: public limits and domestic purposes

15 September 2026. Five new reading objects supplement the participant and literature work. Three were read completely; two were read in specified sections. The [register](climate-policy-boundaries-register.json) records originals, hashes and exact coverage. Raw captures remain outside Git.

## A public position before the final Copenhagen encounter

**CPB01.** The MFA account of He Yafei's December 17, 2009 briefing, posted December 18, attributes to Wen a distinction between a non-negotiable national target and possible improvements in reporting and international dialogue. This prospective statement is a better anchor for Chapter 3 than relying solely on the government's December 24 defense. It establishes public room for discussion before the final meeting, without establishing authorization of the eventual consultation-and-analysis procedure.

The briefing also favors recording commitments under differentiated responsibilities. It therefore cannot settle Froman's recollection of uncertainty about how commitments would be listed. A publicly defended distinction, a negotiator's interpretation and a final agreed procedure are separate evidence. The chapter retains that separation and does not use this report to determine the disputed object of Wen's assent.

**CPB02.** Wen's March 14, 2010 answer to a *Newsweek* question concerns an alleged failure to notify China of a **December 17 evening** meeting, before Obama had arrived. It is not the December 18 Obama–BASIC encounter. The complete question and answer were read; the rest of the press conference was not systematically read. Wen's public defense supplies an attributed protocol grievance, not the invitation log. No new scene is inserted on its authority.

## Domestic purposes do not amount to a single emissions preference

**CPB03.** The entire 2013 air-pollution plan, all ten sections and 35 numbered measures, was read. Its measures concern public health, industrial emissions and energy use. Section IV combines a lower coal-share objective with support for synthetic gas made from coal; strict water and pollution conditions accompany the latter. Provincial responsibilities and assessments appear later in the plan. It is a published policy instrument, not a record of the 2014 negotiating mandate or proof of implementation.

**CPB05.** Chi-Jen Yang and Robert B. Jackson's three-page 2013 *Nature Climate Change* commentary was read completely, including 15 references, Table 1 and both figures; all pages were visually inspected. The article uses life-cycle estimates to warn that making synthetic natural gas from coal could increase greenhouse-gas emissions. Paired with the plan, it reveals a tension between intended air-quality benefits and estimated carbon consequences. Approved capacity and modeled long-term effects are not realized production or measured national emissions. The chapter uses the direction of this tradeoff, not the numerical projections. Figure 1's gas comparison uses shale gas; Figure 2's cost estimates have different assumptions. The unrelated commentary beginning at the bottom of p.854 is outside this reading. This is complete technical context, listed separately from the social-science/legal scholarly-reading tally.

Together with Williams's pre-announcement interviews, these sources improve the question asked of domestic interests. A negotiator should investigate which measure serves an existing Chinese purpose, which obligation it would add, and whose cooperation implementation requires. It would be a mistake to infer that because Beijing wanted cleaner air, Washington could obtain an absolute emissions commitment or international scrutiny at no additional political cost. Conversely, international pressure need not explain every domestic measure preceding an agreement.

## A dating trap in the energy strategy

**CPB04.** The acquired NEA reproduction of the 2014–2020 energy strategy bears the instrument date June 7, 2014 and web date December 3. Its notice explicitly says the text is abridged and some wording adjusted. Only the notice, introduction, all of Section I and Section II(a), items 1–5, were read. The later substantive sections were not read in full. These selected sections combine non-fossil-energy and gas objectives with coal development and energy security.

The printed instrument date cannot establish that every published term was fixed before the November 12 joint announcement. A dated original version or independently dated quotation would be needed for that claim. The manuscript therefore uses the complete 2013 plan for its pre-announcement policy comparison and keeps the 2014 document as contextual and methodological evidence. The original government's reported November publication was located in search but not separately acquired; no exact first-publication claim rests on it.

## Integration and remaining limits

Chapter 3 now places He's public position beside the disputed witness accounts; Xie's 2012 speech and Zou's retrospective description supply the organizational bridge to 2014. The new domestic-policy paragraph distinguishes possible overlap in interests from the obligations to be negotiated. Högl's participant research adds the conference presidency's need to retain the trust of delegations outside the final conversations. These additions do not recover Chinese instructions, explain each concession, or measure an increase in flexibility under Xi. They make the observable work and the remaining decisions more specific.
