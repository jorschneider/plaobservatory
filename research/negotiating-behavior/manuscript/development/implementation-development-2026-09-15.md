# Developing Chapter 6: what the beneficiary needs

15 September 2026. This is the developmental record for the new narrative and argument, read alongside the [independent review](chapter-06-development-review-2026-09-15.md) and [2025 export-control reconstruction](trade-2025-export-controls-evidence.md). It is not a certification of publication quality.

The earlier chapter repeatedly told the reader that announcements, permissions and operation were different. The revision makes people confront those differences. It opens with Josh Gackle and Geoff Cooper asking the same enforcement hearing for different responses. Their disagreement establishes a choice: how much prospective commerce an administration should risk to pursue past commitments. Their judgments remain attributed testimony rather than narrator-certified descriptions of every market condition.

The finance sequence now follows a company into business. Mark Leung's desire for an integrated operation gives the continued regulatory relationship a commercial purpose. The annual reports show preparation and activity alongside the regulator's decisions. The agreement's measurable contribution remains the eight-month acceleration of the published ownership timetable; the initial operating permit still precedes signature. Revenue is evidence of business, not an estimate of the causal return to foreign ownership.

The seed sequence gives the 2017 American correspondence enough space to show a company seeking access, supplying an official's preparation and pressing a complaint after a political announcement. The shift to the later agreement is analytical: a particular permission differs from a dependable procedure for future applications. The text does not claim that these emails caused the Phase One annex, disclose the Chinese deliberation or identify a missing soybean attachment as Enlist E3.

The new 2025 section asks how an administration can relieve a shortage while retaining a control. Its reciprocal structure matters: Chinese licensing and American rescission both have to be followed. The section supplies a contemporary account of material leverage without treating every delay as a deliberate signal. The closing practical choice is between demanding repeal and accepting usable permissions, with different consequences for immediate production and future exposure. It is a historical inference about bargaining design, not guidance on the export rules currently in force.
