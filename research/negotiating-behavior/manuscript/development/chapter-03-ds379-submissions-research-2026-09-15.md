# DS379: search for the Chinese administrative submissions

Research follow-up, 15 September 2026. This is a bounded acquisition report for Chapter 3, not a new finding of Chinese administrative strategy. No chapter or case file was edited. The companion [register](chapter-03-ds379-submissions-register.json) records the exact documents sought, the new captures, selected reading scopes and search expressions.

## Result

**No original Chinese questionnaire response or administrative brief used in the July 31, 2012 sacks Section 129 decision was recovered.** Consequently, this search does not complete the desired original-submission → agency-decision reconstruction. It makes that remaining gap much more specific. The existing chapter can explain Commerce's account of a contested Chinese argument, but should not imply that its author's reading independently established everything China submitted, omitted or reserved for another forum.

The principal retrieval target is now the government's December 19, 2011 land questionnaire response, especially pp. 4–5, together with its September 16 response and the April 16, 2012 comments. These could test whether Commerce fairly represented China's explanation of the relationship between the county's price circular and comparison transactions. The next-best target is the public-body questionnaire response cited under the date February 21, 2012 in the circular-welded-pipe proceeding, with parallel sacks passages precisely identified in Commerce's footnotes. That would test the fuller legal argument about commercial independence, equal treatment and the role of SASAC.

The failure is an acquisition result, not evidence that the Chinese filings were secret, unavailable to the parties, weak, or nonexistent. Commerce's current public-document system requires a registered account even to retrieve public versions. No authenticated session was provided or used; no account was created, message sent, or access control bypassed.

## What the preserved original actually identifies

The starting document remains DEV-DS01: Commerce's *Final Determinations: Section 129 Proceedings Pursuant to the WTO Appellate Body's Findings in WTO DS 379 Regarding the Antidumping and Countervailing Duty Investigations of Laminated Woven Sacks from the People's Republic of China*, July 31, 2012. Its stamp identifies **C-570-917, barcode 3089955-01, S129—SEC 129, January 1–December 31, 2006**, filed July 31, 2012 at 6:03 p.m. The caption also identifies A-570-916. This is the final memorandum's barcode. The Chinese filings' barcodes are not supplied in its citations; none has been inferred from nearby numbers.

The memorandum combines several records. Page 2 explains that public-body and double-remedy comments from the four product investigations are included across the final determinations. Land specificity is particular to sacks. Thus an off-the-road-tyres submission cited in this memorandum cannot simply be relabeled a sacks submission. The target table in the register preserves these distinctions.

The land sequence is unusually tractable at the level of citations:

- The September 16, 2011 government response, p. 11, is cited at final p. 5 n. 10 for China's objection to authenticating and dating an industrial-park policy. This is a cited passage, not the entire response or its attachments.
- The December 19, 2011 response, pp. 4–5, is cited at final p. 6 nn. 11–12 for the absence of a formal county price survey until 2007 and for the explanation linking Aifudi's location under the circular with surrounding-county prices. Commerce describes this as the second questionnaire response. The full wording of the question, the answer and its exhibits remains unacquired.
- Domestic petitioners submitted new factual material on March 2, 2012; China rebutted it on March 8. Commerce issued its land preliminary determination April 9. China filed comments April 16; the petitioners rebutted April 20 (final pp. 2–3).
- Aifudi's own November 6, 2007 questionnaire response, p. 11, supplies the statement about attracting business and future tax revenue quoted by Commerce (final p. 4 n. 5). This is a company response from the original investigation, not a 2012 Chinese government statement.

The public-body sequence supplies a different test. Commerce's p. 10 n. 21 names the **CWP** government questionnaire response of February 21, 2012. Its footnotes then give parallel page numbers in sacks and the other proceedings. The sacks passages include pp. 4–7, 9–10, 12–14 and 16–18. They concern company and tax law, competition law, sovereign immunity, and SASAC's alleged separation of ownership from government functions. The date is explicit for the CWP document; the same date should not be silently assigned to every parallel response without its cover sheet. A supplemental response is cited at final p. 14 n. 42, but its date is not given there. China's comments on the public-body preliminary findings were filed June 1, 2012 in all four CVD proceedings (final p. 7).

For double remedies, final p. 17 n. 50 cites government responses dated April 11 and April 25, 2012 in discussing petitioners' objections about short-run pricing. Final p. 25 n. 75 specifically cites the April 11 response, p. 5, alongside Aifudi's September 10, 2007 response, Appendix 2. Page 45 supplies the formal title of the **tyres** April 11 letter: *Government of China's Response to the Section 129 Double Counting Questionnaire: Certain Off-the-Road Tires from China*. It likewise identifies China's June 15 comments on the preliminary double-remedy determination in tyres. Page 15 records parallel June 15 Chinese comments in all four CVD proceedings. The complete submissions and the allocation of all exhibits between these records remain unverified.

## The strongest argument we can presently reconstruct—and its limit

The land dispute can already be reconstructed from the **decision-maker's account**. China invoked more than a denial of subsidy. According to Commerce, it said that a county price decision could draw simultaneously on an applicable circular and transactions in a neighboring county. Different nominal prices did not by themselves establish a legally distinct regime. China also challenged the provenance and applicability of evidence connecting industrial-park land preferences to investment commitments (final pp. 3–6).

Commerce rejected that explanation. It found no demonstrated uniform valuation method connecting the circular's prices to objective criteria across the county. It also said that China had not reconciled use of comparison transactions with the circular's stipulated prices. The agency treated foreign-investment preferences and other investment-linked evidence as supporting distinct treatment, while acknowledging that it could not date one policy submitted by the petitioners (p. 5 n. 10). These are consequential evidentiary disagreements, not a simple disagreement about whether land occupies a place.

But three questions cannot be settled without the submissions. Did the December response explain the combination more fully than Commerce's quotation suggests? What authentication efforts and exhibits accompanied China's objections? Did the April comments confront Commerce's proposed objective-valuation distinction in terms the final summary omitted? Repeating the final memorandum's accusation that China failed to supply an explanation would not independently answer them.

The same boundary applies to Commerce's public-body account. Pages 7–9 say that China objected to the demands for information and reserved detailed substantive views for the WTO's Dispute Settlement Body. Commerce used that reservation to explain why it was addressing some arguments from earlier questionnaire responses despite limited comments on the preliminary result. This supports a carefully attributed description of positions in the record. It does not establish, from China's own filing, who chose that allocation of effort, why they chose it, or whether it was a calculated bargaining tactic. The register flags this as an interpretation that remains dependent on the American memorandum.

## Public-access and reproduction checks

The current ACCESS landing page was captured, along with its March 2026 handbook and new-user guide. The handbook's §5, printed/PDF pp. 20–21, says that access to public documents and public versions requires an authorized E-Filer or Guest User login; someone only wishing to view or download them must register as a Guest User. The new-user guide's pp. 1–5 describes email verification and provision of the user's name. These instructions explain why the public landing page does not itself provide an anonymous search of the historical record. The obsolete `termsconditions.aspx` URL returned 404 when opened; its indexed text is not used as the governing current instruction.

Thirty-one search expressions tested exact dates, quoted titles, the known barcode, case number, Chinese terminology, and possible later-litigation reproductions. The relevant exact-title hits repeatedly led back to the already preserved Commerce final memorandum or parallel American decisions. Results about Aifudi's separate-rate litigation concerned other proceedings, not the target government responses. Chinese-language and MOFCOM-domain searches did not locate an original responsive filing. The query log records that limited outcome; it is not a claim that all public repositories have been exhausted.

A plausible reproduction trail arose in DS437 compliance litigation. The United States' February 6, 2017 first submission includes a table of exhibits, read in full at printed pp. x–xix (PDF pp. 11–20). Exhibits USA-1–82 trace the May 18, 2012 Commerce public-body memorandum and its source materials. The list identifies laws, official plans, scholarship and reporting, rather than one of the target Chinese administrative responses. Exhibits USA-118–120 and USA-122 do identify Chinese ministry questionnaire responses, but they are dated **July 6, 2015** and belong to **DS437**. They are not the missing 2011–2012 DS379 filings. The March 27, 2017 second submission's printed pp. vii and 1–2 (PDF pp. 8–10) confirms the earlier source-material cross-reference and adds no target filing to its own short exhibit list. These two submissions were acquired only for that bounded check; neither was read in full, and their separate exhibit files were not acquired.

## Consequence for Chapter 3

No replacement paragraph is recommended on the strength of this search. The current trade narrative already begins the administrative exchange with “Commerce's account records,” and its source note keeps the underlying-file limitation explicit. A useful sentence could be added only if editorial balance calls for it: **“According to Commerce, China also reserved its detailed objections to the public-body findings for the WTO, preserving a dispute over the rules even as the agency completed its own inquiry.”** It would still require attribution and would not add an independently recovered Chinese voice.

The priority for any later authorized retrieval is narrow: C-570-917, Section 129, period 2006; obtain the public versions of the land response dated December 19, 2011 and comments dated April 16, 2012, with the September 16, 2011 response and Commerce's questions for context. Record their actual barcodes from the returned documents. That would test an existing consequential passage without expanding the chapter into another dispute. The missing petrochemical calculation attachment remains a separate gap; none of the present work explains the precise 29.54-to-83.34 percent rate change.
