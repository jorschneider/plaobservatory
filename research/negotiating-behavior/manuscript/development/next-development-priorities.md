## 3. Chapter 7: choose episodes within the comparisons

India, the Russian border and gas each have a strong story. The current chapter often substitutes a calendar of meetings and agreement stages for that story.

**India:** build around Jaishankar's March 2022 meeting with Wang Yi and refusal to call the relationship normal, followed by the concrete PP15 disengagement terms and renewed disagreement over whether local progress justified broader cooperation. Those official exchanges, Chadha's fully read reconstruction and the military/diplomatic releases already show a counterpart using the relationship as pressure. The 2024 endorsement sequence can then be shorter: it confirms that a summit can affirm work already done. Give the reader a brief geographical orientation before moving among Pangong, Gogra–Hot Springs, Depsang and Demchok. Preserve different obligations and the official rather than independently verified character of troop-movement accounts.

**Russian border:** center the local-use accommodation. Iwashita's already-read Chapters 1 and 6, the published 1999 agreement and the Kommersant reports identify people and offices with land, livelihoods, regional authority and ratification at stake. Show why accepting sovereignty over a place did not have to eliminate every established use. Introduce the permit and duration rules as the solution those parties accepted. Do not populate the story with imaginary farmers or infer numbers benefiting from missing annexes. The Russian presidential order can then demonstrate a real mandate in a paragraph, rather than become another methodological aside.

**Gas:** use Putin's May 21, 2014 explanation and the pricing correction prompted by Peskov as the documentary center. Something important had been achieved after prolonged work, yet the public account did not disclose a usable price. Trace the subsequent financing option and actual eastern supply before returning to the western proposals. Introduce the other routes when they become necessary; the opening declaration that four projects “must remain separate” asks the reader to memorize the research system before encountering the commercial problem. Paik and Yermakov–Meidan supply contemporary expectations, geography and alternatives, with projections kept distinct from results.

**Next research:** recover the two omitted local-use annexes and, if available, a narrow implementation record showing how permits worked. Obtain the original FT gas report to replace reliance on Reuters' short summary. Prior offers, price formulas and the newer construction memorandum remain the evidence needed for a stronger claim about a concession extracted from Russia; do not commission another general dependence essay as a substitute. ## Across the four

Keep uncertainty at the decision it changes. Move repeated statements about unmatched comparisons, unavailable private mandates and the existence of several possible mechanisms into notes once their practical consequence is clear. Retain distinctiveness versus uniqueness and the absence of causal identification; do not make every case end by restating them.

A revised episode should leave a reader able to say what each side wanted, what blocked the exchange, what an identifiable participant tried and what the accepted terms changed. Where the record supports only the last of those, write a shorter institutional account rather than manufacturing a negotiation scene. The ambition is compelling, useful nonfiction; these priorities are a route to substantive rewriting, not certification that the manuscript has reached publication standard.
