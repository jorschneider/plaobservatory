# Applicant evidence: a testing sequence, still without a review history

15 September 2026. Bounded follow-up to Chapter 6's Enlist E3 and DP73496 cases. The question was whether an accessible applicant record could connect a Chinese request, a company response and the resulting permission. No such complete exchange was recovered. This memo records a useful lead and prevents unrelated regulatory work from being attributed to China.

## The Qian paper

Zhiyong Qian and six coauthors, [“Safety evaluation of DAS-44406-6 soybeans in Wistar rats”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29199065/), *Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology* 92 (2018), 152–164, DOI 10.1016/j.yrtph.2017.11.016; first published online December 1, 2017. Read: the complete PubMed abstract, author affiliations and bibliographic record. The article's full text was **not** obtained or read. Publisher metadata identifies an open-access article under CC BY 4.0, but the accessible API response contains metadata only.

The abstract reports an initial 90-day Wistar-rat feeding study at a Tianjin laboratory, a kidney finding in two high-dose females, and a larger confirmatory study that did not reproduce treatment-related kidney changes. The authors interpret the findings as supporting safety. Affiliations include Tianjin's disease-control institution, Charles River Laboratories and Dow AgroSciences. This is evidence of a reported testing sequence involving the product and Chinese researchers, not independent adjudication of the scientific conclusion.

It does **not** establish when Chinese regulators requested further work, whether they requested this confirmatory study, when either report entered the application, or how much of the interval before approval that work explains. Publication in 2017 and approval in 2018 cannot supply those missing links. Search-indexed fragments of an author upload add methodological detail but have not been treated as a full reading. The article therefore remains a research lead outside the chapter's narrative and outside the count of fully read scholarly works.

The separate ministry search found a safety-data landing page linking a DAS-44406-6 dossier, but the linked attachment returned 404. Its existence and indexed description are not a recovered dossier. The [administrative-review memo](agricultural-review-process-memo.md) records that search and the original ministry guidance actually acquired.

## An attribution trap in contemporary reporting

Emily Unglesbee's [June 14, 2017 DTN report](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/crops/article/2017/06/14/enlist-corn-trait-gets-green-light-2) describes Chinese approval of Enlist corn and outstanding Chinese and European approvals for Enlist E3 soybeans. Its discussion of an additional-data demand and a delay of more than a year concerns **the US Environmental Protection Agency's review of the Enlist Duo herbicide**. It cannot explain the Chinese soybean review. The complete substantive article body was read through the web tool; a separate raw article capture was not retained. Existing evidence already supports the launch distinction, so this is a rejected inference, not an added manuscript source.

The next valuable document is an identified application report or dated regulator-applicant exchange that names the testing issue, not another announcement of approval. This pass does not change the chapter's conclusion about the unexplained interval or its existing application dates.
