## 1. What should a negotiator actually keep—and stop treating as a forecast?

The manuscript now establishes important continuities, but gives the practical verdict less prominence than the qualifications. The introduction calls the inheritance “a set of questions”; the baseline chapter carefully preserves strong original predictions; the conclusion says those predictions remain unvalidated. A reader can understand all three and still be uncertain whether the recommendation is to use Solomon's account, suspend judgment on most of it, or substitute elementary contract management. The appendix's 38 unresolved full propositions cannot answer that practical question by itself.

The existing episodes support a firmer **working model**, without validating every explanation Solomon attached to it. Chinese officials negotiate concrete accommodations while defending political positions; agreed limits and reservations matter; access is used to obtain decisions and enlist help; permission must pass through officials able to authorize and carry out the requested act. Normalization, accession, the EP-3 letter, Phase One, climate diplomacy and the Russian local-use arrangements repeatedly supply identified acts supporting these conclusions. Their explanatory force does not require a claim that the practices are uniquely Chinese or always successful. S-C10/12, S-P12/19/22 and the observed component of S-P25 are useful anchors, with their original qualifications retained.

The corresponding update in **use** can also be stated firmly. Do not commit resources on the assumption that rigidity diagnoses factional conflict or that a leader's appearance establishes imminent closure. A senior encounter remains useful; its specific authorization, confirmation or refusal is the information to obtain. This is a decision rule under the demonstrated risk of unfinished obligations, not a finding that S-C19 or S-P24 is statistically false. Similarly, continued negotiation is not itself evidence of asymmetric noncompliance: first establish the obligation and the requested departure.

**Concrete editorial change:** replace the introduction's general “useful inheritance is a set of questions” sentence, or the conclusion's late general summary, with a short verdict that names the retained explanation and the changed use. Do not add another full case inventory. For example:

> Solomon remains most useful as an account of how political purpose enters a bargain. A defended position can leave room for a specific accommodation; a relationship can help obtain an answer without supplying authority to make the commitment. Those features recur in the later negotiations. His stronger forecasts should guide inquiry rather than a decision to close. A hard line does not establish the division behind it, and a senior meeting does not identify which terms have been accepted. The practical update is to use access to test a defined exchange and obtain an attributable answer, while retaining protection against the decisions still outstanding.

The word “recurs” here concerns demonstrated conduct. It does not upgrade unresolved cultural causes, comparative frequencies or effects. The current assessments can remain unchanged while the main text becomes more decisive about what a negotiator should do.

## 2. What has historical change added beyond a longer list of institutions?

The introduction and conclusion now name real developments, but the explanation can distinguish their consequences more confidently. Three different changes are being compressed into “changed responsibilities, capabilities and sources of pressure.”

**Acquired legal capability changes the route an objection can take.** Membership, trained personnel, counsel relationships and working procedures let China develop a claim requiring the United States to answer under accepted rules. The point is not a counted increase in assertiveness. It is the movement from a bilateral refusal to an additional, specified procedure, whose current appellate availability must be checked by counterpart. Chapter 3 and the [legal-capacity bridge](legal-capacity-bridge-2026-09-15.md) already give the reported learning practices and their limits.

**Industrial dependence changes the consequence of an administrative permission.** In the documented 2025 exchange, Chinese control over supplies gave foreign manufacturers reason to seek licensing relief, while American software restrictions gave China a reciprocal act to seek. The licensing authority could survive the transaction that restored shipments. This is an observed use of capabilities in a particular exchange, not a claim that export leverage began under Xi or that China dictated the whole bargain. It can be asserted without calculating the confidential price of the London agreement.

**Xi-era evidence changes some working conditions of contact more clearly than it demonstrates a change in concession flexibility.** The formal reforms specify coordination and execution responsibilities. Loh's fieldwork additionally reports tighter informal access and loyalty expectations alongside continuing initiative. That is closer to the user's behavioral question than a list of offices alone. Its practical consequence is bounded: an accompanied meeting or unavailable private dinner need not be read as a personal rebuff; a counterpart may need a format through which an exploratory proposal can be discussed or referred. It does not show that every official has less discretion in 2026. See [Loh reading](../../literature/readings/loh-diplomatic-control.md), especially original pp. 126–129 and its sampling limits.

**Concrete editorial change:** in the conclusion's institutional discussion, replace part of the repeated general instruction to identify the responsible body with this observed contact constraint and its consequence. In the historical verdict, name the two external options—legal challenge and control over commercially necessary supplies—rather than concluding mainly with what the study cannot measure. Preserve that legal-capacity development began before Xi. The result is an assertive, differentiated account of change, not a national-style verdict.

The appendix currently weakens that synthesis through two stale statements. **F07** says the learning trajectory is unidentified and asks for counsel/resource evidence while citing only DS379 and climate outcomes. The later acquired Shaffer–Gao/Jiang material supplies reported pairing with experienced counsel, third-party apprenticeship, policy-directed training and one-year secondments from 2011. **F12** says no training or mentoring record has been acquired. The corpus now includes research reporting on-the-job legal learning, although it still does not trace the reproduction of particular bargaining practices across staff turnover. Update those positive observations and narrow the remaining question. Preserve both full-proposition statuses and every baseline field.
