## Two corrections recommended

1. **Chapter 4, May paragraph's final sentence:** “Washington was offering contact while retaining a restriction that Beijing said made contact unacceptable” is more definite than Mao's answer. She begins by acknowledging necessary communication, criticizes sanctions on officials, institutions and enterprises, and demands their withdrawal to create conditions. The preceding chapter sentences and note preserve that scope correctly, but the final formulation can sound like an explicit single-restriction prohibition on contact. Suggested replacement: **“Washington offered contact without delisting Li; Beijing called for sanctions relief to create the conditions for dialogue.”** This retains a concrete disputed choice without turning the reported objection into a guaranteed exchange or a statement that all contact was unacceptable.

2. **Conclusion, institutional-example sentence:** “access through Wang Yi did not secure a defense-minister meeting” can suggest an established attempt to use Wang's channel to obtain that meeting. The public Vienna readout establishes strategic contact; it does not disclose such a request or Wang's response. Suggested replacement: **“strategic contact with Wang Yi coexisted with refusal of the proposed defense-minister meeting.”** The subsequent point—that presidential agreement still required departmental arrangements—has direct public support and can remain. This changes no substantive conclusion about different forms of access.

## Findings requiring no correction

The August paragraph accurately identifies sanctions on CMC leaders as one expressly stated obstacle within wider objections. It does not misattribute the reporter's hypothesis to Wu or claim a necessary-and-sufficient price.

The October paragraph reports **planned exchanges**, and its modal “could be arranged,” followed by the prospective-source note, does not claim the talks actually occurred. The decree supports removal, absence of a named successor and absence of a stated reason. The November paragraph correctly separates a broader agreement from the still-awaited ministerial appointment and unfinished arrangements. Its refusal to infer that dismissal caused restoration is appropriate. No further chronology or causation caveat is needed.

The conclusion's institutional description is faithful to the selected originals: the 2018 text transfers and consolidates coordinating duties; the 2023 law **states** responsibilities rather than necessarily creating them; the local-affairs report combines central direction, initiative and discipline. The note correctly excludes attributing the May events to a law adopted afterward. The practical advice concerns identifying responsible bodies and observable acts, not obtaining confidential mandates. No inference of interagency conflict or a general rule that subordinates cannot act is introduced.

The additions improve the narrative by giving the reader a specific disagreement, then distinguishing the ministerial vacancy from wider communications authority. Apart from the two sentence refinements above, further methodological qualification would repeat limits already preserved in the notes.
