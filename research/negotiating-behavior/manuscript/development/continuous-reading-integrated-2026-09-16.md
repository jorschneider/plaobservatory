## Historical argument and reading experience

The draft now sustains a connected historical argument. Political purposes, relationships and the organization of authority remain necessary to understand a bargain; changes in legal capability, international procedures and industrial dependence alter what can be offered, withheld or challenged. The later chapters do not require a claim that China discovered precision, legal argument, conditionality or partial agreements after Solomon's period. Chapter 1 makes those practices visible in the older record, allowing the later institutional changes to carry the historical comparison.

The sequence has a clear progression. Normalization and claims/assets establish the difference between proceeding with a reserved disagreement and failing to settle the content of an exchange. Accession then shows what a negotiator can actually trade around a defended ceiling. DS379 follows capabilities made useful by membership, while the climate half shows specialists helping construct procedures still awaiting political acceptance. The crisis chapter distinguishes settling an immediate action from securing a channel that must remain usable. Chapters 5 and 6 move from designing a partial settlement to the different kinds of evidence needed to assess it. India and Russia then make the counterpart's choices and dependencies part of the explanation rather than treating American experience as a universal template.

The newer material generally improves pace because it follows choices made by named participants. The EP-3 draft, authority letter and delivery sequence provide a sustained account of bargaining, with the plane's inspection and removal presenting a different problem afterward. The claims follow-up supplies substance to the alleged disagreement over commitments. Neither sequence needs an inferred private cause to make the conduct intelligible. The dense figures in accession and the implementation chapter also have jobs: they distinguish control from equity, acceleration from an existing opening, and a review average from a deadline for every applicant.

The principal repetitions are purposeful. Authority, partial agreement and remaining discretion recur, but each encounter changes the practical question. The conclusion reprises those findings while developing the difference between an accomplished act and continuing permission. The Gackle–Cooper return at the end of Chapter 6 completes the chapter's enforcement problem rather than merely restating its opening. I found no newly broken transition or redundant passage consequential enough to recommend a cut in this bounded pass.

The practical advice is strongest where it identifies a decision within the visitor's control: specify the benefit a firm needs; ask what status an apparent agreement has; seek compensation the affected constituency can use; distinguish a retained right from an available remedy; and decide which uncertainty is worth bearing. The closing return to Prueher prevents the methodological caution from implying that a negotiator must obtain inaccessible instructions before acting. The manuscript is now an explanatory historical account with practical judgments, rather than a sequence of access audits. That assessment does not turn its unresolved causal claims into established findings.

## One actionable issue

**Chapter 4, “An agreement that had to be explained,” paragraph beginning “At Sun Yuxi's April 11 briefing”: preserve the Chinese contrast accurately.** The sentence says the journalist contrasted a demanded apology with “an expression of deep sorrow.” In the cited Chinese question, the contrast is **道歉** versus **深表歉意**. The latter expresses being deeply sorry or deep regret; “sorrow” risks importing the English letter's grief framing into the Chinese public account. This matters precisely because the paragraph argues that the semantic compromise was questioned inside China's own presentation.

Suggested replacement for the relevant sentence: “China had demanded an apology—道歉—but had allowed the crew to leave after the American expression 深表歉意, being deeply sorry.” The following questions about compromise and additional undertakings can remain. The immediately preceding paragraph already glosses 深表歉意 as “an expression of being deeply sorry,” so using the same wording would also avoid an unexplained shift. This is a translation refinement, not a reversal of the paragraph's argument or proof that the Chinese and English instruments were identical.
