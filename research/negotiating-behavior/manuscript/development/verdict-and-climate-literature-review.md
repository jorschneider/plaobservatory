## One substantive correction, in two locations

**Avoid turning Lamy's request for confirmation into an established sequence of European authorization and compensation.**

The introduction's “Foreign choices” paragraph says Europeans could obtain authoritative confirmation “before asking their own governments to reconsider what they would accept.” The conclusion's first Lamy paragraph says he sought confirmation, “then considered compensating gains.” Both compress the episode into a sequence the detailed chapter correctly declines to establish.

The Chinese published history says that, at the May 19 meeting, Lamy reported consultations with member states and industry during the preceding two days. It also says the European compensation proposal had been presented that morning, before the meeting's conclusion. Chapter 2 already describes earlier compensation proposals and explicitly leaves the exact moment of Brussels's authorization unknown. The original printed p.385 / PDF p.420 was visually reread for this review from the preserved official scan, `tmp/pdfs/research-2026-09-15/eu/general-420.png` (EWT-01). This is a later Chinese-published account, not the internal European mandate.

Suggested surgical substitutions:

- Introduction: “European negotiators could seek authoritative confirmation of a Chinese constraint **while consulting their own governments about what they would accept**.”
- Conclusion: “He **sought authoritative confirmation while negotiating compensating gains** in a package he could defend to his principals.”

These retain the practical point and avoid claiming that Chinese confirmation preceded European consultation or first prompted the search for compensation.

## The positive verdict is supported within its stated scope

No further substantive correction is required to the new continuity/change verdict. The introduction's claim that political purposes organize the bargain is an interpretive synthesis of identified choices, rather than a purported estimate of Chinese national behavior. Its examples immediately distinguish defending one condition from accommodating another and access from decision authority. The accompanying verdict note expressly preserves the original strength of S-C19 and S-P24 rather than counting uncertainty as refutation.

The conclusion also retains the necessary separation: senior intervention's comparative reliability is unvalidated, not disproved; continued work after signature does not itself establish S-P25's asymmetric obligation claim; Freeman's earlier qualifications remain part of the baseline. The concrete changes concern usable legal capacity, different accepted procedures, industrial dependencies and documented institutional responsibilities. None is made to stand for a measured increase or decrease in bargaining flexibility under Xi.

The passage on newer trade procedures correctly contrasts particular mechanisms with the already detailed 1995 arrangement. It does not claim that China only recently began negotiating about domestic administration. The discussion of constrained contact likewise says supervision can change how a proposal is explored, not how much substantive concession is possible.

There is thematic repetition between introduction and conclusion, but it performs a legitimate function: the introduction states the argument and the conclusion evaluates the choices through the episodes. Within the conclusion, the “These developments change the choices” paragraph synthesizes the preceding material and makes the positive verdict explicit. Cutting it would weaken the requested answer about what changed. No essential repetition cut is recommended in this bounded review.
