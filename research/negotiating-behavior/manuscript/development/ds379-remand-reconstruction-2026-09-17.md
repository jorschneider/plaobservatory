# Why the sacks adjustment was withdrawn

September 17, 2026. [Two-source register](ds379-remand-reconstruction-2026-09-17.json), with prior sources explicitly reused. This inquiry closes the earlier gap in the agency's published remand reasoning. It does not recover the companies' reasons for nonresponse, the missing 2011–2012 Chinese administrative submissions, or a WTO ruling on American compliance.

## What the new originals establish

The earlier chapter knew that Commerce withdrew the sacks double-remedy adjustment in 2016 but could not explain the intervening decision. The recovered ten-page remand decision supplies that explanation. Its account runs through parallel litigation over circular welded pipe, reconsideration of the method and new company-specific requests. It identifies the evidence Commerce wanted and says it did not receive it.

The prerequisite court decision is more discriminating than a general rejection of Chinese evidence. In *Wheatland*, Judge Leo Gordon rejected the domestic producers' arguments that the statute plainly confined the evidentiary burden to China or categorically prohibited indirect evidence. He nevertheless required Commerce to explain why its broad Chinese manufacturing data could stand in for the relevant import prices despite contrary evidence. The court remanded for explanation; it had not yet decided the separate challenge to the size of the estimated offset. **DSR16-01**, full 30-page opinion, particularly pp.18–30. That is a pipe case. It is not silently relabeled as sacks litigation.

The sacks decision itself records the connection. The sacks litigation had been stayed by agreement pending the pipe proceedings. Commerce subsequently obtained a voluntary remand and issued December 21, 2015 questionnaires to the two original mandatory respondents. It wanted company-level evidence, including a recent example within the relevant period of a price reduction following lower input or manufacturing costs. According to the agency, neither company submitted a response, comments or a request for an extension by the deadline. The domestic industry committee supported the draft withdrawal; no other party commented on that draft. **DSR16-02**, pp.1,4–8.

This changes the account from an unexplained reversal to an observed administrative sequence. The new record still cannot show whether the firms lacked the data, considered further participation commercially pointless, objected to the request or had another reason. Nor does Commerce's decision establish that the revised duty complied with the WTO ruling. The court's eventual one-page judgment and the Federal Register notice remain the already acquired **DEV-DS05/04**, reread here for the judgment and implementation stages.

## What was read and checked

The root read all 30 pages of the November 26, 2014 opinion, including notes. Page29 was visually checked. The root also read all ten pages of the sacks remand: extraction covered pages1–7 and9–10; page8 was a scan and was read visually and transcribed separately. Page6's request and nonresponse finding were visually checked. This is not a new reading of the underlying party briefs, questionnaires or company files.

The signature on page8 is dated **March21,2016**. The Federal Register notice says the redetermination was issued on **March23**; the court judgment is **March30**, and the subsequent cash-deposit instructions take effect **April11**, subject to intervening review rates. These are distinguishable stages, not grounds to replace one date silently with another. The narrative does not calculate final duties, refunds or lost sales from deposit rates.

The opinion names counsel, including Robert Lighthizer for US Steel. This is a documented professional role, but it has not been added to the narrative merely to anticipate Chapter5. The sources do not establish a personal causal connection between this litigation and his later interstate bargaining choices.
