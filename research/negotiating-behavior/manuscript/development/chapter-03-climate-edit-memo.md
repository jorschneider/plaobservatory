Reporting, review and target obligations arrive as answers to what governments were willing to accept. They no longer form a separate legal inventory after the people disappear.

The text does not infer a negotiated exchange of numerical increments, a Chinese instruction to concede, or a general change of national style. It also avoids making domestic technical disagreement equivalent to paralysis: competing estimates can inform a political choice.

## New acquisition that materially changed the passage

**CLD01** is the complete published Chinese *Caixin* interview with Xie Zhenhua, conducted November 15, 2014 and posted November 21. It was publicly retrievable without authentication. The entire article, Q&A and attached biographical note were read. ChinaFile's December 2 English publication was also fully read, but is explicitly an excerpted version. Its omissions matter: the Chinese section “中美共赢” carries the exchange on how the two sides treated one another's targets. That original supports pairing Xie's account with Stern's. Neither published interview supplies underlying audio, notes, draft texts or the leadership approval record.

The two accounts organize the beginning differently. Stern recounts the US team's January–February initiative; Xie foregrounds Zhang Gaoli's September communication. The note preserves that difference rather than manufacturing a single detailed chronology or declaring a contradiction that the evidence cannot establish. Stern's target-memory error remains corrected against the November announcement, with no extra interruption in the narrative. The new source is a complete participant interview, not a new scholarly reading.

**CLD02** is Ed King's December 16, 2014 report of closing Lima remarks. It provides attributed contemporary statements by Stern and other participants. It is journalistic reporting, not an acquired official transcript or private meeting record. A bounded search did not recover the original press-conference transcript or recording. The current page displays a 2025 update date; its original version was not retrieved. The narrative therefore attributes the meeting and remarks to King and does not adopt the headline's stronger claim that the two envoys saved the conference. The final Lima text independently establishes where the language appeared, while ENB supplies a separate conference-reporting perspective.
