## Result and recommended use

A complete, publicly accessible Chinese transcript supplies a consequential addition: the responsible official's account of a choice inside the litigation team. **DSP17-01**, a university-hosted discussion with Li Chenggang, is appreciably stronger evidence for that purpose than another report celebrating the ruling. It permits a short participant-led passage before the existing legal explanation. It does **not** close the missing-submission gap or explain the government's later decision about compliance litigation.

The most useful editorial change is to connect the professional network already described in Chapter 3 to an actual disagreement over presentation. Retain the existing ruling and administrative sequence as the subsequent test of what the effort delivered. The Huantai opening can remain as the problem to which the narrative returns, or follow the participant passage. There is no evidentiary reason to expand the chapter into DS437.

### Proposed passage

Li Chenggang wanted a different way to present the case. In a university discussion published in May 2011, the director of the commerce ministry's legal department recalled rejecting counsel's initial litigation strategy. Instead of beginning with whether particular products had suffered double counting, he wanted the adjudicators first to understand how the two methods worked together. Counsel returned to the question with senior partners and accepted his approach, he said. This was Li's account of the choice and its success; it did not reveal the adjudicators' deliberations.

The ambition extended beyond four investigations. Li said the team had selected problems affecting Chinese firms more broadly and sought an argument under general WTO rules, rather than one resting on China's special circumstances. Yet, with the appellate result secured, he warned that a victory over interpretation still had to become a benefit for industry. The next choice would concern implementation.[^ds-participant]

## Exact source and attribution boundaries

The original page is on `ielaw.uibe.edu.cn`, the international-economic-law society site hosted by the University of International Business and Economics. Its title identifies a transcript of the seventy-second Clifford Chance forum. Its visible date is **2011-05-23**. The introduction identifies the officials and university participants. No separately identified transcriber, editor, recording, slide deck or event notice was acquired. The safest dating is therefore **a 2011 discussion in a transcript posted May 23**, rather than an independently verified May 23 event. The 2025 English translation by David Cowhig was a discovery aid; its political introduction and translation are not the source of the proposed prose.

The entire substantive Chinese transcript, saved extraction lines **51–138**, was read in three nontruncated blocks. It is approximately 21,000 Chinese-language characters, not an 84-slide presentation: Li mentions a slide deck, but the deck itself was not acquired. Useful complete boundaries for a reviewer are:

This is a public account by an interested participant after an important appellate result. It offers evidence of what Li said he chose, not independent corroboration that the choice caused success. It does not date the internal exchange, identify which counsel proposed the initial strategy, or reproduce either version. His broader reflections cannot be relabeled a particular DS379 instruction. Nor should another participant's prediction about future duty levels become the author's finding about realized commercial relief.

The transcript also contains detectable slips. At line 125, its gloss of SCM Article 19.4 uses **不低于**; the treaty prohibits duties exceeding the subsidy amount, verified against the WTO original in **DSP17-04**. At line 62, the text labels the 2002 steel safeguards “301.” This contradicts the Section 201 identification already established in the research; this pass did not reacquire that separate case. Other obvious wording irregularities include “专项机构” at line 81. These defects counsel against silently treating the transcript as an exact recording. They do not justify correcting its saved body or discarding the carefully attributed account of a presentation choice.

## Counsel interview and the academic distinction

**DSP17-02** is a complete interview/profile by *法人* reporter **Chen Yufeng (陈玉峰)**, reproduced on MOFCOM's site. The HTML publication metadata is **October 15, 2014, 18:16:05**; the exact interview day and the journal's original publication date remain unidentified. All substantive paragraphs, including all eight displayed questions and answers, were read. The site's official hosting does not turn a journal profile into an official ministry statement.

Its strongest first-person contribution is counsel **Xiao Jin's** explanation that government representation requires consistency across disputes because China can be complainant in one and respondent in another. His interview also describes the work of interpreting Chinese law to an international tribunal. The DS379 description of additional appellate preparation is chiefly reporter narration. It neither verifies Li's particular exchange with counsel nor establishes who authored a submission. The profile's unsupported attribution of a US injury determination to Chinese pressure should not be imported into the chapter.

**DSP17-03** completes the previously selected reading of Chen Weidong's “对华贸易救济的争端解决与后续执行——以中国起诉案件为重点.” The reacquired HTML is byte-identical to existing **DEV-DS03**. All five sections, author identification and notes were read this time; no new journal edition was discovered. Chen's discussion of resource constraints and limited anticipated benefits as possible explanations for the absence of a compliance case remains expressly conjectural. His recommendations concern which claims and timing China should choose. They do not establish a ministerial decision. The existing register's date, statutory and specificity cautions remain in force. In particular, Chen's note 30 cites Commerce's July **31**, 2012 determination, while the body says July **13**.

The three accounts should not be pooled as independent corroboration of one decision. Li supplies an official participant's public recollection. Xiao's interview supplies counsel's professional perspective, without confirming that recollection. Chen supplies contemporary scholarly evaluation, partly based on the same publicly available legal decisions already used in the chapter.
