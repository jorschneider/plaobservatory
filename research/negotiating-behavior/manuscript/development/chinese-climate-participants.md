# Chinese participants on Copenhagen and negotiating method, 2009–2015

Research date: September 15, 2026. This bounded follow-up adds five complete published reading objects, not five complete interviews. It supplies new Chinese accounts of bargaining and preparation, but does not recover a Chinese transcript of the disputed Wen–Xie exchange. No manuscript or shared apparatus was edited. Capture paths, hashes, dates and exact scopes are in the [source register](chinese-climate-participants-register.json).

## What this changes

The strongest addition is a Chinese account of work between meetings: research on counterpart positions, daily assessment of negotiations, preparation of counterproposals and transmission of expert work to political officials. Some of this is described in a speech dated February 2012. It therefore cannot simply be attributed to the establishment of the 2013 bilateral working group or to Xi's accession. A later, named participant recollection makes the expert work behind the 2014 announcement more concrete.

The new material also resists a smooth conversion story. Chinese participants defended the Copenhagen compromise while disputing the American interpretation of it. Greater outreach did not remove conflict over obligations. And a contemporary 2009 account already claims intensive media and NGO engagement, complicating any assertion that such engagement began only after Copenhagen.

## Five additions and their proper uses

### CCH05: Xie on Copenhagen, interviewed December 26, 2009

Jiang Guocheng's Xinhua report, preserved by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/news/2009/12-27/2040296.shtml), attributes to Xie a description of BASIC coordination, preparatory drafting, meetings with other delegations and NGO officials, and daily media briefings. It is useful contrary evidence to a categorical absence of outward engagement in 2009. It does not measure accessibility or effectiveness, and it says nothing about the precise Wen–Xie exchange. The full short report was read; the interview recording, questions and complete answers were not recovered. Sohu's reproduction is the same source chain.

This is a public defense issued after the conference, not independent verification that the delegation influenced every draft in the way Xie claimed. The proposed manuscript use is narrow: outreach existed before the later participants' claimed improvement in method. The rest belongs in the research apparatus rather than as a second celebratory account of Copenhagen.

### CCH01: Su Wei at Tianjin, October 9, 2010

The complete [Sohu Green press-conference transcript](https://green.sohu.com/20101012/n275560789.shtml), published October 12, separates accepting the Copenhagen Accord from accepting Washington's reading of it. Su explicitly accepts international consultation and analysis while distinguishing that process from the verification attached to supported mitigation actions. He also disputes the charge that Chinese negotiators ignored the leaders' agreement.

A *Southern Weekly* question reports greater engagement with journalists and NGOs during that week's meeting. Su explains its usefulness for communicating Chinese positions and advancing negotiations. That contemporary question supports a perceived change in accessibility at Tianjin; it is not a comparative survey of 2009 and 2010. The transcript also contains pointed criticism of the United States. Technical contact and public confrontation coexisted.

Use the speaker's distinctions, not Sohu's adversarial headline or Su's interpretation of Stern as a verified description of American intent. No audio was checked. The published Chinese transcript has obvious typographic imperfections; exact English wording attributed to Stern should be checked against his own speech before quotation.

### CCH02: Xie's work-conference speech, February 9, 2012

The [National Center for Climate Change Strategy and International Cooperation publication](http://www.ncsc.org.cn/SY/bwldjh/202003/t20200316_769124.shtml) is dated March 16, 2020 but identifies the speech as delivered in Xiamen on February 9, 2012. It is a later official publication of a dated speech, not a newly recovered contemporaneous meeting record.

Sections I(2)–I(3) describe research on other countries' policies for central decisions, a centrally approved Durban work plan, daily examination of negotiating developments and preparation of responses. Section III(3) calls for relatively stable interagency teams and detailed positions for individual meetings. The speech also acknowledges growing external expectations and divisions among developing countries. These are concrete organizational claims alongside an official assessment of success.

This supports a history of preparation predating 2013. It does not reveal the approved plan, specify negotiators' concession authority, or establish that every prescribed procedure operated effectively. The complete speech was read; its statistics and broad success claims were not independently validated. Its description of a first “China Corner” should not be generalized into the first Chinese public outreach at a COP.

### CCH03: Xie before Paris, published November 3, 2015

The full [China.org.cn interview transcript](https://www.china.com.cn/fangtan/2015-11/03/content_36962784.htm?show=p) makes a useful distinction: Xie presents acceptance of common but differentiated responsibilities in principle as progress, while saying its application to mitigation, finance, technology and transparency still required negotiation. He anticipates a national-contribution approach differing from Kyoto's design and describes the desired outcome as mutually acceptable rather than one side's victory.

This is a prospective statement before Paris, not evidence that later provisions were already agreed. It also locates China inside BASIC and describes efforts to extend that coordination into practical cooperation. Neither US–China agreement nor envoy rapport had displaced other negotiating relationships. The host's introduction should not be used to date the origin of BASIC solely to the conference itself. The transcript was read completely; linked video was not viewed or downloaded.

### CCH04: named Chinese participants on the work behind 2014

Huo Siyi's [China Newsweek profile](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2024/01-07/10141840.shtml), posted January 7, 2024 for the January 8 issue, contains original named interviews. Zou Ji recalls repeated expert video meetings in 2014, overnight minutes sent to Xie the next morning, and reporting across simultaneous negotiating relationships. This is the most usable addition for a narrative of how proposals were prepared. It is retrospective testimony; no minutes were obtained.

The profile also reports a post-Copenhagen review and tacit rules against public blame and for identifying limits early. The review is narrated without a discrete source citation; Zou explicitly endorses the later working principles. Do not flatten these attribution levels. Nor does the source independently corroborate the dramatic Copenhagen scene: that paragraph expressly relies on Stern's recollection. Its imprecise reference to “2020” NDC targets should not replace the dated original announcements. The article was read completely in its Sina and China News Service renderings; they are one reporting chain.

## Integration judgment

The 2012 speech and Zou's 2014 recollection are worth considering for Chapter 3. They could replace an abstract sentence about “usable expertise” with an attributed description of research being turned into briefing material and proposals. The distinction to retain is between knowledge reaching a decision-maker and the specialist possessing authority to settle the question. The new records establish a described route between those stages; they do not disclose the final instructions.

The 2010 transcript is best used as a check against an overly harmonious transition. Accepting a term did not end the struggle over its meaning. A later recollection of a no-public-blame understanding cannot establish an uninterrupted practice without dates and a fuller sequence. Su's October 9 remarks show contemporary friction, but cannot by themselves establish a breach of an understanding whose precise formation date has not been recovered.

The 2009 report should remain visible as contrary evidence when discussing a post-Copenhagen improvement in outreach. An existing practice can be expanded, made more accessible or used differently; those are empirical claims requiring comparison. “They began speaking to outsiders” would be stronger than these sources warrant.

No new Chinese participant account recovered here confirms that Wen exceeded his mandate, identifies an authorized concession ladder, or transcribes Xie's objection in the leaders' meeting. That gap survives this search. The absence of a recovered account is not evidence that no objection or internal review occurred. The parent researcher's separate He Yafei and Wen public texts, and the existing He Jiankun/2014 Caixin materials, remain the appropriate other Chinese-side checks.

## Reading and acquisition boundary

The existing climate register, 2014 interview register and machinery register were inspected to avoid counting old documents again. Searches covered Xie, He Yafei and Su Wei accounts concerning Copenhagen, transparency, retrospective explanations and later negotiating method. Several results were only interview notices, index pages or repetitions of material already held. They were not counted as substantive acquisitions. Wen's March 2010 press conference and He's December 17 briefing were explicitly left to the parent's parallel work.

All five substantive objects were acquired as HTML and read through their published substantive endings. Raw and derived files are outside Git under `tmp/pdfs/research-2026-09-15/chinese-climate/`. Some browser fetches failed; direct public downloads succeeded. NCSC's HTTPS certificate did not match its hostname; the same public page was retrieved through HTTP without disabling certificate checking. No private archive, paywall, purchase or outreach was used. No new scholarly full reading is claimed.
