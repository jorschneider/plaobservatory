## 1. Cut the repeated lesson after the lesson has become clear

The recurring interruption is a paragraph telling “the negotiator” to identify the decision, authority, usable permission or remaining risk. Most of these paragraphs are sensible individually. Read continuously, they give markedly different episodes the same cadence. The narrative produces a particular discovery; the author then restates it in the book's general vocabulary. Another section or chapter often makes the same practical point again.

The remedy is to keep the precise choice beside the evidence and concentrate the broad practical synthesis in the conclusion. Preserve, for example, the costs of leaving versus extending Barshefsky's deadline, the inability of minority access to substitute automatically for control, the difference between an individual approval and an average processing commitment, and the identity of those paying for enforcement. Those are useful decisions with consequences. Repeated instructions to determine what a bargain is worth add less once the reader has watched the participants doing it.
