## Findings

**One small source-fidelity refinement is recommended in the domestic-policy paragraph.** The current sentence says that coal-derived gas “could improve local air quality while increasing greenhouse-gas emissions,” citing the plan and Yang–Jackson. The plan clearly establishes the clean-air purpose and its support for coal-derived gas. Yang–Jackson establishes the life-cycle greenhouse penalty under identified comparisons, and also warns about pollution associated with producing the gas. It does not independently demonstrate the local-air improvement asserted in this sentence. Williams p.17/n.81 expressly states the paired air/carbon proposition, but derives it from a separate November 2013 reporting source. Do not silently make Yang–Jackson the source of both halves or imply that local-air improvement was measured.

A compact replacement using the existing primary and technical sources would be: **“A policy meant to clean local air could therefore increase greenhouse-gas emissions, a risk identified in contemporary research.”** This preserves the argument and separates policy purpose from modeled consequences. The note already correctly distinguishes life-cycle estimates from measured national effects. Neither the chapter nor this review treats the planned projects as completed capacity.

**A minor precision improvement is available in the He paragraph.** “The distinction narrowed the disagreement” can read as an observed effect on the negotiations. The briefing establishes a public distinction and room for discussion, not how another delegation responded to this particular statement. **“The distinction left room to discuss scrutiny without negotiating the national target”** would state exactly what the source supplies. The current wording is not a major causal claim, but the alternative is clearer and requires no additional caveat.

The remaining new claims are supported within their stated bounds:

- **He:** the December 17/18 chronology is correct. National-target non-negotiability, refusal of international MRV for unsupported domestic action, and willingness to improve reporting, explain or clarify, and consider international dialogue all appear together in the briefing. The chapter does not convert this public position into authorization of final ICA language. Its note also correctly resists using the differentiated-recording statement to settle Froman's uncertainty about listing commitments.
- **Xie:** the pre-Xi evidence is substantive rather than an inference from an organizational diagram. The speech describes research used for central decisions, a centrally approved Durban plan, daily analysis and preparation of responses; its later section prescribes stable interagency participation and detailed positions. The chapter distinguishes description from prescription and dates the 2020 acquired publication separately from the 2012 speech. “Daily tactical adjustment” is reasonable as a short synthesis, although “daily tactical preparation” would stay closer to the evidence of making response plans without suggesting that a changed concession was documented each day. No new discretion under Xi is inferred.
- **Zou:** every concrete detail in the new paragraph has an identifiable source in Zou's attributed recollection. The chapter calls his work expert discussions; the note explicitly specifies the second track. Reporting minutes to Xie is kept distinct from authorizing concessions. The narrative does not claim contemporaneous access to those minutes, turn the profile's public narrative into a private scene, or count its repeated Stern Copenhagen recollection as independent Chinese corroboration. The 2024 retrospective date is visible in the note.
- **Domestic allocation:** the Williams sentence accurately reports interviews and does not make the provincial or enterprise evidence the approval chain for the November announcement. The surrounding practical judgment distinguishes overlapping domestic interests from additional international obligations. The manuscript appropriately uses the 2013 plan, avoiding the dating problem in the edited/abridged 2014 strategy.
- **Högl:** the AOSIS adviser's November 17, 2016 interview supports the attributed account of early transparency and predictability making less-open final negotiations tolerable. Both the earlier communication quotation on p.370 and the later trust quotation on p.371 matter. The chapter uses this as one participant's explanation of confidence in the presidency, not as evidence of China's intentions or a measured cause of its concessions. The French presidency, Brazilian and Singaporean facilitators, and other delegations retain agency.

The revised arc also preserves the important substantive distinctions: different objects of remembered assent at Copenhagen; consultation versus supported-action MRV; the 2009 channel versus more specified 2013 duties; nationally chosen ambition versus mutual acceptance of a joint announcement; bilateral language versus wider assent; and Paris text adoption versus consent to be bound and performance. None of the new passages claims that a technical relationship could supply a leader's authority or the rest of the conference's agreement.

## Narrative economy

The strongest addition is Zou's description of concrete work reaching the official coordinator. It earns the discussion of expertise more effectively than another general statement about relationships. One optional cut is its closing sentence, “The expert relationship had a practical product: a briefing for the official coordinating China's talks.” The borrowed rooms, minutes and calls have already shown that product. If retained for emphasis, it causes no factual problem.

The Xie paragraph can also lose one layer of explanation: combine its final two sentences into **“Xie thus described central direction and daily tactical preparation before Xi took power.”** The note already preserves the absence of concession instructions. This is an optional cut, not a request to remove the chapter's essential distinction between public accounts and mandates. Elsewhere that distinction explains a live decision; it is not gratuitous methodology.

No larger rewrite is needed for these additions. The chapter now gives the reader identifiable work and choices, including a reason domestic purposes could overlap without determining the whole bargain. The new sources improve that account without supporting a national before-and-after claim about flexibility.
