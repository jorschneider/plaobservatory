## Assessment of the argument

The rewrite now explains a consequential choice instead of opening with a typology of implementation. Gackle and Cooper agree that China fell short but want different American responses because they assess the remaining opportunity differently. Their disagreement gives the later treatment of remedies a purpose. It establishes stated preferences and the costs witnesses ask the government to consider; the chapter correctly stops short of claiming those preferences caused American policy or that Chinese officials deliberately engineered the division.

The finance and seed sections now make the distinctions worth learning. Leung has an ambition beyond entry; a firm must hire, build systems and complete transactions; Hassinger seeks access to an American official and supplies the information used in preparation; a pending Chinese import approval affects a crop launch outside China. These are changes in explanatory substance, not just added names. The text preserves the important favorable result: an eight-month acceleration and a business that operated. It also preserves the unfavorable one: aggregate purchase performance was far short under both examined benchmarks.

The practical advice follows the sources. Identify the remaining permissions before pricing a concession. Negotiate information sufficient to assess a processing-time promise. Ask whether beneficiaries will support a costly remedy. Each has a real tradeoff, and none is represented as a newly estimated universal rule. Solomon's attention to implementation remains useful; the chapter does not claim that its ordinary qualifications or these different outcomes refute a stronger national prediction the cases have not tested.

## Remaining literary decision

The last Board section is the place most likely to pull the reader back into an announcement audit. Its important contribution is that the proposed institution itself still required a bargain over products and operation. The repeated warning about what a briefing did not establish is less useful to the narrative than the concrete decision facing a prospective user.

If the forthcoming rare-earth material adds substantial length, preserve the user-centered finance, seed and enforcement passages. Consider shortening the Board's reporter-question detail into its note and keeping the body on the unfinished product choice and the commercial autonomy emphasized by China. Do not remove the difference between national accounts or imply that the proposed mechanism had started operating. The current section is accurate enough to retain; this is a question of emphasis and narrative momentum, not a required factual repair.

The ending does useful work: it returns from mechanisms to the permission, launch and order a user needs, and then connects the prospect of future business to the willingness to enforce an old commitment. It does not need another general summary about agreements and implementation.

### Substantive finding

This section improves the book's explanation of power. It identifies something Chinese officials could condition that American counterparts valued, a corresponding American restriction that China wanted removed, and a subsequent exchange involving identifiable administrative acts. It does not rely on the general fact that China's economy grew. Nor does it infer success from a summit's tone: the company notices identify rescission and restoration under way or completed, and the shipment report shows physical flows resuming within its stated product denominator.

The chapter preserves the consequential limits. April's instrument was a control on specified items with global application, not a ban on all rare earths or a rule only for the United States. The IEA estimate concerns four magnet rare earths and separation/refining, not all elements or mine production. Geneva did not publish a product-specific licensing schedule. The account of a private Geneva understanding is attributed to one briefed source; the military-use and apparent AI-chip linkage accounts retain their reported status. A rejected or unresolved proposed linkage does not become a Chinese concession obtained. Neither supplier statements nor shipment totals establish access for every end user.

Most importantly, the material position and exchange are not used to read every administrator's motive. The staffing, information and end-use review problems remain possible contributors to delay. China retained the control instrument while transactions could resume under it; that is different from proving that each delay was designed to secure a particular American concession. Public approval before London and the parallel European channel also prevent the talks from becoming an artificial date on which all world access switched on.

The treatment of the older WTO dispute is sound at its stated scope. It shows a previous formal export-restriction episode and attributed implementation positions. It does not make that judgment a ruling on the alleged Japan-specific interruption in 2010, or on the legality of the 2025 system. The present section adds a contemporary mechanism, not a claim that China invented export leverage in 2025.

The final Board section remains the likeliest place to lose momentum. Its 459 words include two valuable concluding paragraphs about users and the negotiator's choices. Preserve those.
