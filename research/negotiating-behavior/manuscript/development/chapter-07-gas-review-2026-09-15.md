## Three recommended refinements

1. **Keep the overnight chronology literal.** The opening says the experts resumed “almost as soon as the next day began.” The May 21 Kremlin transcript (RUS-G03) describes work until 3:30 in the morning and renewed work in the morning. It does not place the restart near midnight. “The experts had worked until half past three in the morning and resumed in the morning” preserves Putin's account without inventing a more precise clock sequence.

2. **Distinguish leader endorsement from its causal effect.** “Leader sponsorship had already helped carry the parties to an important intermediate agreement” goes beyond the September 2013 report (RUS-G01). That report establishes a signing witnessed by Putin and Xi, a document described by Gazprom as binding, and a still-unresolved price. It does not establish how their intervention changed the negotiations. “The leaders had endorsed an important intermediate agreement. It had not resolved the term on which the project depended” preserves the substantive contrast.

3. **Make the corporate Q&A attribution self-sufficient in the narrative.** Prefer “a public corporate Q&A hosted on CNPC's website” to “an investor-relations response,” and identify its relevant answer as January 29, 2014. The preserved page is headed 新闻留言 and uses 中国石油 and 公司; it identifies neither an individual respondent nor, on its own, a clearly specified legal entity. The note already warns against collapsing CNPC and PetroChina. Matching the note and DEV-GAS06 creator description to the narrower attribution would keep that distinction visible without requiring the reader to discover it later. The January 22 answer concerns broader company finances; the narrative correctly presents it as another answer rather than a Russian negotiating instruction.

## Substantive checks

The new domestic-cost discussion is warranted. DEV-GAS04, printed page 29, reports RMB41,872 million in net imported-gas losses and RMB28,888 million in operating profit for the broader Natural Gas and Pipeline segment. The replacement's approximately RMB41.9 billion figure and its contrast with a profitable broader segment are accurate. Printed page 14 establishes CNPC's controlling shareholding. These figures do not identify the profitability of the later Russian contract, and the replacement does not claim that they do.

DEV-GAS05 explicitly identifies low domestic prices as an import disincentive and describes an adjustment for non-residential gas, unchanged household prices, and differentiated treatment of existing and incremental consumption. DEV-GAS06 explicitly names border delivery price, domestic selling price, pipeline costs and taxes as profitability factors. Together these support the practical instruction to investigate domestic cost recovery. They do not reveal the private reservation price or prove which domestic decision enabled the final deal. The text maintains this boundary without burying its useful conclusion.

DEV-GAS01 describes its unnamed source as a person close to Chinese negotiators, not an identified participant. The relevant passages distinguish a defended limit from a preferred outcome and acknowledge pressure on both parties. The replacement preserves attribution and does not use this reporting as independent proof of the private mandate. The same-source and anonymous-access qualifications in the register are appropriate.

The retained September 2026 Novak passages (RUS-G32–33) support the distinction between a construction memorandum and continuing work on a commercial contract. They do not establish all confidential terms, and the replacement does not supply them. The passage on reported 2025 deliveries remains explicitly corporate reporting; it should retain that attribution in integration.

## Reading scope and narrative judgment

This pass read the complete replacement, notes supplied with it, memo and six-source register. It independently read all four DEV-GAS06 exchanges; all six DEV-GAS05 exchanges and introduction; DEV-GAS04 printed pages 14 and 29, with visual inspection of page 29; and the decisive DEV-GAS01 sections “普京很着急” and “中国坚守底线,” plus selected later passages. It did not independently reread the complete DEV-GAS01 article, DEV-GAS02–03 or the SEC filing. It read the complete substantive RUS-G01 report, complete RUS-G03 transcript, and complete RUS-G32–33 dispatches. All six preserved new-source file hashes and byte counts match their register records. No new sources were acquired.

The replacement has a coherent narrative argument. It moves from the unanswered price question to a buyer with several cost variables, then tests political friendship against completed delivery and an unfinished second transaction. The domestic material explains a concrete negotiating choice rather than decorating a chronology. Its distinction between a limit and a preferred result is especially useful. The practical concluding alternatives—price, route, volume risk, financing and domestic cost recovery—follow from the discussion as considerations for a negotiator, not proven private causes of every delay. Further methodological caveats in the main text would dilute that gain; the three targeted refinements above are sufficient for this bounded review.
