## Argument and evidence

The chapter makes a useful historical argument through a successful normalization sequence and an unfinished financial bargain. It demonstrates that partial settlement, technical detail and disputed obligations were present in Solomon's original period. Those features therefore cannot serve, without further discrimination, as discoveries of a later transformation. The chapter's treatment of American authorization and constraints makes this comparison substantive rather than a list of national traits.

The principal distinctions survive review:

- The records support Zhou's specific monetary offer and the later American report of its withdrawal. They also record an earlier agreement in principle. The chapter does not convert either into an acquired completed instrument or deny the existence of the agreement in principle.
- Solomon's December 1973 memorandum supplies contemporary provenance for his political explanation, with redacted reporting and unavailable attachments. Its repetition in his later book is not independent confirmation of the Chinese decision process.
- Deng's reference to the December 11 exchange supports knowledge before his meeting with Woodcock. Receipt before scheduling and causal persuasion remain separate, unproved claims.
- The final communiqué is distinguished from unilateral positions and closing exchanges. Chinese opposition to arms sales is not treated as retraction of consent to those sales.
- Freeman's late-1990s institutional explanations are distinguished from Solomon's principal empirical period and from mechanisms still requiring later tests.

## Corrections verified

The chapter now accurately acknowledges the strength of Solomon's senior-intervention prediction on p. 149. Narrowing that prediction belongs to this reassessment. It also states that the selected success and failure cases cannot establish comparative predictive reliability. The failed case exposes a limitation without pretending to supply a representative test.

Note 1 now identifies cable 237 as an unacquired transcript referenced in the FRUS source note, rather than a published full transcript. The November 1973 passage now describes the absence of a concluded settlement, avoiding the ambiguity of “executed” between conclusion and performance. The claims discussion in the November 19 report is correctly located on p. 439.

## Readability and remaining work

The revised opening is shorter, and the forecasting caveat now belongs with the discussion of prediction. The dated normalization sequence and the explanation of the $17 million payment route carry the chapter's value and should remain. Future editing should continue to reduce repeated methodological explanation once a documentary distinction has become clear.

The chapter is ready to be developed alongside the later manuscript. Missing Chinese instructions, the June 14, 1974 message, successive claims drafts and the full closing normalization transcript continue to limit causal conclusions; their absence need not prevent drafting within those limits. The chapter now supplies the successful and contrary original-period checks requested in the earlier audit. It is a selected historical baseline, not a comprehensive period history or an explanation of subsequent change. The later comparisons, current proposition assessment and complete conclusion remain manuscript-level obligations described in the [editorial audit](editorial-audit-2026-09-15.md).
