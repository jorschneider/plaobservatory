## Outcome and revisions

The chapter works as a first substantive draft. Its comparative contribution is clear: the 2001 arrangement separated the disposition of detained people and an aircraft from responsibility and reconnaissance, while the 2023 sequence made access to particular institutions of communication a disputed object. It does not turn these unlike crises into a controlled estimate of historical change or national character.

## Consequential findings

**The EP-3 settlement is neither a Chinese acceptance of the American account nor an American promise to stop reconnaissance.** The April 11 letter distinguishes sorrow, absent verbal clearance, agreement to meet, expected crew departure, aircraft-return planning, and China's intention to raise reconnaissance. Tang's announcement authorizes departure while preserving demands. The chapter correctly treats the Chinese publication as selected excerpts and official interpretation, not a full parallel translation whose omissions alone demonstrate mistranslation. The briefing question about the adequacy of the apology supplies public contestation; it does not establish a uniform national audience response.

**The legal packet establishes a bounded American preparation process.** The April 6 cover memorandum and clearance page corroborate consultation and clearance of aircraft-immunity guidance. They do not prove presidential approval of the later letter, an already authorized crew-first strategy, or universal American interagency coherence. The chapter maintains those limits. Its account of a partly conditional attribution of responsibility matches the talking points.

**Deferred performance is not silently recoded as repudiation or delay as deliberate attrition.** Aircraft inspection, a transport agreement in principle, engineering arrangements, physical removal and payment discussion remain separate. The June 7 briefing provides concrete logistical obstacles and an explicit statement that the communications arrangements were not a hindrance. The chapter incorporates this contrary evidence instead of inferring strategy from elapsed time. The July 3 announcement is attributed and does not become a reconstructed log of every transport flight.

**The 2023 chronology preserves failed and partial efforts.** The August 2022 baseline, February American postponement, acknowledged Chinese military-call refusal, February Munich encounter, May Vienna channel, unsuccessful ministerial meeting request, and June failure to obtain military-channel agreement prevent a simple silence-to-summit narrative. The May 31 Chinese answer expressly says that military contact had not entirely ceased. A June meeting with Xi is properly retained as an observation relevant to Solomon's strong senior-intervention forecast, without claiming to measure that signal's comparative reliability from one case.

**The intermediary section retains evidence on both sides of the inference.** Formal diplomacy preceded Kissinger's July visit. His private status does not prove absence of consultation: Blinken later acknowledged seeking his advice. Conversely, welcome, advocacy, prospective debriefing and that acknowledgment do not reconstruct a transmitted July message or demonstrate a changed official instruction. The chapter neither proves the old-friend mechanism nor refutes influence from missing public evidence.

**The November tradeoff is specific.** The Commerce rule establishes removal of the forensic institute and its November 16 effective date; the November 17 publication date is different. Miller's named statements, reported by VOA, connect removal to counternarcotics cooperation. They do not establish a bargain purchasing military communication, the private sequence of concessions, or successful future enforcement. The chapter preserves the human-rights objection rather than treating delisting as an uncontested administrative adjustment.

## Remaining limits

The causal alternatives remain real: language could have enabled a release decision or supplied a defensible formulation after leaders had decided to release; public refusal could reflect bargaining pressure, internal control or both. The chapter identifies these alternatives without presenting its preferred chronology as privately documented. Its 2023 endpoint is November 30, expressly before proof that every announced military mechanism had resumed. Later operation, durability and renewed interruption would require additional evidence and belong to a subsequent extension, not an implied finding of this review.
