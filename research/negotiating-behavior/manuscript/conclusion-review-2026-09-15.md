## Outcome

The conclusion answers the substantive question rather than merely listing missing evidence. It retains political purpose, authority, obligations and implementation as useful questions from Solomon's account; cautions against treating the senior-intervention forecast as validated or continued bargaining as proof of asymmetry; and identifies documented institutional developments without asserting a single evolution of national character. Its positive findings survive its qualifications. The financial opening, selected legal arguments and procedural agreements, climate architecture, crew arrangement and actual gas deliveries are not reduced to uncertainty merely because their full causal histories remain unavailable.

The strongest synthesis is **variation within a politically governed relationship**. Phase One can accelerate one published regulatory timetable while failing to achieve aggregate purchase commitments and leaving an application-processing promise contested. A close Russian relationship can contain a working pipeline and an unfinished commercial negotiation. These examples support following the particular obligation and implementing decision. They do not establish a general rate of compliance or prove that institutions replaced politics.

The introduction's accession opening remains a strong entry into that argument. It supplies actual contemporaneous offers, deadlines and conditionality rather than a retrospective success story. Its updated crisis and intermediary paragraphs now report findings from the completed draft instead of promising work that has already been done. The explicit November 2023 crisis boundary prevents the manuscript's 2026 endpoint from silently becoming the endpoint of every case.

## Requested revisions and verification

Three substantive refinements to the conclusion were requested and their application checked:

1. **Bound the historical expansion claim.** The original wording about diversified forms and an increasingly varied repertoire could imply an unmeasured, continuous national trajectory. The opening now names particular expanded opportunities established by the examined developments: WTO adjudication after accession, climate reporting arrangements and explicit bilateral implementation remedies. The ending now refers to the institutions and relationships examined. This preserves the positive historical claim while specifying its evidence.
2. **Distinguish revised interpretation from changed behavior.** The revisions section now states that narrowing an older proposition is not itself proof of historical change. Its old-friends discussion is explicitly a qualification about what modern evidence establishes. This agrees with the unresolved causal components in S-C04, S-C05 and S-P08; missing private transmissions do not establish that cultivation ceased to work.
3. **Avoid making sequence sufficient for influence.** The practical guidance now calls a linked transmission and decision stronger evidence, while requiring evidence that the transmission changed the choice. A proposal followed by a congenial outcome does not automatically establish causal influence.

The author also replaced the introduction's future crisis/intermediary tasks with bounded results and added note 11. That change was inspected. One final, small clarity request concerned the introduction's compound climate sentence: distinguish the 2014 endorsement of different national intentions from the September 2015 review/transparency principles. This was applied and checked. The conclusion's broader account of the sequence did not have that problem. Its remaining generic “expansion” sentence was also revised to refer to the distinct points at which commitments are specified and contested across these episodes. All requested revisions are resolved in the final versions identified above.

## Fidelity and evidentiary checks

**Solomon is not weakened into an easier opponent.** The conclusion retains the original strong senior-intervention prediction, the nearby failed 1973 case, the relevance of concrete drafting and the inability of selected observations to rank predictive reliability. Its treatment of S-P25 distinguishes continued implementation bargaining from the stronger claim of asymmetric adherence and accommodation. It also preserves his cooperation-based scope and Freeman's preexisting account of specialization and institutional participation. The institutional argument is not presented as a discovery that the predecessors never considered.

**The representative opening is supported.** Crew release did not resolve the underlying responsibility and reconnaissance disputes. Securities ownership and revenue are different observations, neither an estimate of the ownership change's commercial effect. The working eastern gas line does not establish the commercial closure of another route. The examples show different stages and obligations without claiming comparable outcomes or a controlled sample.

**The modern instruments remain distinct.** A WTO ruling and subsequent procedural bargain are not identical to delivered commercial relief. Paris adoption is separate from ratification and from achieving a contribution's announced result. Phase One's consultation and conditional remedy provisions do not create an independent judge. The 2026 trade mechanism remains a design and consultation process at the chapter's substantive endpoint. The conclusion does not transform any of these into completed performance.

**The other counterparts do analytical work.** Russian delegation documents show what an authority record can establish, without being passed off as a Chinese mandate. Indian conditions on the wider relationship and American accession deadlines make the interaction visible. Neither the conclusion nor the revised introduction claims that these cases isolate nationality, culture, leadership or relative power. Commercial alternatives and institutional competence remain mechanisms to specify rather than substitutes for a documented choice.

**The assessment was substantively corrected after this review's initial count check.** The initial data contained 17 retained within tested scope, 7 revised or narrowed, 30 unresolved and 5 untested. Those counts were correctly reproduced, but this review did not then identify the classification problem subsequently found by the independent original-text audit. The corrected data contain **16 retained within tested scope, 38 unresolved and 5 untested**, with no revise-or-narrow verdict. The initial seven revision labels and S-C01's retained label are superseded, not presented as current judgments. The conclusion now omits numerical scorekeeping and explains the substantive distinction instead. These dependent propositions and repeatedly used cases are not a set of independent statistical observations.

## Follow-up: correction of assessment interpretation

The independent appendix audit found that the seven provisional revision judgments rested on qualifications already present in Solomon or Freeman, or on missing evidence for a comparative, frequency or causal claim. The later corpus does not establish those claims as false. S-C01's evidence demonstrated channel-specific variation without identifying relationship purpose as its cause. Moving these eight entries to unresolved corrects an inference from the evidence; it does not add new case evidence or erase the observed practices.

The conclusion's new closing paragraphs accurately distinguish an unvalidated forecast from a falsified one. Freeman already allowed efforts to cultivate relationships to fail or have uncertain results. Unfinished gas negotiations therefore cannot refute an automatic trust-producing mechanism he did not assert. Solomon's qualified discussion of political principles already allowed exceptions and detailed instruments. Finding detailed later provisions is not a matched test of his stated preference or evidence that it disappeared.
