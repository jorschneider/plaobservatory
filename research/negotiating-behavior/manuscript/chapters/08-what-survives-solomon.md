# Conclusion: What survives Solomon

In the summer of 2025, a useful agreement with China could be measured in materials leaving a port and software becoming available again. After the Geneva accommodation faltered, officials met in London to work out its implementation. Beijing subsequently described an exchange: China would approve eligible export applications, while Washington would remove particular restrictions. Chip-design software companies reported relief from American restrictions. Chinese rare-earth magnet shipments recovered. The controls under which China approved exports remained in place.[^1]

The bargain changed the exercise of authority without abolishing it. That made relief possible while leaving another application, another customer or another intended use to be considered later. For manufacturers needing supplies, an immediate permission could be valuable. For the government negotiating on their behalf, it could also preserve a vulnerability. The decision was whether the relief obtained, on its particular terms, was worth accepting with that exposure still present.

This is a central problem in negotiating with China today. A politically defended limit can coexist with room for a substantial accommodation. The challenge is to identify the decision that would make the accommodation useful, what Beijing values in exchange, and who will carry the remaining risk. Solomon’s attention to purpose, relationships and authority remains essential to that work. WTO membership, new agreements governing scrutiny and a powerful position in supplying foreign industries have given those questions different practical consequences. A ruling, an operating license, an export clearance or permission to reopen a military channel can each be worth obtaining. None is adequately valued by the warmth of the encounter that produced it.

## The price of a limited bargain

The limits defended in these negotiations did not all have the same standing. China retained the 49 percent mobile-telecommunications ceiling in its accession bargain; years later it removed the foreign-ownership ceiling for securities companies. In the earlier rare-earth dispute, China reported removing the condemned measures. In the 2025 exchange, licensing authority survived the relief. These are different sectors, instruments and circumstances, but they establish a necessary caution: a rule defended today is not thereby proved permanent.[^2][^7][^1]

A government may protect an objective while changing the means by which it pursues it. Foreign ownership can be permitted while the business remains regulated. Shipments can be approved while export controls remain. The negotiator must therefore keep open two possibilities: compensation within the stated limit, and a proposal that requires changing the limit itself. Treating every refusal as a fixed political boundary would concede too much before the bargaining began.

Lamy's response to the ownership ceiling illustrates one available move. He sought authoritative confirmation while negotiating compensating gains in a package he could defend to his principals. That did not make compensation interchangeable. Earlier minority access might serve one company while leaving another without the control its business required. A government negotiating on their behalf needed to decide which of those interests it was securing, and what it was asking the disappointed beneficiary to accept.[^2]

The same judgment applies to relief under an export-control system. A license can restore a factory's supplies promptly, while repeal may offer broader protection and take longer to obtain. The government must compare that difference with the concession sought in return. If it would make a lasting concession for relief too brief or too narrow to secure the intended benefit, declining the package can be the better choice. The public London record does not reveal enough of the terms and alternatives to calculate that judgment for Washington. It makes the exposure visible.

A partial agreement should be judged by what the unfinished business can still prevent. Once the EP-3 crew had left, continued disagreement over responsibility could not undo their departure. Washington had obtained the immediate benefit without winning the larger argument. The military arrangements agreed in 2023 produced contact, but China's refusal of a proposed ministerial meeting in November 2024 showed that the next encounter still required consent.[^3] One completed magnet shipment did not guarantee continuing supply. Russian border residents likewise received access subject to expiry and individual refusal.[^limited-exposure]

These bargains call for different reciprocal offers. A completed act may justify payment of the agreed price at once. Continuing access gives the recipient reason to seek a longer permission or a staged exchange. Those protections have costs and may be refused. The negotiator must then decide whether immediate relief is valuable enough to accept without them—and whether today's concession leaves anything useful to offer when the next permission is needed. Counting the subjects left unsettled will not answer that question.

## Authority that can be used

Both sides' authority mattered in accession. Chinese specialists could work out a concession that still required permission across sectors; American negotiators could prepare a package that the White House might decline. Barshefsky's inclusion of Gene Sperling addressed the second problem. The American departure letters made more time conditional on an opportunity to address the first. Neither step removed the need to decide what the package was worth.[^4]

Authority was also being negotiated during the EP-3 crisis. On April 7, Zhou described the Chinese draft as cleared at a high level. Prueher nevertheless objected and returned with a new American text. The assurance identified an approved proposal; it did not establish the furthest China would move. Washington's April 25 aircraft instructions made a different distinction explicit: Prueher could discuss the proposed document but needed approval of its complete text before committing to sign. Permission to explore, approval of an offer and authority to conclude were separate acts.[^authority-cables]

A negotiator has reason to ask which of those acts has occurred. The answer can indicate where a counteroffer should go and what it would have to change. It cannot make a claim of finality self-verifying. When Chinese officials said there was no time to alter the crew-transfer memorandum, the Americans withheld agreement and obtained changes. Their objection concerned the wording they were being asked to accept; it did not require first discovering why that wording had been presented as final.

Nor did caution require every uncertainty to be removed. On April 11, the Americans refused to supply an advance signed copy of Prueher's letter while seeking the assurance that would accompany its delivery. They obtained a description of departure procedures and a window for the charter's arrival. They did not obtain Tang's exact words in advance. At the meeting, Prueher stated his understanding and handed over the letter before Tang read the prepared statement.[^assurance-risk]

The crew returned the next day. That result does not make every oral assurance sufficient. It shows what the Americans chose to secure before proceeding: an acceptable statement of their own position, a commitment to departure and enough practical detail to judge the promised exchange. They accepted a remaining risk about its execution. A demand for greater certainty would also have had a price if it postponed or prevented release. Preparing a negotiation includes deciding which uncertainty to reduce and which one's government is prepared to bear.

Solomon’s stronger prediction—that senior intervention was the most reliable sign of imminent agreement, in an account that also emphasized concrete drafting—remains unvalidated at that comparative strength. The episodes here do not rank every available signal or refute his forecast. They do show why a negotiator should identify the intervention’s function. Lamy’s March meeting did not close the bargain; his May meeting supplied confirmation and approval in principle around which negotiators could finish. In October 2024, Modi and Xi endorsed a border arrangement already announced. A senior encounter can authorize, confirm or celebrate different stages of a negotiation.[^5]

Kissinger's July 2023 visit demonstrated exceptional access and Chinese efforts to enlist his help. American officials valued his counsel, but the available record does not identify a July message that produced the November agreements.[^6] For an official considering such a channel, the next question is what proposal the intermediary should carry and whose decision it is meant to influence. A relationship becomes useful to a negotiation through that work.

## Performance and the cost of complaint

The work continues after a commitment is made, but the next act is not always another concession. JPMorgan’s securities business obtained operating permission before Phase One and later became wholly foreign owned. The regulator expressly linked an eight-month acceleration of the ownership opening to the agreement. Company disclosures then recorded ownership and substantial fee income. Further regulatory requirements did not erase those delivered benefits.[^7]

Agricultural biotechnology supplied a less satisfactory experience. Individual approvals could enable an identifiable product launch while leaving the promised review process in dispute. A certificate’s duration and the time needed to obtain it mattered to a business planning a crop launch. Aggregate purchase commitments posed another problem: the examined measures showed a large shortfall, even though their different coverage prevented combining them into one estimate. A useful financial opening did not excuse that shortfall; the shortfall did not make the financial opening imaginary.

Those distinctions matter before agreement. The official seeking access should know whether the firm needs removal of an ownership ceiling, an operating license, an approval for a particular product, or an order on workable commercial terms. Different promises require different evidence of performance. The firm may also value timing differently from the government announcing the bargain. A permission that arrives after a commercial opportunity has passed can satisfy a narrow formal requirement while providing much less of the expected benefit.

Solomon's warning that bargaining continues after signature survives these cases. His stronger claim—that China demands adherence from others while seeking accommodation for itself—requires further evidence. Some agreements expressly left permissions and consultations for later. Further work under those provisions was part of the bargain. To establish an asymmetric demand, the critic must identify the duty and the different treatment being sought. That distinction gives a government firmer ground for complaint when a promised benefit is withheld.[^13]

Learning what happened is itself a negotiating problem. In 2019, Lighthizer told Congress that companies feared identifying themselves when they complained about Chinese conduct. Phase One allowed an appeal to omit a company’s identifying information and required confidentiality for the material. This recognized a conflict inside the enforcement relationship: the firm best placed to document a failure might still depend on the authority it would be reporting.[^8]

Confidentiality could help the government obtain evidence. It could not ensure that the circumstances would not identify the complainant, or that the firm would risk the disclosure. The foreign negotiator therefore needed more than a well-written duty and an available remedy. The intended beneficiaries needed a feasible way to tell their government that the duty was not being performed. Where that route is weak, a quiet public record is a poor measure of whether the concession works.

Even a well-documented failure leaves a further political decision. The soybean and ethanol witnesses at the December 2025 hearing agreed that China had fallen short but differed over the response: prospective sales gave one constituency something to protect while lost access gave another reason to demand action.[^9]

A government promising to enforce an agreement must anticipate that division. The beneficiaries of retaliation may differ from the companies or farmers bearing its immediate costs. Prospective new purchases can make action on an old shortfall harder to sustain. This does not make a threat worthless. It means its credibility depends partly on whether the government will still consider it worth carrying out after the next offer arrives. The price of enforcement belongs in the original bargain.

## What the update requires

The 1973–78 records already contain legal objections, conditional offers, instructions from capitals and close attention to wording. Zhou's willingness to supply money did not settle the status of old debts or the conditions for identifying assets. Habib and Lin later disagreed about whether they were completing a bargain or still negotiating a condition for making it. Deng and Woodcock, by contrast, established what each government would continue to oppose and proceeded with normalization. Political disagreement could survive a useful agreement; disagreement about the content of the exchange could prevent one.[^historical-choice]

This is the continuity that the later cases make most useful. An insistence on principle does not specify every act an official will permit. Neither does it guarantee that a tolerable accommodation exists. The work is to discover the available exchange while preserving the ability to reject it. Solomon followed that work through personal relationships and political pressures. Freeman already recognized specialization, institutional learning and the possibility that relationships would fail to produce a settlement.[^14]

One concrete change was the development of the people and organizations needed to use an external legal procedure against powerful trading partners. Membership supplied the rights; officials, domestic and foreign lawyers, and academic specialists learned how to turn a commercial objection into a claim. Their work also connected litigation to the Chinese agencies whose measures had to be defended or altered. An American official could refuse China's bilateral request yet still have to answer its treaty argument.[^capacity][^10] With ordinary WTO appeals unavailable, appeal arbitration requires agreement between the parties: China and the EU have used that route, but China's participation alone supplies no such agreement with Washington.[^appeal-now]

Detailed demands on domestic administration had a longer history. In 1995, the intellectual-property bargain prescribed Chinese enforcement machinery, contacts and deadlines for complaints, reciprocal information and continuing consultations. Washington retained its domestic-law route back to trade action. Phase One's particular addition was a bilateral procedure linking a government complaint to specified levels of consultation and a contested remedy. The respondent could not counterrespond to a measure it judged taken in good faith; withdrawal was the specified remedy if it judged the measure taken in bad faith. No independent adjudicator would decide that question. A dispute over performance could put the wider agreement at risk. That was a consequential allocation of discretion, built on a long history of detailed demands backed by trade pressure.[^ip-comparison][^11]

Other bargains allocated discretion differently. Climate diplomacy specified international scrutiny while preserving national determination of targets. China could accept common procedures without accepting an internationally binding obligation to achieve each announced national result.[^12] In the 2025 exchange, permission for Chinese-controlled supplies to reach foreign industries became central to the bargain. Obtaining access to China and obtaining supplies from China could expose a counterpart to different decisions by the Chinese state.[^17]

Here the later record requires a direct departure from one of Solomon's premises. He described a China whose capacity to influence international events fell short of its ambitions, and treated negotiating stratagems in part as compensation for weakness. In the supply relationships examined here, China possessed an immediate power to affect foreign production. Washington sought decisions that Beijing could make consequential through an established administrative system. The visitor had to reckon with that capacity before considering how the Chinese official used a relationship or framed a principle.[^power-premise]

That power still depended on the issue and the alternatives. Russia’s need for gas customers did not settle the route, finance or purchasing commitments for another pipeline. The operation of one line and the unfinished commercial terms of another showed both the value and limits of the partnership. India made progress at the border a condition for the wider relationship, while local arrangements, de-escalation and a final boundary settlement remained distinct objectives. The bargaining position belonged to the particular dependency and alternative, as well as to the countries involved.[^15]

Under Xi, the 2018 reforms consolidated foreign-affairs coordination and supervision. The Foreign Relations Law of 2023 then stated distinct responsibilities for central direction, the State Council, military exchanges, diplomatic coordination and authorized provincial activity. A subsequent conference on local foreign affairs demanded discipline while also encouraging initiative. Officials were being told to exercise initiative within central objectives.[^authority-now]

The working conditions of contact could change without extinguishing initiative. Loh's fieldwork records foreign diplomats encountering Chinese officials accompanied by colleagues and a hoped-for private dinner taking on the appearance of an official meeting. A visitor could mistake a constrained setting for a personal rebuff. The more useful response would be to test whether an official format would let the counterpart discuss or refer the proposal.[^contact-now] Political support must then reach the body expected to issue the permission, arrange the call or carry out the project. In 2023, strategic contact with Wang Yi coexisted with refusal of the proposed defense-minister meeting; the later presidential agreement still required departmental arrangements.

These developments change the choices available across the table. Legal capability can carry a Chinese objection into a procedure the other government has accepted. Industrial dependence can turn an administrative permission into something foreign negotiators urgently seek. Tighter supervision can alter how an exploratory proposal reaches the official able to decide. The evidence for these changes is more specific than a claim that China has become harder or easier to bargain with under Xi. It tells the visitor where an additional demand may arise, what Beijing can offer, and how to seek an answer.

Solomon made Chinese negotiating behavior intelligible as purposeful political activity. The later record enlarges the range of purposes, participants and instruments that a counterpart must understand. A government seeking Chinese supplies may accept licenses that restore production while continuing to seek more durable access. A government confronting a broken promise may choose to enforce it despite the next offer of sales. Each needs an account of what the proposed bargain will accomplish, what it leaves dependent on another decision and what its own side is willing to risk.

Prueher could not obtain every assurance he sought before handing over his letter. He could protect its wording, test the departure arrangements and decide whether the remaining risk was worth taking to bring the crew home. The preparation made that decision possible. It could not make the decision for him.
