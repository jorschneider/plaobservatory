# Conclusion: What survives Solomon

In the summer of 2025, a useful agreement with China could be measured in materials leaving a port and software becoming available again. After the Geneva accommodation faltered, officials met in London to work out its implementation. Beijing subsequently described an exchange: China would approve eligible export applications, while Washington would remove particular restrictions. Chip-design software companies reported relief from American restrictions. Chinese rare-earth magnet shipments recovered. The controls under which China approved exports remained in place.[^1]

The bargain changed the exercise of authority without abolishing it. For manufacturers needing supplies, an immediate permission could be valuable. For the government negotiating on their behalf, it could also preserve a vulnerability: another application, customer or intended use would be considered later. The decision was whether the relief obtained, on its particular terms, was worth accepting with that exposure still present.

## What changed at the table

Here the later record requires a departure from one of Solomon's premises. He described a China whose capacity to influence international events fell short of its ambitions, and treated negotiating stratagems in part as compensation for weakness. In the supply relationships examined here, China possessed an immediate power to affect foreign production. Washington sought decisions Beijing could make consequential through an established administrative system. The visitor had to reckon with that capacity before considering how the Chinese official used a relationship or framed a principle.[^power-premise]

Sophisticated conditional bargaining itself was already present in the 1970s. Zhou Enlai's willingness to supply money did not settle the status of old debts or the conditions for identifying assets. Habib and Lin later disagreed about whether they were completing a bargain or still negotiating a condition for making it. Deng and Woodcock, by contrast, established what each government would continue to oppose and proceeded with normalization. Political disagreement could survive a useful agreement; disagreement about the content of the exchange could prevent one.[^historical-choice]

Chinese participants also explained bargains through domestic purposes. In 1999, Zhu Rongji defended the competition arising from WTO concessions as a benefit to China's development. In 2014, Xie Zhenhua described the emissions target as a means of pushing economic restructuring. The Chinese corporate account of gas negotiations placed the foreign purchase beside domestic selling prices, costs and taxes. These public explanations did not reveal private instructions. They did give a counterpart something concrete to investigate: whether an exchange served an undertaking Beijing already valued, and which domestic decision still stood in its way.[^domestic-purposes] Solomon's attention to interests remains indispensable; treating the Chinese official chiefly as the defender of a limit would miss reasons to make a bargain.

The means of pursuing those interests expanded. WTO membership supplied rights; officials, domestic and foreign lawyers, and academic specialists learned how to turn a commercial objection into a legal claim. Their work connected litigation to the Chinese agencies whose measures had to be defended or altered. An American official could refuse China's bilateral request yet still have to answer its treaty argument.[^capacity][^10] The route now depends on the counterpart. With ordinary WTO appeals unavailable, appeal arbitration requires agreement between the parties. China and the EU have used it; China's participation alone supplies no such agreement with Washington.[^appeal-now]

Detailed commitments on domestic administration also predated Xi. The 1995 intellectual-property bargain prescribed enforcement machinery, complaint contacts and deadlines, reciprocal information and continuing consultations. Washington retained its domestic-law route back to trade action. Phase One added a particular interstate complaint and remedy procedure. A respondent judging a measure taken in good faith could not counterrespond; if it judged the measure taken in bad faith, withdrawal was the specified remedy. No independent adjudicator would decide between those judgments.[^ip-comparison][^11] The change from the earlier bargain lay in this allocation of discretion, not the arrival of detailed demands backed by trade pressure.

Other negotiations gave different content to a defended principle. Climate diplomacy combined international scrutiny with national determination of targets, without an internationally binding obligation to achieve each announced national result.[^12] In the 2025 exchange, permission for Chinese-controlled supplies to reach foreign industries became central to the bargain.[^17] Obtaining access to China and obtaining supplies from China could make foreigners dependent on different decisions by the Chinese state.

Under Xi, the 2018 reforms consolidated foreign-affairs coordination and supervision. The Foreign Relations Law of 2023 stated responsibilities for central direction, the State Council, military exchanges, diplomatic coordination and authorized provincial activity. A subsequent conference on local foreign affairs demanded discipline while encouraging initiative within central objectives.[^authority-now] These changes did not establish that every official had lost discretion. Loh's fieldwork recorded foreign diplomats encountering accompanied Chinese officials and a hoped-for private dinner becoming an official meeting. A visitor could mistake the constraints of contact for a personal rebuff. An official format might instead enable discussion or referral of a proposal.[^contact-now]

The result is more specific than a claim that China has become harder or easier to bargain with. Legal capability can carry an objection into a procedure the other government has accepted. Industrial dependence can give Chinese permissions urgent value abroad. Closer supervision can change how an exploratory proposal reaches a decision maker. Power remains particular to the issue and alternatives: Russia's need for customers did not settle another pipeline's route, finance or purchasing terms; India could condition the wider relationship on progress at the border.[^15] None of these developments supplies a national formula for the concession China will make. They change the choices a counterpart must consider.

## A completed benefit or another permission

A limited bargain can deliver a benefit that the unfinished dispute cannot undo. Once the EP-3 crew had left, continued disagreement over responsibility could not reverse their departure. Washington had secured its immediate purpose without winning the larger argument. Continuing access is different. The military arrangements agreed in 2023 produced contact, but China's refusal of a proposed ministerial meeting in November 2024 showed that the next encounter still required consent.[^3] One completed magnet shipment did not guarantee continuing supply. Russian border residents likewise received access subject to expiry and individual refusal.[^limited-exposure]

These differences affect what a government should offer. A completed act may justify payment of the agreed price at once. Continuing access gives the recipient reason to seek a longer permission or a staged exchange. Those protections have costs and may be refused. The negotiator must then decide whether immediate relief is valuable enough to accept without them—and whether today's concession leaves anything useful to offer when the next permission is needed.

Nor should a defended restriction automatically become the starting assumption for every later bargain. China retained the 49 percent mobile-telecommunications ceiling in accession; years later it removed the foreign-ownership ceiling for securities companies. In the earlier rare-earth dispute, it reported removing the condemned measures. In the 2025 exchange, licensing authority survived the relief. Different sectors and circumstances produced different changes.[^2][^7][^1] A government can protect an objective while changing its means. The negotiator needs to consider both compensation within a limit and a proposal to change the limit itself.

For a factory short of inputs, a license may restore supplies promptly while repeal offers broader protection but takes longer to obtain. If the requested reciprocal concession is lasting and the relief too brief or narrow to secure the intended benefit, rejecting the package may be better. The public London record does not reveal enough of the terms and alternatives to calculate that judgment for Washington. It does make the exposure visible.

Performance also has to be judged against the benefit promised. JPMorgan's operating permission predated Phase One, while the regulator expressly linked an eight-month acceleration of the ownership opening to the agreement. Subsequent ownership and fee income were delivered benefits. An individual biotechnology approval could likewise enable a product launch while leaving the promised review process disputed. Neither excused the large aggregate purchase shortfall.[^7] The beneficiary may need an operating license, a timely product approval or a commercially workable order; announcing access does not establish which has been obtained.

Solomon's warning that bargaining continues after signature survives. His stronger claim that China demands adherence from others while seeking accommodation for itself requires more than evidence of further talks. Where an agreement expressly leaves permissions or consultations for later, the critic must identify the duty and the different treatment being sought.[^13] That distinction strengthens a justified complaint. It also prevents the government from promising an automatic benefit when it has negotiated a further procedure.

Even documenting a failure may be difficult. Lighthizer told Congress in 2019 that companies feared identifying themselves when complaining about Chinese conduct. Phase One permitted an appeal to omit a company's identifying information and required confidentiality. The firm best placed to report a failure might still depend on the authority it would be reporting.[^8] Confidentiality could help, without ensuring that the circumstances would not identify the complainant. A quiet public record is therefore a poor measure of success where beneficiaries lack a feasible way to report problems.

Once a failure is established, enforcement still has a price. The soybean and ethanol witnesses in December 2025 agreed that China had fallen short but differed over the response: prospective sales gave one constituency something to protect while lost access gave another reason to demand action.[^9] A government's threat depends partly on whether it will still consider retaliation worth carrying out after the next offer arrives. The bargain must be valued with that choice in view, not on the assumption that the existence of a remedy settles its use.

## Authority without complete certainty

Useful authority is not the same as an assurance that no further movement is possible. On April 7, 2001, during the EP-3 crisis, Zhou Wenzhong described the Chinese draft as cleared at a high level. Prueher objected and returned with a new American text. The assurance identified an approved proposal; it did not establish the furthest China would move. Washington's April 25 aircraft instructions made a different distinction explicit: Prueher could discuss the proposed document but needed approval of its complete text before committing to sign. Permission to explore, approval of an offer and authority to conclude were separate acts.[^authority-cables]

A negotiator has reason to ask which act has occurred. The answer can indicate where a counteroffer should go and what it would have to change. It cannot make a claim of finality self-verifying. When Chinese officials said there was no time to alter the crew-transfer memorandum, the Americans withheld agreement and obtained changes. Their objection concerned the wording they were being asked to accept; it did not depend on first discovering why that wording had been presented as final.

Solomon's stronger prediction—that senior intervention was the most reliable sign of imminent agreement, in an account also emphasizing concrete drafting—remains unvalidated at that comparative strength. The episodes here neither rank every available signal nor refute his forecast. They do show why the intervention's function matters. A leader can approve a new offer, confirm a defended limit or endorse an arrangement already negotiated.[^5] The practical question is what the intervention allows the parties to do next.

Caution does not require every uncertainty to be removed. On April 11, the Americans refused to supply an advance signed copy of Prueher's letter while seeking the assurance that would accompany its delivery. They obtained a description of departure procedures and a window for the charter's arrival. They did not obtain Tang's exact words in advance. At the meeting, Prueher stated his understanding and handed over the letter before Tang read the prepared statement.[^assurance-risk]

The crew returned the next day. That result does not make every oral assurance sufficient. It shows what the Americans chose to secure before proceeding: an acceptable statement of their own position, a commitment to departure and enough practical detail to judge the promised exchange. They accepted a remaining risk about execution. A demand for greater certainty would also have had a price if it postponed or prevented release.

Solomon made Chinese negotiation intelligible as purposeful political activity. The later record enlarges the range of purposes, participants and instruments that a counterpart must understand. Freeman already recognized specialization, institutional learning and the possibility that relationships would fail to produce a settlement.[^14] What changes the negotiator's work is the particular capacity and dependency now involved: the Chinese legal team able to advance a treaty claim, the firm seeking regulatory permission, the official deciding whether supplies or contact will resume.

Prueher could not obtain every assurance he sought before handing over his letter. He could protect its wording, test the departure arrangements and decide whether the remaining risk was worth taking to bring the crew home. The preparation made that decision possible. It could not make the decision for him.
