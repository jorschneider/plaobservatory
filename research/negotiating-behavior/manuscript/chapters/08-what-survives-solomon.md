# Conclusion: What survives Solomon

In the summer of 2025, a useful agreement with China could be measured in materials leaving a port and software becoming available again. After the Geneva accommodation faltered, officials met in London to work out its implementation. Beijing subsequently described an exchange: China would approve eligible export applications, while Washington would remove particular restrictions. Chip-design software companies reported relief from American restrictions. Chinese rare-earth magnet shipments recovered. The controls under which China approved exports remained in place.[^1]

The bargain changed the exercise of authority without abolishing it. That made relief possible while leaving another application, another customer or another intended use to be considered later. For manufacturers needing supplies, an immediate permission could be valuable. For the government negotiating on their behalf, it could also preserve a vulnerability. The decision was whether the relief obtained, on its particular terms, was worth accepting with that exposure still present.

This is a central problem in negotiating with China today. A politically defended limit can coexist with room for a substantial accommodation. The challenge is to identify the decision that would make the accommodation useful, what Beijing values in exchange, and who will carry the remaining risk. Solomon’s attention to purpose, relationships and authority remains essential to that work. WTO membership, new agreements governing scrutiny and a powerful position in supplying foreign industries have given those questions different practical consequences. A ruling, an operating license, an export clearance or permission to reopen a military channel can each be worth obtaining. None is adequately valued by the warmth of the encounter that produced it.

## The price of a limited bargain

The limits defended in these negotiations did not all have the same standing. China retained the 49 percent mobile-telecommunications ceiling in its accession bargain; years later it removed the foreign-ownership ceiling for securities companies. In the earlier rare-earth dispute, China reported removing the condemned measures. In the 2025 exchange, licensing authority survived the relief. These are different sectors, instruments and circumstances, but they establish a necessary caution: a rule defended today is not thereby proved permanent.[^2][^7][^1]

A government may protect an objective while changing the means by which it pursues it. Foreign ownership can be permitted while the business remains regulated. Shipments can be approved while export controls remain. The negotiator must therefore keep open two possibilities: compensation within the stated limit, and a proposal that requires changing the limit itself. Treating every refusal as a fixed political boundary would concede too much before the bargaining began.

Lamy's response to the ownership ceiling illustrates one available move. He sought authoritative confirmation, then considered compensating gains in a package he could defend to his principals. That did not make compensation interchangeable. Earlier minority access might serve one company while leaving another without the control its business required. A government negotiating on their behalf needed to decide which of those interests it was securing, and what it was asking the disappointed beneficiary to accept.[^2]

The same judgment applies to relief under an export-control system. A license can restore a factory's supplies promptly, while repeal may offer broader protection and take longer to obtain. The government must compare that difference with the concession sought in return. If it would make a lasting concession for relief too brief or too narrow to secure the intended benefit, declining the package can be the better choice. The public London record does not reveal enough of the terms and alternatives to calculate that judgment for Washington. It makes the exposure visible.

Limited agreements can also have benefits whose value does not depend on settling the larger dispute. Bringing the EP-3 crew home did not require resolving responsibility for the collision. Obtaining the 2023 commitment to resume named military mechanisms did not require agreement on Taiwan or technology policy.[^3] The case for accepting a partial result rests on the task it accomplishes and the price paid for it. An announcement of progress supplies neither answer.

## Authority that can be used

Both sides' authority mattered in accession. Chinese specialists could work out a concession that still required permission across sectors; American negotiators could prepare a package that the White House might decline. Barshefsky's inclusion of Gene Sperling addressed the second problem. The American departure letters made more time conditional on an opportunity to address the first. Neither step removed the need to decide what the package was worth.[^4]

A foreign official seldom sees the counterpart's instruction record. The useful response is to present a choice that can be carried for approval: which provision would change, what the other side would receive and which parts remain conditional on the whole package. An extension can then be tied to a particular meeting or response. This does not reveal whether a delay reflects limited authority or bargaining intent. It gives the next interval a purpose and allows the delegation to decide whether the expected decision still warrants staying.

That decision has a cost. Departing may cut off an authorization that is close; extending reveals that the clock is movable. The accession accounts disagree about how much pressure caused the result. They support the narrower practical point that the Americans altered their deadline when staying offered another opportunity. A negotiator can make that choice without pretending to know what delay means inside the other government.

Solomon’s stronger prediction—that senior intervention was the most reliable sign of imminent agreement, in an account that also emphasized concrete drafting—remains unvalidated at that comparative strength. The episodes here do not rank every available signal or refute his forecast. They do show why a negotiator should identify the intervention’s function. Lamy’s March meeting did not close the bargain; his May meeting supplied confirmation and approval in principle around which negotiators could finish. In October 2024, Modi and Xi endorsed a border arrangement already announced. A senior encounter can authorize, confirm or celebrate different stages of a negotiation.[^5]

An intermediary also has to be judged by the work the channel can perform. Kissinger’s July 2023 visit combined exceptional access, Chinese appeals for help and a relationship with American officials who valued his counsel. It deserves attention without attributing the November agreements to an unrecovered July message.[^6]

An official considering an intermediary should be clear about the task: explaining an offer, testing whether a proposal is worth advancing, conveying a warning, or seeking support from someone with authority. Those functions require different kinds of access. A visitor who is welcomed as a symbol may be unable to secure an operational commitment. A technically informed counterpart with less ceremonial standing may know which permission matters and how to request it. The relationship’s practical value lies in that connection to a decision.

## Performance and the cost of complaint

The work continues after a commitment is made, but the next act is not always another concession. JPMorgan’s securities business obtained operating permission before Phase One and later became wholly foreign owned. The regulator expressly linked an eight-month acceleration of the ownership opening to the agreement. Company disclosures then recorded ownership and substantial fee income. Further regulatory requirements did not erase those delivered benefits.[^7]

Agricultural biotechnology supplied a less satisfactory experience. Individual approvals could enable an identifiable product launch while leaving the promised review process in dispute. A certificate’s duration and the time needed to obtain it mattered to a business planning a crop launch. Aggregate purchase commitments posed another problem: the examined measures showed a large shortfall, even though their different coverage prevented combining them into one estimate. A useful financial opening did not excuse that shortfall; the shortfall did not make the financial opening imaginary.

Those distinctions matter before agreement. The official seeking access should know whether the firm needs removal of an ownership ceiling, an operating license, an approval for a particular product, or an order on workable commercial terms. Different promises require different evidence of performance. The firm may also value timing differently from the government announcing the bargain. A permission that arrives after a commercial opportunity has passed can satisfy a narrow formal requirement while providing much less of the expected benefit.

Learning what happened is itself a negotiating problem. In 2019, Lighthizer told Congress that companies feared identifying themselves when they complained about Chinese conduct. Phase One allowed an appeal to omit a company’s identifying information and required confidentiality for the material. This recognized a conflict inside the enforcement relationship: the firm best placed to document a failure might still depend on the authority it would be reporting.[^8]

Confidentiality could help the government obtain evidence. It could not ensure that the circumstances would not identify the complainant, or that the firm would risk the disclosure. The foreign negotiator therefore needed more than a well-written duty and an available remedy. The intended beneficiaries needed a feasible way to tell their government that the duty was not being performed. Where that route is weak, a quiet public record is a poor measure of whether the concession works.

Even a well-documented failure leaves a further political decision. The soybean and ethanol witnesses at the December 2025 hearing agreed that China had fallen short but differed over the response: prospective sales gave one constituency something to protect while lost access gave another reason to demand action.[^9]

A government promising to enforce an agreement must anticipate that division. The beneficiaries of retaliation may differ from the companies or farmers bearing its immediate costs. Prospective new purchases can make action on an old shortfall harder to sustain. This does not make a threat worthless. It means its credibility depends partly on whether the government will still consider it worth carrying out after the next offer arrives. The price of enforcement belongs in the original bargain.

## Rules allocate discretion as well as constrain it

China’s use of WTO litigation expanded the ways it could contest a powerful counterpart’s decisions. In DS379, Chinese arguments helped secure important findings on public bodies and the double counting of certain subsidies. Other claims failed. The parties then agreed procedures for a possible compliance dispute, preserving specified rights on both sides. The institution gave a rejected argument a route to an authoritative judgment; it did not ensure that every exporter obtained the commercial result it wanted.[^10]

That route must now be specified by counterpart. As Chapter 3 explains, ordinary WTO appeals cannot be heard under the current vacancies. China and the EU have used agreed appeal arbitration; China's participation alone supplies no such agreement with Washington. The availability of a final ruling has become part of the procedural bargain, and even an issued award leaves questions about domestic performance.[^appeal-now]

Phase One made a different allocation of authority. After consultation failed, its procedure allowed a complaining party to suspend an obligation or adopt a proportionate remedial measure. A respondent that considered the action taken in good faith could not counterrespond; if it considered the action taken in bad faith, the specified remedy was withdrawal. The governments had not entrusted that judgment to an independent adjudicator. A dispute over performance could become a decision about how much of the wider agreement they were prepared to risk.[^11]

These were consequential design choices. An external ruling can constrain a government's interpretation, while its procedure and implementation take time. A bilateral remedy can permit action without such a ruling, while leaving the other government to contest the action's good faith and reconsider participation. Precision alone does not choose between those outcomes. A detailed document can specify a permission, preserve discretion or define how pressure may be applied. Its value depends on which of those things the parties have agreed.

Climate diplomacy illustrates another use of precision. The Paris architecture required governments to prepare, communicate and maintain national contributions and to pursue domestic measures aimed at them. Reporting and review were specified, with capacity-related flexibility. Achievement of each announced contribution was not itself made an internationally binding obligation. China could accept meaningful common procedures while retaining national determination over an essential policy choice. A counterpart seeking scrutiny had obtained something substantial; one expecting an enforceable guarantee of the announced emissions result would misunderstand the bargain.[^12]

Solomon had already urged readers to look beyond signature. The later cases make his advice applicable to a wider array of instruments. They do not, merely by containing further talks, establish his stronger proposition that China demands adherence from others while seeking indulgence for itself. The aircraft negotiations followed an immediate arrangement about the EP-3 crew. Phase One prescribed consultation over disputed implementation. The Russian boundary arrangements expressly reserved consultations and permissions for particular local uses. To establish asymmetric accommodation, one must show the duty, the subsequent demand and the different treatment expected of the parties.[^13]

That discipline strengthens criticism when a commitment is actually diluted or withheld. It also prevents ordinary work under an agreement from being misdescribed as a Chinese reopening. A negotiator should defend the benefit that was obtained, including its conditions, while recognizing what the instrument deliberately left for later. Treating every further demand as a breach is as costly as allowing every breach to disappear into the continuing relationship.

## What the update requires

The history does not replace a China of personal relationships with a China of impersonal rules. Technical preparation, conditional offers and attention to wording were present during accession. Freeman’s 1999 essay already described specialization, institutional participation and adaptation to counterparts. He allowed relationship-building to fail or have uncertain results. Those qualifications belong to the inheritance, not to the discoveries of this book.[^14]

One concrete change was the development of the people and organizations needed to use an external legal procedure against powerful trading partners. Membership supplied the rights; officials, domestic and foreign lawyers, and academic specialists learned how to turn a commercial objection into a claim. Their work also connected litigation to the Chinese agencies whose measures had to be defended or altered. An American official could refuse China's bilateral request yet still have to answer its treaty argument. That alternative depended on professional capability as well as the rules—and, as the current appeal arrangements show, on the procedure the counterpart had accepted.[^capacity]

Other agreements changed what a commitment required the parties to do. The climate bargain specified international scrutiny while preserving national determination of targets. Phase One reached the processing of applications, the confidentiality of complaints and the parties' responses to disputed performance. Those designs gave negotiators choices about procedure and discretion that a broad promise of cooperation could not settle. In the 2025 exchange, permission for Chinese-controlled supplies to reach foreign industries became central to the bargain. Obtaining access to China and obtaining supplies from China could expose a counterpart to different decisions by the Chinese state.[^17]

Power matters through these choices. China’s position in processing magnet rare earths gave a licensing decision consequences for foreign production. Russia’s need for gas customers did not settle the route, finance or purchasing commitments for another pipeline. The operation of one line and the unfinished commercial terms of another showed both the value and limits of the partnership. India made progress at the border a condition for the wider relationship, while local arrangements, de-escalation and a final boundary settlement remained distinct objectives. The bargaining position belonged to the particular dependency and alternative, as well as to the countries involved.[^15]

The cases cannot assign the differences between these episodes to Xi’s personality or measure a national shift toward rigidity, patience or deception. They can identify what makes a refusal costly and what a concession would change. A larger economy, a more centralized leadership or a friendlier summit is context for that explanation. The explanation still needs the official who can act, the choice before that official and the interest served by accepting it.

The enduring lesson is demanding because it leaves the foreign negotiator with decisions to make. Test a specific reciprocal offer, and establish what one's own government can sustain. Use relationships to understand and reach the relevant authority. Value the concession at the point where a person or enterprise can use it, while pricing the discretion the other side retains. Decide how much evidence and protection are needed before committing, and what costs one's own side would accept if performance fails.

Solomon made Chinese negotiating behavior intelligible as purposeful political activity. That remains the right starting point. The update carries that explanation into the institutions, firms and administrative acts through which a bargain takes effect. A government may decide to take the licenses that restore production while continuing to seek more durable access. It may reject an attractive announcement because the permission its firms need remains untouched. It may enforce a broken promise despite the next offer of sales. Knowledge of China helps it understand those choices. The decision still requires its own government to say what it is prepared to obtain, concede and risk.
