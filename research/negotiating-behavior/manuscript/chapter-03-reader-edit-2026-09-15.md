# Chapter 3 reader edit — 15 September 2026

Edited [Chapter 3, “What expertise makes possible”](chapters/03-usable-expertise.md) after reading its complete narrative, all twenty-five notes and the existing independent review. This is a bounded editorial pass on the first manuscript draft, using its established facts and source apparatus. It adds no research and does not certify the finished book or reclassify the baseline propositions.

## What changed

The opening now leads with three demonstrated uses of expertise: China obtained an administrative adjustment, advanced arguments that produced favorable legal interpretations, and negotiated a defined procedure for contesting compliance. The distinction among those results also introduces the commercial question: none automatically establishes lower duties for every exporter. The previous opening gave relatively more space to explaining why a learning claim could not yet be proved.

The DS379–climate bridge now identifies the difference in the route to a decision. In DS379, a rejected argument could proceed to an adjudicator applying existing rules. Climate negotiators were specifying rules for future conduct that governments still had to accept. The cases are connected by the work of defining an obligation and its reservations, without attributing every final provision to Chinese specialists or treating the two settings as matched evidence of historical change.

Within DS379, the accepted inflation adjustment is stated directly as an administrative response, with the unresolved legality of the broader benchmark preserved. The procedural agreement is presented through its contingent route to review and possible suspension of concessions, rather than repeated descriptions of what kinds of missing sources would be required to explain its drafting. The scope correction to Solomon remains a warning against extending findings from high political documents to every instrument; it does not become an empirical verdict against all his propositions.

Within the climate narrative, the Copenhagen heading identifies the substantive distinction between ambition and scrutiny. The working-group and Stern passages now emphasize intended technical tasks, the envoy’s standing and reciprocal endorsement. The target recollection problem and missing draft sequence remain in note 20. The final section is titled “Expertise within political limits,” placing observed capabilities ahead of an unproved general trajectory of increasing sophistication. Its closing explicitly leaves learning, resources, counsel and changed stakes unresolved.

Repeated source-method qualifications were shortened or moved into existing notes: authorship of the submission (3), the status of Prusa–Vermulst’s analysis (10), missing procedural drafts and instructions (11), and Stern’s remembered targets and unavailable clearances (20). The initial draft-status paragraph moved to an end comment. The DS379 and Paris event endpoints remain explicit. Substantive limits that affect the interpretation stay in the narrative.

## Checks and retained distinctions

The public-body definition and separate supplier/bank results, double-remedy holding, unlike anti-dumping measures, exact 2014 target paragraph and Paris duties paragraph are unchanged. The text retains:

- A prohibition on double offsetting, with the investigative failure and absence of an exporter-by-exporter duty calculation distinguished.
- Conditional suspension procedures, not immediate retaliation or a resolved compliance dispute.
- Copenhagen’s take-note disposition; domestic measurement/reporting/verification, international consultation/analysis and verification of supported actions.
- Different US and Chinese targets, capacity-qualified transparency flexibility, and multilateral consent beyond bilateral endorsement.
- Paris text adoption, subsequent consent to be bound, domestic-pursuit duties and national-result obligations as different matters. The edited comparison refers to an agreed text **specifying** obligations, avoiding a suggestion that adoption itself bound every state.

All twenty-five note definitions resolve; external source links are unchanged. The protected legal/target paragraphs were checked against the pre-edit text, and the file passes whitespace validation. The narrative including its initial draft-status text decreased from 3,573 to 3,215 words, approximately ten percent. This is a readability change, not a reduction in recorded source coverage.
