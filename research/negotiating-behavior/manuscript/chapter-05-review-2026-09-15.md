## Assessment

The principal argument is sustained: a publicly incomplete enforcement question in October became a defined procedure in the signed January instrument, while both governments accepted a partial settlement. The chapter generally distinguishes public assertions, reported draft changes, final text and causal inference carefully. Its strongest contribution is reconstructing changes in the object of negotiation and identifying what the published record can actually establish about authority. The discussion of May 2018 includes American inconsistency rather than treating Chinese flexibility as the only behavior to explain.

No numerical error was found in the inspected purchase increments or dispute timelines. The distinction between tariff announcements, suspension of prospective duties and a reduction scheduled for February is preserved in the detailed narrative. The separation of Article 7.4 withdrawal from Article 8.3 termination also incorporates the earlier review correctly. The conclusion’s refusal to infer the stronger asymmetric S-P25 pattern from a formally bilateral procedure is particularly important and should remain.

The following revisions would improve the argument before the next manuscript pass.

## Substantive corrections and additions

1. **Preserve the qualified baseline when discussing leader intervention.** The paragraph beginning “Solomon treated high-level intervention” (original line 37) omits two qualifications already present in S-P24: the conjunction with a shift toward concrete drafting, and Solomon’s own aborted claims/assets example. The chapter’s disaggregation of process authorization and approval of obligations is useful, but should sharpen this qualified indicator rather than appear to refute an unconditional leader-meeting rule. A concise addition and a direct locator to Solomon pp. 147–151 would suffice. This is not a reason to weaken the finding that public senior involvement leaves the actual mandate unknown.

2. **Do not infer temporal evolution solely from unlike instruments.** The paragraph beginning “The evolution since Solomon’s early cases” (line 83) compares a detailed economic instrument with general political undertakings. The observation about specificity is sound; the label of evolution risks conflating time with issue area and document type. Frame it as a limit on generalizing the communiqué-based baseline, or explicitly leave historical change versus functional difference unresolved. Comparable earlier trade or technical instruments would be required for the stronger longitudinal claim. The final paragraph on detailed provisions and continuing political discretion can stand without that inference.

3. **Include the contrary Chinese voice within the Reuters report itself.** The May 8 account’s reported redline rests on the six sources accurately described in the chapter. The same article also quotes an unnamed Chinese official familiar with the talks who invokes the procedures required for legal changes, disputes the backtracking characterization and describes U.S. demands as harder. This does not invalidate the redline reporting, authenticate a Chinese instruction or resolve responsibility. One sentence in the May section, or a fuller note 10, would prevent the source discussion from obscuring contrary testimony within the very article under examination. The subsequent official white paper is a different source and genre.

4. **Mention the urgent-meeting fallback if procedure remains the central explanatory feature.** Article 7.4.5 not only permits direct principal-level consideration: if the urgent meeting cannot be timely scheduled, the complainant may resort to the action described in 7.4.4(b). The chapter mentions skipping lower levels (line 87) but not that fallback. Adding a short clause would make the account of procedural discretion complete enough for the central argument. It would also avoid an implication that an actual unsuccessful principals’ meeting is always a prerequisite to unilateral action. This is a substantive omission rather than a reversal of the interpretation.

5. **Clarify the opening tariff summary.** “China obtained partial tariff relief” in the January 15 opening (line 7) can be read as saying the existing-rate reduction was already operative. The later calendar paragraph is correct. A small distinction between the suspension already obtained and the reduction secured for a later effective date would prevent the opening from undoing that precision. No further tariff research is necessary for this wording change.

## Readability and unnecessary qualifications

The sentence “This was not an unconditional Chinese waiver of retaliation while the United States judged its own case under an independent tribunal” (line 93) combines incompatible ideas in a distracting hypothetical. The affirmative findings are sufficient: the mechanism formally applied to either party; the respondent assessed good faith; there was no neutral adjudicator. Preserve those and remove the awkward contrast.

The missing May redline is consequential and belongs near the first description of that dispute. It is then reiterated in the introduction, May source discussion, end of the May section, implementation section and notes. Most repetitions are defensible individually, but the chapter can be lighter by reserving repeated cautions for a new inferential step. Likewise, the exact “twenty-one plus forty-five does not equal sixty-six here” correction can sit in a note if the main paragraph explains both deadlines run from receipt. These are presentation recommendations, not requests to delete evidentiary limits.

The phrase “an allegedly national attitude toward promises” (line 25) is broader than the specific Solomon propositions being tested. Naming the relevant claim—invocation of prior undertakings, or asymmetric post-agreement demands—would make the revision more precise and less polemical. The partial-settlement conclusion already correctly distinguishes observed issue deferral from an unproved face mechanism.

## Selective source checks actually performed

- **TUS12, October 11 official public transcript:** inspected the enforcement exchange, the qualification that the deal still needed to be put in writing, Trump’s admission it could fail, Mnuchin’s description of work remaining within Liu’s team, Huawei’s exclusion, and the separate tariff discussions. These sustain the opening and the October-to-January trajectory. They do not establish private drafting authorship.
- **TUS08, May 8 Reuters syndicated article:** inspected the description of the draft and source base, administrative/regulatory implementation dispute, the Chinese official’s counteraccount, and still-unresolved subsidies and biotechnology questions. This supports the chapter’s refusal to assume every eventual omission had previously received final assent.
- **TUS05, December 1 White House dinner readout:** complete substantive body read. It explicitly leaves the purchase quantity unagreed and specifies a ninety-day negotiating period, sustaining the identified discrepancy with Liu’s later account.
- **POA-EN:** selected text at physical PDF p. 19 (Articles 1.34–1.36), pp. 77–81 (Article 7.3 onward and Annex 7-A), and p. 82 (Articles 8.1–8.4). The 21/45-day periods share the appeal-receipt origin; the ordinary and urgent routes, good-faith distinction, separate termination clause and implementation discretion match the findings above. This is selected text reading, not a fresh full-agreement or bilingual visual audit.
- **Baseline claim files:** S-P05, S-P12 and S-P24 were checked for their expressed scope; the stronger post-agreement asymmetry was assessed against the chapter’s conclusion. This review did not newly reread the complete Solomon volume.

The economic explanation remains deliberately incomplete. Learning about resolve, changed costs, narrower scope and improved contract design are different hypotheses. The chapter appropriately identifies evidence that could discriminate among them instead of declaring one proven by the agreement’s occurrence. Integrating a distinct contract-theory interpretation may improve the next draft, but it should not substitute a theoretical label for missing process evidence.
