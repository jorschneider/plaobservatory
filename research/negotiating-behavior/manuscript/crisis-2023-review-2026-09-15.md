# Bounded independent review of the 2023 crisis case

Root review, 15 September 2026, separate from the case author's research. The review covers the entire [case memo](../cases/us-china-crisis-2023.md), the [source register](../sources/us-china-crisis-2023-register.json), and selected underlying records. It approves the case as input to a manuscript draft, not as a complete account of private crisis decisions or of subsequent implementation.

The differentiated-channel argument survives the checks. The original Chinese announcement lists three military arrangements cancelled in August 2022. The February 9 defense statement explicitly declines the proposed ministerial call after the shootdown. The May 31 answer says military contacts had not ceased and calls for corrected American conduct, without naming a single sufficient concession. The November 30 answer, in Chinese and official English, describes implementation coordination and future resumption. All four substantive texts were read completely in this review: C23-01, -06, -11 and -19.

The June negative observation is particularly useful. Blinken's opening and his answers to Kylie Atwood and the final follow-up were checked against the preserved official-transcript reprint, C23-15. He reports no immediate military-channel agreement while defending continued diplomacy. This supports an unsuccessful issue within a senior visit, not a conclusion that the entire visit failed or that any senior encounter should invariably close a bargain.

One correction was applied to the case verdict: S-P24 is now evaluated against Solomon's actual comparative forecast, rather than an automatic-closure rule. An unsuccessful senior encounter belongs in the denominator for a future comparative test. It does not by itself rank the reliability of leader intervention against drafting and other signals. The distinction agrees with the corrected historical baseline in Chapter 1.

The counternarcotics tradeoff is better supported than an inference from summit chronology. Both pages of the actual Commerce rule were visually inspected, restricting the reading to the target rule: effective November 16, published November 17, removal of the specifically named Institute of Forensic Science. The full VOA report, C23-25, independently supplies a named spokesman's explanation linking delisting to precursor cooperation. The case appropriately keeps the legal act and motive attribution separate. It does not convert this into proof of the price for military reopening, completed Chinese enforcement or the resolution of human-rights concerns.

The Carnegie reading boundaries were also checked: methodology printed pp. 7–8; selected narrative pp. 101–104; notes 592–641, with adjacent notes incidentally visible. These are retrospective American accounts. The report itself identifies 33 former US officials and the absence of Chinese internal records. The Xi-awareness chain in note 602 contains former American officials and published reporting. The case keeps it as an attributed assessment. The report's stronger statements about a strategic channel's effects should retain their participant/source labels, not become Chinese decision evidence. No raw interviews were obtained.

Two incidental defects in the report do not affect the case's supported claims: its p. 101 description compresses Nixon's 1972 opening into normalization, and its later dating of Wang's return to the foreign-minister post requires correction before use. Neither is adopted in the case. Likewise, the VOA article's broad drug-supply statistics were not checked and are not used.

Other official readouts and the AP/CNN/RSIS records were evaluated through the author's exact-scope apparatus and case citations, not independently reread in full by this reviewer. The separate Kissinger review checks overlapping sources and the intermediary mechanism. No unresolved material correction was found within this bounded review after the S-P24 change. The remaining private instructions, intelligence and draft arrangements are explicit access gaps rather than silently supplied explanations.
