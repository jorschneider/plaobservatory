# Bargaining with China: authority, commitments and change

**Working manuscript in substantive redevelopment, 16 September 2026.** An original reassessment of Richard H. Solomon’s *Chinese Negotiating Behavior*, centered on negotiations with the United States and tested against the EU, India and Russia. The seven chapters, introduction, conclusion and three appendices form a complete first draft. Source review and copyediting did not establish publication quality. A new developmental pass is strengthening narrative, explanatory depth and practical use for negotiators. This is an original research manuscript, not an authorized new edition or a claim of access equivalent to Solomon’s.

The [current development brief](development/README.md) records the publication objective and remaining work. Earlier completion records refer to the first research draft, not achievement of that objective.

The [research dossier](../README.md), original-document registers and critical reading memos are the supporting apparatus. Read the [single HTML edition](reading-edition.html), with contents and linked source notes, or start with the [introduction](chapters/00-introduction.md) or the [conclusion](chapters/08-what-survives-solomon.md).

## The argument

Chinese officials can defend political limits while adjusting commercially or strategically valuable terms; some defended rules can themselves be changed. A counterpart must decide whether the useful bargain requires compensation, a different rule or refusal of an inadequate package. Political relationships shape purpose and access; authority makes particular commitments possible; implementing decisions determine whether their benefits become usable. The cases show why those are different events. They also document substantive developments in WTO legal agency, climate procedures, bilateral remedies and reciprocal relief through export-control administration without treating different instruments as a controlled measure of national or leadership change.

Several of Solomon’s central questions remain useful. His stronger forecasting, interpersonal and implementation claims require more specific tests. The conclusion distinguishes a revision to how a proposition should be used from evidence that Chinese behavior changed over time. The appendix gives every one of the 59 baseline propositions a bounded assessment rather than a verdict based on outcomes alone. Missing validation is distinguished from evidence against a proposition; original qualifications are preserved.

## Limits that remain part of the book

No new interviews, correspondence or private Chinese archive access have been conducted. The spring 2019 draft, several accession annexes, intermediary transmissions and complete application histories remain unacquired. Those gaps restrict particular explanations; they do not prevent a bounded account of accepted instruments and subsequent decisions. The manuscript does not infer motives merely from delay or success.

The cases have different endpoints: Paris text adoption in 2015; DS379 through 2012 with a specified April 2016 domestic-litigation coda and a separate September 2026 check of appellate availability; EP-3 through July 2001; the 2023 crisis with a bounded military-channel follow-up through November 2024; Indian mechanisms through May 2026; gas through specified early September 2026 records; and the trade mechanism through July 23, 2026. September retrieval dates do not extend each case’s substantive history.

The counterpart comparisons are not matched statistical controls. Neither a source count nor a word count establishes book quality. The working manuscript presents the findings that the inspected public evidence can sustain, with unresolved questions recorded as such. Publication would still require an external editorial process; new interviews or private records could change particular explanations.
