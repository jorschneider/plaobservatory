# Bargaining with China: authority, commitments and change

**Complete first manuscript draft, 15 September 2026.** An original reassessment of Richard H. Solomon’s *Chinese Negotiating Behavior*, centered on negotiations with the United States and tested against the EU, India and Russia. The seven chapters, introduction, conclusion and proposition appendix now exist. This is a research manuscript under review, not an authorized new edition or a claim of access equivalent to Solomon’s.

The [research dossier](../README.md), original-document registers and critical reading memos are the supporting apparatus. Read the [single HTML edition](reading-edition.html), with contents and linked source notes, or start with the [introduction](chapters/00-introduction.md) or the [conclusion](chapters/08-what-survives-solomon.md).

## The argument

Political relationships shape purpose and access; authority makes particular commitments possible; implementing decisions determine whether their benefits become usable. The cases show why those are different events. They also document substantive developments in WTO legal agency, climate procedures and bilateral remedies without treating different instruments as a controlled measure of national or leadership change.

Several of Solomon’s central questions remain useful. His stronger forecasting, interpersonal and implementation claims require more specific tests. The conclusion distinguishes a revision to how a proposition should be used from evidence that Chinese behavior changed over time. The appendix gives every one of the 59 baseline propositions a bounded assessment rather than a verdict based on outcomes alone. Missing validation is distinguished from evidence against a proposition; original qualifications are preserved.

## Limits that remain part of the book

No new interviews, correspondence or private Chinese archive access have been conducted. The spring 2019 draft, several accession annexes, intermediary transmissions and complete application histories remain unacquired. Those gaps restrict particular explanations; they do not prevent a bounded account of accepted instruments and subsequent decisions. The manuscript does not infer motives merely from delay or success.

The cases have different endpoints: Paris text adoption in 2015; EP-3 through July 2001; the recent crisis through November 2023; Indian mechanisms through May 2026; gas through specified early September 2026 records; and the trade mechanism through July 23, 2026. September retrieval dates do not extend each case’s substantive history.

The counterpart comparisons are not matched statistical controls. Neither a source count nor a word count establishes book quality. The first full draft now permits the necessary work of checking argument, chronology, repetition and reader navigation across the whole manuscript. The active goal remains the book-quality reassessment; a first complete draft is a substantial checkpoint, not automatic completion.
