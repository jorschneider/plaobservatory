# Appendix 3. Bibliography and source guide

**15 September 2026.** This guide consolidates the works and collections central to the manuscript and its supporting cases. Chapter notes identify individual documents and consequential passages; the registers below provide preserved versions, source IDs, locators and reading limits. It is a bibliography of material used, not the larger list of works identified for possible acquisition.

**Complete** means the specified work or version was read, including its notes; it does not mean every source in its bibliography was acquired. **Selected** identifies a bounded reading. A complete chapter, interview transcript or article is not a complete book, audio review or archive. The [reading ledger](../../literature/readings/README.md) records the broader contextual scholarship and exact version control. Translations, republications and works repeating the same interviews are not independent witnesses.

## The book being reassessed

- **Solomon, Richard H.** *Chinese Negotiating Behavior: Pursuing Interests Through “Old Friends.”* Washington, DC: United States Institute of Peace Press, 1999; supplied 2005 printing. Complete substantive text, notes and bibliography read across the [foundation](../../book/context-lessons-freeman.md) and [process](../../book/process.md) readings; selected page images checked. The principal study concerns 1967–1984. The bibliography was read, but its entire underlying literature was not.
- **Freeman, Chas. W., Jr.** “Chinese Negotiating Behavior Revisited.” In Solomon, 181–196. Complete essay read. Its late-1990s practitioner reassessment is a separate baseline, rather than a description of conditions in 2005 or independent corroboration of Solomon.

The [proposition assessment](01-proposition-assessment.md) preserves the distinction between the inventory’s paraphrases and the book’s stronger original formulations. The [factual-correction note](../../book/verified-corrections.md) records three verified errors in the supplied printing without treating them as automatic refutations of its behavioral explanations.

## Primary collections and documentary practice

The **Foreign Relations of the United States** series supplies selected contemporary American memoranda, instructions and editorial source notes: *1969–1976*, volumes XVII–XVIII, and *1977–1980*, volume XIII. The claims/assets and normalization reconstructions inspect bounded sequences, not the complete volumes. Clinton and George W. Bush presidential archives, USIA/State mirrors, and later archived White House records supply additional official releases. The acquired EP-3 legal-guidance packet is a Department of Defense disclosure. State Department case F-2010-07070 supplies nine newly read contemporary cables on crew-release bargaining, including reported revisions and the exchange of authority, letter and oral assurance; [exact scope and hashes](../development/ep3-drafting-cables-register.json). Six further cables reconstruct [aircraft assessment and return negotiations](../development/ep3-aircraft-cables-register.json), including the actual US instruction and unilateral non-papers. The Ford Library scans add Chinese–American claims exchanges in November 1974 and October 1975; [scope](../development/claims-1974-archive-followup-register.json). These records are American accounts of Chinese representations, not Chinese internal instructions. A Bush Library finding aid identifying a separate NSC release does not establish that its underlying assets were downloaded or read.

China’s Ministry of Commerce collection, **《中国加入世界贸易组织谈判文件资料选编》**, compiled by its WTO Department under editor 索必成 (中国商务出版社, 2013), provides selected readings from **综合卷①** and **双边卷③**. Reproduced dated offers and signed bodies are distinguished from retrospective editorial narration. Most of both volumes remains unread; missing annexes are not supplied by a later summary. The participant collection above is another genre, not an additional archive.

**WTO** accession schedules and DS379 submissions, reports and procedural records; **UNFCCC** Copenhagen, Cancún, Durban, Lima and Paris instruments; paired US–China climate announcements; and **IISD Earth Negotiations Bulletin** conference reports supply institutional rules, positions and observed proceedings. They do not confer equivalent legal status on every national target or provide private authorizations. Final-text adoption, consent to be bound and implementation remain separate events.

For recent cases, paired Chinese and counterpart ministry texts are read alongside original tariff notices, the bilingual Phase One agreement, Chinese regulatory decisions, company disclosures, hearings and released correspondence. Indian and Russian official texts, treaty reproductions and contemporary reporting extend the comparison. Original treaty maps, commercial price formulas, complete licensing files and undisclosed negotiating drafts remain absent where the registers say so. Corporate or official announcements establish their stated milestone; they do not automatically verify every operational consequence.

The [public-position and domestic-policy supplement](../development/climate-policy-boundaries-register.json) adds the complete December 17, 2009 He briefing and 2013 air-pollution plan, a selected Wen press-conference answer and selected 2014 energy-strategy sections. The strategy's editing notice limits backdating its published terms. The [Chinese participant supplement](../development/chinese-climate-participants-register.json) distinguishes published speeches, transcripts and retrospective reporting from private instructions.

The [2009–2013 climate machinery supplement](../development/climate-2009-2013-machinery-register.json) distinguishes initialing/publication, formal signatures, authorized consultation, reported meetings and later specified tasks. The [Brazil energy supplement](../../literature/readings/brazil-energy-authority-register.json) follows the institutional scholarship into a bilateral action plan, a coordination meeting, auction results and a conditional Petrobras agreement. These original documents define formal responsibilities and terms; they do not recover Chinese company instructions.
