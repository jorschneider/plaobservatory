# Independent review of the proposition assessments

**Review date: 15 September 2026. Scope: 17 consequential judgments, including all seven entries initially classified “revise or narrow.”**

The review found a classification problem rather than a new historical discovery. Several entries treated a qualification already present in Solomon or Freeman as something the later research had established against them. Others used observations from one audience or institutional setting to assess a claim about another. The strongest corrections are to Freeman’s relationship argument and public-diplomacy diagnosis: he already allowed relationship-based bargaining to fail, and executive access does not test effective outreach to legislators, interest groups or the public.

After coordination with the manuscript author, eight verdicts were changed to **unresolved**: the seven former revise-or-narrow entries—S-C13, S-C19, F01, F06, S-P01, S-P05 and S-P24—and the former retain verdict for S-C01. S-C10 remains retained within a more precisely stated observed component. The resulting classification is 16 retained within tested scope, 38 unresolved and 5 untested, with no current proposition-level revise-or-narrow or correct-baseline verdict. These are assessment categories, not a score measuring how much of the book is right.

This does not mean the manuscript has nothing to update. Later institutions, instruments, bargaining objects and implementation records materially expand the account. It means that an instruction to use the old framework more carefully is different from a demonstrated change in the frequency, comparative advantage or cause of Chinese behavior. The appendix now states those distinctions directly. Its three separately verified factual corrections remain unaffected.

## Corrections to the seven revision judgments

### F01: failure is already part of Freeman’s account

Freeman p. 183 attributes the proposed growth of trust and common interests to the Chinese view of reserving difficult issues. He then describes success in an appropriate context, failure, and—continuing on p. 184—uncertain success. The original is not a rule that elapsed time or warm relationships necessarily settle disagreements.

The Russia boundary instruments establish reservation followed by later settlement (RUS-T1991-CN, RUS-T2001, RUS-T2004). They do not by themselves identify why the parties reserved the issues or what produced the later settlement. The gas records show disagreement persisting amid political endorsement (RUS-G14, RUS-G17, RUS-G28), which is compatible with Freeman’s stated qualification. The Kissinger readouts show recognition and cultivation of a relationship (K23-01, K23-02), without independently demonstrating a deferred bargain or accumulated trust.

The proper result is **unresolved** for the intended and actual causal contribution. The recurring repertoire remains an observation, but it cannot support either an automatic trust mechanism or a claim that the study has refuted such a mechanism in Freeman. The original qualification is now explicit in the entry.

### F06: the audience and causal chain must match

Freeman pp. 188–190 already acknowledges expanded press contact and growing congressional outreach while criticizing their effectiveness. At p. 189 he expressly dates the diagnosis to the end of the twentieth century. Simply finding a later channel does not revise a claim that channels existed but functioned poorly.

Xie’s 2023 account of difficulty bringing forward Americans favorable to relations is relevant as an official diagnosis (K23-07), not an independent measurement. Blinken’s later statement that he consulted Kissinger before his June visit is positive evidence of an executive consultation channel (K23-10). Neither that statement nor the Chinese cultivation of Kissinger measures rapport with legislators, interest groups or the public, and neither reveals a resulting change in negotiating instructions.

The verdict is **unresolved**. A persuasive test requires audience-specific outreach, recipient responses and the formation of negotiating positions, with policy disagreement distinguished from poor communication. The revised entry preserves the positive access evidence without presenting it as the missing test.

### S-C13 and S-P05: unlike instruments cannot decide a qualified preference

Solomon p. 5 already says that a negotiator seeking an accord can set aside early principles and reach a concrete agreement. His discussion at pp. 170 and 173 concerns political relationships, while p. 151 specifically describes a preference in political agreements. The claimed preference is not a prohibition on legal argument or technical detail.

DS379 demonstrates detailed Chinese legal argument and acceptance of precise procedures (DS379-03, DS379-07). Phase One and the agricultural process records contain specific obligations (POA-EN/CN; AG01, AG06, AG07). Those findings are substantial additions to the book’s institutional coverage. They do not establish that Chinese political negotiators ceased preferring broad understandings when choosing among comparable alternatives.

The 2023 readouts show framing and invocation of principles (C23-14, C23-16, C23-18). They do not establish the alternatives rejected, mutually accepted meaning, the resulting constraint on the United States or the claimed asymmetry in Chinese discretion. Both judgments are now **unresolved**. S-C13 identifies the comparative preference; S-P05 identifies the fuller preference-and-constraint mechanism. Repeating the original scope restriction is not counted as a new behavioral revision.

### S-P01: an unsuccessful ending is not a counterexample to the original diagram

The table at p. 58 explicitly includes agreement, reservation and abortion. The prose at p. 147 also identifies alternatives to settlement. The private-claims failure therefore does not, simply by being a failure, disprove the sequence. Nor are diplomatic and military contacts necessarily different stages of one negotiating object: the 2023 channel comparison cannot be substituted for a test of typical within-negotiation order or duration.

The October 2019 announcement, January 2020 signature and later implementation exchanges supply a useful chronology (TUS12, TUS15, POI01, POI03, POI04), but they do not establish the model’s general regularity. The verdict is **unresolved** pending specified bargaining objects, entry and exit points, stage durations and failed or repeated sequences. The diagram can remain a descriptive aid without becoming a validated national cycle.

### S-C19 and S-P24: separate empirical diagnosis from advice

These two entries require special care because the original wording is strong. S-C19 says rigidity usually signals leadership conflict. At p. 149 Solomon identifies senior intervention as the most reliable sign of approaching agreement; p. 150 describes a highly consistent endgame. The unchanged S-P24 inventory’s softer “can signal” remains visible as an extraction qualification, not substituted for the printed proposition.

The current evidence does not estimate either frequency or comparative predictive rank. The claims/assets record contains a contemporary internal-political interpretation with a partly inaccessible source chain (CLA06), not independently recovered Chinese deliberations. The EU account documents a collective ceiling and an unsuccessful effort to improve the offer, but does not establish the absence or presence of leadership conflict (EWT-01). Regulatory review and delay provide possible alternative mechanisms in another setting, not a population-level test of high political rigidity.

Likewise, the normalization records establish a successful sequence involving concrete text and Deng’s decisions (NOR-159, NOR-166, NOR-170). Zhou’s claims/assets movement, Zhu’s March 2000 meeting and Xi’s June 2023 reception of Blinken did not settle all the relevant objects (CLA04, CLA05, CLA10; EWT-01; C23-15, C23-16). These episodes show why a senior encounter cannot be equated with completion. They cannot establish that another signal is more reliable across comparable cases. In the later examples, the full concrete-drafting condition and prospective information available to observers are not uniformly recovered.

Both empirical verdicts are therefore **unresolved**. The practical advice remains: do not diagnose leadership conflict from rigidity alone, and identify what a leader authorizes, confirms or leaves unsettled. That is a disciplined restriction on use, not a finding that the original frequency or ranking has been empirically overturned. The face mechanism also remains unproved.

These retained verdicts name substantial observable components of the original propositions. None treats the observable component as proof of its cultural origin, independent efficacy, general frequency or national uniqueness. In particular, S-P25 appropriately declines to retain a full claim merely because its weaker continuation component is supported; S-C01 now applies the same discipline to variation and its proposed explanation.

## Applied changes and verification

Changes are confined to the current [assessment data](proposition-assessment-2026.json), [Appendix 1](appendices/01-proposition-assessment.md) and this review. All 59 entire baseline objects and both baseline inventories remain unchanged. Nine current entries were edited: eight status changes plus the S-C10 clarification. Coverage language now acknowledges all seven substantive chapters while preserving dossier-specific endpoints.

Validation checks the 59 unique IDs, equality of every embedded baseline object to the appropriate frozen inventory, the status counts, agreement between Markdown headings and JSON verdicts, matching current text for the nine edited entries, and local links in the changed appendix and review. This review does not extend beyond the stated 17-entry inference audit to recertify every proposition or every original source.
