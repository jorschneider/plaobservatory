# Independent review of Chapter 3

Review date: 15 September 2026. Reviewed the complete first draft of [What expertise makes possible](chapters/03-usable-expertise.md), against the already reviewed DS379 and climate cases and their reading registers. This is a chapter review, not approval of the entire book or a finding about subsequent implementation through 2026.

## Outcome

The chapter is usable as a first substantive draft. Its paired argument is supported: China could use institutional procedures and technical arguments to contest a major power's position while preserving disagreement over substantive obligations. The chapter does not establish that learning caused the observed behavior, locate all relevant expertise inside the Chinese government, or mistake later visibility of detailed argument for its historical emergence.

One clarification was requested and its application verified. The double-remedy paragraph now explicitly states that SCM Article 19.3 prohibits offsetting the same subsidization twice before explaining the violation established in this dispute: the investigating authority failed to assess whether and to what extent duplication occurred. This avoids making the holding sound like an obligation merely to study the issue. Appellate Body paragraphs 582–583 and 599–606 support the distinction. No material correction remains outstanding from this pass.

## Consequential checks

- **Public bodies:** ownership alone is insufficient, governmental functions and meaningful control can remain evidence, and the state-owned commercial bank determination receives different treatment from the input-supplier determinations. The chapter does not turn the ruling into blanket SOE immunity. The bank result remains tied to the record and challenge presented.
- **A documented use of expertise:** the inflation proposal originated in the earlier coated-free-sheet-paper investigation and entered the tyre record. Commerce accepted that adjustment; this is a concrete administrative response. The chapter correctly distinguishes it from a favorable ruling on the entire benchmark methodology, which the Appellate Body could not complete on the available findings.
- **Procedure and implementation:** the May 2012 text establishes accepted procedural undertakings, including contingent access to compliance review and later suspension authorization. It does not establish the drafts, bargaining initiative, private instructions, actual retaliation, or adjudication of the subsequent compliance objection. The published text records signing but does not reproduce autographs. The rate discussion preserves the distinction between matched CVD rates and unlike investigation dumping margins/revised AD deposits, including the significance of intervening reviews and prospective application.
- **Copenhagen:** domestic measurement, reporting and verification; international consultation and analysis; and international verification of supported actions remain distinct. The COP took note of the accord. Conrad's explanation of specialist displacement remains attributed, with mandates and interview records unavailable; it is not converted into proven conflict within China's supreme leadership.
- **The later climate sequence:** the 2014 national intentions retain their different coverage, dates and baselines. Stern's retrospective recollection does not override the dated joint text. Cancun and Durban prevent an unsupported claim that willingness to negotiate the architecture originated with Xi. Paris adoption remains separate from later consent to be bound; procedural and domestic-pursuit duties remain separate from a binding duty to achieve each NDC result.

## Argument and presentation

The opening agreement provides a concrete entry into the problem. The comparison adds value because adjudication under existing rules and negotiation of future rules give expertise different routes to influence. Solomon and Freeman are represented as already recognizing technical competence and institutional variation.

Two optional improvements were also applied: reducing duplication between the opening's two learning qualifications, and replacing the concluding “obtain some legal changes” with the more concrete “secure favorable rulings and an accepted methodological adjustment.” The specific caveats remain attached to particular claims; they explain the evidence rather than merely announcing caution.

This review reread the complete chapter and case prose, the DS379 register, selected original Appellate Body passages, the entire published May agreement, the implementation notice's consequential tables and application provisions, the correction's operative provisions, relevant Prusa–Vermulst passages, the 2014 announcement, and selected Conrad and Paris reporting/treaty passages. It does not claim a new complete reading of every underlying volume or article. The chapter's notes accurately preserve the project's wider recorded reading scopes.
