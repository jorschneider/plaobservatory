## The scholarly proposition and its limits

Fravel argues that threats to regime security can make territorial concessions attractive by increasing the value of neighbors’ cooperation. His evidence combines published Chinese histories and documents, memoirs, and interviews conducted without attribution. He distinguishes attempting compromise from completing settlement. The 2004 Russian settlement is explicitly outside the proposed domestic-insecurity explanation; an external relationship motive is inferred instead. For 1991, he attributes separation of eastern and western settlements to priorities after Tiananmen. His territorial categories and concession shares describe a study ending in 2005, not a current inventory or a general measure of bargaining success. The article acknowledges limited archival access. Its p. 78 incorrectly dates the eastern agreement to April 1991: the official treaty dates signature to **16 May 1991**, and entry into force to **16 March 1992**. [Fravel, especially pp. 51–54, 59, 63–64, 76–78](https://www.taylorfravel.com/documents/research/fravel.2005.IS.regime.insecurity.pdf); [official treaty](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/web/wjb_673085/zzjg_673183/bjhysws_674671/bhgjty/ldbjty/202303/P020230320538669617544.pdf).

Our analytical task is to explain the decision margin. A smaller land share may purchase a more valuable security relationship; it is not automatically evidence of weak bargaining. Conversely, cooperation after domestic trouble does not identify a causal exchange. To explain an offer, seek a dated assessment of the threat, a change in the value assigned to expected counterpart assistance, and an authorized concession. Whether the counterpart subsequently supplied that assistance is a further test of the realized exchange, not a prerequisite for explaining the offer. General doctrinal compatibility supplies a plausible mechanism, not the missing decision record.

The distinction between opening talks, offering a concession, signing a partial agreement, and implementing it is especially useful. A theory can explain willingness to offer without explaining why the other party accepted. A failed offer may be positive evidence about the first decision and negative evidence about a claim of predictable closure. We should preserve those separate outcomes throughout the Solomon update.

## A paired primary check: what changed between 1989 and 1991?

The **18 May 1989 Sino-Soviet communiqué** expressly sought a mutually acceptable means of resolving the eastern and western border sectors simultaneously. This appears in paragraph 7 of the Chinese original, *State Council Gazette* 1989 no. 9, printed p. 413, and in the contemporary English version, *Beijing Review* no. 22, p. 12. Both complete communiqué versions were read: Chinese printed pp. 412–415, visually; English pp. 11–13, with the central page checked visually. The Chinese text confirms that simultaneity is not an English translator’s embellishment. [Chinese original](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/shuju/1989/gwyb198909.pdf); [contemporary English publication](https://massline.org/PekingReview/PR1989/PR1989-22.pdf).

This gives the earlier public sequencing a documentary anchor. The subsequent eastern agreement, followed by later settlements examined in the [Russian border case](../../cases/russia-border-settlement.md), establishes that the sequence ultimately became separable. It does **not** identify the date of an internal Chinese decision to drop package linkage, the author of the proposal, or the concession that secured Soviet acceptance. Nor does an aspiration to simultaneous settlement necessarily establish a binding all-or-nothing condition on every earlier Chinese offer.

The inference should therefore be recorded in layers:

| Question | Present evidence | Remaining discriminating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Was simultaneous resolution publicly contemplated? | Yes: authenticated Chinese publication and contemporary English text. | Earlier negotiating instructions would show whether this was also a reservation point. |
| Did final settlement proceed in separate stages? | Yes: dated agreements establish the sequence. | Successive drafts would identify when the parties accepted separation. |
| Did domestic insecurity cause the change? | A plausible scholarly interpretation; the public sequence alone is insufficient. | A dated mandate or decision account weighing domestic pressure against alternatives. |
| Could preexisting convergence explain part of the movement? | Must be tested against negotiations preceding the crisis, as the border case does. | Contemporary offers and minutes, not only later recollections of progress. |
| Was partial settlement itself a diplomatic failure? | No classification follows from incompleteness alone. | Each side’s objectives, outside options, implementation costs and gains from settling the agreed sectors. |

Public agreements can also preserve differences openly. Paragraph 5 of the 1989 communiqué records different Chinese and Soviet approaches to Cambodia, while paragraph 9 says differences on some matters should not obstruct bilateral relations. That architecture provides a concrete test of partial accommodation: identify what is jointly accepted and what remains expressly attributed to one side. It does not establish agreement on every principle or a hidden common interpretation. [Gazette, pp. 413–414](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/shuju/1989/gwyb198909.pdf).

## Two published accounts of Deng’s historical argument

The Chinese selection, “结束过去，开辟未来,” dated **16 May 1989**, explicitly labels itself part of Deng’s conversation with Gorbachev. All five substantive pages, printed 291–295, were read in the official Theory China edition. The volume’s separate explanatory endnotes were not read. On p. 292 Deng allows the two sides to state their historical views without requiring an answer or debate; p. 294 recalls China’s consistent demands that Moscow acknowledge the unequal treaties and historical injury, yet accepts existing treaties as the basis for resolving the boundary. The no-debate invitation should therefore not be read as proof that recognition had never been demanded or was definitively abandoned. This is evidence that historical grievance and a workable negotiating basis could coexist in the same stated position. [Chinese selection, beginning p. 291](https://ebook.theorychina.org.cn/ebook/upload/storage/files/2022/07/26/b7469def27f222558b43b4f322630ac465633/files/basic-html/page307.html); [p. 294](https://ebook.theorychina.org.cn/ebook/upload/storage/files/2022/07/26/b7469def27f222558b43b4f322630ac465633/files/basic-html/page310.html).

The Wilson Center document is **a translated excerpt from Gorbachev’s 1995 memoir**, with other published versions identified on its cover. It is not an independently recovered contemporaneous verbatim meeting transcript. All five PDF pages, including provenance, were read in the university-hosted copy. It portrays Gorbachev disputing Deng’s historical account while accepting normalization and future cooperation. The extract does not contain a precise territorial exchange or a final island disposition. An archive portal’s hosting label should not erase the underlying document’s retrospective and excerpted character. [Memoir-derived account, translated by Sergey Radchenko](https://web.unica.it/static/resources/cms/documents/MeetingGorbachevandDengMAY1989.pdf).

These two accounts supply different editorial selections, not two recordings that establish every word spoken. They nevertheless make the research question more precise. When a negotiator recites historical grievances, we should check whether recognition, apology, territorial revision, or merely an opportunity to state a position is demanded in return. Those are different requests. The mere presence of a grievance narrative cannot tell us which is at stake.

## Consequences for the update

**Retain S-C01 and S-C18 as organizing propositions.** Political purpose, the value of a relationship and substantive interests help explain why the same repertoire can accompany different concessions. An update must specify what relationship preservation is worth in a given decision rather than use friendship as a universal explanation.

**Use S-P05 and S-P12 at the level of particular clauses.** Broad principles, explicit differences, procedural commitments and precise territorial provisions can coexist. An agreed framework is not evidence that operative terms are vague; an acknowledged reservation is not evidence of deception. The observed partial settlement tests one part of S-P12, leaving claims about avoiding conspicuous reversal to be demonstrated separately.

**Narrow S-C19’s diagnostic inference.** Domestic insecurity, elite disagreement, an authorized firm position and routine implementation difficulty are separate hypotheses. They require different observations. Solomon’s inference from rigidity toward leadership conflict should not be applied without evidence distinguishing these alternatives. Public rigidity does not reveal which institution resisted an offer, and domestic instability does not determine whether the external response will be confrontation or compromise.

**Specify what would falsify the preferred explanation.** For the 1989–1991 sequence, seek a pre-crisis authorization for separable settlement, a Soviet initiative accepted for a different documented reason, or evidence that the unresolved western question made the earlier package impractical regardless of domestic conditions. Such findings could weaken a crisis-specific explanation without undermining the broader proposition that leaders trade across interests.

The priority archive target is now the draft and authorization sequence around package separation, rather than another general statement about Chinese patience. Later scholarship and memoirs are valuable routes into that sequence; references discovered in them remain unread sources until separately acquired and examined.
