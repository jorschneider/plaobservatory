# Direct negotiating behavior: substantive reading record

Read on 8 September 2026. **This pass completes Wei–Summers’s article and the Faure–Zartman introduction only. Zhang’s complete chapter body was read, but its notes are incomplete.** The 2025 volume’s case chapters remain unread. The accompanying [evidence register](direct-negotiation-evidence.json) records versions, hashes, page coverage, book-claim mappings and unresolved source chains. Full texts and reading derivatives are stored outside git.

## What changes in the assessment

The strongest addition is a correction to the historical baseline. Recent research on the 1984 Hong Kong agreement helps distinguish Chinese bargaining positions from British interpretations of Chinese competence. It does **not** demonstrate a change after 1984 merely because the research was published in 2025–2026. It also supplies a useful comparison: British officials sought private American business intermediaries to influence Beijing. A tactic can be important in Chinese negotiations without being uniquely Chinese.

Zhang supplies a mid-2000s account of professionalization and constraints, useful as an intermediate hypothesis. The 2025 editors supply a contemporary research agenda. Neither warrants assuming that technical competence, delegation, public rhetoric and substantive willingness to compromise move together.

## Wei–Summers: correcting an original-period inference

Rong Wei and Tim Summers, “The Chinese Government’s Negotiating Strategy Over the Future of Hong Kong: Revisiting the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration,” *The International History Review* 48(1) (2026), 134–151, [DOI 10.1080/07075332.2025.2466463](https://doi.org/10.1080/07075332.2025.2466463). First published online 17 February 2025; the downloaded publisher version carries the 2026 issue assignment. It is licensed CC BY 4.0.

**Coverage:** introduction, sections I–IV, conclusion, all 138 notes, disclosure and contributor statements were read sequentially in the public publisher HTML. The complete 19-page publisher PDF was then recovered, extracted and read against that text: PDF 1 is the cover; PDF 2–19 correspond to printed 134–151. Consequential facsimiles checked: printed 136, 138, 142, 144 and 151. No unresolved substantive extraction problem was found; visual coverage is selective, not every PDF page.

The study reconstructs Chinese participants’ understanding, principally from Zhou Nan, Lu Ping, Li Hou, Wu Jiping and Xu Jiatun. British, Hong Kong and American materials supplement that reconstruction. It explicitly calls its findings provisional because relevant Chinese Foreign Ministry files remain unavailable and published recollections may have been scrutinized or amended (136, 139–140). Its interpretive approach emphasizes actors’ perceptions; it does not estimate how much a national cultural trait changes concessions across comparable negotiations.

The central challenge is to the claim that resistance to continued British administration revealed ignorance of capitalism. The authors reconstruct prior research into Hong Kong’s financial arrangements and engagement with local business elites (144–147). For this project, the useful distinction is **understanding an opponent’s argument versus accepting its preferred institutional solution**. Evidence of financial knowledge weakens a competence-based interpretation of refusal; it does not establish that every Chinese assessment was accurate. Nor does knowledge of market institutions establish subsequent compliance with an agreement.

This refines S-C08 and F05 on preparation, and strengthens the need to separate S-P05/F01 principles from implementation choices. The sovereignty commitment and compromise over subordinate arrangements coexist in the reconstruction (139, 145). We should code each requested concession and its relationship to the agreed objective, rather than score the entire negotiation as either culturally rigid or pragmatic. The article’s account also makes S-C18—interests and circumstances matter more than isolated techniques—particularly useful.

Zhou Nan’s procedural skill appears before Solomon’s cutoff: Cradock’s account describes authoritative telephone answers and faster agenda negotiations in early summer 1983 (144, note 104). This is consistent with Freeman’s F13/F14 differentiation and learning, while also fitting Solomon’s own recognition of mixed influences. It argues against writing an exaggeratedly uniform early baseline merely to make later change look larger. **Source independence matters:** note 104 says Zhou’s memoir quotes Cradock. These are not two independent observations of the same learning mechanism.

The intermediary episode is especially valuable for comparison. On 23 November 1982, a U.S. consular cable recorded Governor Youde’s request for help from prominent private Americans. Washington’s 1 December reply endorsed selective, discreet briefings and mentioned possible Japanese business messengers (138; notes 41–46). This supports a counterparty check for S-C05/S-P08: identify who recruits intermediaries, their actual access, the message and any observable response. It does not prove that either side’s campaign succeeded. The underlying Reagan Library cables have **not** been independently read in this pass; their document identifiers are recorded as leads.

Two internal tensions should remain visible. First, the article recounts Chinese suspicion that Britain manufactured the September 1983 currency crisis, alongside British recollections of harmful Chinese rhetoric and urgent financial stabilization (142). Evidence that London feared a collapse corroborates vulnerability, not deliberate British orchestration. Second, Chinese firmness is paired with a clear leadership mandate (142–143). This is a plausible counterexample to treating rigidity as evidence of leadership conflict under S-C19. Yet stronger local leverage and the approaching lease expiry offer explanations beyond superior command arrangements. We need competing predictions, not a new single-cause account.

The immediate source chain is unusually concrete. Chinese financial knowledge rests heavily on Li Hou’s *回归的历程*, 70–74 and 82–85, and Lu Ping’s *鲁平口述香港回归*, 14–21. Zhou’s *周南口述：身在急风骤雨中*, 260–262, should be inspected together with Cradock’s *Experiences of China*, 186. The article’s notes give a [Rothschild memorandum of 1 December 1983](https://www.margaretthatcher.org/document/139412) and a [No. 10 currency briefing of 12 September 1982](https://www.margaretthatcher.org/document/138553); both remain leads rather than independently verified primary evidence here. This is a manageable route for a later case dossier, without inflating the number of independent sources already read.

## Zhang: a mid-2000s hypothesis with incomplete notes

Zhang Qingmin, “Continuities and Changes in China’s Negotiating Behavior,” in Pauline Kerr, Stuart Harris and Qin Yaqing, eds., *China’s ‘New’ Diplomacy: Tactical or Fundamental Change?* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2008), 153–175, [DOI 10.1007/978-0-230-61692-9_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-230-61692-9_8).

**Coverage limitation:** the [Freie Universität Berlin teaching packet](https://www.jura.fu-berlin.de/fachbereich/einrichtungen/oeffentliches-recht/lehrende/bolewskiw/dokumente/7__Culture_Language/Kerr_China_New_Diplomacy_One.pdf) contains the entire body, printed 153–171, but stops during note 5. Printed 172–175 are absent. All ten relevant two-page scans, PDF 18–27, were read sequentially after rotation and OCR, then visually checked throughout. Underlining and column order damaged OCR; visual reading resolved body text. Notes 1–4 are complete, note 5 is partial. Publisher references do not restore the missing notes. **This is not a complete chapter reading.**

Zhang describes organizational expansion and professional learning alongside continuing reliance on principles, relationships and senior leaders (154–168). Public opinion and interagency coordination complicate bargaining (158–160, 168–171). The design is an illustrative historical essay using official material, media and secondary works, with no explicit systematic sampling protocol or controlled comparison.

The contribution is to distinguish dimensions of change. F09/F10 and S-C11/S-C12 already anticipate complexity within the state; Zhang should extend those hypotheses, not be credited with discovering them. A quotation from Kreisberg’s 1994 analysis in the later discussion of coordination (168–169) is inherited evidence, not an additional observation of a new millennium mechanism. Public congratulations to leaders after WTO accession negotiations (165) do not by themselves reconstruct who could authorize which concession.

The update should therefore compare technical capability, actual delegated authority and substantive objectives independently. Zhang’s integration-to-convergence expectation (171) is a proposition requiring later tests. A more confrontational public posture would not automatically falsify improved technical skill; improved skill would not establish greater flexibility. Missing notes reduce confidence in tracing several consequential examples, so this pass records them as source-chain leads rather than converting them into independently corroborated events.

## Faure–Zartman: a complete introduction, not a completed 2025 synthesis

Guy Olivier Faure and I. William Zartman, “Introduction,” in their edited *China’s Negotiating Mindset and Strategies: Historical and Cultural Foundations* (Routledge, 2025), 1–7, [DOI 10.4324/9781003582113-1](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003582113-1). The lawful [publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781040388969_A50837262/preview-9781040388969_A50837262.pdf) contains all seven pages. They were read sequentially; printed 1, 4, 5 and 7 were visually checked. The volume as a whole is unread.

The editors define their focus on underlying representations and describe variation across issues and counterparts (1–7). Their summaries of Celico’s U.S. trade chapter and Hu’s European chapter appear on 4–5. **These summaries are not readings of either chapter’s evidence.** The preview ends far before Celico, whose chapter starts at 152; a bounded lawful-access search did not locate her full text.

The analytic caution is that an expansive, partly unobservable definition of mindset can absorb both flexibility and rigidity after the event. To test S-C02 or S-P05, specify an observable mechanism and a rival explanation before reading an outcome. Separate editorial framing from each contributor’s methods and evidence. The introduction helps frame tests; it does not independently resolve whether China’s behavior changed after the 1990s.

No complete Faure 1998 or Liu 1992 text was obtained through the attempted lawful routes. They are not counted as substantive readings. The complete Wei–Summers article supplies this pass’s empirical comparison, while further acquisition is deferred.
