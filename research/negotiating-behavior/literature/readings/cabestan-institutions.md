# Cabestan on institutions: a full-text reading and selective primary-source audit

Research date: 10 September 2026. Jean-Pierre Cabestan, “China’s foreign and security policy institutions and decision-making under Xi Jinping,” *British Journal of Politics and International Relations* 23(2), 2021, pp. 319–336, DOI 10.1177/1369148120974881. Online publication was in December 2020; the ResearchGate author upload is dated 7 December 2021. These are not three different studies. Complete author-uploaded article text, including the entire bibliography, was read; there are no separate numbered endnotes. The HTML preserves printed page markers. PDF download was unavailable; this is not a claim of PDF visual inspection. [Author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347816666_China%27s_foreign_and_security_policy_institutions_and_decision-making_under_Xi_Jinping).

Cabestan’s central contribution is that concentration of authority can coexist with bureaucratic fragmentation. His institutional map distinguishes Party commissions, their offices, ministries, military bodies, and other participants. He repeatedly qualifies inferred membership, meeting practices, and effective office power. The article’s stronger concluding claims about speed and coordination do not rest on a comparative dataset of decisions. Its discussion of the May 2019 trade dispute retains uncertainty about the deciding body and individual votes. It therefore cannot independently establish a factional veto of a known bargain. [Cabestan, pp. 320, 323–327, 331–333](https://doi.org/10.1177/1369148120974881).

The right use is as a set of institutional hypotheses and leads, with confidence assigned to each underlying observation. A formal organizational chart establishes neither who drafted an offer nor who could block it. Public attendance establishes presence, not the relative influence of attendees. An office’s proximity to the leader makes it a plausible information filter; demonstrating its influence requires a traceable transmission, revision, or authorization. Likewise, a claim that coordination improved requires a comparison: the same task before and after reform, or comparable tasks with different coordination arrangements. Creating a commission is an intervention to be explained, not automatically a successful treatment.

## Dates and responsibilities checked against originals

The NSC chronology needs correction and disaggregation. A contemporaneous Xinhua item published by People’s Daily on **12 November 2013** reports the plenum’s decision to establish it. The **24 January 2014** Politburo account announces its leadership and institutional position, including responsibility to the Politburo and its Standing Committee. The first meeting occurred on **15 April 2014**. “Established in October 2013,” as printed on Cabestan’s p. 322, should not be copied into a timeline. [November decision excerpt](https://cpc.people.com.cn/n/2013/1112/c368480-23518979.html), [January organizational decision](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/2014-01/24/c_119122483.htm), [first-meeting account](https://www.hunan.gov.cn/topic/zgmhnm/zyjs49752/zyjc/201404/t20140416_1068997.html).

For the cybersecurity leading group, the contemporary record dates its first meeting to **27 February 2014** and reports approval of group rules, office rules, and the year’s work program. A companion announcement describes its establishment. The article’s December 2013 date requires independent evidence before use; the verified public organizational milestone is February 2014. Internal preparation and public establishment should not be collapsed. [First meeting](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2014-02/27/c_133148354.htm), [establishment report](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2014-02/28/c_126397488.htm).

The **21 March 2018** reform plan provides firmer evidence for the four leading-group conversions to commissions and their offices. Section IV assigns strategic design, coordination and implementation oversight. Section XVII transfers the maritime-rights group’s responsibilities to the Foreign Affairs Commission and its office. Section XXXVIII establishes CIDCA but explicitly leaves concrete aid execution with relevant departments. Thus, formal centralization and distributed execution are visible together in the original design. Whether that distribution creates productive specialization or obstructive rivalry must be established separately. [Reform plan, selected sections IV, XVII, XXXVIII](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2018-03/21/c_1122571024.htm).

The first Foreign Affairs Commission meeting, **15 May 2018**, publicly identifies some senior participants, approves working rules, and emphasizes coordination and implementation oversight. Its language is evidence of organizational responsibilities and leadership intention. It does not supply the unpublished rules or all membership, establish an ordinary meeting frequency, or measure policy effectiveness. [Original meeting account](http://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/leaders/2018-05/15/c_1122836914.htm).

## A quantitative claim that should remain an attributed expectation

Tracing the NSC’s supposed 60/40 domestic/external allocation leads to David Finkelstein’s answer in a Brookings transcript, printed p. 22. He reports what he has been told the commission **will** do while emphasizing unknown membership and procedures. No observed time-use series is supplied. The numerical division should not become a measured fact about the organization, still less a 2026 description. The source’s status is stronger for what Finkelstein publicly said than for the underlying internal practice. [Original conference transcript, p. 22](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/20150227_china_japan_transcript.pdf).

The event date also illustrates a source-chain trap. The transcript cover says February 2014; subsequent headers say February 2015, and the organizer’s event page confirms **27 February 2015**. The journal bibliography follows the earlier year. Preserve the discrepancy and use the event organizer’s dated record; the PDF filename alone would not be enough to decide. [Brookings event record](https://www.brookings.edu/events/chinas-security-and-foreign-policies-comparing-american-and-japanese-perspectives/).

## Consequences for the Solomon update

For S-C09–S-C12 and the related coordination propositions, code four separate variables: formal jurisdiction, control over information, authorization to offer a concession, and ability to implement it. A stronger Party coordinating structure can increase supervision without eliminating specialized agencies’ expertise or local execution. This is a testable alternative to both a unitary-state account and an assumption that every delay reflects factional conflict.

For S-C19, continue to reject rigidity as a sufficient diagnostic of leadership conflict. Rival explanations include a firm authorized position, different operational assessments, withheld information, implementation difficulty, or a changed valuation of the bargain. An institutional analysis should help identify these mechanisms, rather than turn unknown internal deliberations into a general explanation for every disappointing negotiation.

For S-P24, obtain dated drafts and negotiating mandates. Senior contact is an observable event; authorization is a particular decision. The India case shows why this separation matters: recurring high-level contact in 2023 did not establish immediate closure, and Kazan in 2024 publicly endorsed an already-announced arrangement. Neither observation rules out an earlier, unobserved senior authorization.

The highest-value continuation is therefore decision-specific: locate a proposed concession, identify the institution asked to approve it, trace objections and revisions, and check whether implementation required another agency’s cooperation. Ask the same questions in a trade case and a military case.
