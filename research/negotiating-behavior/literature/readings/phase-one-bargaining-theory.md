# Testing the information explanation against the documents

**15 September 2026.** Cathy Xuauxuan Wu, “A Bargaining Theory of US–China Economic Rivalry: Differentiating the Trade and Technology Wars,” *The Chinese Journal of International Politics* 17(4), 2024, 323–345, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/cjip/poae017). The publisher displays the given name as *Xuauxuan*; this citation preserves that spelling. The complete publisher HTML, including all 106 notes, was read. The university PDF could not be retrieved. Exact provenance is in the [source register](../../sources/phase-one-bargaining-theory-register.json).

Wu distinguishes uncertainty about resolve from difficulty making durable commitments. Her account attributes early tariff escalation mainly to the former and technology restrictions mainly to the latter, while allowing both mechanisms to coexist. This supplies a useful alternative to explanations based primarily on national negotiating style. The empirical narrative draws on published material rather than a new set of interviews or private instructions. [Full article](https://oup.silverchair-cdn.com/article-minimal/7718347).

Three checks constrain its use. First, the May 19, 2018 joint statement contains no tariff-pause clause; the pause was publicly described separately. Second, the May 2019 narrative relies on Davis and Wei, especially their book’s pages 14–24, and the Reuters exclusive already in this project. The book passages remain unread here. Third, the no-new-tariffs description of Biden policy needs a dated endpoint: USTR issued a proposal for increases in May 2024 and announced final modifications in September. These checks concern the “Information Problem and the Outbreak of the Trade War” and “Trade Truce with Old Tariffs in Place” sections, particularly notes 49–51. [Joint statement](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefings-statements/joint-statement-united-states-china-regarding-trade-consultations/); [May proposal announcement](https://www.ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/may/ustr-issues-federal-register-notice-section-301-proposed-tariff-modifications-and-machinery); [September final-action announcement](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2024/september/ustr-finalizes-action-china-tariffs-following-statutory-four-year-review).

The resulting research test is our own. Costs followed by settlement do not distinguish learning about an unchanged limit from a limit changed by those costs. Nor do they distinguish either mechanism from agreement on a smaller set of issues. A stronger test would identify each government's earlier expectation, the observation that changed it, and a subsequent instruction or offer consistent with the revised belief. A public official's retrospective statement can contribute, but should be compared with contemporary instructions and alternative reasons for the same decision.

The retained tariffs present a similar identification problem. They could preserve bargaining pressure for performance, protect domestic producers, insure against future competition, or serve several purposes. Their persistence alone does not establish which explanation predominates. Targeted strategic-sector increases also overlap the article's trade/technology distinction: their existence corrects a broad descriptive claim without automatically disproving every conditional prediction of the theory.

For the manuscript, use the information mechanism as a competing explanation, not additional testimony to Xi's private decision or proof that escalation was necessary. Keep the report-to-report source chain visible. Phase One Article 1.34 still requires full implementation and includes legislative proposals where necessary; domestic choice of methods cannot support a blanket conclusion that legal change disappeared from the bargain. [Agreement, Articles 1.34–1.35](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf).

This reading is a new scholarly source, not an independent observation of the spring draft. The two 2024 press releases were read completely; their linked review, tariff schedules and implementation record were not read in this check. No actual importer-level duties or effects are inferred from the announcements.
