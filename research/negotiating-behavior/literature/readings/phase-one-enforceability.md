# Critical reading: Phase One enforceability in a 2026 article

**Read 8 September 2026.** George Yijun Tian and Carrie Shu Shang, “A co-regulatory approach to bilateral trade: assessing and enhancing enforceability of U.S.–China Phase One Agreement in the Trump 2.0 era,” *Journal of International Economic Law* 29(2):264–291, DOI [10.1093/jiel/jgag019](https://academic.oup.com/jiel/article/29/2/264/8726689). The issue is dated June 2026; the publisher records July 8 online publication. The complete publisher HTML, all 147 notes and four figures were read. Section headings and note numbers are the locators; journal pagination was not independently mapped from a PDF. See the [evidence register](phase-one-enforceability-evidence.json).

This is useful as a framework for questions about participation and implementation incentives. It is not a documented account of the negotiating room: no original interviews, internal instructions or successive draft texts are established by its method. Its policy proposals must remain distinct from observed behavior and existing agreement requirements.

## What it adds to the research

The authors ask how participation and perceived legitimacy affect compliance, and propose arrangements involving government and non-government actors. Their Figure 1 presents a stylized contrast between American and Chinese participation. This suggests a testable research question: which firms supplied information before commitments, through which channels, and what influence did they have over targets and implementation? It does not answer that question through the diagram itself. [Article, “Legitimacy challenges of POA in China” and Figure 1](https://academic.oup.com/jiel/article/29/2/264/8726689).

For the Solomon update, the productive move is to identify the organizations that had information, authority and implementation responsibilities at each stage. Publication of a final text is different from consultation over a proposal. Conversely, a lack of reported public hearings does not prove absence of private consultation. We should seek contemporaneous submissions, delegation briefings and procurement instructions before explaining performance through exclusion of Chinese firms. The same standard applies to the American side: participation in tariff hearings does not establish meaningful influence over every part of the final bargain.

## Four checks that change how we can use the article

**First, the purchase baseline needs correction.** In “Data on POA enforcement,” the discussion compares Chinese purchases with a $200 billion commitment in a way that risks confusing the increment with the total. Article 6.2.1 requires the increment **above** corresponding 2017 baselines over 2020–2021. This is not an interpretive difference between customs series. Any performance ratio needs the covered goods and services, baseline, increment and observed purchases stated together. The [agreement audit](../../sources/phase-one-agreement-audit.md) and [implementation memo](../../cases/phase-one-implementation-2020-2026.md) use those distinctions rather than reproducing the article's formulation.

**Second, legal chronology constrains causal claims.** The same section places the Foreign Investment Law's effectiveness after the agreement was sealed. Note 58 and a later section instead supply the correct earlier date. The official law confirms March 15, 2019 adoption and January 1, 2020 effectiveness, before January 15 signature. Earlier negotiations might have influenced the law; the completed agreement cannot simply be treated as the cause of an already effective measure. [Official law, introductory dates and Article 22](https://en.ndrc.gov.cn/policies/202105/t20210527_1281403.html).

**Third, legal outcomes and bargaining success are different.** The authors acknowledge that DS542 did not reach a panel decision, then infer American success from reform. The WTO chronology corroborates suspension and lapse, not a merits victory. DS543 concerned specified US tariffs, not Phase One's validity; its findings do not establish that the agreement became inoperative. The subsequent US appeal is a separate procedural qualification. Those propositions require evidence about decisions and the relationship between proceedings. The [WTO DS542 history](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds542_e.htm) and [DS543 history](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds543_e.htm) were read as Secretariat summaries, not substitutes for full reports.

**Fourth, the figures have old observation windows.** Figure 2 ends in February 2020. Figures 3 and 4 use data through August 2020; Figure 3's note expressly calls its product-level and monthly targets illustrative. These cannot establish a legally binding monthly semiconductor target or performance through 2026. Their reuse is not new independent evidence. The figures are useful leads to underlying definitions, which must be checked before combining them with later series.

## What remains a hypothesis

A tally of asymmetric obligations does not measure economic sacrifice or prove how pressure shaped the draft. A move toward legal prohibitions does not establish enforcement in administrative practice. Proposed burden-shifting for technology-transfer disputes is not an existing general rule under Chapter 2; it must not be confused with the agreement's actual trade-secret provisions. These distinctions matter when using normative legal scholarship to reconstruct behavior.

The article directs attention to incentive compatibility and stakeholder information. It does not discriminate among pandemic disruption, an unrealistic baseline, procurement incentives, export restrictions, administrative implementation failures and deliberate nonperformance as causes of the observed shortfall. Those mechanisms require separate measures and process evidence. Nor should dated projections cited in a 2026 publication become observations about what actually happened by 2026.

The useful conclusion for this project is therefore bounded: participation and implementation incentives belong in the case design, but this article cannot supply a verified causal verdict on Phase One. The independent agreement reading and bilateral implementation record now provide a firmer base for that inquiry. Exact reading scope, figure dates and checks are retained in the register; the complete downloaded article remains outside Git.
