## How this changes the research design

| Question | Explanations to distinguish | Reading pair or group | Evidence to recover afterward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is there a persistent national negotiating style? | Cultural repertoire; issue-specific incentives; counterpart power; variation among agencies and individuals | Solomon/Freeman; Zhang Qingmin; Liu; Faure–Zartman | Matched moves across counterparts and periods; negative cases; independent accounts of the same exchange |
| Does “I need instructions” signal a real constraint? | Delegated authority limits; interagency conflict; tactical delay; discretion within central goals | Pearson; Cabestan; Zhao; Jakobson–Manuel; Taylor–Garlick | Requests for authorization, delegation mandates, draft circulation and approval timing |
| Have relationships and general principles become less central? | Professionalization and legal learning; changing institutions; continued relational practices with new instruments | Shaffer–Gao; Gao; Hopewell; the 2025 trade chapters | Legal-team formation, draft language, forum choice, dispute records and negotiators’ working documents |
| Does tough public language constrain agreement? | Domestic approval incentives; deliberate ambiguity; actor/audience differences; translation | Weiss–Dafoe; Mokry; Solomon’s public-sentiment proposition | Paired Chinese/English texts, private demands, audience timing and actual room for concessions |
| Why do talks stall or fail? | An objective of nonsettlement; fear of signaling weakness; domestic costs; incompatible offers | Garver; Mastro; Fravel | Rejected proposals, decisions about entering talks, instructions and evidence against inferred intent |
| What actually changed, and when? | Behavior; resources; counterpart policy; reporting visibility; analytical labels | Johnston; Chubb; the period-specific case literature | Consistent move definitions, stable source coverage, pre-change comparisons and cases of continuity |

## Full-reading and citation-chain protocol

For each priority work, produce a short structured memo after reading the entire relevant article or chapter and notes. Record the precise argument; actual period and cases; interview dates and participant roles; archive collections or document identifiers; source languages; strongest process evidence; rival explanations tested; contrary evidence; and which Solomon/Freeman proposition it addresses. Keep the author’s inference separate from what the cited record establishes.

Then follow citations **backward** to original records and earlier scholarly disagreements, and **forward** to substantive critiques, later replications and updated cases. Search by exact title and DOI as well as topic. A later chapter version, a review quoting the original article, and repeated use of the same participant interview do not supply independent confirmation.

Every proposed change in our assessment must identify the evidence that would discriminate between explanations. A broad institutional article supplies context; it cannot establish who authorized a specific offer. A business-negotiation study may generate a hypothesis, but its results cannot be transferred automatically to state diplomacy. A 2026 publication may analyze only earlier negotiations; record empirical cutoff separately from publication year.
