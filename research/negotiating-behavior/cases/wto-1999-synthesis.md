# First synthesis: what the WTO pilot can and cannot tell us

Status: 5 September 2026, preliminary. Read with the [Chinese reconstruction](wto-1999-chinese-reconstruction.md), [American reconstruction](wto-1999-us-reconstruction.md), [dated US proposals](../sources/wto-us-proposals-in-chinese-archive.md), and [Deng Hongguo account](../sources/wto-financial-participant-note.md). This is not a conclusion about Chinese behavior through 2026.

The records make **document status and negotiating authority essential distinctions that a simple firmness-versus-flexibility narrative misses**. The April 10 MOU acknowledges substantial agreement while naming reservations. November's Chinese and American proposals specify detailed, conditional packages. Later recollections compress these distinctions and disagree about the causes of closure. We can now ask questions that particular missing documents could answer.

## Four findings with particularly strong anchors

**An April agreement was partial and qualified.** The signed MOU's reproduced body states both agreement and remaining work. The unacquired attachments are the main barrier to a precise April–November comparison. Calling April either a complete deal or no agreement loses evidence already in hand. B3 p.191/PDF198, [official facsimile](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/dl/sms/sbj_3_17.pdf).

**Ownership is not control.** The Chinese November 12 proposal already offers 50% life-insurance equity. The American November 13 proposal separately seeks management control and sector-specific equity arrangements. A single last-minute percentage anecdote cannot stand in for the negotiation. B3 pp.1078–1089, same facsimile; the two [document notes](wto-1999-chinese-reconstruction.md) and [US offer comparison](../sources/wto-us-proposals-in-chinese-archive.md) give the exact entries.

**The Americans also used deadlines and leadership appeals.** McCahill's November 12 and 14 letters provide contemporaneous evidence of those moves. Their causal success remains disputed. The relevant comparison is reciprocal behavior and its effects, not assuming that the Chinese side alone organized pressure. B3 pp.1067–1070,1094.

**Retrospective accounts require contemporaneous controls.** Barshefsky's April 8 public briefing defends unresolved issues and rejects a business-mobilization explanation of publication. Her later interview and Summers's account allocate responsibility differently. The briefing identifies an NEC–NSC memorandum recording advisers' views: an unusually precise archival target. [April 8 briefing, preserved USIA mirror](https://usinfo.org/usia/usinfo.state.gov/regional/ea/uschina/whwtobrf.htm); [American source audit](../sources/us-wto-archive-audit.md).

## What is not established

We have not determined the decisive cause of the November agreement; recovered each government's private instructions; compared every April and November clause; verified all implementation claims; conducted new interviews; or systematically read contemporary reporting. We have not tested a representative sample of negotiations with other major powers or completed the post-1999 period.

The next output should be the four-issue document comparison specified in the [reporting brief](../reporting/wto-1999-next-requests.md), followed by an EU accession comparison and the 2018–2026 US trade track. That sequence can distinguish a genuine change from a behavior Solomon already observed, a counterpart-specific practice, or an effect of the accession setting.
