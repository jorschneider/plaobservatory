# From an ownership promise to an operating business: JPMorgan Securities (China), 2017–2022

Research date: **10 September 2026**. This is a historical implementation case for the Solomon reassessment, with a January 2023 fund-management comparison. It does not establish the firm's position or the state of financial opening in 2026. The [source register](../sources/financial-access-register.json) records provenance, reading scope and preserved copies; the [move ledger](financial-access-moves.json) separates announcements, applications, administrative decisions, registration and operation.

**Assessment.** China implemented a commercially meaningful opening in this case. The evidence also identifies a specific contribution of Phase One: securities ownership liberalization moved eight months ahead of the CSRC's October 2019 timetable. It does not establish that American pressure originated the reform, that Phase One caused JPMorgan's initial operating license, or that political leaders personally secured the firm's later ownership filing. The explanatory advance over an account organized around summit promises is the chain connecting international negotiation to domestic regulation, shareholder transactions and operational preparation.

This is deliberately a successful case. It can disprove a categorical assertion that negotiated access produced no usable business, but cannot establish general Chinese compliance or explain failures in other sectors. Much of the observable action is administrative implementation and commercial negotiation; those categories should remain distinct from government-to-government bargaining.

## The baseline was already moving before Phase One

On 10 November 2017, Vice Finance Minister Zhu Guangyao announced a prospective 51% foreign ownership ceiling for securities, fund-management and futures companies, followed by removal three years after implementation. He spoke at a briefing on the economic results of the Trump–Xi meeting. Xinhua's report also records his explanation that the opening would apply to other countries, alongside Chinese requests concerning US treatment of Chinese investment, export controls and CICC's American business. This was a domestic policy offered in a diplomatic setting, rather than an American-exclusive privilege. [FIN-01: Xinhua's full report](https://www.xinhuanet.com/world/2017-11/10/c_1121938058.htm).

Contemporary Reuters reporting situated the announcement in longstanding foreign complaints about restricted access. That establishes an international demand, not the relative influence of American officials, European institutions, domestic reformers and financial firms on the decision. The important chronological distinction is **before the 2018 tariff war**, not **before bilateral pressure or negotiation**. [FIN-02: Reuters, 10 November 2017, syndicated by Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1191146/world).

The CSRC implemented new foreign-invested securities-company rules on 28 April 2018. Its announcement cited the Nineteenth Party Congress and State Council documents issued in 2017, permitted foreign control and widened prospective business scope. It also described a consultation beginning on 9 March: eleven organizations and five individuals submitted 52 comments. The organizations included financial institutions, industry associations, government departments and a law firm. This is evidence of a regulatory input channel; the announcement does not identify every contributor or show whose preferred language prevailed. [FIN-03: CSRC rule announcement](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1001309/content.shtml).

Two subsequent revisions matter. In July 2019 the CSRC explained the acceleration of complete ownership liberalization from 2021 to 2020 in terms of financial-sector development and competition. On 11 October it specified **1 December 2020 for securities**, while fund management would open on 1 April. Phase One then committed China to eliminate the relevant ownership limits no later than **1 April 2020**. [FIN-10: July explanation](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1000951/content.shtml); [FIN-11: October timetable](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1000907/content.shtml); [FIN-12: agreement, Article 4.7](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf).

The documentary connection is unusually explicit. On 13 March 2020 the CSRC announced the April securities date to “落实中美第一阶段经贸协议要求”—implement the requirements of the US–China Phase One agreement. This is stronger than an analyst inferring causation from temporal proximity. The agreement altered the publicly announced schedule. It does not tell us who proposed the April date, what was exchanged for it, or whether China would otherwise have accelerated again. [FIN-15: original CSRC notice](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1000819/content.shtml).

The PBOC's January explanation characterized the financial commitments as principally previously announced autonomous reforms, with reciprocal American undertakings. That account and the narrower acceleration can coexist. This pass read the complete text reproduced by *Beijing News*, not an accessible original PBOC page; its attribution is retained accordingly. [FIN-14: attributed PBOC explanation, 16 January 2020](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/157914791515208.html).

## Establishment approval was a conditional intermediate decision

Reuters reported on 10 May 2018 that the regulator had received JPMorgan's application for a majority-owned venture. On 14 May it reported the group's own statement seeking 51% and eventual full ownership when permitted. These are receipt and corporate-intention records, not evidence that a complete file had already passed administrative acceptance. An October *Beijing Business Daily* report, citing the CSRC docket, dated formal acceptance to 12 October; the underlying docket has not been recovered. [FIN-04: Reuters receipt report](https://www.brecorder.com/news/417205/china-receives-jpmorgan-application-to-set-up-jv-brokerage); [FIN-05: Reuters corporate-statement report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/jpmorgan-submits-application-for-majorityowned-china-securities-business-1444971); [FIN-06: contemporary docket reporting](https://biz.tom.com/syzx/201810/1155092624.html).

The original CSRC establishment decision, **证监许可〔2019〕513号**, is signed **13 March 2019**. The company subsequently reported receiving it on **29 March**. Those dates describe different events and should not be silently reconciled into one approval date. The decision names both the initial 2018 application and a corrective submission. Their contents and submission dates remain unavailable; one can establish an amended file, but cannot characterize the regulator's request as obstruction, bargaining over policy, or a merely clerical correction. [FIN-07: original decision](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c101841/c1022649/content.shtml); [FIN-26: company history, printed pp2–3](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report.pdf).

The authorized company had RMB800 million capital, a 51% JPMorgan shareholder and five Chinese shareholders. Its approved scope covered brokerage, investment consulting, proprietary trading, and underwriting/sponsorship. Crucially, the decision imposed further steps: obtain the foreign-investment approval certificate, organize and capitalize the company, register it within six months, and then apply for a securities/futures operating permit. It expressly prohibited business under the company's name before that permit. An establishment decision therefore neither delivered a functioning brokerage nor exhausted the regulator's authority. [FIN-07](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c101841/c1022649/content.shtml).

The later consolidated rules illustrate the importance of administrative clocks. They require an operating-permit decision within fifteen working days **after receipt of compliant application documents**, permit supplemental explanations, and prohibit securities business before licensing. This is the version revised on 20 March 2020, not an unmodified reproduction of the 2018 rules. Its wording cannot supply missing dates for JPMorgan's 2019 compliant submission. Counting from the first press report would create a false finding about deadline observance. [FIN-16: Articles 8–13 and 22, consolidated rules](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c106256/c1653966/content.shtml).

## Licensing, launching and doing business were different events

The company registered in Shanghai on **22 August 2019**. Its operating permit followed on **18 December 2019**, before Phase One was signed. The company's December release has a migrated website header dated April 2020; the internal dateline and annual disclosures establish the December event. Attributing this permit to implementation of the January agreement reverses the chronology. Earlier negotiations could still have affected the broader opening, but that requires a different argument. [FIN-19: 2020 disclosure, printed pp2–3](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/2020.pdf); [FIN-09: permit announcement](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/CN/zh/news-announcements/jpm-securities-china-company-limited-permitted-to-open-for-business).

The group announced commencement on **20 March 2020**. A *Yicai* interview with China CEO Mark Leung, conducted on 23 March and published on 25 March, independently documents the virtual opening ceremony. Neither source dates the first transaction of every business line. [FIN-17: company launch announcement](https://www.jpmorganchina.com.cn/en/about-us/2020/jpmorgan-securities-china-company-limited-opens-for-business); [FIN-18: Zhou Ailin's reporting](https://www.yicai.com/news/100565060.html).

Indeed, the 2019 disclosure describes a preparatory business. Its brokerage section says staff and systems had been built, registration and regulatory inspection completed, and formal operations were expected around mid-2020 after connectivity and testing with exchanges, clearing and custody institutions. This is a forecast, not a recovered first-trade date. The 2020 disclosure subsequently reports business development and two completed investment-banking projects. These records expose work hidden by the word “launch.” [FIN-08: 2019 disclosure, printed pp5–7](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/2019%E5%B9%B4%E5%B9%B4%E5%BA%A6%E6%8A%A5%E5%91%8A%E5%85%AC%E5%BC%80%E4%BF%A1%E6%81%AF%E6%8A%AB%E9%9C%B2-20200429.pdf); [FIN-19: 2020 disclosure, printed pp5–6](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/2020.pdf).

Leung's interview also identifies the firm's next preference: being able to operate China activities as an integrated group, rather than as fragmented entities, and extending capabilities as regulation allowed. This is observable commercial advocacy after initial entry. It is not evidence that the Chinese government demanded a new American concession, or that JPMorgan invoked Phase One's dispute procedure. [FIN-18](https://www.yicai.com/news/100565060.html).

## The supposed 71% stage illustrates why ownership needs its own audit

In November 2020, *Securities Times* reporter Yan Jingying reported an October 23 transaction at the Shanghai United Assets and Equity Exchange: Waigaoqiao's 20% stake went to JPMorgan for RMB178 million. The report said JPMorgan's stake **would rise** to 71%. Later reporting sometimes treated 71% as already achieved, including a June 2021 *Securities Times/券商中国* account. [FIN-20: original report as syndicated by Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2020-11-02/doc-iiznezxr9350006.shtml); [FIN-23: June account](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/quanshang/qsyj/2021-06-04/doc-ikqcfnaz9004739.shtml).

The stronger company records do not support coding an accomplished 2020 registered-ownership change. JPMorgan Securities' 2020 shareholder table still lists **51% JPMorgan and 20% Waigaoqiao**. Waigaoqiao's own 2020 accounts retain the investment, while its 2021 accounts record its disposal. These are separate corporate reporting chains, not multiple newspapers repeating one auction notice. They support distinguishing a transaction agreement from its completed ownership implementation. The signed transfer contract and closing conditions remain missing, so this finding does not determine the precise date every economic right changed hands. [FIN-19: shareholder table, printed p2](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/2020.pdf); [FIN-21: seller's 2020 report, p217](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202105011488837074_1.pdf); [FIN-22: seller's 2021 report, long-term equity investment notes](https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=7943257&stockid=600648).

On **6 August 2021**, JPMorgan announced the CSRC had accepted the filing for its shareholder to acquire the holdings of **all five** Chinese partners. Its Chinese release uses **准予备案**, while the English headline uses “approval.” Neither should be confused with a newly issued initial operating license. The company disclosed completion of shareholder-change registration on **25 August**, together with recapitalization; the registered entity became wholly foreign-owned. The original CSRC filing response and the full transaction application were not located. [FIN-24: English announcement](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/CN/en/news-announcements/jpmorgan-receives-approval-from-the-china-securities-regulatory-commission); [FIN-25: Chinese announcement](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/CN/zh/news-announcements/jpmorgan-receives-approval-from-the-china-securities-regulatory-commission); [FIN-26: 2021 disclosure, printed p3](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report.pdf).

Even the start of that final administrative process requires care. The June 2021 report repeated foreign reporting that JPMorgan had applied, but recorded the company's response that it had no related disclosure. June 4 is therefore the publication date of a contested application report, not a confirmed filing date. Without the complete application history, the interval since April 2020 cannot be converted into a measured regulatory delay. [FIN-23](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/quanshang/qsyj/2021-06-04/doc-ikqcfnaz9004739.shtml).

## Commercial implementation was real, while scope still mattered

The published income statements provide a narrower and better operating test than celebratory language. Net brokerage fees rose from approximately **RMB0.93 million in 2020 to RMB138.44 million in 2021**; investment-banking fees rose from **RMB3.80 million to RMB41.29 million**. In 2022 brokerage fees reached **RMB555.95 million**, while investment-banking fees fell to **RMB37.60 million**. The business thus generated identifiable securities-service income, with different trajectories across activities. [FIN-26: 2021 income statement, PDF p23](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report.pdf); [FIN-28: 2022 income statement, PDF p23](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report-2022.pdf).

These figures establish operation, not a causal effect of full ownership. Capital, staffing, client acquisition and market conditions also changed. The disclosed statements do not establish the counterfactual performance of a continuing 51% venture; nor should total revenue be treated as arm's-length client fees without the underlying notes. An October 2021 interview with chairman Park Hakyum supplies the participant's proposed mechanism—greater group support and easier strategic execution—but remains a management account of anticipated advantages. [FIN-27: Liu Chaofeng's CLS interview](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2021-10-19/doc-iktzscyy0605387.shtml).

Nor did full ownership end regulation. The 2022 disclosure lists a Beijing branch registered on **2 August 2022**, with its securities operating permit still being processed. That demonstrates a further permission boundary; absent a completed-file date and comparator, it does not demonstrate discriminatory obstruction. [FIN-28: branch table, printed p4](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report-2022.pdf).

A small within-group comparison reinforces the distinction. The CSRC approved the shareholder/control change for **China International Fund Management (上投摩根)** on **19 January 2023**, referencing an application numbered in 2020. The decision specifically required compliance in cross-border transfers of fund-business data and investors' personal information. It did not itself complete commercial registration. The application number cannot establish when a complete file was accepted, and the different transaction structure prevents a simple comparison of processing speed. Nevertheless, one US group's securities ownership success did not automatically authorize its separate fund company or remove data-governance requirements. [FIN-29: original CSRC decision, 证监许可〔2023〕151号](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c101881/c7007757/content.shtml).

## Competing explanations and the literature

Martin Chorzempa's January 2020 PIIE analysis correctly directs attention to prior reforms and the additional securities acceleration. Its pressure argument is qualified: he expressly says the US contribution cannot be precisely identified. Its mistaken reference to an August 2017 Trump visit should not enter the chronology; contemporary records place the announcement in November. This is a policy analysis, not a reconstruction from confidential negotiating instructions. [FIN-30: Chorzempa](https://www.piie.com/blogs/trade-and-investment-policy-watch/did-us-china-phase-one-deal-deliver-win-us-financial).

Nicholas Lardy and Tianlei Huang's December 2020 policy brief supplies an alternative to treating strategic conflict as uniform financial separation. Its ownership-policy chronology, portfolio data and five figures describe increased but asymmetric integration, with outbound controls persisting. It does not isolate the cause of JPMorgan's licensing decisions. Its forecasts and early announcement-based assessments are period evidence, not verified descriptions of 2026. The complete brief, including figures and notes, was read. [FIN-31: *China's Financial Opening Accelerates*](https://www.piie.com/sites/default/files/documents/pb20-17.pdf).

Mark Austen's 2022 chapter is particularly useful as **industry advocacy**, despite appearing in an academic publisher's volume. Writing from ASIFMA, he identifies entity fragmentation, cross-border information restrictions and uncertain licensing as operating problems after ownership reform. He also suggests licenses sometimes track political relations, but provides no case-level identification sufficient to establish that mechanism here. His requests and claimed burdens can be tested against firm records; his account of deliberate regulatory opacity should not become an independently proven government intention. [FIN-32: Austen, especially sections 4–5](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-8603-0_12).

The peer-reviewed counter-hypothesis is Domenico Lombardi and Anton Malkin's analysis of external financial opening as a tool of domestic reform. Their earlier foreign-listing and Shanghai Free Trade Zone cases propose that domestic coalitions can use foreign-market pressure without relinquishing state control. This pass read selected publisher-indexed sections, not the complete article or its figures. It supplies a hypothesis, not evidence about the JPMorgan decision: seek Chinese coordination records showing whether an international commitment empowered a reform coalition, rather than inferring one from the outcome. [FIN-33: *Journal of Contemporary China* 26(108), 2017, pp785–800](https://doi.org/10.1080/10670564.2017.1337291).

## What this changes in the Solomon assessment

**S-C18, interests and official discipline: retained within scope.** The case fits reciprocal commercial and regulatory interests better than a claim that a distinct national psychological tactic explains the outcome. That is compatibility, not proof of policymakers' private rankings. Full ownership could attract expertise and capital while leaving prudential and data controls intact.

**S-P05, broad principles: narrowed to specified conditions.** Here a dated obligation changed a dated domestic schedule, alongside detailed licensing rules. Chinese officials accepted concrete provisions; the analytical issue is how they interacted with administrative discretion. One cannot extrapolate a preference observed in political communiqués into an aversion to precise financial commitments.

**S-P24, senior intervention: unresolved at the firm level.** The 2017 announcement's summit setting establishes high-level sponsorship of policy. It does not establish a leader's intervention in JPMorgan's corrected application or 2021 filing. Those are different propositions requiring different records.

**S-P25, implementation as bargaining: revised mechanism.** This sequence shows continuing applications, commercial acquisition, operational preparation and advocacy. Some actions could become bargaining points, but their mere existence does not prove that China reopened the agreement. The same implementation arena also delivered a usable American-owned business. An updated account should distinguish a new demanded concession, a preexisting regulatory condition, a private shareholder negotiation and ordinary business preparation.

The most discriminating remaining records are the initial and corrected application; acceptance and compliant-file notices; inspection and operating-permit files; the 2020 transfer contract and closing conditions; the 2021 CSRC filing response; and contemporary US/Chinese instructions concerning the April acceleration. These would separate bureaucratic work, commercial timing and intentional political linkage. The existing record already supports a substantive conclusion: **international bargaining accelerated a general reform, and implementation passed through several independently consequential domestic decisions before ownership and operational capability converged.** It leaves the private causal chain open rather than filling it with a national-style explanation.
