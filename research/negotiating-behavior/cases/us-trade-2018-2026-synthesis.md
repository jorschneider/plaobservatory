# Updating Solomon: the US trade negotiations and Phase One's afterlife

**Subsequent research, 10 September:** the [implementation and major-power reassessment](implementation-and-major-power-reassessment.md) follows the financial and agricultural priorities below and adds an India security comparison. The text here preserves the preceding 8 September assessment.

**Research pass: 8 September 2026.** This case reconstructs the 2018–2020 bargaining sequence and selected implementation episodes through July 2, 2026. It adds contemporary reporting, Chinese white papers, paired official statements, a bilingual agreement reading and implementation evidence to the [1999 WTO pilot](wto-1999-synthesis.md). The latest date is a boundary of substantive coverage, not a claim that every intervening negotiation or all of 2026 has been researched.

**The strongest conclusion is continuity in political control and bargaining after signature, operating through detailed legal, regulatory and economic instruments.** China accepted precise obligations and formal procedures while preserving domestic implementation choices and avenues for further negotiation. The United States also used provisional understandings, shifting deadlines, senior intervention and retained pressure. Neither a timeless cultural account nor a simple transition from politics to rules adequately explains the observed combination.

This updates the *weight and application* of Solomon's conclusions more than it overturns his most careful qualifications. His core cases concern 1967–1984. Freeman's 1999 commentary already recognized learning and differentiated negotiating institutions. What requires new investigation is how those changes operate under confrontation, across different issues and with more consequential economic instruments—not whether Chinese officials first discovered technical negotiation after 1999.

## 1. Reconstruct what was agreed before explaining a reversal

The May 19, 2018 joint statement committed the parties to further commercial work and Chinese legal changes, but specified neither a purchase total nor a tariff pause. Liu He publicly described a truce. The next day Mnuchin also announced tariffs were on hold while the framework was developed; Reuters simultaneously reported Kudlow's denial that a deal was complete and Lighthizer's insistence on structural reform. There was a publicly asserted understanding broader than the joint text, alongside unresolved scope. [Joint US statement](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefings-statements/joint-statement-united-states-china-regarding-trade-consultations/); [Liu's remarks](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/leaders/2018-05/20/c_1122857996.htm); [Reuters, May 20](https://www.business-standard.com/article/reuters/u-s-china-putting-trade-war-on-hold-treasury-s-mnuchin-says-118052000509_1.html).

Washington then announced tariffs on May 29. China's June 3 statement made negotiated outcomes conditional on avoiding tariffs and reserved confirmation of details. This is consequential evidence of both American policy movement and Chinese conditionality. It does not settle the content of any private oral promise. Treating all these documents as one operative agreement would manufacture certainty about who broke what. [White House, May 29](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefings-statements/statement-steps-protect-domestic-technology-intellectual-property-chinas-discriminatory-burdensome-trade-practices/); [Chinese June 3 statement](https://mds.mofcom.gov.cn/tpxw/art/2018/art_06bf885645c94d8c95bc4e037fea2fca.html).

The May 2019 breakdown is more serious than a mere disagreement in public tone. Reuters reported extensive Chinese revisions to a large draft, attributing its account to three US officials and three private-sector sources briefed on the talks. The reported dispute included implementation through legal amendments versus administrative or regulatory measures. The article does not say Reuters obtained the draft. USTR's tariff notice records the administration's accusation and action but does not attach the disputed undertakings. We have neither an authenticated redline nor the approval trail for individual provisions. [Reuters, May 8, complete syndicated report](https://www.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-china-backtracked-nearly-aspects-084554623.html); [USTR notice, 84 FR 20459–20460](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-05-09/pdf/2019-09681.pdf).

China's June white paper contests the interpretation of changes during unfinished negotiations; it does not establish that no Chinese edits occurred. The reported revisions and that defense can both be true. Crucially, the report describes some subjects as continuing sticking points. It cannot prove every demand omitted from the final agreement had previously been accepted and then withdrawn. [2019 white paper, Section II](https://asean.china-mission.gov.cn/chn/lmbf/zgsy/201906/t20190604_8875502.htm).

**What this changes:** retain Solomon's attention to prior undertakings and unresolved reservations, but make document status an explicit part of the explanation. The missing variables are the terms, brackets, conditions and authority—not simply whether a participant sounded cooperative. Xi's personal veto, a staged retreat or a delegation's exceeded mandate remain hypotheses. Repetition of the original reporting does not resolve them.

## 2. A smaller bargain changed the feasible terms

After the May 2019 round, Liu publicly demanded removal of all additional tariffs, realistic purchase commitments and a balanced text. By December, Chinese officials accepted a first-stage settlement with only partial tariff relief. That is an observable adjustment. It would be evasive to call the positions identical, but also premature to interpret the movement without accounting for the narrowed package. [Liu, May 11 publication](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/2019-05/11/c_1124481543.htm); [December 13 Chinese briefing](https://interview.mofcom.gov.cn/detail/201912/ff8080816e67e5c2016f0cb2a51c00b0.html).

The October 11 public meeting supplies direct evidence of American acceptance of phases. Trump defended the narrower approach; Lighthizer said enforcement consequences still needed work; Mnuchin said Liu needed further discussion with his team. This was substantive unfinished bargaining after an announced understanding. The December Chinese briefing similarly separated agreement on text from legal review, translation, signing and related tariff arrangements. [October 11 White House transcript](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefings-statements/remarks-president-trump-vice-premier-liu-peoples-republic-china-meeting/).

The signed agreement addresses intellectual property, technology transfer, agriculture, financial services, currency, purchases and dispute procedures. USTR's announcement explicitly retained substantial tariffs. Industrial-policy disputes did not thereby disappear; Tai later distinguished practices outside Phase One from its implementation problems. [USTR, December 13](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2019/december/united-states-and-china-reach); [Tai, March 2022 China section](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/speeches-and-remarks/2022/march/testimony-ambassador-katherine-tai-senate-finance-committee-hearing-presidents-2022-trade-policy).

**What this changes:** Chinese public demands were negotiable at the level of package and sequencing, and the counterparty's willingness to accept a partial outcome mattered. This supports a conditional account of adaptation. It does not identify whether economic pressure, domestic politics, substantive exclusions or revised expectations drove the leadership's choice. Those explanations can operate together and need evidence of the decision itself.

## 3. Specific rules and political authority coexist

Phase One gives concrete form to that adaptation. It contains detailed IP obligations and an action-plan deadline while Article 1.34 preserves choice of implementation methods within domestic systems, including proposed legislative amendments where necessary. It therefore combines substantive requirements with procedural discretion. Formal recognition of both parties does not imply equal new burdens: the IP chapter repeatedly requires Chinese changes while affirming existing US measures. [Agreement, Chapter 1](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf).

This is important for interpreting “equality” and “balanced text.” They cannot safely be reduced to ceremonial face-saving, nor assumed to require identical concessions. The final instrument reveals an acceptable combination; it does not reveal which bargaining demand produced each clause. The [bilingual audit](../sources/phase-one-agreement-audit.md) sets out the distinction and the precise reading boundaries.

Chapter 7 establishes regular channels and escalation from working officials to senior political principals. It formally allows either party to complain, and specified unresolved disputes can lead to a proportionate remedy or suspended obligations. It creates no independent adjudicator. Appeals are normally confidential; the absence of published complaints cannot establish non-use. Rights under other international agreements are also expressly preserved. These features institutionalize bargaining rather than place every disagreement outside politics.

A WTO procedural check reinforces the coexistence of forums. The Secretariat's DS543 account records the panel's rejection of the argument that bilateral talks had settled the WTO matter: China had not intended that substitution. The findings concerned the challenged tariffs, not the validity of Phase One. The US subsequently appealed; the report must not be described as an adopted final disposition. This pass read the case summary, not the full report. [WTO DS543 history](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds543_e.htm).

**What this changes:** senior authorization remains central, but a modern account must specify the procedure, deadline, implementing regulator and available forum. “Relations matter” is too coarse to describe the mechanism. At the same time, a signed legal text is insufficient evidence of autonomous enforcement or an internally unified state. Because both governments chose this arrangement, its existence alone cannot establish a national preference unique to China.

## 4. Implementation is a set of processes, not one score

The purchase targets were missed substantially in the examined series. Bown's revised July 2022 calculation reports $290.8 billion in covered US goods-and-services exports against a $502.4 billion benchmark, or 58 percent. It excludes certain commercial-presence services covered by the text. His headline about none of the additional $200 billion refers to failure to exceed the aggregate baseline, not zero purchases. The figures are a defined measurement, not a comprehensive adjudication of the agreement. [Bown, analysis and notes, revised July 19, 2022](https://www.piie.com/blogs/realtime-economics/2022/china-bought-none-extra-200-billion-us-exports-trumps-trade-deal).

The agreement combines numerical obligations with market considerations, US supply responsibilities, consultations over conflicting data, and a provision for unforeseeable events causing delay. The latter requires consultation; it is not an express automatic waiver. USTR's 2025 initiation notice uses a different total benchmark and shortfall. Those figures should not be spliced into Bown's series before reconciling coverage and methods. The [implementation memo](phase-one-implementation-2020-2026.md) preserves the alternative denominators.

Performance elsewhere is mixed. USTR's January 2025 report acknowledges foreign-ownership liberalization and some financial licenses, while describing remaining licensing difficulties and slow biotechnology approvals. Even the complaining government's account therefore supplies evidence against a uniform failure narrative. Ownership permission, operating permission, approval timing and actual market entry are separate implementation stages. [USTR compliance report, printed 12, 47–48, 65–66](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/reports/2025/2024USTRReportCongressonChinaWTOCompliance.pdf).

Paired 2020 and 2021 readouts establish continuing senior discussions, with different emphases on obligations, tariffs and conditions. China's 2025 white paper additionally reports meeting counts and says neither party formally invoked dispute resolution in 2020–2021. That is an attributable Chinese assertion, rather than a conclusion inferred from public silence. [August 2020 US release](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2020/august/statement-call-between-united-states-and-china); [Chinese white paper, II–III](https://chinawto.mofcom.gov.cn/article/ap/p/202504/20250403576920.shtml).

**What this changes:** Solomon's proposition that bargaining continues after signature survives strongly as a description of observed activity. It does not by itself explain nonperformance. Distinguish an intentionally withheld concession from unavailable supply, an unattractive purchase, a slow regulator and a contested reading of the obligation. The useful next test compares successful and delayed administrative cases within the same agreement, where leadership and broad diplomatic context are held more nearly constant.

## 5. In 2025–2026, old obligations entered new bargains

The US opened a separate Section 301 investigation in October 2025. Its statutory consultation track is not automatically a Chapter 7 appeal, and initiation is not a final determination. The December hearing also reveals division among American agricultural interests: the soybean representative worried that investigation could harm current negotiations, while the renewable-fuels representative supported investigation and potential action. This is direct testimony about competing preferences, not proof that China manufactured the split. [Initiation notice](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Issue_Areas/Enforcement/Section%20301/Published%20Phase%20One%20Initiation%20Notice.pdf); [hearing, first panel, especially 18–19 and 25–28](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Issue_Areas/Enforcement/Section%20301/Hearing%20transcript%20full%20without%20index.pdf).

China's February 2026 response claims fulfillment, attributes difficulties partly to US restrictions and invokes repeated discussion of Phase One during the 2025 rounds. In May, new public outcomes included agricultural trade and proposed Trade and Investment Boards. There is also a specific documentary connection: Chinese complaints in the 2025 white paper about dairy, seafood and Shandong poultry reappear as US-side steps in MOFCOM's May 2026 description. Implementation grievances had become items in a later bargain. The public announcements do not establish completion of the administrative steps. [MOFCOM, February 25](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/xwfb/xwfyrth/art/2026/art_c24476d86cec4869b95082708db09a91.html); [May 20, II–III and VII–VIII](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/syxwfb/art/2026/art_3e89743a8d5d4a95bccdda685830afbd.html).

There is also evidence of institutional design beyond the summit. USTR's June 2 public consultation asks about eligible products, Board meeting frequency and bilateral data sharing; the linked notice requests product codes and 2022–2024 trade values. This documents an American input channel into the proposed mechanism. It does not establish which submissions influenced the design, equivalent access on the Chinese side, or non-government membership in the Board. Those are concrete questions with recoverable records, rather than assumptions about national consultation styles. [USTR release](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/june/ustr-seeks-public-comment-scope-and-operation-mechanism-promote-balanced-and-reciprocal-trade-china); [linked notice, especially II(A–B)](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/June%202%20FRN%20FINAL%20for%20upload.pdf).

The paired public accounts also repeat a familiar interpretive problem. The White House states a quantified agricultural purchase commitment; MOFCOM calls agricultural expansion a guiding target and emphasizes enterprise decisions, already in May and again in July. The July reporter's question contains an arithmetic premise that the answer does not confirm. We should neither harmonize these formulations without evidence nor declare a private breach from their difference. [White House, May 17](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/05/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-secures-historic-deals-with-china-delivering-for-american-workers-farmers-and-industry/); [MOFCOM, July 2 Reuters exchange](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/xwfbzt/2026/swbzklxxwfbh2026n7y2r/).

**What this changes:** by 2026, the prior agreement remained an object and instrument of bargaining, while the parties proposed additional machinery. This is stronger evidence than simply observing that another summit occurred. But institutional announcements, design consultations, signed implementing rules and actual deliveries are different stages. The available corpus does not yet demonstrate the final design or effectiveness of the new Boards, or the final disposition of the investigation.

## 6. What stays, what needs revision, and what remains unproven

The proposition IDs refer to the project's [Solomon process audit](../book/process-claims.json) and [context/Freeman audit](../book/context-claims.json). These are conditional judgments about this case, not votes on all Chinese negotiations.

| Proposition | Provisional assessment | Required update |
|---|---|---|
| Senior intervention as a signal of approaching agreement, S-P24 | **Senior involvement corroborated; the closure diagnostic remains untested.** Public meetings and the signed mechanism establish an assigned role, not predictive reliability. | Separate visible endorsement, delegated authority over particular clauses, and actual decisions. Include senior encounters that fail to close a deal. |
| Implementation as continued bargaining, S-P25 | **Retain strongly as an observed pattern.** Reviews, reciprocal complaints and later packages supply concrete examples. | Identify the obligation, regulator, forum and requested remedy; continued talks do not establish deliberate evasion. |
| Partial settlements and reservations, S-P12; competing accounts of prior commitments, S-P19 | **Retain as distinct questions.** Partial scope and contested restatements help explain why actors describe the same stage differently. | Code proposal, provisional assent, authorized text, signature, effectiveness and implementation; compare later claims with the original record. |
| Broad principles and detailed provisions, S-P05 | **Narrow generalizations.** This case includes both expansive public principles and precise legal obligations. | Measure specificity clause by clause and by stage; do not assume a uniform preference for vagueness. |
| Indirection to elicit responses while limiting commitment, S-P07 | **Not tested by final-text specificity.** | Reconstruct exploratory exchanges and subsequent clarification; detailed final terms do not disprove an indirect opening tactic. |
| Deadlines, pressure and counterpart divisions, S-P13/S-P23/S-C20 | **Use comparatively.** US extensions, tariff decisions and stakeholder disagreements are observed too. | Establish strategic intent and influence rather than classify ordinary bargaining devices as uniquely Chinese. |
| Interests constrain tactics, S-C18 | **Retain as a core explanation to test.** Scope, tariff relief, domestic implementation and commercial incentives are concrete variables. | Use evidence of costs and decision-making to distinguish mechanisms; rhetoric and final outcomes alone are insufficient. |
| Learning and differentiated institutions, F07/F09/F13 | **Develop, not rediscover.** Freeman already raised them. | Trace the growth and interaction of technical, legal, regulatory and leadership channels; ask which actors learned what and whether it altered behavior. |
| Stable culture as the main cause of reversals or delay | **Not established by this corpus.** | Require evidence that outperforms draft-status, authority, scope, material-interest and counterpart explanations. This is not a claim that culture has no effect. |

The 1999 comparison supplies a useful warning about evidence quality. For that pilot, dated facsimiles and the signed April MOU body reveal specific qualifications; for spring 2019, the decisive draft remains unavailable. We can compare the mechanisms each record suggests, but cannot pretend the two episodes have equally complete documentary foundations. Nor can two US commercial cases establish how China bargains with Russia, India or in a military crisis.

## Next evidence that would change the verdict

The immediate priority is **a matched implementation study**, with one financial-access sequence and one delayed agricultural-approval sequence. Obtain the applications, requests for information, decisions, bilateral representations and implementing rules. This can test whether escalation solved a regulatory obstacle, whether earlier delay reflected delegated authority, and how much of the outcome followed autonomous reform. It also creates a more credible comparison than a national purchase total.

In parallel, seek the May 2018 truce terms, Argentina purchase schedule, successive spring 2019 drafts and approval reservations, and the written arrangements behind the 2026 targets. Reconcile the 2022 and 2025 purchase benchmarks before making a longitudinal compliance series. The EU accession comparison and a recoverable security case remain necessary controls on generalization.
