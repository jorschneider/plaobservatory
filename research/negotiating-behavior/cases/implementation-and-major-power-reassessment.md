# What survives Solomon: authority, implementation and the terms of a relationship

**Research assessment, 10 September 2026.** The strongest conclusions to retain concern political purpose, negotiating authority, partial settlements and continued bargaining over implementation. The most needed extension is a much fuller account of the institutions and dependencies through which those processes operate. A modern account needs to follow regulatory files, ownership transfers, scientific assessments, military protocols and competing negotiating forums alongside leaders and diplomats.

Three new reconstructions make that argument more concrete. JPMorgan's securities business shows a negotiated acceleration of an opening that was already underway, followed by real commercial operation. Agricultural biotechnology shows repeated bargains over administrative procedure, an identifiable improvement in authorization duration, and a very old application finally cleared. The India border case shows partial security bargains, a failed period of apparent diplomatic momentum, and new technical mechanisms under continuing senior supervision. Two Chinese-language scholarly readings, checked against original WTO positions, help identify competing explanations rather than simply confirm a national style.

These are selected comparisons, not a representative sample of Chinese negotiations. Finance and biotechnology hold the US–China agreement broadly constant while changing the implementation problem. India tests whether the US-focused propositions travel to a large territorial rival; because the subject also changes, it cannot isolate a pure counterpart effect. The evidence supports the revisions below within those limits. It does not yet settle the book-length question for Russia, Europe, every US administration or every episode through 2026.

The supporting studies contain the full chronologies, original reporting, document links, source dependencies and reading limits: [financial access](financial-access-implementation.md), [agricultural approvals](agricultural-approvals-implementation.md), [India border bargaining](india-border-2020-2024.md), and [Chinese scholarship and WTO positions](../literature/readings/chinese-negotiation-and-institutions.md). This assessment also builds on the [1999 WTO pilot](wto-1999-synthesis.md) and [2018–2026 trade reconstruction](us-trade-2018-2026-synthesis.md).

## 1. The most useful unit is a decision that changes what the parties can do

“China opened its securities market” compresses several consequential decisions. So does “China approved a crop,” or “China and India resolved the border standoff.” The deeper records show why this compression distorts the causal story.

| Case | What the political bargain addressed | Separate implementation stages | What the records support |
|---|---|---|---|
| JPMorgan Securities (China) | General ownership limits and the timetable for removing them | Establishment, registration, a business permit, operating preparation, shareholder transactions and registration of full ownership | A functioning business; Phase One accelerated the announced securities timetable by eight months, while the initial operating permit preceded signature |
| Enlist E3 soybeans and DP73496 canola | The speed, predictability and conditions of Chinese biotechnology review | Scientific assessment, a ministerial decision, certificate delivery, other countries' permissions and company launch decisions | An original Enlist approval before Phase One; a later five-year renewal; a much older canola application authorized in 2023; incomplete evidence on why files waited |
| India border arrangements | Separation at confrontation points, restored access and the relationship between border conditions and wider ties | Local withdrawal and verification, coordinated patrols, further force adjustment and boundary talks | Specific partial bargains and official implementation reports; de-escalation and boundary settlement remained distinct subjects |

This matters for assessing both compliance and negotiating behavior. A new requirement can be a demand for another concession, a condition written into the original framework, a technical implementation task, or a private transaction. Those categories can interact, but they cannot all be called renegotiation. Conversely, treating a summit announcement as complete delivery overlooks the officials and procedures that determine whether its promised benefit becomes usable.

## 2. Financial access: domestic reform and a negotiated concession can coexist

China announced a move toward majority foreign ownership in securities in November 2017, during the Trump–Xi economic discussions. The reform was general rather than an American-exclusive privilege. The CSRC issued implementing rules in April 2018, and in October 2019 announced that the securities ownership ceiling would disappear on **1 December 2020**. Phase One brought that date forward to **1 April 2020**. Crucially, the CSRC's March 2020 notice explicitly linked the acceleration to implementing the agreement. This is a documentary connection, not just proximity between a deal and a reform. [CSRC's October timetable](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1000907/content.shtml); [March implementation notice](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1000819/content.shtml).

The defensible finding is narrower than either government's preferred victory narrative. The agreement changed a published timetable by eight months. The documents do not establish that US pressure originated the opening, what was exchanged for the accelerated date, or whether China would otherwise have advanced the schedule again. Chinese domestic objectives and an American negotiating gain are compatible explanations. The [finance reconstruction](financial-access-implementation.md) traces both the earlier diplomatic context and the regulator's reform rationale.

JPMorgan's actual route into the market further limits attribution. A March 2019 CSRC decision authorized establishment of a majority-owned company but prohibited business before a separate operating permit. The company registered in August and obtained that permit in **December 2019**, before the January 2020 agreement was signed. It announced commencement in March 2020. Initial entry therefore cannot be counted as implementation of a later signed obligation merely because it appeared in the same political story. [Original establishment decision](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c101841/c1022649/content.shtml); [company's operating-permit announcement](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/CN/zh/news-announcements/jpm-securities-china-company-limited-permitted-to-open-for-business).

The full-ownership transition has its own documentary sequence. A reported 2020 purchase of a Chinese partner's stake was sometimes retold as completed 71% ownership. The original news account used future tense for the projected 71% stake. The venture's 2020 shareholder table still showed JPMorgan at 51%, and the seller's accounts retained the investment. The venture's 2021 report dates registration of the transfers from all five Chinese shareholders to **25 August 2021**. This correction distinguishes a transaction announcement, regulatory filing and registered ownership. It does not settle the date every economic right passed under the unavailable transfer contracts. [2020 company disclosure](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/2020.pdf); [2021 disclosure](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report.pdf).

Nor was the opening merely nominal. The disclosed accounts report substantial growth in brokerage fee income during 2021–2022 and identifiable investment-banking business. That establishes commercial operation. It does not establish how much growth full ownership caused, since staffing, capital, clients and market conditions also changed. Later branch licensing and the separate fund-management transaction show that ownership reform left other permissions consequential. [2022 company disclosure](https://www.jpmorganchina.com/content/dam/jpm/fenix/documents/disclosure-report-2022.pdf).

**Revision to Solomon:** extend Solomon’s and Freeman’s institutional account by reconstructing the specific authorities and decisions through which each commitment became usable. Here, an international commitment accelerated a general regulatory reform. The firm completed administrative and commercial steps both before and after that commitment. The case supplies no recovered instruction showing a Chinese leader personally clearing the firm's corrected application or demanding a fresh US concession for its ownership filing. The opening's delivery and the limits on its causal attribution both belong in the account.

## 3. Biotechnology: detailed procedure was repeatedly negotiated, with uneven results

The agricultural case is valuable because it reveals both continuity and specific performance. In May 2017, the bilateral 100-day plan already addressed scientifically relevant information requests, written explanations, committee meetings and issuance of a certificate within twenty working days after a successful safety evaluation. Phase One did not introduce the idea of detailed administrative commitments from scratch. [Chinese joint text, item 3](https://wjb.mof.gov.cn/gongzuodongtai/201705/t20170512_2599646.htm); [US counterpart](https://www.commerce.gov/news/fact-sheets/2017/05/fact-sheet-initial-actions-us-china-economic-cooperation-100-day-plan).

A public FOIA release now documents part of the American process behind that earlier bargain. May 2017 emails show Dow's representative seeking a CEO meeting with Secretary Perdue and supplying crop-approval background at a USDA adviser's request. On May 16, the adviser challenged the framing of proposed congressional questions in light of the recent China announcement. On June 14, CEO Tim Hassinger credited the 100-day agreement for an **Enlist corn** approval and sought continued work on other traits; the adviser said she forwarded his message to Perdue. This establishes access, transmission of material, official pushback and a participant's causal belief. It does not establish that the supplied wording entered US negotiating instructions, disclose China's decision, or identify an unavailable soybean attachment as Enlist E3. [Released emails, part 4, pp. 197–199, 205–206, 506–509 and 603–605](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Rony204.pdf); [release scope and reading audit](../sources/agricultural-approvals-register.json).

The actual May 17 House hearing contains a related question from Representative Rodney Davis, acknowledging the 100-day plan and asking how China would be held accountable. It substantially rewrites the proposed wording. Perdue's answer discusses diplomatic efforts and the usefulness of Chinese clearance for persuading other markets, without adding a deadline or enforcement procedure in that exchange. The record supports a comparison of private advocacy and public questioning; it does not identify who made each revision. [Published hearing, printed 60–61 / PDF 64–65](https://agriculture.house.gov/UploadedFiles/115-06_-_25545.pdf).

Its Annex 16 added or codified several distinct undertakings. The headline clock is an **average of no more than 24 months** between formal application and a final decision approving or disapproving a biotechnology product for feed or further processing. It is not an absolute two-year deadline for every case. Other clauses concern completeness checks, information requests, committee frequency, complaints and authorization lasting at least five years. The chapter also expressly excludes its implementing measures from Article 8.5's general public-comment provision. An assessment of transparency therefore needs the applicable clause, not a generalized expectation that every measure receives the same comment period. [Agreement, Chapter 3 footnote 1 and Annex 16](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf); [supplemented agreement audit](../sources/phase-one-agreement-audit.md).

The Enlist E3 record provides a bounded positive observation. Its original Chinese import-processing authorization ran for three years from **20 December 2018**. The renewal runs for five years, **17 December 2021–16 December 2026**, consistent with the later authorization-duration obligation. The renewal is not a new-product review, so it cannot demonstrate that first-time approvals became faster. Nor does the public list alone establish when the applicant received the actual document. [Original MARA certificate list, row 5](https://www.moa.gov.cn/ztzl/zjyqwgz/spxx/201901/P020190108439855980836.pdf); [renewal list, row 18](https://www.moa.gov.cn/ztzl/zjyqwgz/spxx/202112/P020211227585561085049.pdf).

DP73496 canola supplies a different observation. [Contemporary Reuters reporting](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/factbox-china-approves-gmo-soybean-corn-canola-traits-163125348--finance.html) dates the Chinese application to July 2012; MARA's eventual certificate became effective on **5 January 2023**. That is an exceptionally long interval. Yet the application's full record is unavailable, and the European Union authorized the same event only in March 2022 after an application in May 2012. The EU comparison does not excuse Chinese delay. It prevents attributing the entire decade before commercialization exclusively to China. [MARA's certificate list, row 6](https://www.moa.gov.cn/ztzl/zjyqwgz/spxx/202301/P020230113566699597851.pdf); [EU decision, recitals 1 and 3 and Articles 2–3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2022/529/oj/eng/pdf).

The commercial mechanism is more securely documented than the Chinese decision mechanism. Companies considered authorization in major importing markets before broadly launching seed in producing countries. Enlist's US launch followed Philippine clearance after the Chinese announcement; Corteva's canola launch followed the later permissions and commercial preparations. A Chinese permission for processing imports could consequently affect American or Canadian planting decisions. This creates potential bargaining value through an international supply chain. It does not prove that the regulator deliberately held a particular application to extract a diplomatic concession. [Corteva's Enlist launch account](https://www.corteva.com/us/press-releases/corteva-and-ms-tech-announce-2019-us-enlist-e3-soybean-launch.html); [canola launch account](https://www.corteva.com/ca-en/about-corteva/media-centre/corteva-announces-commercial-launch-optimum-gly.html).

The issue remained in senior economic discussions in 2026. USTR published Greer's statement that China had agreed to review biotechnology traits; the excerpt named no products, deadline or completed certificates. USTR's 2026 trade-barriers report also alleged that China had stopped publishing the previous year's committee results. That is an attributed explanation of reduced visibility, not proof that no approvals occurred. The case therefore supports **continuing negotiation over procedure** more strongly than an independently measured verdict on the entire post-2020 application cohort. [May 2026 USTR account](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/may/president-trumps-state-visit-china-delivers-historic-deals-and-greater-market-access-american); [2026 NTE, printed 82–83](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/National%20Trade%20Estimate%20Report%202026.pdf).

**Revision to Solomon:** implementation bargaining is not adequately described as informal adjustment after a broad political deal. Negotiators repeatedly addressed the administrative process itself. A modern account should distinguish the value created by a regulatory permission, the ability to withhold it, and evidence that withholding was actually used as a negotiating tactic. The first is well demonstrated here; the final link remains incompletely observed.

## 4. India: the relationship is contested, and leaders do different kinds of work

The border case makes relational bargaining visible on both sides. Chinese officials repeatedly urged a wider view of bilateral relations. Indian officials made border peace a condition for normal ties. Those positions also drew on different parts of the same 2005 agreement: protection of the broader relationship from boundary differences and maintenance of peace along the Line of Actual Control pending settlement. An appeal to the relationship is therefore not self-explanatory. The parties can disagree about what preserving it requires. [2005 agreement, Articles I and IX](https://www.mea.gov.in/bilateral-documents.htm?dtl/6534/); [paired exchanges and chronology](india-border-2020-2024.md).

The local arrangements reveal more detail than the short communiqués. India's September 2022 account of the Gogra–Hot Springs/PP15 process specified phased and coordinated withdrawal, a completion date, removal of temporary structures, mutual verification and restoration of landforms. These were publicly described operational duties within an unfinished wider negotiation. The authenticated protocol and verification logs remain unavailable; an Indian explanation of an agreed process is not a substitute for them. But the existence of specific duties already makes it unsafe to infer an imprecise bargain from a brief joint statement. [Indian explanation, 9 September 2022](https://www.mea.gov.in/response-to-queries.htm?dtl/35691/); [October implementation account](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl/35808/).

Senior contact needs similar disaggregation. In August 2023, unusually long corps-commanders talks and a Modi–Xi interaction were followed by continued unresolved issues. Chadha's interview-based report describes apparent proximity to agreement, but it does not supply a recovered draft or Chinese authorization instruction. This sequence tests the shortcut of treating high-level attention as evidence that settlement is imminent. It does not decisively refute Solomon's more specific proposition that senior intervention **combined with concrete drafting** can signal an authorized agreement. The missing draft matters. [August joint release](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl/37010/); [October round](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl/37185/); [Chadha's process reconstruction](https://assets.carnegieendowment.org/static/files/Negotiating%20the%20India-China%20Standoff%202020_2024.pdf).

The October 2024 sequence shows another role for leaders. India announced the patrolling arrangement on **21 October**; Modi and Xi met in Kazan on **23 October**, welcoming the prior agreement and directing further work. The public chronology identifies endorsement and follow-on direction. The prospect of a summit may have supplied an incentive beforehand, but the meeting itself cannot be described as producing an agreement already announced. [Indian announcement](https://www.mea.gov.in/media-briefings.htm?dtl/38441/); [post-summit briefing](https://www.mea.gov.in/media-briefings.htm?dtl/38459/); [Chinese summit account](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/gjhdq_665435/2675_665437/2711_663426/2713_663430/202410/t20241023_11514914.html).

India's December parliamentary statement reports verification and regular patrols in Depsang and Demchok and restored grazing access. It also distinguishes disengagement from subsequent de-escalation. The earlier buffer arrangements were not all replaced by a universal return to the pre-2020 situation. These distinctions prevent a local implementation claim from becoming a comprehensive boundary settlement or an independently verified military balance sheet. [Jaishankar's parliamentary account, 3 December 2024](https://www.mea.gov.in/Speeches-Statements.htm?dtl/38665/).

There is also a concrete institutional development after the pact. The 2012 WMCC agreement expressly excluded resolution of the boundary question. In August 2025 the Special Representatives commissioned an expert group under that mechanism to explore early progress on delimitation, alongside a border-management group and additional general-level mechanisms. By May 2026, the paired official accounts explicitly described WMCC discussions of delimitation. This is evidence of additional technical work commissioned under senior direction; it does not establish a formal amendment of the 2012 agreement or completed delimitation. [2012 agreement, Article V](https://www.mea.gov.in/bilateral-documents.htm?dtl/17963/); [2025 Indian outcomes](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl/40016/); [Chinese consensus](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/gjhdq_665435/2675_665437/2711_663426/2713_663430/202508/t20250820_11692839.html); [May 2026 Chinese account](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/xw/wjbxw/202605/t20260528_11919791.html).

The 2025 records also put de-escalation at discussion of principles and modalities while permitting steps toward wider economic and people-to-people exchange. But limited engagement was already possible earlier: the same October 2022 briefing that rejected overall normality also described Indian students returning to China and continuing facilitation. The records distinguish conditions for overall normalization from permission for limited engagement; they do not establish a newly adopted principle of partial recovery. [October 2022 briefing, Kavita and Abhishek exchanges](https://www.mea.gov.in/media-briefings.htm?dtl/35739/).

**Revision to Solomon:** distinguish leaders authorizing concessions, coordinating agencies, endorsing an already negotiated arrangement and assigning subsequent work. Preserve partial agreements as meaningful outcomes. Treat relational conditionality, use of earlier commitments and public reticence as interactive practices unless comparative evidence establishes a specifically Chinese mechanism.

## 5. Chinese scholarship and WTO records supply alternatives to a national-style explanation

Liang Xiaojun's 2008 article directly engages Solomon's treatment of relationships. Its useful alternative is that the relationship may be an interest in its own right, not only a means to obtain something else. That changes the evidence needed: a costly concession to preserve future cooperation is more informative than an invocation of friendship. Liang's small classroom exercise cannot establish how Chinese diplomats behave; her argument supplies a hypothesis to test. [Complete article and critical reading](../literature/readings/chinese-negotiation-and-institutions.md).

Tu Xinquan and Shi Xiaojing's 2021 article directs attention to the choice of negotiating format. Their case for narrower WTO plurilateral agreements is a scholarly prescription, not an instruction to China's delegation. Original positions nevertheless show that institutional flexibility was already explicit in China's 2019 WTO proposal and remained so in its February 2026 paper, alongside protections for policy space. This is continuity across those documents, not a newly discovered 2026 embrace of flexibility. [2019 Chinese proposal, 1.10–1.11 and 2.20–2.25](https://docs.wto.org/dol2fe/Pages/SS/directdoc.aspx?filename=q:/WT/GC/W773.pdf&Open=True); [2026 position, paragraphs 8–9](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/cms_files/filemanager/676898164/attach/202511/5ccc1f093ee54112b5b6b107a5e442fc.pdf).

The counterpart positions complicate broad geopolitical categories. India, Namibia and South Africa challenged routes for incorporating joint-statement outcomes into WTO rules without the requisite procedures. The United States' 2020 agenda, by contrast, supported more effective plurilateral arrangements and participated in e-commerce negotiations, while stating different substantive emphases from China's. Agreement about a negotiating format does not imply agreement about the rules it should produce. Governments that cooperate in one coalition can disagree over another proposal. [April 2021 joint communication](https://docs.wto.org/dol2fe/Pages/SS/directdoc.aspx?filename=q:/WT/GC/W819R1.pdf&Open=True); [USTR's 2020 agenda, printed 15 and 19](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/2020_Trade_Policy_Agenda_and_2019_Annual_Report.pdf).

These findings fit Freeman's expectation that institution-specific experience and specialized personnel would matter. They do not by themselves establish learning as the cause: increased resources, altered interests and different coalition opportunities can produce similar observations. That causal question remains open even when procedural adaptation is plainly visible.

Cabestan's complete institutional study adds another distinction: concentration of senior authority can coexist with distributed responsibilities. Its claims about improved speed and coordination require stronger decision-level comparisons than its organizational map supplies. A primary check of the 2018 reform plan gives a concrete example: the new international development agency received strategic and coordinating responsibilities while relevant departments retained aid execution. That demonstrates a formal division of labor, not its effectiveness or a causal explanation of either trade or border decisions. [Critical reading and source audit](../literature/readings/cabestan-institutions.md); [2018 reform plan, section XXXVIII](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2018-03/21/c_1122571024.htm).

## 6. What to retain, revise and leave unresolved

The verdicts apply to the examined evidence, not to China generally. Proposition IDs link back to the [process](../book/process-claims.json) and [context/Freeman](../book/context-claims.json) ledgers; related propositions are not independent tests.

| Original proposition | Current assessment | Consequence for the updated account |
|---|---|---|
| S-C01 and S-C18: relationship purpose, interests and official discipline constrain tactics | **Retained within scope** as an organizing explanation; private priorities remain incompletely observed | Begin with what each side needs and can authorize; do not infer tactical mastery from eventual agreement |
| S-P05: preference for broad principles and flexible political documents | **Narrowed to specified documents and stages** | Compare political statements with operative provisions, domestic rules and implementation instructions; public brevity cannot establish operational vagueness |
| S-P12: partial settlement with reserved issues | **Retained for partial settlements**; the additional mechanism of wording that avoids conspicuous reversal is not separately established | Record what was settled, deferred and left under earlier arrangements; partial access or separation can have value without final resolution |
| S-P19: invocation and reinterpretation of the prior record | **Invocation retained as an interactive practice**; strategic distortion is not established by different emphasis alone | Compare both sides' references with the underlying text; different emphasis is not automatically distortion |
| S-P24: concrete drafting and senior intervention can signal authorized near-agreement | **Combined signal not fully tested; senior-contact shortcut rejected** | Separate authorization from endorsement; high-level contact alone is insufficient, and unavailable drafts prevent a full test of the combined signal |
| S-P25: implementation remains a bargaining arena | **Continuing-arena component retained, with a revised mechanism**; the fuller claim of asymmetric demands for adherence remains to be tested | Distinguish renewed government bargaining, preexisting regulatory conditions, commercial transactions and operational work; test discrete obligations separately |
| F07, F09 and F13: learning, reduced MFA coordination and acquired counterpart-specific practices | **Research questions sharpened; these causal effects remain untested here** | Map specialized authority within senior control; participation and new mechanisms do not establish learning, reduced MFA coordination or acquired substyles |
| Strategic delay, hidden factional conflict or uniquely effective relational pressure inferred from a stalled process | **Unresolved** | Recover information requests, mandates and rejected offers; elapsed time and eventual agreement do not establish intent or efficacy |

The last row is deliberately not a caricature attributed wholesale to Solomon. The book itself qualifies tactical effects, recognizes Chinese exposure to time pressure and leaves the transfer from relationship-building to renewed confrontation open. Likewise, Freeman was already discussing specialization in 1999. Those qualifications are part of the baseline, not discoveries to be credited to a 2026 update.

The new evidence supports a more precise thesis: **Chinese negotiating behavior varies with the interest being protected, the authority needed to change policy, the institutions through which promises become usable, and the counterpart's response. Senior political control can coexist with technical specialization and detailed commitments. Their presence alone predicts neither timely delivery nor failure.** This formulation preserves the strongest parts of Solomon while making the implementation mechanism and the interaction between the parties more explicit.

## What would materially change these judgments

The most consequential missing evidence is now specific. For finance, it is the corrected establishment application, the ownership-transfer conditions, the accepted filing and the instructions behind the April acceleration. For biotechnology, it is the dated submission–request–response–assessment–decision chain and the complete cohort needed to evaluate the average-duration obligation. For India, it is the protocols and maps, dated mandates, verification and patrol records, and evidence connecting changed incentives to particular concessions.

These gaps limit explanations of intent more than they prevent identifying behavior. The records already establish a schedule change, distinct administrative stages, a longer authorization, repeated procedural bargains, specific local security arrangements and newly assigned institutional work. Further research should test the proposed mechanisms against those observations and against failed cases. It should also extend the major-power comparison to Russia and Europe rather than treating the US and India as exhaustive.

This assessment draws on public original documents, corporate disclosures, reporting, scholarly texts and an interview-based policy study. Government accounts and company claims remain attributed; reported source chains are not counted as independent witnesses merely because several publications repeat them. No new interviews or private archival access are claimed. Full reading coverage, preserved-file hashes and excluded material are recorded in the supporting registers. The result is a substantial extension of the research dossier, not a completed replacement for Solomon's book.
