# What the Russian comparison changes in the Solomon reassessment

Research date: **10 September 2026**. This assessment extends the [US implementation and India comparison](implementation-and-major-power-reassessment.md) with [Russian border bargaining](russia-border-settlement.md), [Russia–China gas negotiations](russia-gas-bargaining.md), and a [deeper reconstruction of the 2017 biotechnology bargain](../reporting/agricultural-2017-documentary-followup.md). The supporting cases distinguish original documents, participant claims, reporting and scholarly inference. The [reading ledger](../literature/readings/README.md) records exact coverage. This remains research toward an original book-length reassessment.

The strongest additional conclusion is that **the scope of a bargain is itself a central object of negotiation**. Countries can separate territorial settlement from local economic use, one border sector from another, a political commitment from a commercial contract, or review of an application from an import certificate, and that certificate from a developer’s commercial launch. Disagreement at one of these boundaries can look like delay or reversal when a compressed account treats everything as a single deal. Recovering those boundaries gives us better explanations than an outcome alone can supply.

The Russian cases also strengthen Solomon’s emphasis on substantive interests while narrowing the use of psychological shortcuts. Historical grievance can coexist with territorial compromise. A close political relationship can facilitate demanding technical work without guaranteeing agreement on a new gas project. Senior authority becomes more informative when attached to a specified instrument and mandate. None of these observations makes a general national style unnecessary as a hypothesis, but each identifies evidence that a style-based explanation must account for.

## 1. Historical grievance did not fix the terms China would accept

The newly examined 1989 record is valuable because it lets us observe a historical argument and a negotiating basis together. In the published Chinese selection of Deng’s **16 May 1989** conversation with Gorbachev, condemnation of Russia’s historical acquisitions accompanies willingness to use existing treaties as the basis of boundary settlement. Deng also allows each side to state its view without requiring a debate or reply, while recalling China’s earlier demands that Moscow acknowledge the unequal treaties and historical injury. The passage does not establish that recognition had never been demanded or was definitively abandoned. The memoir-derived Russian account preserves Gorbachev’s disagreement with Deng’s historical narrative alongside agreement to normalize relations. Neither is an unedited verbatim transcript; the [paired reading](../literature/readings/fravel-compromise-and-1989-records.md) explains the different provenance. [Deng, printed pp. 292–295](https://ebook.theorychina.org.cn/ebook/upload/storage/files/2022/07/26/b7469def27f222558b43b4f322630ac465633/files/basic-html/page308.html); [Gorbachev memoir excerpt](https://web.unica.it/static/resources/cms/documents/MeetingGorbachevandDengMAY1989.pdf).

The practical question is therefore what recognition of history is being asked to accomplish. A demand for an apology, a demand for territory, a demand to acknowledge unequal status, and an opportunity to state a grievance are different negotiating moves. We should not infer the first three merely because a historical narrative is lengthy or forceful. Nor should we assume that a counterpart’s willingness to listen amounts to accepting the narrative as an operative principle.

A contemporary negotiated text supplies a further check. Paragraph 7 of the **18 May 1989 communiqué** contemplated simultaneous resolution of the eastern and western border sectors. The Chinese original and contemporary English publication agree on this. The eastern agreement was nevertheless signed separately on **16 May 1991**, with subsequent settlements taking additional stages. The public sequence changed; the earlier statement does not by itself prove every Chinese offer had an inflexible all-or-nothing condition. [State Council Gazette, pp. 412–415](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/shuju/1989/gwyb198909.pdf); [Beijing Review, pp. 11–13](https://massline.org/PekingReview/PR1989/PR1989-22.pdf); [1991 treaty](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/web/wjb_673085/zzjg_673183/bjhysws_674671/bhgjty/ldbjty/202303/P020230320538669617544.pdf).

This changes how we test S-P05 and S-P12. Broad principles can organize talks while specific reservations remain negotiable. The relevant observation is which condition was retained, waived, deferred or incorporated into another instrument. To explain why, we still need the decision and draft sequence. A later partial agreement cannot reveal by itself whether domestic urgency, counterpart concessions, technical progress or the difficulty of an outstanding sector caused separation.

The full Fravel reading is useful here because it supplies a competing explanation with limits, rather than merely more examples of compromise. His domestic-insecurity account is not an explanation he applies to every settlement, and he specifically treats the **2004 Russian compromise** as an exception requiring another explanation. Our [critical memo](../literature/readings/fravel-compromise-and-1989-records.md) tests the 1989 sequencing against original publications while keeping the causal attribution separate. These cases do not support treating rising power alone as a sufficient explanation of territorial terms.

## 2. Implementation could accommodate local interests without sharing sovereignty

The border case supplies something the more recent India reconstruction lacks: a published instrument specifying how a settlement could preserve established local economic practices. The **9 December 1999 joint-use agreement** defines a sovereign state’s permission for the other country’s established border residents to continue specified activities. It identifies areas, restricts eligibility, provides for permits and inspections, and sets a five-year use period whose dates would be agreed diplomatically. It also requires recurring consultations. Its two annexes were not published in the recovered copy, so the precise maps, numerical user limits and detailed permit arrangements remain outside this reading. [Published agreement body, Articles 1–12](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/bi-63147.pdf); [source audit](russia-border-settlement.md).

This is more than a reassuring statement about friendship. It provides a mechanism by which actors affected by a new boundary could retain some practical benefits while the states clarified territorial jurisdiction. The distinction matters analytically: sovereignty and use can be allocated differently. Treating every concession to a local user as a reopening of territorial sovereignty would misclassify the bargain.

The author’s fieldwork in the selected Iwashita chapters helps reconstruct local resistance and implementation adjustments. The treaty body independently establishes the available institutional arrangement; it does not independently confirm every interview-based account of why a governor or central official accepted it. Nor does the existence of permission establish how many people used it, whether every consultation occurred, or whether local opposition disappeared. Those require implementation records. [Chapter access and publication](https://src-h.slav.hokudai.ac.jp/coe21/publish/no3_ses/contents.html); [case and reading limits](russia-border-settlement.md).

For S-P25, the important extension is that post-agreement negotiation may **make a settlement workable**, rather than simply erode a commitment. A later instrument can redistribute the costs of implementation, establish verification, or preserve an economic activity under new jurisdiction. To distinguish that from opportunistic reopening, compare its terms with the prior obligation, identify whose rights change, and establish whether the adjustment was accepted by both sides.

This offers a disciplined comparison with India. A border-use arrangement after an agreed boundary and a patrol arrangement along a disputed line are not equivalent legal or operational outcomes. Both may separate local activity from a larger territorial question, but the Russian settlement cannot establish that India’s disengagement arrangements resolved sovereignty or restored every previous form of access. The [India case](india-border-2020-2024.md) retains those distinctions.

The 2004 sequence also supplies unusually concrete evidence of authority. Putin’s **13 October order 479-rp**, recovered in a legal reproduction, accepts a coordinated MFA proposal, identifies three draft instruments and authorizes negotiation and signature upon agreement, with discretion for changes of a nonfundamental character. It names federal and affected regional executive bodies in the coordination process. This is stronger evidence of a formal mandate than a photograph of leaders meeting, though it does not establish each participant’s preferences or the Chinese authorization process. [Order, paragraphs 1–2 and attached drafts](https://rulaws.ru/president/Rasporyazhenie-Prezidenta-RF-ot-13.10.2004-N-479-rp/).

That record improves the test of S-P24 in two ways. It shows what a documented authorization can look like, and it establishes that the mechanism is not uniquely Chinese. Its value as an advance signal is a further question: a present-day reproduction of an order dated before signature does not establish when an outside observer could actually have obtained it. Retrospective explanation and prospective prediction need different information sets.

## 3. A close relationship did not eliminate commercial bargaining

The gas case examines a completed supply project alongside repeatedly advanced but unfinished commercial arrangements. That pairing avoids explaining China’s behavior from a successful summit alone. It also requires careful project identification: the earlier western/Altai proposal, the existing eastern route, the Far Eastern route and the later project through Mongolia are not interchangeable names for one unchanging contract. Their route, resource base and commitment stage must travel with each observation. [Dated gas reconstruction](russia-gas-bargaining.md).

A leader-witnessed agreement on principal terms was already reported in **September 2013** as legally binding, with price explicitly reserved and a year-end contract target. The final supply contract followed in May 2014. Political sponsorship and reported legal force therefore coexisted with a remaining commercial obstacle before the Ukraine crisis; the signed 2013 instrument itself remains unavailable. [Contemporary reporting](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2013/09/05/gazprom-cnpc-agree-on-gas-supply-terms-a27419).

Putin’s **21 May 2014** public answers are unusually revealing about the limits of a triumphant announcement. He described difficult expert negotiations and mutual compromises, distinguished the completed contract from government decisions still required, and discussed construction and investment still ahead. After initially misstating the pricing basis, he corrected it to an oil-and-petroleum-products basket. His account does not disclose the contract’s numerical formula. Dividing a headline lifetime value by planned volumes cannot recover a fixed actual price or measure the discount China extracted. [Kremlin transcript](http://en.kremlin.ru/events/president/news/21064).

The subsequent financing sequence is a separate negotiation margin. Reporting of a proposed Chinese prepayment was conditional; the later account that it was dropped should not be rewritten as proof that a binding payment duty was breached. The [gas case](russia-gas-bargaining.md) follows these statements through their original reporting and specialist analysis. It distinguishes an agreed supply relationship from unresolved or revised financing arrangements and from the eventual physical delivery record.

The later project is a useful limit on interpreting political closeness. A September 2025 announcement described a binding memorandum, but the document’s complete operative text was not recovered. In **September 2026**, Novak’s public account still described discussion of commercial terms and work to help companies finalize agreements, using the newer project name *Power of Baikal*. This establishes an observable stage of continuing work; it does not reveal the Chinese reservation price or prove that every unresolved item was a deliberate attempt to exploit Russian dependence. [Interfax, 3 September](https://www.interfax.ru/amp/1112914) and [4 September 2026](https://www.interfax.ru/amp/1113123); [full status and source limits](russia-gas-bargaining.md).

Chinese planning documents add the buyer’s public policy context. The 2026 materials distinguish preparatory work from construction and emphasize diversified supply and domestic capacity. They are evidence of stated priorities and differentiated project stages, not the unpublished negotiating mandate for an individual price or financing concession. The July sector plan’s domestic pipeline annex should not be treated as an exhaustive list of international gas negotiations. [Original July sector plan](https://zfxxgk.ndrc.gov.cn/web/fileread.jsp?id=XrxvOgd%2FQm5XfSiQjS5UXw%3D%3D&oId=YMUHt3IAigiwOYKlKu0TDA%3D%3D); [other planning records and complete-reading scope](russia-gas-bargaining.md).

The interpretation to test is that a buyer with alternatives can value a strategic relationship and still decline unattractive commercial terms. To establish this in a particular round, obtain the offered price, volume flexibility, financing obligations and delivery schedule; compare credible alternatives available at that time; then trace the response. Dependence measured at the level of whole economies does not tell us which party could more cheaply postpone a particular pipeline.

There is also a useful American control in the **2017 US–China 100-day fact sheet**. Item 4 welcomed Chinese companies’ negotiations with US LNG suppliers while expressly leaving contracts subject to commercial considerations. A diplomatic statement of eligibility and welcome remained separate from a commercial contract; the fact sheet did not itself grant a project export authorization. This is a comparison of instrument design, not evidence that a particular American supply offer caused a particular Chinese refusal of Russian terms. [Contemporary fact sheet reproduced in USTR’s 2017 report, Appendix 3](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Reports/China%202017%20WTO%20Report.pdf).

## 4. The agricultural follow-up exposes another boundary: which applications were covered?

The new 2017 reconstruction identifies four original Chinese certificates, separates them from fourteen renewals in the June list, and distinguishes NBC meetings from later certificate and announcement dates. It also traces the corporate correspondence into a broader channel involving Dow, DuPont and Syngenta. These are direct improvements in the process record. They do not supply Chinese instructions or a complete application cohort. [Documentary follow-up](../reporting/agricultural-2017-documentary-followup.md).

The most consequential dispute concerns the eight applications. The joint text required a meeting by the end of May to evaluate them and specified conditional next steps. It did not unconditionally promise eight approvals by the 100-day deadline. Contemporary industry reporting later attributed to Dow a disagreement with the Chinese ministry about whether its soybean application was covered. The recovered materials neither authenticate the jointly agreed event list nor establish that the unnamed soybean was the later Enlist E3 event. A finding that China departed from an accepted commitment would need evidence of that earlier common scope and the later departure. A changed Chinese interpretation is a distinct claim requiring evidence of its earlier and later readings. [Joint release](https://wjb.mof.gov.cn/gongzuodongtai/201705/t20170512_2599646.htm); [original July reporting](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/9566-china-oks-two-gmo-products-for-import).

This adds a concrete qualification to S-P19. Invoking a previous bargain is observable; distorting it requires comparison with what both sides actually accepted. Dow’s understanding of which applications US Commerce considered included may be accurate yet still fall short of proving Chinese assent. Any contemporaneously exchanged list or schedule would therefore be more valuable than another general complaint about delay.

## 5. Which conclusions stay, and which need updating?

These are proposition-level judgments within the examined cases, not a score for China generally.

| Solomon proposition | Current judgment after the Russian and 2017 follow-ups | Required update to its use |
|---|---|---|
| **S-C01; S-C18:** political relationship and substantive interests condition tactics | **Retained within scope** | Specify which interests relationship preservation serves, and which costs a party is still unwilling to bear. Closeness is not a price formula. |
| **S-P05:** broad principles and flexible political documents | **Narrowed to document and stage** | Separate a shared framework from precise operative provisions, acknowledged differences and reserved questions. Do not infer vagueness from a communiqué alone. |
| **S-P12:** partial settlements with reservations | **Retained for observed partial settlement** | Follow package separation and separately allocated rights. The additional proposition about avoiding conspicuous reversal requires its own evidence. |
| **S-P19:** invocation and favorable reinterpretation of the record | **Invocation observed; favorable reinterpretation and distortion unresolved** | Compare China’s earlier and later readings to establish reinterpretation; establish common original scope before alleging repudiation of an agreed commitment. |
| **S-P24:** senior intervention together with concrete drafting as a possible signal of authorized near-agreement | **Better evidence standards; general predictive validity unresolved** | Inspect the actual instrument and delegation. A memorandum or senior meeting alone does not test the full proposition. Russian authorization evidence also tests how distinctive the mechanism is to China. |
| **S-P25:** bargaining continues during implementation | **Continuing-arena component retained; asymmetric demands unresolved** | Separate consensual accommodation, operational consultation, unresolved commercial terms and alleged nonperformance. These have different implications for reliability. |
| **S-C19:** rigidity usually indicates leadership conflict | **Narrowed; unsuitable as a stand-alone diagnosis** | Test mandate limits, substantive disagreement, domestic costs and technical work separately. Public rigidity does not identify a factional veto. |

The original book and Freeman’s commentary already recognize interests, institutions and learning. The update’s contribution should be better specified conditions and evidence about changed instruments, not presenting those insights as discoveries made only in 2026. Nor does finding an old detailed treaty refute later institutional development: it establishes that precision itself cannot be treated as a wholly new Chinese capability.

## 6. What this comparison can—and cannot—establish

Holding the counterpart broadly constant across two Russian issue areas is useful, but it does not control for time, leadership, the Soviet collapse, military conditions, commercial infrastructure or the value of the disputed assets. Comparing Russia with India and the United States adds variation, not a representative sample. The cases were deliberately chosen for process visibility and contrasting outcomes. They can expose an overbroad claim or demonstrate an available mechanism; they cannot estimate how frequently China uses it or isolate the effect of nationality.

The next decisive evidence follows directly from the remaining ambiguities: the 1989–1991 draft and authorization sequence; the missing border-use annexes and consultation records; the gas memoranda, pricing and financing offers; and the jointly exchanged 2017 application schedule and event-level review records. Some will remain inaccessible. Meanwhile, the public record supports a more precise account of how agreements are divided, authorized and implemented—and prevents several consequential claims from being made more confidently than their sources permit.
