# Chinese public record of US trade bargaining, 2018–2020

Research pass: 8 September 2026. Read with the accompanying [source register](../sources/us-trade-2018-2020-chinese-register.json), the US reporting reconstruction, and the separate examination of the signed agreement. This memo reconstructs public statements and their documentary status. It does not claim access to Chinese negotiating instructions or the disputed spring 2019 drafts.

The strongest finding is a change in publicly acceptable **scope and sequencing**, alongside repeated arguments about what constituted an agreement. In May 2019 Liu He presented removal of all additional tariffs as necessary for agreement; by December Chinese officials endorsed a first-stage settlement with partial tariff relief. The public record establishes that difference. It does not, by itself, explain the internal decision or demonstrate that a comprehensive settlement and a partial settlement had identical conditions. That distinction prevents both an uncritical narrative of Chinese consistency and an equally premature diagnosis of simple capitulation.

## 1. May–June 2018: joint text, public interpretation, conditional results

The May 19 joint statement followed May 17–18 Washington talks. Its seven paragraphs identify the delegations, substantial US goods-deficit reduction, increased Chinese purchases, later discussion of agricultural and energy details, manufacturing and services, intellectual property—including proposed legal revisions—and investment and continuing communication. It contains **no explicit tariff suspension, no purchase total, and no deadline**. The Chinese consular reproduction is the actual published joint wording, rather than a retrospective description. Its posting date is May 21, despite a migrated URL containing May 29. [Joint statement, complete text](https://chicago.china-consulate.gov.cn/chn/zzxw/201805/t20180529_4611271.htm) (`CN-TRADE-JOINT20180519`).

Liu's contemporaneous interpretation was more expansive. Xinhua reported his May 19 morning Washington remarks describing the principal achievement as consensus against a trade war and further tariffs. The same article presents increased trade as meeting Chinese needs, stresses exporters' competitiveness and consumers' willingness to buy, and anticipates further difficulties in a structural relationship. This is a direct source for what China's chief negotiator publicly claimed, but it is a reporter's account of remarks, not an authenticated bilateral tariff undertaking. [Xinhua, May 20 publication, complete article](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/leaders/2018-05/20/c_1122857996.htm) (`CN-TRADE-LIU20180519`).

The comparison matters: absence from the joint statement does not disprove an oral understanding. It identifies the evidence needed to establish its content, duration, conditions and authorization. Liu's statement and the joint text should be paired with contemporaneous US statements, rather than allowing either country's later narrative to supply the missing terms.

After the June 2–3 Beijing talks with Wilbur Ross, China's June 3 unilateral statement reported concrete progress but reserved final confirmation of details. It made all negotiated outcomes contingent on avoiding a trade war and said US sanctions, including tariffs, would prevent those outcomes from taking effect. This is explicit prospective conditionality. It should not automatically be coded as repudiation of an already operative comprehensive agreement. [Xinhua statement, MOFCOM reproduction, all three paragraphs](https://mds.mofcom.gov.cn/tpxw/art/2018/art_06bf885645c94d8c95bc4e037fea2fca.html) (`CN-TRADE-STATEMENT20180603`).

These two comparisons trace consequential news claims to original public texts: **the reported tariff truce** to Liu and the joint statement, and **the conditional purchase package** to the June declaration. Neither reconstruction relies solely on reporting that a round succeeded or failed.

## 2. The 2018 white paper: an argument about responsibility

The September 24 white paper's preface locates trade within longstanding bilateral mechanisms. Section IV(v) treats the May understanding and subsequent US measures as evidence of American unreliability. Section VI combines resistance to tariff pressure with willingness to negotiate, intellectual-property reform, further opening at China's chosen pace, and other trade relationships. These are publicly authorized positions and justifications. The selected sections do not establish the actual reasons for American decisions, the credibility of Chinese alternatives, or whether reform commitments were implemented. [2018 white paper, preface and Sections IV and VI](https://www.locpg.gov.cn/jsdt/2018-09/26/c_129961268.htm) (`CN-TRADE-WP2018`).

Its retrospective presentation should therefore be controlled against the May and June texts. In particular, collapsing a joint statement, a publicly asserted oral understanding, and later conditional negotiating results into a single “agreement” would erase the very distinctions the reconstruction needs. The accusation of American bad faith is evidence of China's case against its counterpart; it is not an independently established verdict.

## 3. Argentina and May 2019: which understandings were being defended?

Wang Yi's December 1 Argentina briefing, published December 2, described a stop to new tariffs, purchases suited to Chinese demand, reciprocal market opening, and further talks toward removal of all additional tariffs. It called the leader-level result a set of principles from which teams should develop a concrete agreement. This short readout supplies neither a purchase figure nor a 90-day deadline. Their absence here cannot establish their absence from private discussion or other announcements. [MFA readout, both substantive paragraphs](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbz_673089/zyhd_673091/201812/t20181202_7579186.shtml) (`CN-TRADE-WANG20181201`).

After the May 9–10, 2019 Washington round, Liu publicly identified three remaining concerns: removal of all additional tariffs, realistic purchases, and a balanced text respecting national dignity. His purchases claim was unusually specific about the baseline: he said the two sides had already reached numerical consensus in Argentina and should not arbitrarily change it. The article does not disclose the figures. Liu also challenged the propriety of accusing China of backtracking while talks continued. [Xinhua, May 11, third substantive paragraph](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/2019-05/11/c_1124481543.htm) (`CN-TRADE-LIU201905`).

This makes the Argentina figures a precise unresolved target. Which products, baseline, period, market conditions and qualifications did the purported numerical consensus cover? Was it a leader's aspiration, an agreed schedule, or a negotiating reference point? Without that record, a later difference in reported totals cannot determine who moved first or whether the figures are commensurable.

“Balanced text” also needs substantive reconstruction. It might concern reciprocal obligations, domestic legislative commitments, enforcement, sequencing, presentation, or some combination. The public phrase does not justify reducing the dispute to a cosmetic question of face. Conversely, invoking sovereignty does not show that negotiators lacked room to change particular provisions.

## 4. June 2019: normal draft revision or reversal of agreed obligations?

The June 2 white paper assigns the setbacks to the United States and describes three American reversals. Its most consequential process argument appears in Section II(iv): proposing changes to wording and content during unfinished negotiations is ordinary practice, and American demands had also changed. Section II(iii) criticizes demands involving Chinese sovereign affairs; II(iv) repeats the three conditions. Section III(iii), however, expressly rejects expecting one agreement to resolve every problem. [2019 white paper, Sections II and III](https://asean.china-mission.gov.cn/chn/lmbf/zgsy/201906/t20190604_8875502.htm) (`CN-TRADE-WP2019`).

The wording does **not** establish that China left the spring draft unchanged. It contests the status and interpretation of changes. A report that Chinese officials deleted provisions and this official argument that revisions remained legitimate can both describe aspects of the same episode. Resolving the dispute requires the versions, reservations, approval conditions and exchanges accompanying them. Repetition across a white paper, ministry briefing and state-media story would not produce three independent witnesses to the same private event.

The document also leaves room to investigate the later move toward a narrower settlement without pretending that “first stage” was already a settled negotiating plan in June. Permission to leave some issues unresolved and authorization of a particular partial bargain are different propositions.

## 5. December 2019–January 2020: announcing and legitimizing a partial settlement

At the December 13 late-night briefing, Wang Shouwen announced agreement on text while reserving legal review, translation checks and signing arrangements. Liao Min specified **some** planned and existing US tariffs would be removed, distinguishing text from associated tariff arrangements. Ning Jizhe declined to confirm a Reuters question's $50 billion agricultural figure pending review. Liao said phase-two timing remained for discussion and depended on phase-one implementation; the published answer did not substantively resolve CNN's enforcement question. [Complete SCIO briefing, MOFCOM transcript; especially 23:07, 23:16, 23:42 and 23:48](https://interview.mofcom.gov.cn/detail/201912/ff8080816e67e5c2016f0cb2a51c00b0.html) (`CN-TRADE-SCIO20191213`).

Questions in a transcript are not official confirmations. Announcement of an agreed text is also distinguishable from signature, entry into force and implementation. A comparison of promised tariff relief requires the contemporaneous tariff instruments as well as the trade text; those two components should not be silently merged.

After signing on January 15, Liu's published briefing again described partial tariff removal, linked the settlement to economic confidence and the broader bilateral relationship, and placed reform and opening within continued Party leadership and the socialist system. His assertion of WTO and market compatibility is an official legal-policy claim, not an independent assessment. [Xinhua published remarks and all four Q&As, January 16](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/leaders/2020-01/16/c_1125469423.htm) (`CN-TRADE-LIU20200115`).

The comparison supports an observable evolution in how agreement was packaged: a comprehensive tariff-removal demand was followed by a publicly defended partial settlement. To explain that change, test several mechanisms against decision evidence: narrowing the issues, changing economic costs, counterpart incentives, altered expectations about enforcement, and political authorization. The official account's domestic-reform framing identifies how the deal was justified. It does not establish which mechanism caused acceptance or whether the framing neutralized domestic opposition.

## Implications for the Solomon update and the next evidence

The 1999 WTO pilot already showed why a “kept versus broken promise” classification needs a document baseline. Its signed April 10 MOU expressly recorded qualifications and open issues. Here the disputed spring 2019 drafts have not been acquired. The comparison therefore strengthens the research method, not a claim that the episodes are substantively identical. See the [1999 synthesis](wto-1999-synthesis.md).

Solomon's propositions on partial commitments and records of prior undertakings (`S-P12`, `S-P19`) remain useful questions. His attention to principles and final drafting (`S-P05`, `S-P24`) should be applied to specific obligations and approval stages. The public material cannot reveal whether Liu's May position reflected an inflexible mandate, a tactical demand, or disagreement within Beijing. Nor does it test the implementation proposition (`S-P25`): signature and a promise of compliance are only the starting point for that investigation.

The next bounded comparison should seek:

1. The May 2018 tariff-pause terms and June purchase schedules, including conditions and final-confirmation requirements.
2. Argentina purchase figures and their agreed qualifications, with corresponding American records.
3. Successive spring 2019 drafts, cover messages, brackets and approval reservations; distinguish legislative wording, substance, reciprocity and enforcement.
4. The authorization of a partial settlement and the associated tariff arrangements, followed by the first implementation disputes.

The corpus comprises nine public texts. All substantive text in the short statements and published December/January briefings was read. The 2019 white paper's body and notes were read completely; two graphics were excluded. For 2018, the complete preface and Sections IV and VI body text, plus notes 53–75, were read; figures and other sections were excluded. No claim here rests on an unread graphic. The November 7, 2019 MOFCOM briefing is a further lead: its original URL returned 404, so derivative quotations are not treated as primary evidence in this memo. Exact versions, hashes and reading scopes are preserved in the register; archived source files remain outside Git.
