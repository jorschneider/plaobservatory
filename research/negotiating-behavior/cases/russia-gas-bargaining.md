# Russia–China gas bargaining: a working pipeline and a succession of incomplete bargains

Research cut-off: **10 September 2026**. Latest substantive reporting used: 9 September. This is a comparison of commitment stages, not a reconstruction of confidential negotiating instructions. The [source register](../sources/russia-gas-register.json) records 36 sources, exact reading scopes and preserved-copy hashes; the [move ledger](russia-gas-moves.json) separates statements, agreements, construction and deliveries.

The central finding is that a valuable political relationship can sustain both successful implementation and prolonged disagreement over a new project. The May 2014 eastern-route contract led to operating infrastructure and substantial deliveries. Repeated advances on a western route and then the Mongolia-transit Power of Siberia 2 project did not establish a completed supply bargain by the latest public evidence. On 3 September 2026, Alexander Novak said commercial terms were still being discussed and final routing depended on company agreement. The next day he described further contract preparation. These admissions are stronger evidence of incompletion than silence in summit communiqués. They do not reveal which Chinese demands were decisive. [Interfax, 3 September (RUS-G32)](https://www.interfax.ru/amp/1112914), [4 September (RUS-G33)](https://www.interfax.ru/amp/1113123).

This case qualifies a simple reading of Solomon's senior-contact and patience propositions. It supports his more cautious view that interests and official discipline can outweigh interpersonal technique. It also corrects a misleading premise: commercial memoranda, government planning entries and bilateral sales contracts are different commitments, even when leaders witness them and participants describe them as legally binding.

## The eastern bargain: important work preceded the geopolitical shock

The eastern deal did not originate in Russia's post-Crimea isolation. On **5 September 2013**, with Putin and Xi present at the G20 summit, Gazprom and CNPC reached an agreement on principal terms. Contemporary reporting described it as legally binding and covering matters including volumes, commencement and minimum purchases, while leaving price outstanding. Gazprom hoped for a final contract before year-end. That target passed. The episode supplies both a pre-2014 baseline and an early warning that a binding document can leave the commercially decisive issue unsettled. [Medetsky, contemporary report (RUS-G01)](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2013/09/05/gazprom-cnpc-agree-on-gas-supply-terms-a27419).

During Putin's May 2014 China visit, price remained unresolved on the first day. Peskov said negotiations continued; the record includes Gazprom optimism as well as reported financing disagreements. This was a live negotiating round, not a retrospectively inferred impasse. [20 May report (RUS-G02)](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2014/05/20/russia-china-still-haggling-on-gas-price-as-putin-arrives-in-beijing-a35578/pdf). Reuters's next-day reporting placed the eventual agreement in Russia's deteriorating Western relationship but also included divergent judgments about its commercial attractiveness. Neither that setting nor anonymous reports of a Russian price floor establishes how much the Ukraine crisis changed the final formula. [Reuters, 21 May (RUS-G05)](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/china-russias-gazprom-sign-gas-101157004.html).

The **21 May 2014** Chinese account is unusually useful about obligations. CNPC and Gazprom signed the thirty-year eastern-route purchase-and-sale contract. Supply was then envisaged from 2018, with annual growth to 38 bcm. The report divided infrastructure responsibilities: Gazprom would develop Russian fields, processing and transmission; CNPC would build the Chinese pipeline and associated storage. This is evidence of a concrete division of work, although the actual contract was not published. [National Energy Administration/Xinhua, complete article (RUS-G04)](https://www.nea.gov.cn/2014-05/22/c_133352383.htm).

Putin's **21 May transcript** gives a more balanced account than the familiar story of unilateral Chinese extraction. He described expert negotiations continuing until 3:30 a.m., acknowledged hard Chinese bargaining, and said both parties compromised. He distinguished the completed deal from government decisions still required. His roughly $55 billion Russian and at least $20 billion Chinese investment figures were estimates of project investment, not an announcement that China had advanced the latter amount to Gazprom. At the transcript's end, prompted by Peskov, Putin corrected his earlier explanation: the price mechanism was tied to a basket of oil and petroleum products. Read the correction, not merely the opening answer. [Kremlin transcript, gas answers and final correction (RUS-G03)](http://en.kremlin.ru/events/president/news/21064).

Miller's **$400 billion** headline valuation likewise does not disclose the coefficients, base price, review provisions or minimum-payment obligations. Interfax's division of this figure by maximum lifetime volume produces an arithmetic average, not an authenticated contractual price. Treating the deal as a fixed-price promise at that implied average would erase its oil-linked structure and ramp-up. [Interfax, 21 May (RUS-G06)](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/43023/).

Financing demonstrates why apparent post-signature retreat requires careful coding. On **23 May**, **Alexander Medvedev, Gazprom Export's chief executive**, said a $25 billion prepayment was agreed in principle, with details under discussion. He was not Russian prime minister Dmitry Medvedev. [Reuters (RUS-G07)](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/russia-china-agree-25bln-advance-payment-in-gas-supply-deal). By November, Miller said an advance had been a possible element of a price reduction, but was no longer being considered following price settlement. Possible Chinese bank loans were still discussions, not completed financing. [Panin, reporting Miller via Interfax (RUS-G08)](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2014/11/10/china-gets-upper-hand-in-gas-deals-amid-russia-west-tensions-a41201). The defensible finding is that an incompletely specified financing option disappeared. There is no acquired document proving a binding Chinese prepayment obligation was breached.

Implementation involved additional instruments. Gazprom's **17 December 2015** regulatory release described the cross-border design/construction and communications agreements, technical procedures and joint working groups, under the eastern sales agreement. On the same occasion, western-route commercial talks were still scheduled for January 2016. The contrast appears within one corporate record. [Gazprom RNS 4589J, release and Background (RUS-G10)](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/gazprom-and-cnpc-signed-an-agreement-mn748ixhiygkf3q.html).

On **4 July 2017**, the companies announced an agreed delivery start of **20 December 2019**; actual first pipeline supply began **2 December 2019**. The former is supported by contemporary multi-agency reporting, the latter by Gazprom's later corporate account. Neither date should be inferred from the 2014 announcement's 2018 expectation. [2017 report (RUS-G11)](https://www.rferl.org/a/russia-gazprom-start-supplying-gas-china-power-siberia-pipeline-december-2019/28596838.html), [Gazprom release, Notes (RUS-G12)](https://www.euro-petrole.com/gazprom-and-cnpc-sign-agreement-to-supply-russian-pipeline-gas-to-china-via-far-eastern-route-n-i-23387).

By April 2026, the operational record included continued deliveries during scheduled maintenance, using specially agreed quantities rather than a complete interruption. Interfax, citing Gazprom, reported the 2024 and 2025 annual volumes shown above. This demonstrates an operating relationship with coordination and adjustment; it does not independently audit every contractual obligation or establish either firm's profitability. [Maintenance and throughput report, 3 April 2026 (RUS-G26)](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1081809).

## The unsuccessful western rounds are part of the evidence

The **9 November 2014** Altai framework and **8 May 2015** heads of agreement concerned a different supply geography from the successful eastern route. Paik's April 2015 OIES analysis explained competing route and market considerations and treated stronger political support as a reason to expect a western bargain. Its discussion of Wang Yi's March remarks was a contemporary interpretation, not inside knowledge of a final authorization. Reading it against the later record is valuable precisely because the anticipated conversion into a sales contract did not promptly occur. [Paik, selected printed pp.7–10 and 40–41 (RUS-G09)](https://www.oxfordenergy.org/wpcms/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/WPM-59.pdf).

The December 2015 corporate release confirmed that, despite the heads of agreement, commercial negotiations remained ahead. Leader attendance and repeated senior support therefore belong in the denominator of attempted agreements, not only in a gallery of successful summits. They may sustain negotiations without resolving the allocation of transport costs, demand risk or price exposure. The failed forecast also disciplines this study: a plausible geopolitical interpretation is not a verified causal account.

Later use of the PS2 name can conceal a substantive redesign. The Mongolia-transit proposal offers a route toward different Chinese demand centers from the original Xinjiang entry. The full-read **Yermakov–Meidan March 2022 OIES paper** distinguishes Russian engineering work in Mongolia from a necessary future Chinese supply agreement. It also shows why eastern and western gas cannot simply be relabeled or redirected without major infrastructure. Its alternative demand and import scenarios are hypotheses about market space, not proof that Beijing instructed CNPC to delay. The paper's map retains an older Altai/PS2 label, so its figure must be read against its text. [OIES, pp.2–5 and scenario figures (RUS-G13)](https://www.oxfordenergy.org/wpcms/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Russia-and-China-Expand-Their-Gas-Deal-Key-Implications.pdf).

The **4 February 2022 Far Eastern sales agreement** was a real additional commercial step, separate from that Mongolia proposal. Gazprom announced a second long-term sales contract with a 10 bcm annual increment. Its release supplied neither the duration nor start date sometimes attributed to it in secondary accounts. The same document's reference to a completed Soyuz Vostok feasibility study did not convert that engineering milestone into a third sales agreement. This is a particularly useful control against portraying every post-2014 energy announcement as either empty symbolism or a completed gas sale.

## PS2: political consensus repeatedly preceded commercial closure

On **21 March 2023**, Putin said almost all parameters of the PS2 agreement had been agreed. The Chinese-hosted joint statement committed to advancing research and consultation on the new trilateral pipeline. Those formulations have different evidentiary force. The joint text did not publish price, purchases or financing, and the unilateral claim should not fill those gaps. [Interfax on Putin (RUS-G14)](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/88903/), [joint statement, section III (RUS-G15)](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/ziliao_674904/zt_674979/ywzt_675099/2023nzt/xjpdelsjxgsfw/zxxx/202303/t20230322_11046188.shtml).

In **June 2024**, the Financial Times reported, using three people familiar with talks, Chinese demands for prices close to Russian domestic levels and purchases covering only a small portion of the proposed capacity. The accessible Reuters article explicitly summarized the FT. It is one reporting chain, not two independent confirmations. The FT full text was unavailable here, and neither source supplies an authenticated Chinese term sheet. This is evidence of a reported dispute over price and volume risk, not a known Chinese reservation price. [Reuters's complete four-paragraph summary (RUS-G16)](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/latest/Russia-China-gas-pipeline-deal-stalls-over-Beijing-s-price-demands-FT-46882795/).

On **2 September 2025**, Miller announced a legally binding memorandum concerning construction of PS2 and Soyuz Vostok, at 50 bcm annually. He separately announced increases on the existing eastern and contracted Far Eastern routes. The description of legal force deserves recording, but the MOU itself was not obtained. Its enforceable clauses, conditions and remedies therefore remain unknown. It cannot be assumed to contain a finished purchase obligation simply because its overall label is “binding.” [Interfax's complete Miller account (RUS-G17)](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/113563/).

That day the Chinese spokesperson referred detailed questions to competent authorities. On **3 September**, Putin defended a commercially rational, long-term pricing approach and the political consensus. These records establish neither Chinese rejection nor full commercial completion. Referral can reflect ordinary allocation of expertise; a president's defense of a market formula is not the formula itself. [MFA gas question and answer (RUS-G18)](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/fyrbt_673021/jzhsl_673025/202509/t20250902_11700474.shtml), [Kremlin, gas question and complete response (RUS-G19)](http://en.kremlin.ru/events/president/news/77914).

Contemporary specialist interpretations sharpen competing explanations but do not remove those limits. Corbeau, Downs and Mitrova emphasize Russian export options, Chinese portfolio leverage and geopolitical signaling. Hashimoto distinguishes the memorandum from final commercial arrangements. However, their descriptions of what a legally binding MOU normally entails are not access to this document's actual clauses. The precise take-or-pay percentages circulating through FIEF-linked commentary should likewise remain attributed estimates or reports. [Columbia article and note 1 (RUS-G20)](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/power-of-siberia-2-russias-pivot-chinas-leverage-and-global-gas-implications/), [IEEJ, complete six-page paper (RUS-G21)](https://eneken.ieej.or.jp/data/12728.pdf).

## What the 2026 record adds

China's **March 2026 Fifteenth Five-Year Plan**, chapter7, energy infrastructure box5, item07, says to build the Far Eastern gas pipeline and advance **preparatory work** on the China–Russia middle-route pipeline. The phrase is embedded in an image in the acquired PDF and was visually checked at **PDF p.14**, against an official Beijing legislature summary. It is a Chinese project-stage distinction, not a published Chinese refusal. [Plan PDF, p.14 (RUS-G22)](https://cdn.climatepolicyradar.org/navigator/CHN/2026/15th-five-year-plan_b4e98362c0810f1fc3fc7524fa546866.pdf), [official summary (RUS-G23)](https://www.bjrd.gov.cn/zyfb/zt/14jqgrd04chybjdbthdztbd/fjshww/202603/t20260316_4557421.html).

The **20 May 2026 Kremlin joint statement's energy paragraph** endorses cooperation and stable cross-border infrastructure without specifying a new gas sales bargain. Novak that day said political decisions had been reached earlier while companies were finishing contracts. The next day's Chinese press conference added no commercial terms. RFE/RL's contemporary reporting of Peskov's more optimistic route/construction comments runs through TASS; it should not displace the explicit distinction between political decisions and company contracts. [Kremlin energy paragraph (RUS-G27)](http://kremlin.ru/supplement/6487), [Novak via Vesti/Interfax (RUS-G28)](https://m.interfax.ru/1090720), [MFA Reuters question (RUS-G29)](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/fyrbt_673021/jzhsl_673025/202605/t20260521_11915149.shtml), [RFE/RL (RUS-G30)](https://www.rferl.org/a/putin-xi-power-of-siberia-iran-war-trump-china-russia/33760781.html).

More revealing than another summit slogan is the **3 July 2026 NDRC–NEA oil and gas plan**, issued as **发改能源〔2026〕1055号**. The complete sixteen-page text combines domestic production, diversified imports and flexible operation. Its annex includes Far Eastern domestic connections and expansion associated with the existing eastern route. Crucially, annex notes limit its listed scope to interprovincial Chinese pipelines and allow adjustments to routes, technical parameters and pace. Absence of PS2 from that annex cannot demonstrate rejection of an international project. Nor are its domestic gas-power contracting recommendations instructions for CNPC's import negotiations. [Issuing notice (RUS-G24)](https://zfxxgk.ndrc.gov.cn/web/iteminfo.jsp?id=20656), [plan, pp.2,7–8,15–16 (RUS-G25)](https://zfxxgk.ndrc.gov.cn/web/fileread.jsp?id=XrxvOgd%2FQm5XfSiQjS5UXw%3D%3D&oId=YMUHt3IAigiwOYKlKu0TDA%3D%3D).

Xi and Putin met again on **31 August**, maintaining the relationship's political value. Yet Novak's September statements still distinguished commercial agreement from support for the project. The Chinese **4 September Ding–Novak commission account** recorded signed meeting minutes and called for addressing enterprise requests and sustainable oil/gas trade; it disclosed no PS2 sales terms. Interfax calls the meeting the seventeenth commission session and MFA the twenty-third. This unresolved numbering discrepancy does not erase their common date and participants, but counsels against mechanically merging their descriptions. An Interfax quotation of Ding also appears to conflate the eastern and Far Eastern route labels; it is not used here to date operation. [Chinese leader account (RUS-G31)](https://eu.china-mission.gov.cn/eng/mhs/202609/t20260901_12013552.htm), [MFA commission account (RUS-G34)](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/zyxw/202609/t20260904_12016498.shtml), [Interfax commission remarks (RUS-G36)](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1113114).

Finally, **9 September SCMP reporting** described continuing price disagreements using multiple unnamed sources. Only its public opening portion was read. It adds a fresh reported commercial dispute, while the named Russian official evidence already establishes incompletion. The anonymous price figures cannot responsibly be promoted into a verified final offer, actual tariff or explanation of Chinese intent. [Wong, partially accessible report (RUS-G35)](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3366888/power-siberia-2-pipeline-talks-stall-over-china-russia-price-dispute-sources).

## Causal assessment and the precise Solomon update

The strongest material explanation is **project-specific outside options and risk allocation**. A buyer can value secure overland gas while declining to commit prematurely to a particular price, route, minimum purchase or financing burden. Domestic output, other pipeline suppliers and LNG create alternatives whose relevance varies with location and time. Russia's loss of Western markets increases the importance of an eastern outlet, but does not itself prove a Chinese strategy of extracting concessions through delay. The missing discriminating evidence is dated internal instructions or draft changes showing that an otherwise acceptable bargain was withheld to exploit a specific Russian deadline.

The strongest political explanation is that both states value continued cooperation even when a new transaction is not ready. This is consistent with the successful eastern route, a second Far Eastern contract, technical coordination and repeated political endorsement alongside unresolved PS2 terms. A memorandum may preserve an option and organize subsequent work. Calling it either a sham or a completed sale skips the central question: exactly which obligations changed at that stage?

The strongest rival to strategic patience is **commercial and technical uncertainty**. The deferred Altai route, a redesigned Mongolia route, costly dedicated infrastructure and evolving Chinese demand all create reasons to renegotiate project parameters. Conversely, public Russian near-completion announcements might seek political reassurance or momentum. The record establishes that Russia made them; it does not prove their intended audience effect. China's comparatively sparse disclosures do not uniquely identify cultural indirection.

These are within-case judgments tied to the [registered Solomon propositions](../book/process-claims.json) and [context/Freeman claims](../book/context-claims.json). Confidence is high about public instrument stages and established eastern operation; medium about reported bargaining constraints; low about exact price demands, concessions, intent and the legal contents of undisclosed instruments. The remaining work is to acquire actual agreements or authoritative clause descriptions, dated draft/offtake and financing changes, and contemporaneous company or government records of why offers were rejected. Until then, this case supports a more institutional and commercially precise account of bargaining, rather than a claim that China's negotiating character either remained timeless or changed wholesale.
