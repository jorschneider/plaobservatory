# Chinese sources: access, provenance, and reading status

Audit date: 5 September 2026. Source identifiers and retrieval details are in [chinese-source-register.json](chinese-source-register.json). This is an audit of an initial corpus, not a claim to have read all its contents.

## The accession documentary collection

MOFCOM's [WTO accession library](https://sms.mofcom.gov.cn/liberary/index.html) provides *中国加入世界贸易组织谈判文件资料选编* (*Basic Instruments and Selected Documents on the Negotiations for China's Accession to the World Trade Organization*), compiled by the ministry's WTO department, edited by 索必成, China Commerce and Trade Press, 2013. The general volume's editorial note is dated December 2012.

This is **20 physical volumes**, arranged as five general volumes, nine multilateral volumes, and six bilateral volumes, totaling over 20,000 pages. The editors describe selection from accession archives covering 1946–2001, concentrated in 1986–2001. It is a published selection, not unrestricted access to the archive. Most documents are facsimiles, some were reset, and some markings survive. Asterisks identify documents shortened in print with additional material on a companion CD. General volume 1, editorial note pp. v–vi (PDF 8–9), was visually checked.

The foreword's description of digitizing the archive and preserving originals should not be read as a claim that every underlying file is publicly accessible. The edition exposes genuine negotiating documents, but selection, omissions, editorial framing, and translation remain research questions. The companion CD has not been acquired.

Page systems matter: in general volume 1, the examined main-text printed page is PDF page minus 35; in bilateral volume 17 it is PDF page minus 7. Cite the printed volume page, PDF page, document number, and document date. Original documents may also have their own pagination. Do not transfer offsets between volumes.

The general narrative is mostly extractable Chinese, but many digits and Latin characters are corrupted by font encoding. The bilateral volumes have searchable editorial text around scans whose bodies may extract only a document number. Search hits therefore substantially understate their content. Consequential terms in this dossier were read from rendered images.

## Chinese participant history

[中国“入世”谈判风云录](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/dl/sms/04.pdf), edited by 张向晨 and 索必成, People's Publishing House, June 2023, ISBN 9787010257013, is a separate 550-page PDF. Its table of contents identifies accounts by participants in law, customs, agriculture, finance, distribution, and central negotiating coordination. The front matter describes gathering recollections around accession's twentieth anniversary; chapter dates and genres must be checked individually.

The key initial selection is 邓红国, “我亲历的加入WTO金融部门谈判,” printed pp.284–315. The [financial participant note](wto-financial-participant-note.md) records the full chapter's reading and its description of internal approvals. Other chapters have been located and selectively sampled, not fully audited. This is a valuable expansion beyond chief-negotiator memoirs, but it is an edited retrospective collection with a commemorative and institutional purpose. Hosting by MOFCOM does not convert recollection into a contemporaneous instruction.

The overlap in institutional authorship between the 2013 documentary collection and the 2023 participant history should be visible in the source map. It does not make the sources useless; it prevents treating them as wholly independent Chinese accounts.

White papers are only one strand. The next collection pass should add ministry press conferences, original negotiating texts, legal instruments, Party and state institutional records, contemporaneous Chinese reporting, and accounts from officials outside the trade ministry. A white paper's omission is not proof that the omitted action did not occur.

## Immediate source gaps

The highest-value gap is the complete **10 April 1999 MOU with attachments 1 and 2**. The published page gives signatures and substantive qualifications, but not the attached schedules and protocol commitments. Without these, a precise April–November comparison cannot be completed. Next are authenticated successive November packages, the final bilateral schedules, and Chinese authorization records. See the [specific request brief](../reporting/wto-1999-next-requests.md).

Downloaded source files and extracted full text are held outside this repository. The register records hashes for acquired PDFs so later readers can identify the edition examined without committing copyrighted source books.
