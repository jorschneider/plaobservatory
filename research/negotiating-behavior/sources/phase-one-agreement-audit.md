# Phase One: what the signed agreement actually institutionalized

**8 September 2026.** This is a bounded reading of the January 15, 2020 agreement, with Chinese–English checks on implementation, purchases, dispute settlement and final provisions. It is an analysis of the published instrument, not a reconstruction of who proposed each clause. The [reading register](phase-one-agreement-register.json) records exact coverage and exclusions. Read alongside the [bargaining and implementation synthesis](../cases/us-trade-2018-2026-synthesis.md).

The central finding is that detailed legal obligations coexist with senior political control over unresolved disputes. That combination gives a more useful description than either a wholly informal relationship bargain or a conversion to independent legal enforcement. The agreement makes further negotiation part of implementation, while specifying what parties must do, how they may complain, and where interpretation remains contested.

## 1. Domestic implementation form and substantive commitments

Chapter 1 contains detailed changes to trade-secret protection, judicial procedures and intellectual-property enforcement. Examples include the staged criminal-enforcement threshold in Article 1.7, confidentiality duties in 1.9, and penalties in 1.27. Article 1.34 nevertheless leaves each party to select appropriate implementation methods within its legal system and practice, including proposing legislative amendments where necessary. Article 1.35 requires a Chinese action plan within 30 working days of entry into force, specifying measures and effective dates. These are simultaneous provisions, not mutually exclusive interpretations. [English agreement, Chapter 1](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf); [Chinese agreement, Articles 1.34–1.36, printed 1-17–1-18](https://www.gov.cn/guowuyuan/2020-01/16/5469650/files/0637e57d99ea4f968454206af8782dd7.pdf).

This helps interpret—but cannot resolve—the reported May 2019 conflict over legal amendments versus administrative implementation. The signed text demonstrates an accepted way to combine domestic procedural discretion with obligations. It does not disclose the earlier draft, the changes between versions, or who obtained a concession. Nor does discretion over means make the promised result optional. A defensible comparison needs both the draft history and subsequent measures, enforcement decisions and firm experience.

The provisions are not uniformly reciprocal in their required adjustments. Article 1.36 affirms that existing US measures conform to the chapter, while many preceding articles require Chinese changes and affirm existing American equivalents. Formal agreement between equal parties can therefore coexist with asymmetric adjustments. Counting occurrences of “shall” cannot establish their economic cost, whether China independently wanted a reform, or the bargaining leverage that produced it.

Chapter 2 prohibits specified forms of pressure for technology transfer, including informal pressure, and addresses investment practices. The scope is consequently wider than an express written technology-transfer condition imposed by an administrative agency. Whether those obligations changed behavior requires evidence at the point of licensing, investment approval or commercial negotiation; the presence of a prohibition is not its enforcement record. [English agreement, Articles 2.1–2.4, printed 2-1–2-3](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf).

## 2. Date reforms before assigning them to the deal

Article 4.6 sets April 1, 2020 as the latest date for eliminating foreign-equity limits in life, pension and health insurance. Article 4.7 includes financial-market ownership commitments but also expressly acknowledges a July 5, 2019 reduction in an asset requirement. The final agreement thus incorporates measures with different temporal relationships to signature. [English agreement, printed 4-3–4-4](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf).

A second check concerns the Foreign Investment Law. Its adoption on March 15, 2019 and effectiveness on January 1, 2020 both precede signature. Article 22 prohibits administrative compulsion of technology transfer. This corrects an erroneous sequence in one paragraph of the 2026 Tian–Shang article, whose own later discussion supplies the correct dates. The law could still reflect earlier bargaining or overlapping Chinese reform objectives; chronology alone cannot choose between those causes. [Official law text, introductory dates and Article 22](https://en.ndrc.gov.cn/policies/202105/t20210527_1281403.html); [critical article memo](../literature/readings/phase-one-enforceability.md).

For the comparison with 1999, life insurance is a promising matched issue, but a 1999 offer and a 2020 binding provision are different document states. Before inferring a changing preference for foreign ownership, reconstruct the final accession schedule, actual opening dates and intervening domestic policy. A longer list of sectors in a later agreement is not by itself a measure of improved negotiating skill.

## 3. Purchases: distinguish the obligation, the denominator and the qualification

Chapter 6 and Annex 6.1 separate several different types of commitment. [English text, printed 6-1–6-3 and 6-23](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf); [Chinese text, printed 6-1–6-6](https://www.gov.cn/guowuyuan/2020-01/16/5469650/files/0637e57d99ea4f968454206af8782dd7.pdf).

| Provision | What must be preserved in an assessment |
|---|---|
| 6.2.1 and Annex 6.1 | The $200 billion is an **increment above corresponding 2017 baselines across 2020–2021**, not the total two-year purchase target. Annual increments sum to $76.7 billion and $123.3 billion. |
| 6.2.3 | Continued growth in 2022–2025 is projected; this does not simply renew the two-year numerical obligation. |
| 6.2.4–5 | US supply facilitation, market prices, commercial considerations and within-year timing are part of the text. They do not expressly erase the quantified obligation. |
| 6.2.6 | Both countries' official data are to be used. Conflicting assessments require consultation. Coverage, valuation and services definitions must be reconciled before comparing totals. |
| 6.2.7 | China can request consultation over American action, inaction or other circumstances arising in the United States affecting its ability to perform. A consultation right is not an automatic exemption. |
| Annex 6.1 agricultural note | The additional $5 billion annually is an undertaking to strive, distinct from the scheduled minimum increases. |
| Attachment, services notes | Most listed services use Mode 1; financial, insurance and cloud services also include Mode 3. A series excluding commercial-presence sales cannot silently become a complete measure of all covered services. |

The practical consequence is that purchase shortfall, legal nonperformance, responsibility for the shortfall, and the remedy are separate questions. Performance data can answer the first within a defined coverage. The other questions require the remaining provisions, representations and decisions. This distinction does not minimize a missed target; it prevents a measurable shortfall from doing the work of an unexamined causal explanation.

## 4. Political escalation was written into the mechanism

Chapter 7 creates working-level, deputy and principal-level channels. The USTR and a designated Chinese vice premier oversee the Trade Framework Group. Unresolved complaints can escalate to those principals; urgent matters may go directly to them. Annex 7-A provides regular meeting frequencies, information procedures and deadlines. Both the 21-day working-level and 45-day deputy-level windows run from receipt of the appeal: they are not sequential periods totaling 66 days. Time limits may be extended by written agreement. [English agreement, Chapter 7 and Annex 7-A, printed 7-1–7-6](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/phase%20one%20agreement/Economic_And_Trade_Agreement_Between_The_United_States_And_China_Text.pdf); [Chinese text, printed 7-1–7-7](https://www.gov.cn/guowuyuan/2020-01/16/5469650/files/0637e57d99ea4f968454206af8782dd7.pdf).

This is a precise institutional form of Solomon's attention to senior authority and continued bargaining. Yet the bilateral text cannot establish a peculiarly Chinese preference: American negotiators also accepted and publicly promoted it. To explain its selection, compare each side's proposals and alternatives, including disagreement about third-party adjudication.

Under Article 7.4.4, failure to agree can lead to a proportionate remedy or suspension of obligations by the complaining party. A respondent considering the action in good faith may not counterrespond or otherwise challenge it; the specified remedy for action considered in bad faith is withdrawal. The text formally applies to either party, rather than giving only the United States the capacity to complain. Material asymmetry in their ability to bear or impose costs is a separate empirical issue. Chapter 7 does not create an independent adjudicator to determine the merits.

Article 7.4.1 makes appeals and associated information confidential unless the parties agree otherwise. Consequently, an empty public docket would not establish that no appeal occurred. An explicit official assertion of non-use is evidence of that government's account and must be labeled accordingly. Routine consultations, a formal appeal under Chapter 7, and a later Section 301 proceeding must also remain distinct.

Article 7.6.2 addresses unforeseeable events outside the parties' control that delay timely compliance by requiring consultation. It does not state an automatic release from obligations. The 2025 Chinese white paper invokes this provision but cites 7.6.1, which instead preserves rights under other agreements. The substantive argument should be assessed against the correct paragraph, without making a numbering error carry more interpretive weight than it deserves.

## 5. Parallel forums and language rules

Article 7.6.1 preserves rights and obligations under the WTO and other agreements. The WTO Secretariat's DS543 summary supplies a concrete parallel-forum check: the panel rejected the American argument that bilateral dealings constituted a mutually satisfactory settlement of the WTO matter; it found that China, at least, had not intended those dealings to replace WTO proceedings. The US appealed the panel report on October 26, 2020. This pass read the Secretariat's case history, **not the complete panel report**. The findings concern the challenged tariffs, not the validity of Phase One; the report is not treated as an adopted final disposition. [WTO DS543 case history](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds543_e.htm).

Similarly, the DS542 history documents a technology-transfer dispute, panel suspension and lapse, rather than a merits judgment establishing that the United States won. Changes in Chinese law and procedural outcomes should be reconstructed independently before attributing reform to a legal defeat. [WTO DS542 case history](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/dispu_e/cases_e/ds542_e.htm).

Article 8.6 states that Chinese and English are equally authentic. The attachment to Annex 6.1 has a specific exception: English alone is authentic until a mutually agreed and verified Chinese translation is completed. The Chinese edition reproduces that qualification. The existence of bilingual wording therefore calls for reading the stated version rule, not presuming either language is universally controlling. No later agreed attachment translation was acquired in this pass.

Chapter 8 also distinguishes amendment, termination and further talks. Article 8.4 leaves the timing of further negotiations to agreement; it does not require an automatic Phase Two deadline. Article 8.2 requires written consent and the specified domestic-approval notifications for amendment. New summit statements or buying targets should not be labeled formal amendments without that evidentiary bridge.

## Coverage and limit

The English preamble and Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 8 were read completely; Chapter 6 body, Annex 6.1 summary and specified attachment pages were read. Chinese Chapters 6–8 and Articles 1.34–1.36 were checked. Consequential tables, remedies and time limits were visually inspected. The agricultural chapter, intermediate commodity tables and most appended scanned side letters were **not** read. The available file has 91 physical pages, including image-only appendices; extraction gaps are not blank documents. This audit makes no claim to have read the whole instrument or measured implementation of every substantive chapter.
