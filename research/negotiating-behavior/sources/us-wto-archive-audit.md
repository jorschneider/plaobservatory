# U.S. source audit: China's WTO accession negotiations

Audit date: 5 September 2026. Scope: sources for reconstructing the 1999 U.S.–China bilateral negotiation, with 1997–98 preparation and 2000–01 ratification/accession as boundaries. This is an acquisition and evidence audit, not a claim to have completed the archival research. Stable source identifiers are in `us-source-register.json`; the companion case file evaluates the evidence already read.

## What we can already use

The most useful immediately accessible private document is the **16 November 1998 Gore–Jiang memorandum of conversation**, especially its WTO discussion on PDF pages 11–14. It is an American record of an actual bilateral exchange, with named participants and a named note-taker, rather than a later recollection of Chinese motives. A second unusually useful item is **Lael Brainard's handwritten notes of two meetings on 6 August 1997**. Together they establish that the pilot need not depend exclusively on memoirs and press accounts. Neither supplies the missing April or November 1999 decision record. [US-MEMCON-19981116](https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memcon2013-0804-m11-16-1998zemin.pdf), [US-BRAINARD-19970806](https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/items/show/49411).

Three distinctions govern this audit:

- **A document read** can support a proposition within its actual contents. An interpreter-mediated U.S. memcon still requires comparison with any Chinese record.
- **A finding aid read** supports a statement about described holdings. Folder titles cannot establish what negotiators said or whether their instructions changed.
- **A download or catalog link** establishes a route to a source. It does not establish that all its contents have been read, that it is complete, or that the archive has released everything relevant.

Downloaded research copies are outside the repository, under `/tmp/uswto_20260905`. No complete copyrighted books or oral-history transcripts have been added to the repository. Temporary files are an acquisition aid, not durable preservation; a later research archive should retain permitted copies with checksums and document-level metadata.

## The Clinton Library: accessible records and the misleading catalog boundary

### The actual 1998 conversation

The stand-alone Gore–Jiang PDF has 19 pages. Its cover/transmittal identifies a meeting at the Shangri-La Hotel in Kuala Lumpur, 16 November 1998, 11:30 a.m.–1:30 p.m. Ted Osius is the note-taker; the document was formerly classified and subsequently released. The related **2013-0804-M** PDF, hosted in a `2026-03` directory, contains 148 pages of released records, including this conversation. The directory date is not the meeting date. The larger file was acquired and searched, and its relevant China section read; the whole 148-page packet was not read. [US-MEMCON-BUNDLE](https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding_aids_a_z/2026-03/2013-0804-M.pdf).

The Library's online memcon/telcon table was downloaded and searched across its rows for China, Jiang, Zhu, and Zemin. The relevant Chinese interlocutor entry located was this **Gore–Jiang 1998** meeting. The search did not locate a 1999 Clinton–Jiang or Clinton–Zhu transcript in that table. That is a statement about the searched index, not proof that such records were never created, remain classified, or are absent from other releases. [US-MEMCON-INDEX](https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/memcons-and-telcons).

The next archival inquiry should give dates and subjects, not ask for “everything on China”: April 7–8 Clinton–Zhu meetings; the April follow-up call; the September 11 APEC bilateral; October and November leader calls and letters; and the final Beijing negotiating mission. Request the memcon/telcon, presidential briefing memorandum, talking points, interpreter or note-taker record, distribution/transmittal, and associated withdrawal sheets separately. Presence of a publicly acknowledged call does not establish that a verbatim transcript exists.

### 2010-1024-F is useful, but is not USTR's negotiating archive

The 11-page finding aid describes 181 folders, approximately 10,824 pages. It explicitly says the Clinton Library does **not** hold the records of the Office of the United States Trade Representative. Its scope emphasizes public correspondence, public statements, legislative material, and some negotiation-related email. Its catalog page says no items have been scanned online, yet the separately released Brainard notes derive from this request, and searches expose folder PDFs in NARA storage. Treat online coverage as uneven and the catalog wording as insufficiently current, rather than declaring the whole collection either digitized or unavailable. The finding aid was read in full; the underlying 181 folders were not. [US-CLINTON-WTO-FA](https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/files/original/5dd81b79cdeba0890d543024e6bdc4b6.pdf), [catalog](https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/items/show/46230).

### Brainard notes: a small document with a large methodological payoff

The four-page **2013-0694-M** release comprises a cover and three handwritten pages, associated with two meetings on August 6, 1997. All three handwritten pages were rendered and visually read because extraction was unreliable. The WTO portion records assessments of leadership readiness, ministerial obstacles, American prioritization, and different U.S. negotiators' approaches. Its named sectors make it a useful guide to further files and interview questions. But the notes do not identify every speaker or establish whose assessment each fragment records. Illegible words and a difficult numeric notation must remain untranscribed; neat OCR would create false precision. This is contemporaneous American information about Chinese decision-making, not a Chinese internal minute. [US-BRAINARD-19970806](https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/files/original/10f3d416d477c09cc744f33be1cc079b.pdf).

## USTR and NARA Record Group 364

The legacy RG 364 guide is based on older descriptive work and describes records ending well before 1999. It cannot establish the present absence of later records. NARA's digitization statistics also cannot be read as an inventory of all accessible holdings: the reported count of textual scans excludes the full universe of born-digital records. Ask for current accession/transfer lists and series-level finding aids for the Office of China Affairs, the USTR and deputies, General Counsel, and relevant service/agriculture offices for 1995–2001. Request retention and transfer information where records remain with the agency; a records schedule's planned transfer interval is not proof of actual custody or declassification. [US-RG364-GUIDE](https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/364.html), [US-RG364-DIGITIZATION](https://www.archives.gov/findingaid/stat/discovery/364), [US-RG364-SCHEDULE](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/executive-office-of-the-president/rg-0364/daa-0364-2016-0001_sf115.pdf).

NARA does identify three public electronic series that overlap the case: Advisory Committee for Trade Policy and Negotiations transcripts, April 1996–July 2001 (**NAID 148414384**); Intergovernmental Policy Advisory Committee on Trade transcripts, April 1996–October 2000 (**148414379**); and Trade and Environment Policy Advisory Committee transcripts, April 1996–April 2001 (**148373497**). Their catalog/download links were identified, but the meeting records were not downloaded or read. They could reveal consultation, objections and information available to advisory constituencies; they are not automatically transcripts of negotiations with China. [US-RG364-ELECTRONIC](https://www.archives.gov/research/foreign-policy/electronic-records).

A declassification inventory also identifies bilateral Asia–Pacific material for **1989–1992**, entry UD-16W 23, HMS identifier HS1-297261873. It is a lead for the pre-WTO bridge, not a located 1999 endgame file. [US-RG364-1989-1992](https://declassification.blogs.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/16/2018/06/IOD-List-5-9-2018-Civilian-PDF.pdf).

## Oral histories, reporting and interview leads

The complete WTO narrative in Charlene Barshefsky's March 2, 2005 Miller Center interview was read. Lawrence Summers's October 17, 2002 China/WTO passages supply a materially different account of the April failure and an additional account of his October mission. Mickey Kantor's June 28, 2002 China/MFN/IPR passages concern the earlier period; they should not be presented as an eyewitness account of November 1999. The Robert Goldberg ADST interview provides direct embassy-level observation for a June 1998 meeting, but its later narrative compresses the 1999 bilateral deal and 2001 accession and needs date controls. These are edited retrospective interviews, often produced with participant review, not independent corroboration merely because multiple websites host them. [US-BARSHEFSKY-2005](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-oral-histories/charlene-barshefsky-oral-history), [US-SUMMERS-2002](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-oral-histories/lawrence-summers-oral-history-2002), [US-KANTOR-2002](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-oral-histories/michael-mickey-kantor-oral-history), [US-GOLDBERG-2011](https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/Goldberg-Robert.pdf).

The Institute for International Economics case chapter is particularly valuable as a **map to original reporting and interviews**. Its notes identify March 2001 interviews with Richard Fisher and Lael Brainard by Charan Devereaux. We have acquired the chapter and read the relevant 1999 sequence, but not the underlying interview recordings or complete transcripts. Seek the Harvard Kennedy School case's unabridged research file, subject to whatever access conditions actually apply. The chapter is a secondary synthesis containing selected participant testimony; reusing its quotations does not create a new independent witness. [US-IIE-CASE](https://www.piie.com/publications/chapters_preview/392/06iie3624.pdf).

Specific reporting to acquire in full next, identified in that chapter's footnotes:

- David E. Sanger, “How U.S. and China Failed to Close Trade Deal,” *New York Times*, April 10, 1999, A2: identify the contemporaneous source chain for the April decision.
- Helene Cooper and Bob Davis, “Barshefsky Drove Hard Bargain, But Lost to Politics,” *Wall Street Journal*, April 12, 1999, A24: test the immediate allocation of responsibility against later memoirs.
- Helene Cooper, Bob Davis and Ian Johnson, “To Brink and Back,” *Wall Street Journal*, November 16, 1999, A1: obtain the complete endgame account and identify sourced versus unattributed scenes.
- John F. Harris and Michael Laris, “‘Roller-Coaster Ride’ to an Off-Again, On-Again Trade Pact,” *Washington Post*, November 16, 1999, A26: independently check the departure-threat sequence.
- Matt Forney, “China's Press Plays Down Zhu's Visit to U.S. Lest It Upstage Jiang at Home,” *Wall Street Journal*, April 14, 1999, A23: compare the article's evidence with actual Chinese newspaper prominence and published leadership accounts.

These articles have been bibliographically located through the chapter; their full texts have not been acquired in this audit. Do not promote footnote-derived propositions into verified reporting.

Possible interview targets arise from particular evidence gaps: **Gene Sperling and Lael Brainard** for the April decision memorandum and mission authorization; **Richard Fisher, Robert Cassidy, Bob Novick, Christina Lund and Catherine Field** for draft histories and sector disagreements; **Kenneth Lieberthal and relevant NSC staff** for the leader channel; **Robert Goldberg and Bill McCahill** for embassy reporting and the reported backchannel. Current availability, personal holdings, and willingness to speak have not been checked. No contact has been made. Interviews should begin with chronology and documents shown to the participant, separate witnessed events from reports by others, and ask who disagreed before inviting broader judgments about negotiating culture.

## The next acquisition threshold

The pilot becomes a defensible process study when we can compare the April package and November agreement at clause/sector level; identify who had authority to close on both sides; trace the suspension and restart through dated communications; and reconstruct the final days with at least two distinct participant records plus contemporary documents. A white paper can establish a government's public account of these events. It cannot, by itself, meet that threshold. The precise unresolved issues are recorded in the companion case file, so archival acquisition can answer questions instead of merely enlarging a bibliography.
