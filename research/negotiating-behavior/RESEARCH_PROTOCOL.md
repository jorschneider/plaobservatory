# Updating Solomon through 2026: research protocol

Research cutoff: **5 September 2026**. Status: foundation research, not a completed update. The intended product is an original reassessment of Richard H. Solomon's *Chinese Negotiating Behavior*, identifying which propositions survive, under what conditions, and which require revision. The central comparison concerns China negotiating with major powers, especially the United States.

## What would count as an answer?

For each proposition, the final assessment will identify: Solomon's actual wording and scope; the behavior to be observed; the evidence for continuity and change; alternative explanations; the cases where it fails; and the limits imposed by access. A stronger Chinese bargaining position, a louder public statement, or an unfavorable American outcome is not, by itself, evidence of a change in negotiating behavior.

Use five verdicts: **retained within scope; revised mechanism; narrowed to specified conditions; contradicted by examined evidence; unresolved**. A case can support the presence of a behavior while leaving its purpose or effectiveness unresolved. Avoid assigning a verdict for China generally on the basis of one negotiation.

Add new propositions where the record requires them. Modern regulatory mechanisms, new instruments of pressure, or changes in public communication should not be forced into Solomon's categories. Give new claims separate identifiers and the same requirements for evidence and disconfirmation.

The empirical baseline is not 2005. That is the supplied book's printing date. Solomon's principal study concerns 1967–1984; its research and publication history, and Freeman's 1999 commentary, are documented in the [book audit](book/context-lessons-freeman.md). Freeman's revisions are separate propositions, not silently attributed to Solomon.

## Start with a recoverable negotiation

The first pilot is the **1999 US–China WTO accession negotiation**, with its 1997–1998 preparation and 2000–2001 completion kept in view. It is unusually useful because the available record includes American contemporaneous documents, competing American participant accounts, Chinese participant accounts, and Chinese-published facsimiles of dated offers. It also provides an early test outside the core normalization period.

This is a deliberate choice based on source availability, not a representative sample. A successful accession negotiation cannot establish how Beijing behaves in territorial crises, prolonged coercion, or an unsuccessful negotiation. Its value is to establish a method that survives conflicting accounts before extending the project to records with less internal access.

The pilot is complete only when we can reconstruct several decisive exchanges from dated documents; distinguish preliminary offers from accepted terms; identify who had authority to move; show the strongest disagreement between accounts; and state which consequential questions the documents cannot answer. The first dossier is progress toward that standard, with missing MOU attachments and internal instructions explicitly recorded.

## Unit of analysis: a bargaining move within an episode

An agreement is an endpoint, not the unit from which to infer a negotiating style. Build a sequence of moves with these fields:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Time and setting | Event date, local time/time zone if material, venue, channel, participants; keep publication and recollection dates separate |
| Issue and baseline | The actual disputed clause or demand; which text each side believed governed the discussion |
| Package and version | Document date/version; cross-issue conditions; whether an element was provisional, accepted, or dependent on agreement elsewhere |
| Move | Offer, refusal, condition, request for instructions, linkage, threat, suspension, concession, interpretation, implementation action |
| Authority | Who proposed it; evidence of mandate; which superior or other agency had to approve; unknowns |
| Audience | Private counterpart, domestic leadership, industry, public, third country, multilateral institution |
| Response | The next observable response, including no response; elapsed time and competing reasons for delay |
| Evidentiary basis | Source ID and exact page/paragraph; document type; language; firsthand/secondhand; redactions and missing attachments |
| Interpretation | Proposed mechanism, strongest rival explanation, and what additional evidence would discriminate |

Do not infer that a negotiator's public statement describes their instructions. Do not label disagreement over a conditional draft as repudiation of a completed agreement. Do not turn a pause into a deliberate exhaustion tactic without evidence about the work and approvals occurring during it.

## Source architecture

Work outward from documents generated by the negotiation: successive proposals, schedules, agreed minutes, memoranda of conversation, cables, decision memoranda, delegation lists, authorization requests, and implementation records. White papers, speeches, official press conferences, memoirs, interviews, reporting, and scholarship have different evidentiary jobs; none is sufficient alone.

Assess each source on four independent dimensions:

1. **Access and proximity:** Was its author present, briefed, reconstructing from documents, or repeating another account? What could the author actually know?
2. **Purpose and incentives:** Was it an internal recommendation, bargaining communication, public justification, retrospective defense, commercial pitch, or edited commemorative history?
3. **Provenance and completeness:** Who created, held, selected, translated, and published it? Is it a whole document or an excerpt? Are attachments, handwritten annotations, or distribution lists missing?
4. **Independence:** Does apparent corroboration come from distinct access to the event, or from one interview, wire story, briefing, or editorial narrative repeated many times?

There is no mechanical three-source rule. Two governments' accounts can corroborate the occurrence of a meeting while disagreeing about its content. Three retellings of the same memoir remain one evidentiary chain. The Chinese general narrative and the introductions to its bilateral documentary volumes overlap extensively and must not be counted as independent confirmations.

For Chinese documents, keep the original title, author/institution, Chinese text for short decisive phrases, a literal translation where needed, and any official English translation separately. Where the text states which language is authoritative, record that. Inspect scan images for consequential numbers and wording; extracted text in the MOFCOM collection sometimes corrupts numerals. Identify whether an English rendering is contemporaneous, official, editorial, or our translation.

For journalism, find the original report and its attribution, not a later summary. Record whether a named participant supplied documents, whether anonymous sources had distinct access, what the reporter observed directly, and any correction or subsequent challenge. Reporting can recover process unavailable in an archive; anonymity and publication incentives still constrain inference.

## Secondary literature as a parallel research strand

Use the [literature map](literature/README.md) to put competing explanations into the research design before interpreting additional cases. Read direct continuity/change studies alongside work on institutions, legal learning, domestic audiences, coercion and counterpart-specific bargaining. Revisit the original historical baseline using later archival scholarship; new evidence about an old event is not itself evidence of behavioral change.

For each fully read article or chapter, extract its actual cases and period, evidence-collection method, interview and archive provenance, central mechanism, strongest contrary evidence, and relevant Solomon/Freeman propositions. Follow citations backward to original records and forward to critiques and later research. Related versions and scholarship repeating the same interview are not independent confirmations. Keep business studies, journal articles, reported books and policy reports visibly distinguished.

The literature register separates metadata/abstract screening, selected-section reading, full reading and subsequent evaluation. Public availability is an access status, not a reading status. A recent publication date is not its empirical cutoff. The first output from reading should identify which observations or missing records would distinguish the competing explanations, rather than tallying scholarly agreement with Solomon.

## White papers and doctrinal material

Read Chinese white papers as primary evidence of the government's public account, stated red lines, legal arguments, audience, and narrative revision. Test their descriptions of negotiating events against the underlying agreement and the other side's contemporaneous record. Compare Chinese and English versions. Preserve document versions and retrieval dates.

For the trade track, the starting set is the 2018 trade-friction white paper and launch Q&A, the 2019 consultation white paper, the Phase One agreement and implementation materials, the 2025 economic-and-trade white paper, and the 2025–2026 round-by-round records. The [Chinese source audit](sources/chinese-source-audit.md) distinguishes what has actually been read from what is only located.

Broader contextual reading should cover foreign-policy speeches and white papers; official accounts of central foreign-affairs decision making; institutional responsibilities of trade, finance, foreign affairs, and military bodies; and public discussion of negotiating strategy. Do not infer a secret operational instruction from a general Party slogan. Date institutional changes before using them to explain a case.

## Comparative sequence after the pilot

These are research priorities, not completed cases or claims about their outcomes.

| Priority | Proposed comparison | What it can test | Required expansion of the source base |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | WTO US talks, 1999, followed by EU accession talks, 2000 | Counterpart-specific behavior within the same Chinese leadership and accession campaign; different commercial and political constraints | EU negotiating records and participants, Chinese sector accounts, matched offer histories |
| 2 | US–China trade negotiations, 2018–2020, and 2025–2026 | Draft status, enforcement design, tariff linkage, public communication, leadership authority under renewed confrontation | Chinese white papers and ministries; US decision records where open; texts and annexes; original reporting; participant interviews; customs and implementation records |
| 3 | An earlier and a recent US–China crisis episode, selected after an access audit | Whether the relationship-building model transfers to acute confrontation, coercive deadlines, and military involvement | State/NSC/Defense records, Chinese MFA and military statements, operational chronology, participants; avoid equating military signaling with diplomatic bargaining |
| 4 | A Chinese negotiation with India and a negotiation with Russia, selected for recoverable process | Whether US observations travel to a large territorial rival and to a strategically close major power | Original-language counterpart archives, parliamentary or ministerial records, Chinese records, and issue-specific reporting |
| 5 | A failed or stalled negotiation alongside an agreement, with matched issues where possible | Selection on success; when authority, threats, or relationship appeals do not produce movement | Records of abandoned drafts and failed rounds, including non-events missing from memoirs |

Use period labels (late Mao/normalization, reform/accession, later institutional development, Xi-era confrontation) as organizing aids, not explanations. Within-period variation may be as important as differences between leaders. A change in China's bargaining resources, an issue's domestic costs, the opponent's demands, international rules, or information technology may explain a change without a new national negotiating style.

A limited comparison with American behavior toward other major trading partners should test whether supposedly Chinese tactics—deadlines, appeals to presidents, domestic-constraint claims, conditional packages, and public pressure—also appear on the American side or in ordinary major-power bargaining.

## Reporting and archive work

Prepare document-specific requests before interviews. Begin with the [reporting brief](reporting/wto-1999-next-requests.md), which specifies missing April MOU attachments, decision papers, final schedules, and instructions behind particular November moves. Interviews should first elicit an independent chronology; only then introduce documents and contradictions. Ask what the participant personally witnessed, what notes survive, who disagreed, and what could prove their account wrong.

Do not promise Solomon-equivalent access. Recent Chinese internal deliberations and recent American records may remain unavailable. Public documents, old declassifications, and selective retrospective publication are not equivalent to contemporary internal files and confidential interviews. The study should explain this asymmetry and limit causal claims accordingly. No new interviews, archival requests, or correspondence have been conducted for this foundation dossier.

## Publication and verification

The [8 September full-text assessment](literature/first-full-text-synthesis.md) adds several operational rules: record a work's version and empirical cutoff separately from publication date; distinguish public approval from actual negotiating mandates; compare complete language versions before interpreting omissions; and trace the timing of authorization rather than infer it from a coherent final outcome. The [reading ledger](literature/readings/README.md) distinguishes complete works, bounded readings, and related versions. Unavailable texts remain acquisition leads without blocking research from available online sources.

The subsequent [2018–2026 trade case](cases/us-trade-2018-2026-synthesis.md) adds four controls: distinguish ordinary reviews, confidential agreement appeals and statutory investigations; retain the scope and denominator of performance measures; separate institutional announcements from design consultations and actual operation; and test senior involvement separately from its reliability as a signal of imminent agreement. A change from comprehensive negotiations to a partial package changes the relevant comparison of demands.

Publish original analysis, source metadata, short necessary quotations, and links. Keep downloaded books, full OCR, and interview materials outside the public repository unless redistribution rights and interview permissions are clear. A government-hosted copy is evidence of availability, not blanket permission to republish every page.

Before releasing a case: check citations against the original; distinguish event date from later publication; inspect consequential scan passages; trace source dependence; preserve contrary evidence; separate an offer from an agreement and both from implementation; and have a second reader challenge the causal interpretation. Mark uncertainty in the claim itself, not only in a general disclaimer.

The final reassessment should answer **what persists, what changed, why, and for which counterpart and issue**. A catalogue of dramatic negotiating stories or a tally of who won does not meet that standard.
