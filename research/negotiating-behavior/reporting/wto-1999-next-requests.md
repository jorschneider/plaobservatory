## Interview sequence

Prepare separate question sets for principal negotiators, sector negotiators, political advisers, embassy staff, translators, and contemporary reporters. Senior recollections should not substitute for the staff who drafted or transmitted the offer. Confirm the person's role and access at the time before choosing a question.

Start with an unprompted chronology: which meeting did they attend, what document was on the table, what authority did they have, and what had to go back for approval? Ask whether they retained notes, drafts, calendars, messages, or a contemporaneous report. Distinguish memory from later reading.

Only after that account, introduce specific documents:

- **April consent:** How did the team distinguish an accepted provision from one awaiting verification? What did “rectification” permit? Who held the April 10 attachments? Which items in the April 8 public list were still contested, and by whom?
- **Publication:** Who decided to publish the April 8 list, for which audience, and with what expectation of Chinese consent? Was the decision discussed before the publication? What contemporaneous record captures the decision and the response?
- **November authority:** What could MOFTEC change without the sector regulator? What required the premier or State Council? What changed in the banking mandate on November 13? Ask for a specific instruction or witness rather than an impression of centralized control.
- **Equity and control:** Was50%life insurance already acceptable on November 12? What remained at issue over management rights? Separate value-added/paging, mobile, other basic telecom, insurance, and securities; use the original schedules.
- **Deadlines:** Were flights or onward commitments fixed? Who requested extension? Did instructions or offers change after a departure warning? What would the delegation have done if no meeting with Zhu had been arranged?
- **Internal disagreement:** Who favored waiting, who favored closure, and what costs did each identify? What is firsthand evidence of that disagreement? Did the participant's view change during the week?
- **Counterfactual and disconfirmation:** Which demand would have broken the deal? What evidence would show that the participant's preferred explanation of success is mistaken?

Show both sides' strongest accounts where relevant, but do not ask witnesses simply to affirm that an opponent was manipulative or that their own side was flexible. Ask how they knew. Record whether disagreement concerns the occurrence of an event, the wording of a proposal, its acceptance status, or its causal effect.

## Contemporary reporting to reconstruct

Build an original-report chronology around April 7–13, May 7–14, September 6–13, and November 9–16,1999. Search contemporaneous Chinese, American, and third-country reporting, preserving original publication dates and updates. For each report, record named sources, anonymous-source descriptions, direct observation, documents quoted, syndication, and subsequent corrections. Track whether ostensibly independent stories derive from a common briefing.

Reporters who covered the rounds may identify which public claims were contested at the time and which explanations appeared only years later. Their notebooks or original dispatches could be valuable; availability and permission must be established before using unpublished material. Interviews and archives should answer the dossier's unresolved questions, not merely add colorful scenes to an already chosen conclusion.
