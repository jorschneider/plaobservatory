# Agricultural biotechnology in 2017: testing the corporate causal account

Research cut: 10 September 2026. This is a documentary follow-up to the [agricultural implementation case](../cases/agricultural-approvals-implementation.md), with a separate [source and reading register](../sources/agricultural-2017-followup-register.json). It deliberately examines the approvals invoked in recovered Dow correspondence; it is neither a complete application cohort nor a comparison selected independently of outcomes.

The stronger finding is a sequence of company access, a specific intergovernmental procedure, identifiable regulatory permissions, and continued disagreement over the bargain’s coverage. The record supports Dow’s belief that senior intervention helped secure its corn approval. It does not recover the Chinese instruction or scientific recommendation needed to establish that intervention caused the decision. It also reveals why “China promised eight approvals but delivered four” is an inadequate description of the written undertaking.

## The bargain specified a process before it produced certificates

The Chinese Ministry of Finance’s 12 May joint release, attachment item 3, required the National Biosafety Committee (NBC) to **hold a meeting by the end of May** to evaluate eight submitted US biotechnology applications. For products not passing, applicants were to receive a complete written information request with an explanation of relevance to the intended use’s safety. Resubmissions were to receive accelerated consideration without improper delay; successful evaluations were to yield certificates within 20 working days. The English text makes the same conditional distinction. Neither recovered language version names the eight events or makes approval of every application by 16 July unconditional. Several neighboring commitments explicitly use that July date; the biotechnology paragraph does not. [Chinese joint text, AGF07](https://wjb.mof.gov.cn/gongzuodongtai/201705/t20170512_2599646.htm); [English text, AGF08](https://www.commerce.gov/news/fact-sheets/2017/05/fact-sheet-initial-actions-us-china-economic-cooperation-100-day-plan).

This was already a repeated procedural negotiation, as the earlier case’s 2015–16 material demonstrates. Domestic administrative change was also underway before the May bargain. A Ministry of Agriculture (MOA) notice signed 22 February, published 24 February 2017, implementing a 13 January State Council decision, removed applicant-provided composition, environmental and food-safety testing as a condition of accepting specified import and production applications. Testing remained necessary, but the approval department would commission it and arrange budget funding. That notice establishes a change in responsibility and acceptance conditions, not when an individual 2017 application became complete. It is a concrete alternative process influence that a purely summit-centered account would overlook. [MOA notice, AGF14](https://bgt.moa.gov.cn/zcjd/201702/t20170224_5495469.htm).

The first list has **16 rows, two original approvals and fourteen entries explicitly marked 续申请**, renewal applications. Cotton 531, 1445 and MON88913 renewals already have five-year effective periods, ending 12 June 2022. The Chinese reporter’s statement that all sixteen lasted three years is therefore incorrect. Likewise the later Enlist E3 five-year renewal is a useful event-specific observation, not evidence that five-year certificates first appeared after Phase One. This is an example of why the original table takes precedence over a contemporary summary. Two further distinctions prevent false acceleration claims. The 20-working-day clock starts after a successful safety evaluation; the certificate lists do not supply that starting date. Commercialization is also separate: Dow announced availability for the 2018 season, whereas Syngenta said it would continue its grain-use marketing program for both 2017 and 2018 despite Chinese approval. The dates cannot be treated as equivalent measures of commercial access. [AGF04](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/9384-enlist-corn-on-the-way-as-china-approves-trait); [AGF06](https://www.syngenta-us.com/newsroom/syngenta-receives-china-import-approval-for-agrisure-duracade-corn-trait-expands-corn-hybrid-portfolio).

## The eight applications: an industry reconstruction with a contested boundary

On June 14, *The Paper* reporter Wang Can interviewed US Soybean Export Council China representative Zhang Xiaoping. He described the eight as two soybean, four corn and two alfalfa products. This is useful contemporary Chinese-language reporting, but its source is a US trade association representative, not a Chinese official’s authenticated application schedule. [AGF13](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1708523).

| Dow corn | DAS-40278-9, Enlist corn | Original certificate No. 002 |
| Monsanto soybean | MON87705, Vistive Gold | Original certificate No. 001 |
| Syngenta corn | 5307, Agrisure Duracade | Original certificate No. 033 |
| Monsanto corn | MON87427 | Original certificate No. 034 |

| Dow soybean | “Enlist soybean(s)”; exact event identifier not supplied in the recovered 2017 materials | Pending; coverage disputed in Dow’s account |
| Two Monsanto alfalfa applications | Two Roundup Ready alfalfa products; individual event codes not established by the recovered 2017 statements | Pending according to BIO |

The first five event identities are recoverable by joining named contemporary reporting to official lists or the applicant’s identification. This does **not** turn the whole table into an agreed bilateral annex. Most notably, on July 17 Dow told *Agri-Pulse* that MOA was indicating Enlist soybeans fell outside the plan, contrary to Dow’s understanding of what US Commerce had included. The underlying MOA message and Commerce list have not been recovered. The report establishes an attributed disagreement over coverage, leaving unresolved whether China changed an accepted scope, the governments initially understood different scopes, or the company misunderstood its inclusion. [AGF03](https://www.agri-pulse.com/ext/resources/Archives-Newsletters/Agri-Pulse-05242017.pdf); [AGF05](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/9566-china-oks-two-gmo-products-for-import).

There was evidence of substantive contact before that dispute: Dow’s Joe Vertin said on June 14 that NBC had supplied soybean questions which the company expected to answer quickly. No questions, response or resubmission receipt accompanies that statement. It cannot establish either irrelevant demands or a completed dossier subsequently withheld. Nor should the unnamed soybean be silently relabeled Enlist **E3/DAS-44406-6**. The prior case’s later E3-specific reporting gives a 2013 application year; the May 2017 article gives 2012 for an unspecified product. Without the absent regulatory summary or application record, those dates cannot be harmonized into one measured chronology. [AGF04](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/9384-enlist-corn-on-the-way-as-china-approves-trait); [prior E3 case](../cases/agricultural-approvals-implementation.md).

## What the public US files add—and do not add

The public FOIA production comprises 917 pages in four PDFs, supplied under a scope substitution described in USDA’s signed 2 August 2019 letter: existing external-email records covering 1 April 2017–31 August 2018. It is not access to internal Chinese files or a complete US interagency record. The retained underlying stamps also differ across component productions. [Release letter, AGF19](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Signed20Final20Response20Letter.pdf); [public request docket, AGF20](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/rebeckah-adcock-external-emails-77518/).

This follow-up keyword-searched extracted text across all four parts, checked PDF embedded-file inventories, and read selected additional chains. **None of the three May 9 documents was recovered:** “Enlist Corn China Regulatory Summary (2017 May).docx”; “Enlist Soybean China Regulatory Summary (2017 May).docx”; “China Biotech Approval Backgrounder May 2017.docx.” Part 4 p. 507 names them; all four PDFs report zero embedded files. Exact-title public searches also failed to find their contents. That is a bounded search result, not proof they were never supplied, retained elsewhere or withheld; unread images and unindexed records remain possible.

The useful addition is a parallel DuPont chain. On June 8 Adcock requested material for the coming Tuesday meeting with Perdue; Aaron Whitesel proposed relationship-building with Jim Collins, a merger update and China/100-plan biotechnology concerns. On June 12 she requested attendees and documents while preparing the event brief. These are full physical pp. 110–111, stamped 000110–000111, of part 4. The actual brief and event-specific Chinese response are absent. [AGF18](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Rony204.pdf).

Syngenta’s Laura Peterson also sent an FAQ on May 10 and forwarded BIO’s favorable May 12 statement to Adcock and Heidi Green. The latter appears twice: part 1 pp. 46–47 and part 2 pp. 140–141, the latter stamped 000831–000832. These are duplicate evidence of the same communication, not two independent endorsements or meetings. Together with DuPont, they show the access channel extended beyond Dow. They do not reveal a common Chinese decision process. [AGF15](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Rony201.pdf); [AGF16](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Rony202.pdf).

Dow CEO Tim Hassinger’s June 14 message remains the clearest private contemporaneous attribution: he credited Perdue and the 100-day agreement for **corn**, while asking for continued assistance on remaining Enlist traits. The certificate identifies the corn precisely. The chain verifies corporate belief and subsequent solicitation; it does not identify the NBC’s reasoning, who ordered any acceleration, or how much earlier approval occurred than it otherwise would have. [AGF18, pp. 205–206](https://cdn.muckrock.com/foia_files/2019/09/17/2019-DA-05664-F20Rony204.pdf).

## Consequences for the Solomon update

Implementation clearly continued as a negotiating arena. By January 2018 USTR alleged continued requests unrelated to intended use and stalled work on the remaining applications. BIO’s April 2018 testimony similarly complained that no NBC meeting had occurred since June and sought systemic reform. These are primary records of US governmental and industry positions, potentially drawing on overlapping applicant information; neither provides the missing technical requests or Chinese minutes. [AGF09, PDF pp. 108–109/printed pp. 100–101](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Reports/China%202017%20WTO%20Report.pdf); [AGF11, pp. 5–6](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/USCC%20Hearing%20on%20China%20Agriculture%20-%20Testimony%20of%20the%20Biotechnology%20Innovation%20Organization.pdf).

For **S-P25**, this strengthens the continuing-arena component, not the stronger claim that China systematically treats its own obligations as optional while enforcing the other side’s strictly. For **S-P19**, it supplies a contemporaneous scope dispute worth investigating, not proven distortion of a shared record. For claims about senior authority and delay, the sequence supplies a plausible mechanism—firms turn regulatory bottlenecks into high-level bilateral demands and continue lobbying after partial results—but leaves the decisive Chinese causal link unobserved.

The most valuable next documents are therefore specific: the jointly exchanged eight-application schedule; May/June event-level NBC recommendations; the soybean information request and response receipts; the three Dow summaries; and US or Chinese minutes connecting a ministerial request to an administrative instruction. Until those appear, the case should be written as **partial implementation plus disputed scope under an already detailed procedural bargain**, with corporate and official explanations attributed separately.
