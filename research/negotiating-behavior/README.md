# Chinese negotiating behavior: reassessing Solomon through 2026

**Research dossier — updated 10 September 2026.** This project asks which conclusions in Richard H. Solomon's *Chinese Negotiating Behavior* remain useful, which need revision, and under what circumstances Chinese behavior toward major powers has changed. The United States is the main comparison; other major counterparts are necessary controls. This is original research and commentary, not a reproduction of the book.

The work combines a sequential audit of the supplied book, including Charles W. Freeman Jr.'s 1999 commentary; a documentary pilot on the 1999 US–China WTO accession negotiation; a secondary-literature program; reconstruction of later US trade negotiations and selected implementation episodes; and comparisons with India and Russia. The latest pass adds Russian border settlement and gas bargaining, including specified September 2026 records, and a deeper 2017 agricultural documentary trail. The aim is to reconstruct offers, instructions, disputes, and decisions before drawing conclusions from outcomes.

## What has already changed our approach?

**The baseline is earlier than the printing date.** Solomon's central study concerns 1967–1984. He explicitly leaves open whether the findings transfer to renewed confrontation, limits what negotiating tactics can accomplish, and recognizes bureaucratic constraints. Freeman already identifies institutional specialization, learning, and counterpart-specific practices. An update should not rediscover those points and call them new.

**There is a substantial Chinese documentary starting point.** The MOFCOM collection publishes selected archival documents, including dated proposals and the signed April 10, 1999 MOU body. Its editorial narrative, original facsimiles, translations, and later participant accounts need separate treatment. Access to the collection is not access to every internal decision.

**Draft status and authority are central.** The April MOU names unresolved issues; November offers distinguish ownership, management control, phase-ins, and safeguards. Chinese sector testimony describes approval chains; American letters impose deadlines and request senior Chinese intervention. These records complicate a story told solely through psychological tactics or the final result. Details and qualifications are in the [first synthesis](cases/wto-1999-synthesis.md).

## What the later trade case adds

The [new synthesis](cases/us-trade-2018-2026-synthesis.md) finds continued political control and bargaining after signature alongside detailed legal and administrative procedures. Both sides moved from provisional understandings toward a narrower settlement; the evidence does not establish the private authorization behind China's disputed May 2019 revisions. Purchases fell substantially short in the examined series, while structural implementation varied by sector. In 2025–2026, reciprocal implementation complaints entered new bargains and the US opened public consultation on a proposed trade mechanism's design.

The conclusion is conditional: Solomon's attention to interests, authority and implementation remains useful, while broad descriptions of national style need to be tested against specific obligations, institutions and counterpart behavior. The 2026 design records establish more than an announcement, but do not yet establish final operation or effectiveness.

## The next research step

The [new comparison](cases/implementation-and-major-power-reassessment.md) delivers selected financial and agricultural implementation sequences and an India security case. It finds real financial delivery, repeated procedural bargaining in agriculture, and local security agreements whose operational and political consequences must be distinguished. It also tests failure and delay rather than examining successful summits alone. These are deliberately selected comparisons, not statistically matched causal estimates.

The next discriminating evidence is now more specific: corrected financial applications and transfer conditions; agricultural submission–request–response–decision files and a complete application cohort; and border protocols, maps, mandates and verification records. Reconcile the purchase benchmarks and recover the implementing records behind the 2026 targets and trade mechanism. Continue seeking the spring 2019 drafts and authority trail without making all progress dependent on their availability.

Recover the **April 10 MOU attachments**, the **NEC–NSC memorandum behind the April decision**, the **successive November offers and final bilateral schedules**, and the **Chinese authorization records identified by sector participants**. Build a dated comparison for telecom, life insurance, bank/auto-finance access, and safeguards. These records can distinguish an actual reversal from a conditional draft, a genuine mandate limit from bargaining tactics, and a material concession from retrospective compression.

The [Russian extension](cases/russia-and-scope-reassessment.md) now tests the earlier assessment against two additional issue areas. Its next targets are the 1989–1991 package-separation decisions, missing border-use annexes and implementation records, gas memoranda and commercial offers, and the jointly exchanged 2017 application list. The EU accession comparison remains especially valuable for holding the Chinese leadership and accession campaign more nearly constant. The [first literature assessment](literature/first-full-text-synthesis.md) and later critical readings supply competing explanations and source leads; unavailable commercial texts remain acquisition leads, without blocking online research.

The 2026 cutoff is the intended endpoint of the study. **The full book-length update is not yet complete.** The later trade case now has substantive readings of specified white-paper sections, agreements, reporting and implementation records, with exact limits in its registers. Other cases and much internal decision evidence remain outstanding. No new interviews, archival requests, or correspondence have been conducted. Downloaded source books and full transcripts are kept outside the public repository.
