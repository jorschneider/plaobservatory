# Chinese negotiating behavior: reassessing Solomon through 2026

**Research dossier and manuscript in development — revised 17 September 2026.** This project asks which conclusions in Richard H. Solomon's *Chinese Negotiating Behavior* remain useful, which need revision, and under what circumstances Chinese behavior toward major powers has changed. The United States is the main comparison; other major counterparts are necessary comparisons. This is original research and commentary, not a reproduction of the book.

The work combines a sequential audit of the supplied book, including Freeman’s 1999 commentary, with primary-document reconstructions and accessible scholarship. All seven substantive chapters, an introduction, conclusion and 59-proposition assessment exist. The [developmental revision](manuscript/development/README.md) is now testing their narrative quality, research depth and practical value beyond the completed first-draft checks. The newest chapters integrate the 2001 and 2023 American crises, Kissinger’s later intermediary role, the 2018–2020 trade settlement, sector-specific implementation, and Indian and Russian comparisons. The aim is to reconstruct offers, instructions, disputes and decisions before drawing conclusions from outcomes.

The [conclusion](manuscript/chapters/08-what-survives-solomon.md) separates retained observations, narrower interpretations, documented institutional developments and unresolved causal explanations. The [reader’s apparatus](manuscript/README.md#readers-apparatus) consolidates the proposition assessment, chronology and sources. The [single reading edition](manuscript/reading-edition.html) brings the full research manuscript together with its source notes and three appendices.

The current revision also reconstructs the 2025 Geneva–London exchange of export-control relief, pairs American recollections with newly read Chinese interviews, and follows DS379 into a recovered administrative redetermination and a bounded domestic-litigation coda. These records strengthen the explanation of what a concession actually permits. The [development records](manuscript/development/README.md) distinguish new acquisitions, renewed readings and remaining gaps.

The latest archival pass recovers [EP-3 drafting and delivery cables](manuscript/development/ep3-drafting-cables.md) and [contemporary claims/assets discussions](manuscript/development/claims-1974-archive-followup.md), and completes the [Jost institutional study](literature/readings/jost-crisis-institutions.md) with an original-source check of its CIA report. A [continuous book reading](manuscript/development/continuous-reading-66ffce8.md) led to new openings, reordered encounters and cuts to repeated advice. The [integration checkpoint](manuscript/development/archival-and-narrative-integration-2026-09-16.md) records the revisions and remaining gaps.

## What has already changed our approach?

**The baseline is earlier than the printing date.** Solomon's central study concerns 1967–1984. He explicitly leaves open whether the findings transfer to renewed confrontation, limits what negotiating tactics can accomplish, and recognizes bureaucratic constraints. Freeman already identifies institutional specialization, learning, and counterpart-specific practices. An update should not rediscover those points and call them new.

**There is a substantial Chinese documentary starting point.** The MOFCOM collection publishes selected archival documents, including dated proposals and the signed April 10, 1999 MOU body. Its editorial narrative, original facsimiles, translations, and later participant accounts need separate treatment. Access to the collection is not access to every internal decision.

**Draft status and authority are central.** The April MOU names unresolved issues; November offers distinguish ownership, management control, phase-ins, and safeguards. Chinese sector testimony describes approval chains; American letters impose deadlines and request senior Chinese intervention. These records complicate a story told solely through psychological tactics or the final result. Details and qualifications are in the [first synthesis](cases/wto-1999-synthesis.md).

## What the original-period checks change

The [new historical chapter](manuscript/chapters/01-solomons-baseline.md) compares the successful December 1978 normalization sequence with the unfinished 1973 claims/assets bargain. Partial settlement, disputed obligations and consequential technical details were already present. A modern update must explain changes in their instruments and use, rather than presenting those features as recent inventions.

Solomon’s senior-intervention prediction also needs to be stated more faithfully before it is tested. His p.149 wording is stronger than our initial “can signal” extraction. The new chapter restores that strength and then distinguishes an observable authorization function from unestablished predictive reliability. The [independent review](manuscript/chapter-01-review-2026-09-15.md) checked both the original wording and the consequential primary-record claims.

## What the EU and climate cases add

The [EU accession case](cases/eu-wto-2000-comparison.md) and [climate case](cases/climate-2009-2015.md) sharpen the provisional answer before the later trade comparison:

| Question in Solomon’s framework | What the new cases support | What must change or remains untested |
|---|---|---|
| Do concrete drafting and senior intervention signal approaching agreement? | Senior authority can define which package officials may accept. | A leader may confirm a retained limit; the counterpart may need that confirmation for its own mandate. March 2000 did not close, adding a failed round to the test. Solomon himself supplied an aborted-agreement example, but still made a strong prediction at p.149. Our practical focus is the content of authorization; the prediction’s comparative reliability remains unestablished. |
| Are principles and discretion central? | China preserved differentiated obligations and national discretion over climate ambition. | Specific review procedures and a treaty can preserve discretion on particular obligations. Document-form preferences require issue-specific testing; precise rules can also preserve discretion. |
| Does rigidity reveal leadership conflict? | The record identifies specific limits and unsuccessful coordination. | Those observations do not establish conflict within the Chinese leadership; substantive costs, delegated authority and institutional roles remain competing explanations. |
| Does bargaining continue after agreement? | Bilateral accession deals led into further multilateral negotiations; climate arrangements required later procedural work. | Continuing negotiation alone does not establish asymmetric demands for counterpart adherence while China seeks adjustments for itself. |
| Has specialization changed behavior? | Sector expertise, detailed offers and technical institutions are observable. | They were already present at accession, and Freeman recognized learning. A causal claim needs an instruction or choice that expertise changed. |
| Do “old friends” still influence concessions? | These cases identify useful channels and participant relationships. | The later intermediary mechanism remains a major evidentiary gap; access and friendly meetings are insufficient. |

These are bounded findings, not a verdict on every negotiation through 2026. The completed expertise and crisis chapters now supply the DS379, EP-3, 2023 and later intermediary comparisons.

## What the later trade case adds

The [new synthesis](cases/us-trade-2018-2026-synthesis.md) finds continued political control and bargaining after signature alongside detailed legal and administrative procedures. Both sides moved from provisional understandings toward a narrower settlement; the evidence does not establish the private authorization behind China's disputed May 2019 revisions. Purchases fell substantially short in the examined series, while structural implementation varied by sector. In 2025–2026, reciprocal implementation complaints entered new bargains and the US opened public consultation on a proposed trade mechanism's design.

The conclusion is conditional: Solomon's attention to interests, authority and implementation remains useful, while broad descriptions of national style need to be tested against specific obligations, institutions and counterpart behavior. The 2026 design records establish more than an announcement, but do not yet establish final operation or effectiveness.

## What further evidence could change

The [complete manuscript](manuscript/README.md) is the current synthesis. Earlier case memos and acquisition plans remain dated research records; their provisional verdicts and once-missing chapters do not override the final proposition assessment. The crisis, expertise and later-intermediary chapters are now included.

Further research would be most useful where a specific missing record distinguishes competing explanations:

- The April 1999 MOU attachments, complete bilateral accession annexes, the American NEC–NSC decision memorandum and Chinese sector instructions could clarify conditional offers, approval limits and disputed reversals.
- Successive spring 2019 drafts and their clearance trail could establish which provisions changed, who required the changes and whether bargaining costs or revised expectations explain them.
- Intermediary messages and decision records could connect cultivation and access to an actual policy effect. The inspected Kissinger record establishes reception and advocacy without that transmission chain.
- Financial application files, a complete agricultural application cohort, purchase-benchmark reconciliation and implementing records for 2026 arrangements could resolve particular performance disputes.
- Border protocols, maps and verification records, alongside complete gas memoranda and commercial offers, could explain remaining differences between political agreement and operational delivery.

These are limits on particular explanations, not claims that the inspected sources prove less than they do. No new interviews, archival requests or correspondence have been conducted. Downloaded source books and full transcripts remain outside the public repository. The cases state their own event endpoints; a September retrieval date does not extend each history to that date.
