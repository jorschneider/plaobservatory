# Testing the thesis: autonomy can scale while people retain authority

Reviewed 15 September 2026. Three primary-source observations test the [ecosystem guide](README.md). Civilian operating results are comparisons; military transfer requires separate evidence.

The strongest rival explanation is **repeatable products, customer-owned infrastructure and review by exception can make autonomy scale while formal human authority remains unchanged**. The guide allows this possibility. Its human-authority interpretation survives these observations, but it does not by itself explain labor intensity, deployment cost or who captures value. The mere presence of an integrator or operator cannot distinguish a scalable platform from a labor-intensive service.

## 1. EACON: repeat deployment, low on-site staffing in a named case, and a changing commercial model

**Primary evidence and dates.** EACON Group Co., Ltd. (易控智駕科技股份有限公司) announced unaudited H1 results on **26 August 2026**, with operating counts at **30 June 2026**: over **3,100 active autonomous mining trucks**, versus over 1,600 a year earlier; solutions across **38 mining sites**; nine sites with fleets exceeding 100 trucks. Approximately 2,500 trucks used the **customer-provided fleet** model. “Active” means working normally at a mine, including customary repair/maintenance interruptions; it is not simultaneous availability. Customer-provided-fleet gross margin was **32.3%**, versus **−10.0%** for company-provided fleets. Management attributed the more favorable mix partly to fewer implementation personnel. However, total revenue fell **17.3%**, and the customer-provided category still includes truck sales and continuing support. These are issuer disclosures, not independent productivity audits. [H1 results, printed pp. 3–4, 9–11 and definition on p. 30](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082601293.pdf).

The **29 June 2026 final prospectus**, printed p. 148 (PDF page 157), provides a named operating observation: **Fushan Quarry, Shandong**, had **23 autonomous electric trucks and two on-site personnel per shift**; its initial 20 trucks had exceeded **23 months of continuous deployment**. This is an achieved operating claim, not the separate product specification for how many vehicles one operator could supervise. That staffing paragraph does not specify its own census date. The same prospectus reports off-site technical staff and a wide project staffing range; customer-provided arrangements leave daily truck maintenance/repair with customers or contractors. In 2025, truck sales still supplied **70%** of customer-provided-fleet revenue. [Final prospectus, pp. 133, 135–137 and 148](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0629/2026062900135.pdf).

**What this challenges.** A case with retained humans can nevertheless have a small direct operating crew and long deployment history. More sites and a less capital-intensive model support scalable commercialization. They justify making deployment learning a coequal hypothesis with continuing integration burdens.

**What it does not establish.** Two on-site personnel are not the quarry's entire workforce. Lower vendor staffing can reflect a transfer of responsibility to the customer. Gross-margin differences do not isolate software reuse from hardware mix, depreciation, site maturity or outsourced work. Neither filing establishes a military customer or shared military software.

**Discriminating test/falsifier.** Compare successive installations using the same product generation: engineering hours to acceptance, all-party staff-hours per accepted transport output, intervention frequency, and contribution margin after remote support. A stable or falling burden at later sites supports product learning. Costs merely moving to customers, or rising integration effort with each additional site, would weaken it.

## 2. Baishihu: a customer coordinates interoperability across competitors

**Primary evidence and dates.** An **伊吾县人民政府** publication dated **30 March 2026**, based on a **25 March** site visit and named interviews, reports **伊吾广汇矿业有限公司白石湖露天煤矿** using over **500 autonomous trucks** across Sany, EACON and Tage systems. The actual **mixed-system operating subset was 150 trucks**. The mine coordinated the three suppliers in a common communication protocol while their respective systems continued operating. Its deputy chief engineer described current vehicle efficiency as approximately **90% of human operation**, with parity a future aim. These are first-person customer/project-manager statements in local official reporting; the article supplies no reproducible productivity denominator. Its description of extending mixed operation across the whole production process is prospective. [Original government publication](https://www.xjyiwu.gov.cn/xjyiwu/c119607/202603/3e58451d68cf4bed8e1e809d25e586d3.shtml). Locators: first 赵凤秋 interview; subsequent “目前矿区已投入150台” passage; paragraph beginning “伊吾广汇矿业有限公司协调”; final 赵凤秋 interview.

**What this challenges.** Integration is occurring, but the buyer can organize it across competitors. This supports an alternative industrial structure in which a large customer controls interfaces and purchasing power, rather than a single proprietary system supplier necessarily capturing integration rents. Existing scale should be preserved even though performance remains below the stated human benchmark.

**What it does not establish.** A common interface is not shared source code, transferred IP, vendor substitutability or a national adopted standard. The story uses supplier brands; it does not establish each contracting subsidiary. The 150 mixed trucks must not become 500 mixed trucks. This is civilian mining, with no military transfer established.

**Discriminating test/falsifier.** Check whether subsequent suppliers enter through the existing interface with lower commissioning costs, and whether the customer can change vendors without rebuilding site infrastructure. A bespoke three-way arrangement that has to be renegotiated and re-engineered at each mine would support the explanation that integration remains labor intensive instead.

## 3. PLA transport support: delegation reaches planning, and the user develops software internally

**Primary evidence and dates.** **解放军报 / 中国军网, 7 August 2026**, describes an unnamed Air Force brigade's lightweight transport-resource scheduling system in **trial use**. A brigade leader says schedulers now review exceptional data: “复核查验方案中的个别异常数据”. The reporter explicitly observed a **simulated scheduling exercise**. The brigade formed a development team working alongside scheduling staff, shifted technical resources toward data/software teams, established a “数软共创” workspace, and regularly trained personnel in application development and data governance. The report describes locally developed capability and organizational change; it provides no controlled before/after labor measurement. [Original PLA Daily article](https://www.81.cn/yw_208727/16478194.html). Locators: body paragraphs 2, 4–6, excluding heading/byline. Original HTML downloaded and read in full; direct web extraction timed out.

**What this challenges.** Delegation can change the substance of human work from constructing a plan to reviewing exceptions. Internal technical teams also show that users may absorb some software/integration functions. Counting every retained approving official as an unchanged labor burden would miss both changes.

**What it does not establish.** Trial use and a simulated exercise do not demonstrate routine force-wide adoption, an approved doctrine change, eliminated positions or independent machine command authority. Local software development can itself require substantial labor. This record supports an organizational possibility, not sector-wide economics.

**Discriminating test/falsifier.** Seek dated routine-use records and comparable completed support tasks, including exception rates, planning/review hours and maintenance of the local software. Persistent full manual reconstruction behind the nominal exception-review step would weaken the labor-saving interpretation.

## Selection bias and a stronger analytical frame

The current sample deliberately privileges attributable public records. That makes it auditable but does not make it representative:

- Named aircraft, parade platforms, listed issuers and photogenic projects are easier to observe than inexpensive unnamed equipment, routine civilian automation, abandoned deployments or internal software.
- Military publicity often names commanders and personnel while withholding software and staffing denominators. This makes retained human roles more visible than how much their workload has changed.
- A scientific mother ship's crew includes work needed to produce scientific output. It is not a matched counterfactual for an automated warehouse or repeated haulage task.
- One failed procurement package supplies a useful negative case, not a failure rate. Conversely, successful mining deployments are selected successes and do not settle military scalability.
- There is an asymmetry if general references to retained labor count as evidence for service intensity, while quantitative civilian operating claims are discarded because they are not independently audited. Apply attribution and uncertainty symmetrically.

Treat **authority allocation**, **total labor intensity**, **deployment repeatability** and **value capture** as four separate variables. Keep both industrial hypotheses live: organizations may profit from scarce integration expertise, or standardized interfaces and accumulated deployment learning may reduce those costs while customers retain authority. For analysis, the informative observation is the change in all-party cost per accepted output across successive deployments—not whether any human remains in the organization.
