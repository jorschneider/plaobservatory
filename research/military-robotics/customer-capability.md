# The customer is part of the autonomy stack

Reviewed 15 September 2026. These military personnel records and a separate civilian operating study extend the [adoption routes](adoption-routes.md) and [institutional map](institutions.md).

**Military adoption includes building the customer's ability to operate, assess and maintain unmanned equipment.** The public record documents a UAV qualification pilot followed by reported Army expansion, and a separate unit acquiring repair expertise previously supplied by manufacturers. This makes organizational learning observable alongside equipment purchases. A civilian hospital comparison shows a different customer helping develop the system, assigning continuing work and calculating a limited operating saving.

## Military operators: from a pilot to reported expansion

**CC01 — 27 October 2020.** A PLA Daily report republished by MOD describes a recent Army UAV occupational-training and qualification pilot. It names **陆军工程大学军械士官学校**, military units, **陆军无人机装备培训中心** and unnamed national industry bodies. The school describes using civilian training models, online resources and simulation. The mechanism combines military and industry certification through one assessment. This establishes a training collaboration; no commercial contractor or contract value is named. [S1]

**CC02 — 30 March 2023.** PLA Daily reports that, after two years of trials, this work formally expanded across Army units from 2023. An unnamed **陆军参谋部某中心** organized the late-March event, hosted by **陆军工程大学军械士官学校**. Nearly 100 soldiers participated and **46 passed certification**. Candidates completed prior coursework and on-job flying experience; military-unit and academy experts assessed them. The report says successful candidates **would receive** both a military UAV pilot qualification and a civil UAV pilot license. It does not establish individual issuance or total annual throughput. The 2020 “first” pilot and 2023 “first” formal event refer to different stages. [S2]

These are military newspaper accounts of practice and expansion, not the underlying qualification directive. Reported rollout is positive institutional evidence; universal completed coverage and unchanged credential rules in 2026 are separate questions. Names and titles above retain their dates.

## Military maintainers: outside expertise can move inside the user

**CC03 — 26 August 2025.** PLA Daily reports that an unnamed **陆军某保障大队** acquired the ability to repair an unnamed new UAV type, following a repaired aircraft's successful flight. Its leader says the work previously depended on manufacturers. The unit combined workplace learning, manufacturer training and academy instruction, and established a UAV maintenance work area. Partners, contract terms and comparative workload are undisclosed. The broader support center in the final paragraph is a future plan. **自主维修 here means military personnel perform repairs themselves; it does not mean robotic repair.** This unit is not linked to the certification cohort above. [S3]

**Analytical inference:** training can be a supplier contribution while also helping the customer reduce dependence on that supplier. More equipment in service need not produce proportional growth in outsourced repair revenue. Knowledge transfer may reduce total work, move expenditure inside the military, or do both; this report does not measure which occurred.

Together, these cases support an organizational interpretation of the [autonomy agenda](../autonomy/doctrine.md): unmanned force development entails personnel and institutional changes as well as machines. They do not demonstrate that a particular doctrinal essay caused a training program. Nor do civil credentials establish common civil/military software, operating permissions or requirements. The manufacturers in CC03 have no disclosed civilian/defense status.

## Civilian comparison: a hospital helps create the operating system

**CC04 — Shanghai Children's Hospital, historical deployment.** The hospital-authored 2020 study acknowledges Shanghai Science and Technology Commission grant **18511108400**, **智能机器人在数字化医院智慧物流建设中的应用研究**. Preparation with two test robots began in June 2018; cross-floor trials followed in September. January–June 2019 records cover four robots, 148 operating days and 2,924 nurse-station arrivals. Staff loaded, dispatched and received deliveries; hospital administrators coordinated equipment work; security and IT supported infrastructure. The final arrangement used two robots with a staffed elevator and two with automatic elevator operation. This is reported hospital use with a defined observation period. It is not an all-party labor comparison. [S4]

A **2025 CMA award-candidate disclosure**, recommended by the Shanghai Medical Association, explicitly lists the 2020 study and its grant-title project within a broader service-robot project. It assigns work as follows: [S5]

| Participant named in the disclosure | Reported contribution |
|---|---|
| 上海市儿童医院 | Requirements, workflows, hospital-information integration and operating/maintenance arrangements. |
| 上海大学 | Navigation/perception research and application. |
| 上海诺亚木木机器人科技有限公司 | Prototype production/testing, robot and scheduling software, station management and building/system interfaces. |
| 上海清淅智能科技有限公司 | Digital-twin operating platform and disinfection software within the broader project. |

The disclosure supplies participant-attributed workshare, **not an award decision, signed purchase or allocation of IP ownership**. Its broader project covers multiple robot functions; it cannot make every result a Noah logistics result. Grant 18511108400 supplies a funding acknowledgment, without an amount, subsidy share or formal grant-acceptance record.

There is a useful funding correction. The 2021 follow-up paper also acknowledges **21DZ2300700**. The commission's original list identifies that grant as children's food-and-medicine safety education, led by 李志玲 at the hospital, for June 2021–June 2022. The paper covers pharmacy services beyond robotics; its entire funding list must not become a robotics-program funding total. [S6]

## A historical cost account with a checkable arithmetic error

The hospital's **2021 Perspectives article** names NOAH robots and repeats the earlier costs: **one historical estimate**, not independent confirmation. Printed p4762 supplies: [S7]

| Item | CNY | Scope |
|---|---:|---|
| One-time robot-related investment/modification | 1,212,000 | Unitemized project spending; not a robot unit price. |
| Annual manual-delivery wage baseline | 75,600 | Modeled 1.8 workers × 3,500/month × 12. |
| Annual electricity | 4,672 | Four robots. |
| Annual maintenance | 50,000 | Four robots. |
| Published annual saving | 21,828 | Final subtraction substitutes 76,500 for the preceding 75,600 wage baseline. |
| Correct subtraction using the stated baseline | **20,928** | **75,600 − 4,672 − 50,000**; our arithmetic. |

This historical **13-ward** model omits capital recovery and some continuing work, and establishes no actual payroll reduction. The 2019 arrivals cover a different period and narrower ward scope; they cannot supply its cost-per-task denominator.

**Analytical interpretation:** the account supports a modest modeled recurring saving before capital and omitted work. It does not settle full return on investment. Contact reduction and service resilience could matter to the customer without appearing in the wage calculation; those benefits remain unpriced here. The [task-economics method](../industrial-base/task-economics/method.md) requires matching costs, accepted output, period and participating organizations before computing cost per useful task.

## How this changes an analyst's model

The receiving organization can contribute requirements, workflow knowledge, infrastructure, assessment, operators and maintainers. Those contributions belong in the autonomy stack and in the commercial account. Hospital co-development and military skills formation are separate examples of customer participation; neither transfers the other's costs or performance to a military system.

The resulting industrial hypotheses are testable:

- **Training and support sales:** suppliers may earn training/support revenue while building the user's skills; instruction could also be bundled with equipment. Track delivered instruction and contract scope separately from equipment revenue.
- **Customer learning:** repeated use lets the user absorb work. Compare external support bills with internal staffing and accepted service, rather than treating a smaller contractor bill as an equal reduction in total cost.
- **Reusable integration:** a supplier carries interfaces and deployment experience to another customer. Compare new local work with the prior installation; shared branding alone cannot measure reuse.

For this study, the next decisive records are successive training cohorts and one matched before/after maintenance account. Another hospital supplier announcement would add less than evidence about how the military user sustains its installed equipment.
