# Factory automation as a route into defense production

Reviewed 15 September 2026. These historical records connect a commercial equipment supplier to military-aircraft production and a separate civilian contract. They complement the [component and software relationships](lineage.md) and [institutional evidence map](institutions.md). The evidence concerns factory automation, not an unmanned military platform or an industrial-robot installed base.

## DP01 — Rifa and AVIC 625 Institute: Hanzhong final assembly

**Named relationship.** Zhejiang RIFA Precision Machinery Co., Ltd. (浙江日发精密机械股份有限公司, 002520) disclosed that its controlled subsidiary **浙江日发航空数字装备有限责任公司** and **中国航空工业集团北京航空制造工程研究所** (625研究所) jointly won a final-assembly pulse-line system for **中航飞机股份有限公司汉中飞机分公司**. These are the historical names in the disclosure. [S1]

The award notice was received on **14 September 2015**. Procurement **0730-156011010327/01** covered one system package for **RMB43 million**, including installation and associated services. This is the joint package price, not Rifa's recognized revenue or an individual robot price. The formal contract remained unsigned when the award was disclosed. [S1]

**Implementation and military attribution.** The FY2016 report, published 31 March 2017, names the Hanzhong system again, reports shipment and completed **staged acceptance** (阶段验收), and places this business in its military-aircraft discussion before separately describing civil work. The match rests on supplier, customer and distinctive system description; the later paragraph does not repeat the procurement number. [S2]

Record this as **staged acceptance reported by March 2017**. The report does not give the acceptance date or a final acceptance certificate. Its publication must not silently move the milestone to 31 December 2016. The evidence supports implementation beyond the earlier award, without identifying the aircraft model. [S2]

**Software relationship.** The award announcement identifies Italian MCM's **JFMX** software with this project's aircraft-automation application. This is an award-stage relationship: subsequent project-level acceptance does not independently establish the installed software version, configuration or retention. The issuer's descriptions of a domestic “first” and full automation are claims, not comparative measurements. [S1]

The annual report identifies MCM as **Machining Centers Manufacturing S.p.A.**, describes JFMX as workshop management/information integration, and reports Rifa's direct/indirect 100% ownership of MCM at that reporting date. This is a historical foreign-developed, group-owned software route; present ownership and dependence are not established here. [S2]

**Unresolved.** The cited records do not disclose factory-wide military/civil allocation, the joint winners' division of work, constituent equipment suppliers, remaining labor, utilization or measured production gains. Do not assign the system to Y-9, Y-20 or another aircraft. A pulse line does not establish an operator-free factory, a robot-arm count or airborne autonomy.

## DP01-C — The same supplier's civilian Airbus contracts

Rifa's 2016-072 disclosure reports September/October 2016 sales contracts with **Aritex (Shanghai) Machinery Manufacturing Co., Ltd.** for work platforms for the **Tianjin Airbus aircraft assembly project**, totaling **RMB5.155162 million**. It identifies Aritex Shanghai as the Chinese subsidiary of Spanish **Aritex Cading, S.A.**, acquired by **中航国际航空发展有限公司**. [S3]

The filing separates these signed sales contracts from the **2 November cooperation framework** and additional business still under negotiation. No platform-delivery or acceptance milestone is supplied here. This is civilian production-support work through an AVIC-related commercial channel; neither military end use nor JFMX involvement follows. [S3]

## What this adds to the ecosystem

**Analytical inference:** the joint bid shows one concrete route by which a commercial equipment subsidiary and a defense-industry institute enter a military production project together. Its relevant milestones are equipment supply and factory acceptance. This route belongs beside research-component reuse and military unmanned-system procurement, with its own evidence requirements.

The civilian comparison makes company-level labels insufficient: the same supplier participates in different production markets. Track the project, contractual role and acceptance stage. These records cannot turn equipment orders into aircraft output, quantify national military-robotics spending or establish a measured production advantage.
