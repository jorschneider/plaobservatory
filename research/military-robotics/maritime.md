# Chinese maritime robotics: four evidence pathways

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-09-15**. Bounded primary-source map; not a census. Observations, attributed claims, transaction disclosures and analytical inferences are distinguished below. No inference of military transfer follows merely from shared institutions, branding or oceanographic usefulness.

The main finding is institutional. Public evidence supports four different pathways: a military platform displayed under an official service-status statement; a defense conglomerate's named demonstrator and separate export offering; a laboratory-owned research vessel operated with professional crews; and a documented institute-to-company commercialization arrangement. Combining these into one “military autonomy maturity” score would discard the most useful evidence.

| Case | What the evidence actually reaches | Attribution that can be retained | Largest missing link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HSU001** | 2019 military parade observation; organizers' aggregate in-service claim covers displayed equipment | Visible model marking; Navy association in official formation reporting | Named designer/manufacturer, model-specific acceptance record and field performance |
| **虎鲸号 / Orca** | CSSC's 2024 public unveiling | CSSC 716 Research Institute leads a consortium | Domestic customer/acceptance and demonstrated human/machine division |
| **珠海云 / Zhuhai Yun** | Construction, delivery, reported trials, classification milestone and scientific work | Separate shipowner, designer, builder, supplier and operating institute | Consistently defined intervention and staffing data across repeated missions |
| **海翼 / Haiyi** | Scientific observations plus a bounded commercialization transaction and reported product sales | CAS developer/research partners; commercial manufacturer; separately identified scientific partners | Contract-level end-use and comparability between research and commercial variants |

## MM01 — HSU001: keep the positive service claim and the OEM unknown

**Observed identity and event.** A Xinhua photograph from the October 1, 2019 parade shows two underwater vehicles bearing the marking **HSU001**. The marking was visually inspected; the accompanying English caption describes the unmanned combat formation without spelling out the model. This is a photographed identifier, not a recovered manufacturer registry. [M01]

**Military attribution.** Xinhua's same-day report identifies underwater uncrewed vehicles in the third unmanned combat formation. Formation political commissar Li Yuhao describes the underwater platform as a Navy intelligent platform for environmental reconnaissance and surveillance. The formation included an unnamed Southern Theater Command Navy support base; parade participation does not identify a routine operating unit or purchaser. [M02]

**Status.** At the August 29, 2019 briefing, organizer Cai Zhijun stated that equipment displayed in the equipment formations would all be domestically produced equipment already in service. Preserve this as an **attributed aggregate service-status statement applicable to the observed parade equipment**. It is stronger than “a prototype was seen,” while supplying no model-specific acceptance date, fleet quantity or readiness result. [M03]

**Human/machine role.** The sources establish a military formation and attributed platform functions. They do not show how a mission is assigned, when a human intervenes, or how much behavior is automated at sea. A transported vehicle on parade supplies no independent autonomy test.

**Attribution boundary.** Designer, OEM, component suppliers and parent industrial group remain **unassigned** in this record. Do not attach HSU001 to CAS, Harbin Institute of Technology or CSSC merely because those institutions develop other underwater systems.

**2025 extension, kept separate.** Xinhua reported a Navy-led maritime unmanned formation containing underwater vehicles, surface vessels and other unmanned maritime equipment on September 3, 2025, with attributed autonomous detection/recognition functions. Organizers had stated on August 20 that all 2025 parade weapons were domestic in-service principal equipment. These are meaningful official service and capability claims for that formation, but the cited reports name neither individual models nor OEMs. Do not use them to update HSU001's functions, identify Orca as a participant, or infer readiness rates. [M04–M05]

**Analyst implication — inference:** Separate the confidence that a military program exists from confidence in its industrial attribution and operating performance. The first is much stronger here than the latter two.

## MM02 — Orca: program lead, exhibition stage and a distinct export comparator

**Named program and lead.** CSSC's November 14, 2024 report on the Zhuhai airshow says **716 Research Institute led joint development of 虎鲸号 with other industry participants**, and that this was its first public appearance. The institute is a member of **中国船舶集团有限公司 / China State Shipbuilding Corporation (CSSC)** in this source. Preserve “lead developer with unnamed partners”; this source does not identify a sole manufacturing legal entity or assign particular subsystems to its partners. [M06]

**Stage and machine role.** The producer describes a large uncrewed combat surface vessel. The cited unveiling report supplies no independently observed autonomous mission, human supervision arrangement, PLA acceptance or export customer. “Uncrewed” alone does not establish human-free mission decisions.

**Export comparator, not a renamed Orca.** A September 27, 2021 CCTV report hosted by CSSC describes a smaller, 20-ton multi-purpose uncrewed vessel, interviews 716's international-business official Fan Jun, and explicitly says the equipment was developed for military export requirements. It describes autonomous navigation alongside satellite remote control. The text does not name the vessel: retain **“2021 CSSC export demonstrator; model unconfirmed in this report.”** Do not merge it with the 2024 Orca, silently label it JARI-USV-A, or turn intended export sales into a disclosed contract. [M07]

**Institutional overlap that is actually evidenced.** A different CSSC report says 716 developed the vessel for a China Meteorological Administration-led cloud/fog observation project, with CSSC 702, 717 and the Eighth Academy participating. The project began in April 2022 and the November 2023 report describes a completed meteorological observation trial. This demonstrates the same institute participating in defense-oriented development and a named civilian research project. It does **not** identify common source code, components, data pipelines or a transfer to a Navy program. [M08]

**Analyst implication — inference:** CSSC is not a single undifferentiated robot OEM. The case justifies institute-level research and program-specific partner records. Export-office marketing, civilian project delivery and military acceptance require distinct evidence fields even when the same institute appears.

## MM03 — Zhuhai Yun: the operating organization is part of the autonomy stack

**Ownership and roles.** The shipowner is **南方海洋科学与工程广东省实验室（珠海）**, a Guangdong provincial laboratory organized by the Zhuhai municipal government with Sun Yat-sen University leading construction and management. It is not a CAS institute merely because its director is a CAS academician. The lab specified the vessel's concept and requirements. [M09]

The December 18, 2020 contract announcement identifies **中国舰船研究设计中心** as designer and **中船黄埔文冲船舶** as builder. It explicitly identifies **CSSC 704 Research Institute** as a supplier of core systems and equipment, without enumerating the delivered package. The 2022 launch account assigns design/construction general contracting to the design center and construction to Huangpu Wenchong. Use these separately attributed roles; attendance at the ceremony by another company does not establish its supplied subsystem. [M09–M10]

**Documented human organization.** The **Ministry of Natural Resources' Second Institute of Oceanography** states that it manages operations and maintenance. Its January 12, 2023 delivery report says it sent professional research-vessel crew and survey-management specialists, working with the laboratory shipowner's teams under the institute's vessel safety-management system. It reports autonomous-navigation trials and uncrewed-vessel launch/recovery work; laboratory director Chen Dake is quoted reporting 12 continuous hours of autonomous navigation. This is a named participant's trial account, not a third-party reliability audit. [M11]

**Autonomy and acceptance scope.** The lab's original 2020 navigation project explicitly targeted open-water autonomous navigation and **R1 remote control with crew aboard**. The lab's history records a November 23, 2022 CCS open-water autonomous-navigation classification certificate. A laboratory retrospective reports specialist trials completed April 26, 2023 and first scientific operations August 21–26, 2023. These milestones support a working civilian research infrastructure. Classification and laboratory entry into service do not constitute Navy acceptance or authorization for every operating environment. [M12–M14]

**Do not let the mother-ship label erase scope.** A 2023 account of the team's ISOOS research presents the vessel as transport and control center for multiple types of observing platforms, with communications, launch/recovery and scientific task management as parts of the proposed observing system. The same account says practical cross-platform, cross-domain networked applications are at an early stage. Distinguish the research architecture from what each trial demonstrably exercised. [M15]

**Analyst implication — inference:** Civilian oceanographic autonomy rests on a shipowner, vessel operator, crew, survey managers, shipyard, systems suppliers and classification processes. Hardware capability comparisons should measure the work displaced and the supporting work retained. “Unmanned system mother ship” is especially easy to misread as “a ship with no people.”

## MM04 — Haiyi: a concrete research-to-commercial mechanism with a defined boundary

**Research provenance and output.** CAS's Institute of Deep-sea Science and Engineering credits **CAS Shenyang Institute of Automation (SIA)** with developing Haiyi in a March 2017 research report. A July 2021 report describes SIA and the deep-sea institute's laboratories using a 7,000-metre-class Haiyi variant for September 2018 scientific observations. It links the resulting ocean-circulation and upper-ocean research papers. This establishes a scientific observation-to-publication chain, not a military customer. [M16–M17]

**Commercial rights and scale, as disclosed by the applicant.** Deepinfar's May 15, 2026 SSE-filed inquiry response describes SIA's 2016 operating arrangement, 2018 exclusive production/operation license, and **November 20, 2023 RMB10 million transfer agreement** for 13 patents/applications and associated know-how. The agreement covers commercialized **Haiyi products of 2,000 metres or shallower**, with production/sales rights and retained-use restrictions described in the table. This does not transfer every deeper research variant. The same filing reports **24 Haiyi sets sold in 2025, generating RMB16.6513 million**. Its customer cell spans 2023–2025 and names CAS's South China Sea Institute of Oceanology and an anonymized customer; it does not assign the 2025 total to that institute. These are issuer disclosures, not independently inspected contracts or a regulator's technical endorsement. [M18, printed pp. 8-1-128 and 8-1-72]

**Machine and human roles in a named scientific activity.** Deepinfar's April 3, 2025 manufacturer report describes a Haiyi observation project with the **Hainan provincial ocean department** and **三亚海洋实验室**. The glider gathered oceanographic measurements; the team deployed and recovered it. The report does not disclose a continuous human-intervention log, contract value or exact event date. Record it as a manufacturer-reported completed scientific activity, not an independently accepted autonomous-performance result. [M19]

**Institutional boundary.** Keep SIA (developer), CAS Institute of Deep-sea Science and Engineering (research partner), CAS South China Sea Institute of Oceanology (separately disclosed customer) and Deepinfar (commercial issuer/manufacturer) as different actors. Neither a shared product name nor the manufacturer's other defense business establishes military use of these research variants or observations.

**Analyst implication — inference:** A mechanism-level record can answer a question that a “civil–military fusion” label cannot: which institution supplied rights, to which enterprise, for what product scope, and with what disclosed commercial activity. This establishes a research-to-commercial transfer, with no military destination established in the reviewed record.

## Collection priorities that would change the assessment

1. **Resolve military identities with documents:** model-specific acceptance, purchaser or developer announcements that connect an observed identifier to a legal supplier. Preserve official service claims meanwhile.
2. **Measure civilian operating burden:** repeat-mission reports defining autonomous time, interventions, retained crew duties, maintenance, recovery and scientific data quality. Long voyages alone cannot isolate these variables.
3. **Follow the transaction boundary:** obtain the actual Haiyi transfer contract/patent schedule and product-specific customer evidence. Treat research variants and commercial variants separately until mapped.
4. **Track each institute by program:** compare dated civilian and military-oriented contracts or project roles. A shared institution is a real network connection; common software, equipment or operational transfer needs its own evidence.
