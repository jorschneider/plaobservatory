# Reusable autonomy still has to become a working site

Reviewed 15 September 2026. This civilian comparison follows EACON's Australian retrofit project from a partnership plan through reported production use. It extends the [repeat-deployment evidence](thesis-tests.md) and [five-layer reuse comparison](civilian-reuse.md). No military relationship is established for this project.

**The useful finding is that an existing autonomy product can travel across organizations and vehicle assets while local infrastructure, validation and operating practices still need work.** Reuse and implementation are complementary parts of adoption. The records below make both visible; they do not isolate a learning curve or quantify the work saved.

## Two deployment clocks

| Observation | Start and endpoint actually described | What an analyst can conclude |
|---|---|---|
| EACON's final prospectus says some deployments achieved driverless operation within **three days of vehicle arrival**. | Arrival to operation in selected, unnamed deployments; preparation before arrival is outside this clock. | A company-reported short commissioning interval is possible. This is neither a typical duration nor the Australian project's duration. [CD01, p.151] |
| Its June 2026 Australian update reports **more than five months** of preparation after January telecommunications orders. | Infrastructure and operational-readiness work before production operation. | The project had work beyond fitting autonomy to a truck. This is not a five-month algorithm-development measure. [CD04, “A Fleet in Live Production”] |

These are different projects or samples, activities and starting points. Dividing one duration by the other would create a meaningless speed ratio. A useful comparison would hold the product generation, site conditions, work scope and start/end milestones constant, then ask whether later deployments need less work.

## The Australian project, with its stages preserved

**12 May 2025 publication — a partner describes the plan.** Thiess reports a signed tripartite project with EACON and Norton Gold Fields. The initial stage would equip two Thiess Komatsu HD1500 trucks and validate their autonomous functionality; a later phase would extend to an active production pit. Early 2026 was the target for full production trials, not an observed result. [CD02, rollout paragraphs]

**12 January 2026 publication — a retrofit reaches test cycles.** EACON reports a used HD1500 conducting autonomous test cycles without a safety driver at Mulgarrie. It describes a first-phase validation milestone and a second retrofitted vehicle dispatched to the site. This is progress beyond a partnership announcement; it does not yet establish a production fleet. [CD03, opening and phase-one paragraphs]

**18 June 2026 publication — reported day-shift production use.** EACON identifies six retrofitted HD1500 trucks at Havana Pit, distinguishes earlier validation at Mulgarrie, and describes telecommunications, local servers, operating-area preparation, risk assessment and process changes. The update also identifies local support and training work; night-shift operation and further evaluation remain subsequent steps. Retain the two place names and the day-shift boundary. [CD04, opening, “A Fleet in Live Production” and support paragraph]

**20 June 2026 information cutoff — operation and revenue remain different observations.** The final prospectus states that Australian closed-environment autonomous-mining products and solutions had generated no revenue as of its latest practicable date. It also describes a local delivery/support organization. This does not negate the reported trial or operating milestone; nor does it establish later commercial terms. [CD01, p.150; definition on p.17]

## What is reused, and what still has to be established?

The project reuses a supplier's autonomy offering and existing truck assets, with the mining partner and customer supplying a receiving organization. The evidence supports a real implementation route, beyond theoretical technological relevance. It leaves the contract's detailed division of cost, responsibility and IP undisclosed.

The analyst should therefore maintain two accounts. The **asset account** records the product, interface, vehicle and experience brought into the project. The **work account** records the new integration, validation, infrastructure, training and support needed to achieve its accepted output. A reused asset can reduce work without eliminating it; new work can create knowledge useful for another installation. Neither effect should be assigned a monetary value from these announcements alone.

The comparison changes how to read a military-adoption claim. First establish the receiving organization and the relevant requirements, then follow the particular configuration into acceptance and use. The [adoption-governance study](adoption-governance.md) identifies public equipment-quality roles; the [hospital procurement example](procurement.md#p04--prior-hospital-deployments-as-a-qualification-requirement-2024-jl1303-w10136) shows a buyer requesting prior customer-acceptance evidence. These are separate institutional observations, not evidence that the Australian system or its software transferred to the PLA.

For a subsequent project, the decisive comparison is **what carried over, what had to change, who did the work, and what output the customer accepted**. Existing records justify the reuse hypothesis and identify concrete work categories. They do not establish that all-party effort falls on each deployment, that a single integrator captures the savings, or that military adaptation follows the Australian process.
