# Military air autonomy: four systems with different evidence ceilings

Research checked 15 September 2026. This is a selected public-source map, not a fleet census. Latest reviewed issuer reporting covers January–June 2026 and was published in August 2026. Older model-specific observations retain their dates; corporate growth or a newer family-level statement does not silently update an individual model's status. Chinese legal names control; English names below are conventional renderings or translations.

## What changes the analyst's understanding

**The air domain already contains positive evidence of military use.** WZ-7 has an official PLA training report and explicit in-service wording. GJ-2 has a PLA-operated flying display, documented military maintenance work, and a subsequent observation independently attributed by Japan's Ministry of Defense. Neither record establishes the allocation of every decision between software and people. [A1–A5]

**The commercial and military names require separate records.** The manufacturer describes automated flight functions for Wing Loong-2; those claims cannot be transferred to a photographed GJ-2 by resemblance. AVIC is attributable at group level for the two military systems below, while exact legal manufacturers and variant relationships remain incompletely resolved here. [A2–A6]

**Civilian adaptation can be more observable than military autonomy.** CH-4 has a named export-contract channel, civilian customers, individual-aircraft certification work, and a customer account of ground-station commands. This is direct evidence of a defense-industrial product operating in civilian work. It does not establish a civilian algorithm transferred into a PLA aircraft. [A7–A10, A13]

**A real industrial organization and growing unmanned business do not establish that each advertised aircraft is operational.** The FH-97A issuer explicitly reported prototype validation in April 2025. Its H1 2026 report discusses wider business and training/test infrastructure without identifying FH-97A acceptance. Preserve the dated development evidence rather than labeling the model either fielded or permanently stuck at prototype. [A11–A12]

The useful autonomy questions concern task authorization, ground control, supervision, maintenance, and acceptance. These sources show parts of that workflow; they neither demonstrate compliance with weapons policy nor establish an autonomous weapons decision process.

### WZ-7: distinguish in-service evidence from autonomy attribution

**Observation and stage.** PLA Daily's 11 November 2021 report says the Air Force had comprehensively incorporated WZ-7 into realistic training, describing a recent exercise rather than only an exhibition. Its 2022 GJ-2 article separately calls WZ-7 an in-service unmanned aircraft. AVIC's November 2022 press conference places WZ-7 among its equipment that had reached military units. These mutually compatible records warrant a positive military-use entry. [A1–A3]

**Function and authority.** The report describes ordered takeoff and transmission of information to a command post. It does not identify who programs flight tasks, whether a human directly flies any phase, how much onboard planning occurs, or how operators interrupt/recover the aircraft. The grammatical subject “WZ-7” performing a task is not proof that onboard software independently decided it. Do not import “autonomous” from a different product description. [A1]

**Attribution/transaction ceiling.** AVIC group attribution is supported; no reviewed model-specific contract assigns exact legal OEM, supplier revenue or unit count. There is no basis here for assigning every component to a group subsidiary.

**Falsifier or upgrade.** A correction to the named PLA report would reduce the status claim. A formal model-specific acceptance, operating or training document identifying personnel responsibilities would materially strengthen the autonomy claim. A corporate document naming the legal manufacturing entity would close the attribution gap.

### GJ-2: military personnel and an observed aircraft, with a variant boundary

**Observation and stage.** The 13 November 2022 PLA Daily account reports an actual GJ-2 flight at the airshow and Air Force personnel performing assembly, maintenance and test flights. Japan's Joint Staff later identifies one GJ-2 in its 26 February 2025 flight record; the annual report calls this its first identification during air-defense measures. This adds an external observation beyond a Chinese exhibition narrative. [A3–A4]

**Human role.** The military article exposes a support organization and hands-on preparation. It does not provide the division between remote piloting, automated flight control and operator approval. Reported routine unit work is stronger than a concept rendering; it is not an audited measure of reliability or crew workload. [A3]

**Attribution.** A 30 September 2021 on-site report, explicitly attributing its information to AVIC, includes GJ-2 among AVIC products. Retain this as group attribution with mediated provenance. Do not promote the frequently repeated GJ-2 = Wing Loong-2 equivalence to an exact configuration identity without a clearer primary mapping. [A5]

**Useful industrial comparator.** AVIC's own November 2022 post explicitly describes Wing Loong-2 as an AVIC-developed system containing aircraft, ground station and support system. It claims autonomous takeoff/landing and cruise, alongside ground relay control. The same post expressly identifies Wing Loong-2H emergency rescue as a civilian adaptation of the Wing Loong-2 system. These are manufacturer claims and a documented stated product lineage; they do not establish that the GJ-2 observed in 2025 used that control configuration. [A6]

**Falsifier or upgrade.** A Japanese identification correction would affect the 2025 observation. A manufacturer/issuer document explicitly mapping the military designation to its legal manufacturer and variant would resolve the current identity gap. Acceptance or operating records would be needed to assign demonstrated autonomy functions.

### CH-4: a military-export industrial family with observable civilian work

**Attribution and stage.** CASC's 2022 article expressly attributes the upgraded CH-4 research aircraft to its 11th Academy and 航天彩虹无人机股份有限公司. It reports completed research trials including autonomous takeoff/landing and waypoint flight. This is a tested manufacturer claim for that upgrade, not evidence covering every CH-4 configuration. [A7]

**Export transaction.** CASC reports that 航天长征国际贸易有限公司 and 航天彩虹无人机股份有限公司 signed an incremental CH-4 export procurement contract exceeding **US$100 million on 31 August 2022**. This is a named manufacturer–exporter transaction and positive reported order evidence. The final country/customer, unit quantity and revenue-recognition schedule are undisclosed in the article; it does not establish a PLA purchase or completed delivery of the new order. [A13]

**Civilian result and human authority.** Yunnan's Natural Resources Department says its emergency-mapping base received a CH-4 in 2019 and describes the October 2025 qualification test conducted with 彩虹无人机科技有限公司. The ground station issued takeoff and return commands; personnel coordinated preparation, flight authorization and the testing program. The article reports accumulated civilian flights as well as the test. This is customer evidence of continuing use and human task initiation, although it does not provide raw logs or the complete allocation of flight-control responsibility. [A8]

**Certification boundary.** CASC's November 2024 announcement specifically concerns an individual aircraft's special airworthiness certificate and describes work with already-delivered civilian aircraft. It also says standard-airworthiness work was continuing. Do not relabel this a fleet-wide approval, military acceptance, or permission for every operating environment. [A9]

**Industrial relevance.** The H1 2026 issuer report names CH-4 in its product family and explains that qualified military-trade companies mediate export business. Customer concentration at those intermediaries should not be read as concentration of ultimate user states. The issuer also reports domestic equipment business at company level; this does not identify CH-4's PLA user or quantity. [A10]

**Production ecosystem, at family level.** CASC’s February 2025 Taizhou retrospective describes local-government infrastructure, an aviation production base and links to the region’s automotive manufacturing suppliers. This makes local industrial policy and manufacturing networks visible alongside the defense academy. Its asserted supply-chain compatibility is not a verified CH-4 bill of materials, installed localization percentage or audited cost advantage. [A14]

**Falsifier or upgrade.** A civilian qualification correction would narrow the aircraft-specific result. A named PLA acceptance or operating document would change the military-status classification. A cross-domain engineering record identifying reused software, data or people would establish a more specific transfer mechanism than a shared product family.

### FH-97A: keep the aircraft's milestone separate from company-level delivery

**Dated model status.** In its 2 April 2025 investor-relations record, the issuer answered a question naming FH-97A by saying preliminary work was complete and the prototype was undergoing test validation. The answer does not say first flight or military acceptance had occurred. It is the model-specific primary status statement verified in this packet, not an assertion that nothing changed afterward. [A11]

**Current organizational evidence.** The H1 2026 report identifies 航天时代飞鸿技术有限公司 as a controlled subsidiary and CASC as actual controller; it describes broader unmanned business and test/training capability construction. It does not contain an FH-97A/97A model mention in the extracted report. General export or delivery language therefore cannot update this model to operational status. [A12]

**Human role and unknowns.** Prototype validation establishes an engineering stage; there is no model-specific record here of task assignment, remote operation, supervisory workload, intervention authority or recovery. Exact contractual manufacturer, customer, quantity and acceptance remain unverified. Corporate ownership of a relevant subsidiary is not itself a model-specific purchase order.

**Falsifier or upgrade.** A dated primary manufacturer flight announcement would advance the flight-test record; a named military acceptance or routine-unit report would advance service evidence. Neither would, by itself, verify claims of autonomous mission decisions. Subsequent primary material should supersede the April 2025 status rather than being forced into it.

## Leads intentionally not promoted

A 30 June 2026 Securities Star reproduction of an investor-interaction answer repeats that FH-97A remains in development; the original dated exchange answer was not retrieved, so it is not the status anchor here. Likewise, generic flying-wing/loyal-wingman imagery and unnamed new-aircraft competition reports do not identify FH-97A. A manufacturer/issuer statement that an unspecified product won a competition cannot resolve that naming problem.

The 2025 parade organizers' aggregate statement that displayed equipment was domestic and in service is positive official evidence within its scope. It does not retroactively date 2021–2022 exhibits, identify a marketed lookalike, or supply readiness and quantity data. This packet does not use that aggregate statement to classify any unnamed aircraft.
