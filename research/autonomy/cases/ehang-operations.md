# EHang EH216-S: aircraft autonomy, human operations and commercial readiness

Case EH01 • Sources checked 10 September 2026 • Civilian passenger aviation

**A pilotless aircraft can be a real technical achievement while its service still depends on ground personnel and organized support.** EHang's disclosures make that distinction unusually visible: autonomous flight, operator certificates, internal trials, ground-crew preparation and public ticketed service appear as separate claims. The practical analyst task is to keep their dates and scopes separate.

This is an attributed public-record case, not an independent flight evaluation, a legal opinion or evidence of military transfer.

June and August's company-description sections also call EH216-S commercially operated under operator certificates. June's detailed operational section distinguishes internal trial commercial operations from public ticketed launch. Different scopes therefore coexist within the same disclosure; the chronology is not a simple reversal. This is a scope reconciliation, not a finding that certificates were revoked, aircraft could not fly, or no paid flight ever occurred. The evidence reviewed does not settle those claims. It does show why a single “commercialized” checkbox loses information.

## Which function belongs to the aircraft, and which to people?

EHang describes automated flight along predetermined routes without an onboard pilot, while allowing ground control. Its operational platform manages flight schedules, ground crews and landing facilities; the report describes that platform as internally trialed. These are issuer descriptions of the architecture, not independently tested autonomy or staffing ratios. The annual report also distinguishes aerial-media fleet management from passenger aviation; its drone-show scale must not become a passenger-aircraft supervision ratio. [FY2025 report, printed pp69–71 / PDF pages73–75, product and operating-system sections](https://ir.ehang.com/static-files/bf59a860-eb13-4c99-af9d-433626ae1f03).

| Function | Evidence state | Remaining question |
|---|---|---|
| Physical flight on predetermined routes | Issuer-described autonomous function | Exact mode/version and independent task-level performance. |
| Ground monitoring and control | Issuer-described human/control-system role | Staffing, interventions, workload and responsibility allocation in a named service. |
| Crew preparation and service organization | Issuer-reported ongoing preparation | Approved program, current qualifications, actual roster and accepted operating scope. |
| Public passenger service | Dated certification and trial/readiness statements | Route-specific ticketed-flight ledger and revenue/cost reconciliation. |

## A formal civilian training standard makes the human role explicit

CAAC's **MH/T 2019-2026**, *Training requirements for remote pilot of large civil unmanned aircraft system*, was issued **24 April 2026** and implemented **1 May 2026**. The CAAC page was posted in May; that is not the issue date. Its stated scope is preparing training syllabi and training remote pilots for large civil unmanned aircraft systems conducting **segregated flight**; it covers basic-license and type-rating training. Subjects include human factors, workload/crew coordination, automated and manual flight, control handover and emergencies. These subjects do not establish an operator's qualifications or implemented controls. [CAAC record](https://www.caac.gov.cn/PHONE/XXGK_17/XXGK/BZGF/HYBZ/202605/t20260515_230830.html), [final standard, cover and §§1, 5.1.11, 5.2, 6](https://www.caac.gov.cn/PHONE/XXGK_17/XXGK/BZGF/HYBZ/202605/P020260515629041898644.pdf).

EHang links this training framework to its ground-crew preparations in the June release. That establishes the company's stated connection, not a finding that every clause applies to every EH216-S operation. The approved operator-specific program and operating permissions remain separate evidence objects. The February consultation draft is not the final standard.

## The economics question is a service question

In August, EHang credited a battery-cooling vehicle with utilization of **12–15 flights per aircraft per day**. The release supplies no matched site, observation interval, schedule, fare-paying share or support-cost ledger for that number. Keep it as an issuer operating-support claim, not an observed system-wide capacity assumption. [Q2 release, final paragraph of “Deepening Domestic Operational Readiness”](https://ir.ehang.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ehang-reports-second-quarter-2026-unaudited-financial-results/).

For a named service, collect accepted flights, completed passenger trips and paid passenger trips separately. Then attach cancellations, weather/maintenance exclusions, battery/ground infrastructure, crew time, insurance, facility cost and actual period revenue. Aggregate deliveries, successful test flights and aircraft manufacturing margin cannot supply that denominator.

**Analyst inference:** autonomy can remove an onboard role while increasing the importance of ground operations, qualification and infrastructure. This case identifies those work categories; it does not yet quantify net labor savings or establish profitability. The next useful document is a route/operator-specific service log matched to the ground-support ledger, not another cumulative flight milestone.
