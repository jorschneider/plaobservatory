## What changed

This additive release creates **52 supplier × capability assessments** from **66 atomic new evidence records**. 
It separates **25 evidence-qualified scorecards** from **27 provisional/watchlist scorecards**. 
Legacy Atlas research-priority scores are retained but are not used here.

## How to read this ranking

Systemic importance is a bounded analytical triage score, not a claim about revenue, production volume, or wartime indispensability. It rewards source-backed criticality, production evidence, cross-domain capability leverage, and technical frontier. The evidence-qualified lower bound penalizes weaker support. Only scorecards with E3/E4 evidence enter a rank; production automation appears in its own lane rather than being equated to weapons subsystems.

## Analytical takeaways

1. **The strongest scarcity signal is narrow, not generic.** Beijing Rational Navigation Control is the only v1.2 scorecard awarded a C4 because the issuer explicitly says four finalized inertial-navigation variants are sole suppliers. Other technically important companies are capped at C3 without comparable limited-source language.
2. **Production-enabling robotics are now evidenced, not just hypothesized.** Guangdong Xirui has two final CSSC Shanghai Waigaoqiao robotic-grinding workstation awards totaling RMB 10.37m. That is a meaningful tier-2 industrial-automation signal, but it is deliberately ranked in a production-enabler lane, not as a weapons supplier.
3. **C-UAS remains a crowded integrator layer.** Pre-award and comparative-test records are analytically valuable for monitoring, but the 21-of-26 ‘excellent’ trial cohort cautions against treating a generic C-UAS offer as a firm-level bottleneck without a named completed contract or differentiated component dependency.
4. **Robotics/AI demand is increasingly interface- and model-oriented.** The robot-dog tender’s URDF/SDF, CAD, inertial-property, and secondary-control API requirements are a strong R&D integration signal, not proof of fielding or a named supplier.
5. **Foreign dependencies remain bounded and mostly historical.** The newly added export-control cases identify named tools, consumables, or test equipment and direct Chinese counterparties, but they do not establish a current defense-program dependency. They therefore do not change any supplier criticality or fragility score.

## Gap list

- Find delivery/acceptance or sustainment evidence for high-scoring component suppliers; batch-production language is not equivalent to fielding.
- Resolve final award wording and exact dates for PLA C-UAS result notices before they can be promoted beyond provisional monitoring.
- Seek named qualification barriers, dual-source disclosures, or requalification requirements before scoring substitution risk for any firm other than the explicit sole-supplier case.
- Match new private robotics firms to unified social-credit codes, corporate parents, and actual production sites.
- Disaggregate historical foreign tool cases to fabs/programs only when a primary source makes that link explicit.

## Source use

Primary evidence is dominated by Chinese listed-company regulatory filings, official CSSC tender-result pages, PLA procurement notices, and U.S. BIS orders. Government/company profiles and industry-test reports are retained as attributed or contextual corroboration, not used to invent awards. Every scorecard row preserves the exact source URL and a record-level caveat.
